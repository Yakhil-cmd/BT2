Can an attacker who controls a `signer-manager` contract call `grant-signer-key(signer-key, signer-manager, auth-id, signer-sig)` once, and because `used-signer-key-grants` is keyed only by `{signer-key, signer-manager, auth-id}` (not by bond-index or amount), have `verify-signer-key-grant` (checked only against `signer-key-grants`, not `used-signer-key-grants`) authorize stake registrations across two distinct `protocol-bond-memberships` entries for the same staker, violating AUTHORITY == 'one grant per (staker, amount, cycle, chain)'? Preconditions: a live grant recorded in `signer-key-grants`; two open bonds accepting registrations. Call sequence: `grant-signer-key` -> `register-for-bond` (bond A) -> `register-for-bond` (bond B) both calling `verify-signer-key-grant`. Impact: High — signer authorises stacking actions for a second bond it never granted. Proof idea: chainstate test asserting the second `register-for-bond` call fails when the grant is scoped only to bond A.
