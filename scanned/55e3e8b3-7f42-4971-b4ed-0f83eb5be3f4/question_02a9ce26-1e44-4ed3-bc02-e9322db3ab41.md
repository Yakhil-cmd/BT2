[Function: system-borrow] Attacker A takes the maximum single borrow allowed and, rather than being an economically rational borrower who wants the funds, does so purely to grief; victim B, a liquidator (an ordinary unprivileged principal, not a sanctioned executor) who wants to `liquidate` a genuinely unhealthy third-party position and, per the market's design, must route freshly drawn liquidity through `system-borrow` to fund the liquidation bid, is denied because `CAP-DEBT` is saturated. This leaves the unhealthy position (belonging to a separate victim B2, or B acting as both liquidator and the party harmed by the frozen liquidation queue) unresolved. Invariant broken: EVALUABILITY (a liquidatable position cannot be liquidated). Impact: temporary freezing of funds (the liquidation opportunity/collateral value is frozen for as long as the cap is saturated). Proof: simnet test where a distressed position exists, A saturates `CAP-DEBT` via `system-borrow`, then assert B's `liquidate`-driven `system-borrow` call reverts, versus succeeding when A is absent.
