Target: `stake-update` in pox-5.clar and `pox_lock_update_v5`'s `PoxInvalidIncrease` guard. Attacker action: call `stake-update` with `amount-increase=0` and `cycles-to-extend` large enough to push `unlock-cycle` far into the future, then in a SECOND transaction in the same block call `stake-update` again with a large `amount-increase`, attempting to have the FIRST call's `new-lock-amount` (== old amount, no increase) be what's validated by the Clarity `>= unlocked amount-increase` check while the underlying `pox_lock_update_v5`'s `bal.amount_locked() > new_total_locked -> PoxInvalidIncrease` check operates on a live balance already mutated by call 1 within the same block, probing whether `current-info` (from the `staker-info` map) read at the start of call 2's Clarity execution reflects call 1's write. Preconditions: staker has active stake, sufficient unlocked STX for at least one increase. Call sequence: within one block, tx1 stake-update(amount-increase=X1), tx2 stake-update(amount-increase=X2) — Clarity guarantees sequential execution so `staker-info` after tx1 must be visible to tx2; verify `new-lock-amount` in tx2 correctly is `(prior amount-ustx after tx1) + X2`, not `(pre-block amount-ustx) + X2` (which would undercount and let `pox_lock_update_v5`'s own live-balance check `PoxInvalidIncrease` be bypassed since it independently reads the live `STXBalance`, not the map). Scoped impact: Critical — an increase validated against a stale, lower `unlocked` requirement (double-counting funds already committed in tx1) lets `stake-update` lock more STX than the staker's true current unlocked balance covers.
