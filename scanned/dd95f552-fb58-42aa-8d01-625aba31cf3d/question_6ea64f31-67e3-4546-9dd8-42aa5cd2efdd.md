Target: `StackStxOp::parse_from_tx` reward-address derivation (`first_output.address.clone().coerce_hash_mode()`). Attacker crafts a StackStx op whose first Bitcoin output (parsed as `reward_addr`) is a script the attacker doesn't control but which coerces to a hash-mode-compatible PoxAddress belonging to a victim signer, while `sender` (from the PreStxOp chain) is the attacker's own address. Preconditions: valid PreStxOp with attacker's output at vout 1; crafted first tx output pointing at a victim-controlled hash. Call sequence: `parse_from_tx` builds `StackStxOp{ sender: attacker, reward_addr: victim_hash, stacked_ustx, ... }` -> pox-5 `stack-stx` locks `stacked_ustx` from `sender` (attacker, correct debit) but registers `reward_addr` as the victim's PoX payout address. Equality: REWARD CONSERVATION — sBTC/BTC rewards paid for cycle C to `reward_addr` must equal rewards earned by the STX actually locked by `sender`; if `sender` and `reward_addr` can diverge without consent, the victim (who never locked anything) is registered to receive PoX payouts sourced from the attacker's locked STX weight, or conversely the victim's declared reward address absorbs rewards it didn't earn while burdening its unrelated BTC address bookkeeping. Scoped impact: High/Critical depending on whether reward misdirection is later reconciled; classify as Critical since rewards paid could be double counted against a reward_addr not chosen by its own stake. Proof idea: chainstate test asserting `reward_addr` in a persisted StackStxOp always matches an address independently authorized by `sender` (no cross-check currently exists at the Rust layer) and trace whether pox-5's reward set entry keys strictly off `(sender, reward_addr)` pairing so no other staker's cycle rewards are diluted.
