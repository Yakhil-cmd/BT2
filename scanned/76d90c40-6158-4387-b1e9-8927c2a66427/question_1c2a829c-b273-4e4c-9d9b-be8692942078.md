[stack_stx.rs] If `StackStxOp::get_sender_txid` requires `vout == 1` on the burnchain input, can an attacker submit a `StackStxOp` whose input actually references a *different* PreStxOp's second output than the one that funded their own PreStxOp, causing `sender` to resolve to an address that never issued this specific `PreStxOp`? Precondition: two distinct PreStxOps exist on the burnchain from different unprivileged senders. Call sequence: attacker crafts a `StackStxOp` input pointing at vout=1 of *another* sender's PreStxOp txid, `get_sender_txid` returns `Ok(txid)` unconditionally as long as vout==1 regardless of whose PreStxOp it is; the caller then maps `txid` to a `sender` address. Equality broken: STX locked by the applied op == STX owned by the address that actually authorised (via its own PreStxOp) this specific StackStxOp — instead it's authorised by pointing at someone else's PreStxOp output. Impact: Critical — theft/unbacked lock of another sender's committed intent to stack. Whose funds: the impersonated PreStxOp sender's STX. Proof idea: integration test constructing two PreStxOps from two distinct senders, then a StackStxOp whose input vout=1 references sender B's PreStxOp txid while attacker signs as sender A, asserting whether the applied lock's `sender` field is A or B and whether A's balance is what actually gets locked.
