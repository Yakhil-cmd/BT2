Shipit::Hook::DeliverySpec#send! (used to relay Shipit-originated webhooks to third parties) signs outbound payloads with a per-hook `secret`, but nothing in the receiving `Shipit::WebhooksController#verify_signature` or `Webhooks::Handlers` distinguishes GitHub-originated inbound webhooks from a maliciously replayed prior Shipit outbound Hook delivery captured by an attacker who controls an endpoint URL configured on a `read:hook`/`write:hook`-scoped ApiClient (test/models/shipit/hook.rb references). Can an attacker who registers their own callback URL on a Hook they created (needing only `write:hook` permission, which is listed among the non-privileged PERMISSIONS `Shipit::ApiClient::PERMISSIONS`) capture a delivery signed with that hook's own secret and replay a *different, larger* payload body to /webhooks and have it accepted as if from GitHub? Binding: a signature computed with a Hook's outbound DeliverySigner secret == a signature Shipit::WebhooksController accepts as GitHub's own `X-Hub-Signature` for the org's webhook_secret (these are different secrets and must never collide/cross-validate). Scoped impact: Critical if secrets are shared/derived similarly, otherwise this test documents that no cross-check exists. Proof: create a Hook with attacker-controlled secret, sign an arbitrary push payload with `Hook::DeliverySigner`, POST it as X-Hub-Signature to /webhooks with X-Github-Event push, and assert GithubApp#verify_webhook_signature correctly rejects it (regression test guarding against secret confusion).
