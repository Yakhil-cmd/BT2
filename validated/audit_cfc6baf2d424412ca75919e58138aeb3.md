### Title
Post-bad-debt supply-index floor makes spoke supply caps fail open - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
`calculate_scaled_cap` converts an asset-unit cap into scaled shares by dividing by the market index and deliberately saturates at `i128::MAX` instead of reverting [1](#0-0) . Bad-debt cleanup can clamp `supply_index` down to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW = RAY/1000` [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) . `enforce_spoke_cap` then compares scaled usage against that saturated cap, so any configured `supply_cap` becomes ineffective once the market index is below RAY [4](#0-3) .

### Finding Description
The analogous bound failure is not an array write but a domain conversion that can exceed the representable scaled cap and fail open. A normal `supply` reaches the cap through `merge_supply_leg -> apply_leg_usage -> apply_spoke_entry`, which passes the post-supply `market_index` and asset decimals into `enforce_spoke_cap` [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) . After `clean_bad_debt` or liquidation residual cleanup socializes debt through `seize_positions`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` can leave `supply_index` at the floor while `cash` is short [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) . `pool::supply` blocks entry only while the market is under-backed; `recapitalize` restores backing without raising the already-written-down index, reopening supply with the cap still saturated [9](#0-8) .

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged caller can exceed the governance-configured supply cap for an impaired asset after a bad-debt write-down plus a small recapitalization. Caps are the per-spoke exposure limit; bypassing them lets a single account concentrate collateral beyond the intended bound and then use `borrow`/strategy paths to create debt that the listing parameters were meant to cap. Under a subsequent adverse move within accepted oracle bands or another bad-debt event, the over-cap exposure can translate into protocol insolvency rather than a mere configuration violation. Uncertainty: exploitation requires a listed market to reach the supply-index floor via real bad debt and then be recapitalized; the cap bypass is proven, while the magnitude of loss depends on configured LTV/threshold and subsequent price movement.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The trigger needs an insolvent/dust account whose debt is socialized, permissionlessly via `clean_bad_debt`, and the market must be recapitalized enough to pass `require_backed_market`; both are callable without privilege (`clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, then `supply`). Once `supply_index < RAY`, `cap * RAY / supply_index` saturates for admitted caps, so the check stops rejecting. No leaked key, upgrade, oracle manipulation, or privileged call is required.

### Recommendation
Make `calculate_scaled_cap` fail closed on saturation for cap enforcement, or compare caps in asset units using floor/ceil conservative unscaling instead of converting the cap by a depressed index. At minimum, revert with the side-specific cap error when `mul_div_floor_saturating` saturates, and recompute/restore a sane index basis before reopening supply after recapitalization.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists `(hub_id, A)` in `spoke_id` with finite `supply_cap`, nonzero LTV, and normal market params; users borrow `A` until an account is insolvent with collateral <= dust.
2. Permissionless `clean_bad_debt(caller, victim_id)` seizes the debt leg; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `A.supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving a backing shortfall.
3. Any payer calls `recapitalize(payer, {hub_id,A}, amount)` to clear the shortfall; the index remains at the floor because recapitalization credits cash without minting shares.
4. Attacker calls `supply(caller, 0, spoke_id, [({hub_id,A}, supply_cap * k)])` for `k > 1`. `calculate_scaled_cap(supply_cap, decimals, RAY/1000)` saturates to `i128::MAX`, so `next_scaled <= cap_scaled` passes and the pool mints shares for the over-cap deposit.
5. The attacker then calls `borrow` against the newly over-cap collateral position; subsequent adverse movement or cleanup creates losses exceeding what the configured cap was intended to permit.

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
