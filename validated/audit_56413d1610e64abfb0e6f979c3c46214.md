### Title
AAs cannot receive private (indivisible/blackbytes-style) asset payments — funds become permanently unspendable - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
An Autonomous Agent (AA) address has no device pairing and no user-controlled wallet software behind it — it can only react to on-chain triggers processed by `handleAATriggers()`. Private assets (`is_private=1`), however, are only usable once the recipient's off-chain private-payment chain is validated and revealed via `wallet_defined_by_addresses`/`private_payment.js`/`indivisible_asset.js`, a flow that requires a device correspondent. Since AAs have no device, any private-asset payment routed to an AA address can never be revealed, credited, or spent by the AA, permanently freezing the funds. This mirrors the "Lack of NFT callbacks" report: the recipient (smart wallet / AA) lacks the mechanism (`onERC721Received` / private-chain reveal) required to actually take custody of an asset sent to it, causing funds to be stuck.

### Finding Description
Base-layer outputs for private assets store `address CHAR(32) NULL — NULL if hidden by output_hash` [1](#0-0)  and are only filled in once a `private_payment.js`/`indivisible_asset.js` chain reaches the recipient off the DAG and is validated via `validateAndSavePrivatePaymentChain`, which fills in `address` and `blinding` on the previously hidden output row [2](#0-1) . This reveal mechanism depends entirely on a device-to-device message exchange (pairing, `sendPrivatePayments`, chain forwarding), something only a wallet with a device address can participate in [3](#0-2) .

AAs have no device address and no wallet UI; their only interface to the protocol is the trigger mechanism, which explicitly excludes private outputs from ever becoming an AA trigger: `handleAATriggers()` selects candidate triggering outputs with `AND (outputs.asset IS NULL OR is_private=0)` [4](#0-3) . Consequently:
- The AA never receives a trigger for the private payment, so `aa_balances` is never credited (`updateInitialAABalances` only runs from a trigger) [5](#0-4) .
- The private chain reveal that would normally unlock the hidden `address`/`blinding` fields on the output can never happen because no device exists to receive/forward `sendPrivatePayments` messages to the AA.
- Even the AA's own definer/logic has no way to invoke the off-DAG reveal flow, since that flow lives entirely in `wallet_general.js`/`device.js` messaging, not in `oscript`/AA logic.

The base-layer unit itself is still valid and stable (payment validation for private assets doesn't check whether the recipient can process private payments — `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` only checks issuer/definer/transfer conditions and balance) [6](#0-5) , so nodes agree the unit is valid; the coins are simply stuck forever with no protocol path to spend or even acknowledge them, analogous to an NFT `safeTransferFrom` that succeeds in the report's flawed model but leaves the asset unreachable because the receiver lacks `onERC721Received`.

### Impact Explanation
Any user or exchange/marketplace-style AA that advertises acceptance of a private asset (e.g., a private-payment based collectible/voucher system) can have funds sent to it that are irrecoverably frozen — a permanent fund loss for the sender and a denial of the intended AA logic. This is a concrete "AA fund loss/freezing" outcome reachable by any unprivileged issuer of a private asset or any private-payment counterparty who mistakenly (or is tricked into) targeting an AA address.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires a private asset (`is_private=1`, e.g. a blackbytes-style/indivisible asset) to exist and someone (attacker or unaware user) to direct a private payment chain at an AA address. This can happen accidentally (AA authors may not realize their AA cannot accept private assets) or be induced by a malicious counterparty in a private-payment negotiation who is offered/expects to pay an AA but instead locks the sender's asset permanently by directing it to the AA.

### Recommendation
- Short term: Add explicit protocol-level or wallet-level validation to reject/refuse constructing or accepting private-asset payments whose destination is a known AA address (`aa_addresses` table), similar to how `checkNotAAs` already guards other operations against directing certain apps at AA addresses [7](#0-6) .
- Long term: Document and enforce that AAs cannot be principals for private/indivisible assets, and consider surfacing a warning/error in composer flows (`indivisible_asset.js`, `divisible_asset.js`) when `to_address` resolves to an AA.

### Proof of Concept
1. Define a private, fixed-denomination asset (e.g., a blackbytes-like NFT/voucher) with `is_private: true, fixed_denominations: true`.
2. Deploy any AA and note its address is inserted into `aa_addresses`.
3. Use `composeIndivisibleAssetPaymentJoint`/`private_payment.js` flow to send a private payment chain where `to_address` is the AA's address.
4. Observe the base-layer output for that payment remains with `address=NULL` (hidden) until a private chain reveal occurs — but no device exists for the AA, so `validateAndSavePrivatePaymentChain` never runs for this output.
5. Observe `handleAATriggers()` never selects this output (excluded by `is_private=0` filter), so the AA is never triggered and `aa_balances` is never credited — the private asset is permanently unspendable by anyone.
