### Title
Attacker-induced Aquarius LP price outage permanently blocks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup of their own insolvent account - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator computes Aquarius LP token fair value from the pool's own reserves and rejects the read with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever pool value falls below a configured `min_pool_value_wad`. Because the controller evaluates collateral legs pro-rata and panics when any needed price is missing, a borrower who supplies a dust amount of an Aquarius LP token as collateral and then drains their own liquidity out of that pool can make every `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `swap_debt`/`swap_collateral` call against their account revert. This mirrors the CVE-2018-3186 class: a crafted input drives a complex component into a repeatable abort, here freezing the protocol's loss-containment path.

### Finding Description
- `aquarius::read` re-reads reserves and total shares live from the pool contract and returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` when `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-122`). Any LP provider can push the pool under that bound with a single withdrawal — no oracle dishonesty or privileged action required.
- The controller loads prices for all of an account's position assets and panics on a missing feed: `cached_price` panics with `OracleError::OracleNotConfigured` when no entry exists (`contracts/controller/src/context.rs:154-160`), and `fetch_prices` propagates aggregator errors (`context.rs:142-151`).
- Liquidation seizure iterates every supply position and calls `cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset)` per leg (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs:383-385`), so one price-less dust leg aborts the whole transaction. Supply itself does not require a valid price, so the attacker can add the shielding leg at near-zero cost (documented in `docs/explanation/threat-model.md` DoS.1, which explicitly names the LP-withdrawal vector).
- The same missing price also blocks `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` eligibility valuation and any risk refresh, so the insolvency cannot be resolved through the permissionless cleanup path either.

### Impact Explanation
An underwater borrower can keep their account unliquidatable indefinitely while interest accrues, converting a liquidatable position into socialized bad debt written down against suppliers (`supply_index` write-down in `clean_bad_debt`). That is protocol insolvency borne by suppliers — an accepted impact class — not merely the attacker's own funds. The freeze persists only while the pool stays below the threshold, but the attacker controls that entirely since restoring liquidity is their choice; they can also re-enter the LP position, get liquidated nowhere, and repeat.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully attacker-controlled and unprivileged: `supply` a dust amount of a listed Aquarius LP collateral, borrow to near the threshold, then call Aquarius `withdraw` (an allowed "own trades on Aquarius" action) until pool value < `min_pool_value_wad`. Requires the attacker to hold a large share of that Aquarius pool's liquidity, which is plausible for thin pools, and the dust leg costs only the deposit. The threat model already flags this exact mechanism, suggesting it is a known but unmitigated boundary rather than an accepted design choice.

### Recommendation
Do not abort a multi-leg liquidation when one collateral leg's price is unavailable; skip or zero-value the unpriceable leg (it has no seizure value anyway) while still pricing the remaining legs, or fall back to `SeizeMode::Credit`-style share seizure using the last valid cached index rather than a strict price. Alternatively, decouple `clean_bad_debt` eligibility from per-asset prices so dust-priced collateral cannot block socialization.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies dust of Aquarius LP token `L` (collateral `C1`) plus real collateral, borrows max against the real collateral.
2. Price moves; `get_health_factor(account)` < 1.
3. Attacker calls `pool.withdraw` on the Aquarius pool backing `L` until `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < min_pool_value_wad` (aggregator `read`, `aquarius.rs:118-122`).
4. Any `liquidate(account, debt_payments, ...)` reverts: `fetch_prices` for `L` fails with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, or `cached_price` panics in `calculate_seized_collateral` (`math.rs:384`, `context.rs:158`).
5. `clean_bad_debt(account)` likewise fails on the same price fetch, so the debt accrues until suppliers eat the write-down once prices ever resolve — and the attacker can keep the outage alive by staying withdrawn.
