### Title
Unverified "overpayment" refund lets an unprivileged caller drain pool custody via `repay`/`recapitalize` — (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The external report's class — forwarding a declared payment while never verifying or refunding the unspent portion against what was actually received — maps onto XOXNO's pool settlement legs. Both `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` compute a `refund`/`overpayment` purely from the *declared* `action.amount` and immediately `transfer_out` that amount to the `payer`, without measuring any inbound balance delta, debiting `cash`, or passing `require_reserves`. If the declared amount exceeds the actual debt/shortfall (or nothing was transferred in at all), the "refund" is paid out of the pool's real token custody — funds belonging to suppliers.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs`, `accounting` resolves `amount` into `(burned, overpayment)` via `cache.resolve_repay`, credits only `net_repay` to cash, then `apply` executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` at line 32. The refund is derived from the declared `action.amount`, never from a measured balance increase of the pool.

The same shape exists in `contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs`: `applied = amount.min(backing_shortfall)`, `refund = amount - applied`, then `transfer_out(&payer, refund)` — again with no receipt verification.

`contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3611` (`test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`) proves the exploit end-to-end: on a market with zero debt, `client.repay(&payer, amount = custody_before)` with "nothing transferred in" makes the entire declared amount an overpayment (`net_repay == 0`, the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct), and the full refund is paid out of real custody — `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` confirms book/custody divergence.

### Impact Explanation
Pool custody backs supplier shares. A caller who can invoke `repay` (or `recapitalize`) with a declared `amount` larger than the actual obligation — up to the market's whole token balance — receives the excess as a "refund" without ever depositing it, directly stealing supplier funds. This is theft of user funds (High). If `repay` requires the controller as an intermediary, the reachable variant is `recapitalize` (listed in-scope) and any path where the declared amount isn't enforced to match an actual inbound transfer.

### Likelihood Explanation
The payer address is arbitrary (tests use `Address::generate`), no privileged role is needed, and the drain amount is bounded only by pool custody. The only uncertainty I could not fully resolve within the index is whether `pool.repay`'s entrypoint in `contracts/pool/src/lib.rs` restricts the caller to the controller; the pool's own test calls `client.repay` directly with a fresh address, suggesting it is permissionless, and `recapitalize` is explicitly listed as an unprivileged-reachable entrypoint.

### Recommendation
Measure the inbound transfer (balance-delta) or require the controller to pre-transfer exactly `net_repay`, and bound the refund by the *received* amount rather than the declared `amount`. At minimum, `transfer_out` of the overpayment/refund should debit a tracked pending-inbound balance or be skipped when no inbound transfer occurred.

### Proof of Concept
```rust
// contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611 — existing test demonstrating the drain
let t = TestSetup::new();
let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);
// Pool holds `custody_before` tokens, market has zero debt.
// No tokens are transferred in by `payer`.
let credited = t.client()
    .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
    .get_unchecked(0)
    .actual_amount;
assert_eq!(credited, 0);                 // nothing repaid (no debt)
// ...yet `payer` receives `custody_before` tokens as an "overpayment refund",
// draining supplier custody while the cash book stays untouched.
```
The mirror-image path is `recapitalize(hub_asset, payer, amount)` with `amount` far above the backing shortfall: only `applied` is credited to cash, while `refund = amount - applied` is transferred out of custody regardless of what was actually deposited.
