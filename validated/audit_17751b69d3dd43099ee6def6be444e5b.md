### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Reasoning:**

The mechanism described is real in the code — `repay` fully clearing a debt asset removes that debt bit from the position's mask via `debt-remove-scaled`'s `mask-update` call [1](#0-0) , and `borrow` subsequently resolves the egroup from whatever mask is currently stored on-chain [2](#0-1) . However, this does not qualify as an in-scope vulnerability for several reasons:

1. **`repay(... on-behalf-of B)` is an intended, permissionless "repay for another account" feature** — the only check is `(asserts! (is-eq contract-caller tx-sender) ERR-AUTHORIZATION)` [3](#0-2) , with no restriction on `on-behalf-of`. Any protocol allowing third-party debt repayment (a common DeFi pattern) inherently allows a caller to shrink someone else's debt mask; this is a designed side-effect, not a bug.
2. **A must spend real capital to fully repay B's real debt with zero return** — this is not a profitable or cheap griefing vector; A permanently loses funds equal to B's dust debt with no compensating gain.
3. **The claimed harm depends entirely on DAO-configured egroup parameters** (a wider/DEFAULT mask having a lower `LTV-BORROW` than a narrower specific egroup). The rules explicitly place egroup configuration and its invariants out of scope, since "egroup invariants needing global position knowledge are verified off-chain before approval" and "full DAO control of the asset and egroup registries is intended design."
4. **The impact is not a freezing of funds at rest.** B's collateral and existing debt remain fully accessible; only B's *future* borrowing capacity for a *new* loan is temporarily reduced, and B can trivially restore the original mask by re-borrowing a small amount of the same asset. This does not meet the "permanent/temporary freezing of funds" bar as defined — it is a denial-of-additional-credit condition, not a freezing of already-held assets or yield.

Given A's real capital cost with no profit, the dependency on DAO-approved egroup economics (explicitly out of scope), and B's trivial self-remediation, this does not meet the bar for a valid, in-scope finding.
