### Title
Bad-debt cleanup preserves wiped supplier shares at the supply-index floor, freezing the market - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary

**Severity: Medium**

`clean_bad_debt` can socialize debt greater than or equal to the market’s entire supplied value, but `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the resulting supply index to a nonzero floor instead of zeroing the supplier claims. The surviving scaled shares therefore retain positive claims against an emptied market, causing withdrawals to fail for lack of cash and new supplies to fail the pool-solvency check until an external recapitalization covers the residual shortfall. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

`Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` is permissionless and accepts any account whose debt is insolvent while its remaining collateral is at or below the dust threshold. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Cleanup converts all deposit positions into `Deposit` seize entries and all debt positions into `Borrow` seize entries before invoking the pool. [5](#0-4)  In the pool, a borrow entry calculates the unpaid debt, applies it to the supply index, and burns the debt shares. [6](#0-5) 

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at the total supplied value, so complete socialization makes `remaining == 0` and computes a zero `new_supply_index`. [7](#0-6)  The code then applies `max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`, leaving that zero index represented as a nonzero index. [8](#0-7) 

The pool does not remove the corresponding scaled supply shares: it only changes `supply_index`, while `supplied` remains in market state. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9)  Any surviving scaled supply position consequently unscales to a positive amount at the floor because withdrawal and solvency accounting use `unscale_supply_floor`. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

This is demonstrated by the project’s own regression-style tests: after a complete write-down, `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` leaves a “phantom claim,” and a subsequent deposit in the raw-cache model can be drained by that claim. [13](#0-12)  In the production supply path, the same residual makes `backing_shortfall` nonzero, so `require_backed_market` rejects new supplies with `PoolInsolvent`. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

### Impact Explanation

All suppliers in a completely wiped market retain positive residual claims after their economic backing has been removed. [16](#0-15)  Because the debt has already been burned and the borrowed cash is absent, `cash + outstanding_debt` cannot cover those claims. [6](#0-5) [12](#0-11) 

Withdrawals debit cash only after `require_reserves`, so residual suppliers cannot withdraw from an empty market. [17](#0-16)  New supplies are rejected by `require_backed_market`, leaving the market unable to resume normal supply operation until someone funds the phantom shortfall through recapitalization. [18](#0-17) [19](#0-18) 

The impact is temporary freezing of supplier funds and market operations. It is not classified higher because `recapitalize` can restore backing by crediting cash equal to `backing_shortfall`, though doing so requires an outside party to fund the residual claims created by the floor. [20](#0-19) 

### Likelihood Explanation

Triggering the state does not require privileged access: `clean_bad_debt` only needs an authorized caller, an open debt position, insolvency, and collateral at or below the dust threshold. [3](#0-2) [21](#0-20) 

The destructive case requires the ceiled debt value to be at least the market’s total supplied value, which can occur when a wiped account’s debt is socialized into a market whose entire supply is written down. [6](#0-5) [22](#0-21)  The likelihood depends on an account becoming eligible for dust-gated bad-debt cleanup, but any unprivileged observer can then submit the state transition. [23](#0-22) 

### Recommendation

Do not preserve wiped supply shares through a nonzero index floor. When `capped == total_supplied_value`, write the market to an unambiguous zeroed state by setting `supply_index` to the floor only after also burning or otherwise clearing all scaled supply shares, or by explicitly tracking wiped shares so they cannot unscale to a positive claim. [1](#0-0) 

The fix should preserve the invariant that:

```text
sum(unscale_supply_floor(user_scaled_supply))
    <= cash + unscale_borrow_ceil(total_borrowed)
```

after every bad-debt cleanup. [24](#0-23)  Add a production-path regression test that runs `Controller::clean_bad_debt`, verifies surviving controller positions cannot retain positive claims after total wipeout, and confirms subsequent `supply` and `withdraw` behavior is well-defined. [25](#0-24) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Suppliers create a market position in asset `A`, giving the pool nonzero `supplied` scaled shares and lending cash to a borrower.
2. A separate account becomes insolvent with collateral at or below the dust threshold and has an open borrow position in `A`.
3. Any caller invokes:

   ```rust
   clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)
   ```

   The controller builds a `PoolSeizeEntry` for the borrow position and sends it to the pool. [26](#0-25) 

4. The pool computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. [6](#0-5) 
5. If `bad_debt >= supplied * supply_index`, the code sets `capped = total_supplied_value`, calculates `remaining = 0`, and computes `new_supply_index = 0`. [7](#0-6) 
6. The final `max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` stores a nonzero index even though the write-down was total. [8](#0-7) 
7. The debt is burned, but `supplied` remains nonzero; each surviving scaled position therefore has a positive `unscale_supply_floor` result. [27](#0-26) [11](#0-10) 
8. A supplier calls `Controller::withdraw(caller, supplier_account, [(A, 0)], to)`, which resolves the full position and invokes the pool withdrawal path; `require_reserves` rejects the positive claim when the market has no cash. [28](#0-27) [17](#0-16) 
9. A new supplier calls `Controller::supply(caller, 0, spoke_id, [(A, amount)])`; `require_backed_market` rejects it because the residual supply claim exceeds `cash + outstanding_debt`. [29](#0-28) [2](#0-1) 

The existing unit test reproduces the accounting residue directly: after `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` exceeds total supplied value, the index is clamped to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` and the old scaled position unscales to a positive stranded amount. [30](#0-29)

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

fn test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let scaled_a_raw = 1_000_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: scaled_a_raw,
            borrowed: 0,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let scaled_a = Ray::from(scaled_a_raw);

        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(2_000_000 * RAY));
        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "wipeout must clamp supply index UP to the floor, not reset the base"
        );

        let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a);
        assert!(stranded > 0, "floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim");
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "empty market: no cash to extract yet");

        let c = stranded;
        let scaled_b = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(c);
        cache.mint_supply(scaled_b);
        cache.credit_cash(c);

        let b_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b);
        assert_eq!(b_claim, c, "userB's honest claim equals their deposit");

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "stranded position pays out non-zero");
        assert_eq!(
            gross, c,
            "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool"
        );

        assert!(
            cache.cash() < b_claim,
            "pool cash ({}) can no longer cover userB's claim ({}): honest supplier lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            b_claim
        );
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "userA drained the pool to empty");

/// Sizes and books the cash injection without transferring tokens.
///
/// Credits `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to cash and commits. `refund` is
/// `amount - applied`.
pub(crate) fn accounting(
    env: &Env,
    hub_asset: HubAssetKey,
    amount: i128,
) -> RecapitalizationOutcome {
    require_nonneg_amount(env, amount);
    let mut cache = ops::renewed_market(env, &hub_asset);

    let applied = amount.min(guards::backing_shortfall(&cache));
    let refund = amount
        .checked_sub(applied)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));

    cache.credit_cash(applied);
    cache.commit();
