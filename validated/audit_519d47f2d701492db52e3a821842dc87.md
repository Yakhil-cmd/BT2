### Title
Liquidator can force full-collateral seizure beyond the penalty-bound "dust" leftover, stealing excess collateral from the borrower - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
`liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar` computes a proportional collateral seizure (`coll-final-raw`) from the debt being repaid and the liquidation penalty, then checks whether the *remaining* (unseized) collateral is still economically liquidatable. If the debt value implied by that remainder rounds down to zero, the code silently switches from the proportional seizure to **seizing 100% of the borrower's collateral** (`user-coll-balance`) instead of the bounded amount, even though only a fraction of the debt was actually repaid at the intended penalty rate. A liquidator fully controls `debt-amount`, so this "dust" branch can be deliberately triggered to strip a borrower's entire collateral balance while only repaying (and being charged the penalty on) a small, attacker-chosen amount of debt.

### Finding Description
The liquidation flow computes the expected collateral seizure with the configured penalty: [1](#0-0) 

and the final collateral to remove after capping to what the borrower actually holds and to what the (possibly capped) debt justifies: [2](#0-1) 

After computing this bounded `coll-final-raw`, the contract checks how much debt the borrower's *remaining* collateral (`coll-remaining = user-coll-balance - coll-final-raw`) could still cover at the max penalty rate: [3](#0-2) 

If `remaining-debt-to-repay` rounds down to `u0` (dust), `coll-final` is overridden to `user-coll-balance` — the borrower's **entire** collateral balance — rather than the properly bounded `coll-final-raw`:

`(coll-final (if (is-eq remaining-debt-to-repay u0) user-coll-balance coll-final-raw))`

The only guards before executing the transfer are that amounts are non-zero and slippage-bounded: [4](#0-3) 

There is no check that `coll-final` (once escalated to `user-coll-balance`) is still proportionate to `debt-to-repay` and `liq-penalty`. Because the liquidator freely chooses `debt-amount`, they can pick a value that leaves just enough leftover collateral that its USD value, after `div-bps-down`/`mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` rounding, computes to a scaled-debt equivalent of `u0`. This deliberately triggers the "seize everything" branch, letting the liquidator take collateral far beyond what the debt-repay + penalty formula (`calc-liq-collateral-repay`) allows.

### Impact Explanation
This is a direct theft of user funds at rest: an unprivileged liquidator (attacker) can seize a borrower's (victim) entire collateral balance while repaying only a disproportionately small amount of debt, by exploiting integer-rounding of the "remaining collateral is dust" check. The borrower ends up losing collateral value well beyond the intended liquidation-penalty bound, which is a bounded-seizure violation causing theft of funds beyond what the liquidation math permits — this maps to the Critical impact class (direct theft of user funds at rest).

### Likelihood Explanation
The liquidator supplies `debt-amount` and can iterate/compute the exact leftover collateral amount (given known prices, decimals, and penalty parameters) that will round `remaining-debt-to-repay` to zero. Since all the inputs to the rounding chain (`div-bps-down`, `mul-div-down`, `mul-div-up`, `INDEX-PRECISION`) are deterministic and observable on-chain, an attacker can reliably engineer this dust condition against any position eligible for partial liquidation, making this practically exploitable rather than a rare edge case.

### Recommendation
Do not silently promote to `user-coll-balance` when `remaining-debt-to-repay` rounds to zero. Instead, bound the "sweep remaining dust" behavior to a strict, small absolute-value threshold (e.g., a fixed minimum USD/token dust amount configured by governance) rather than deriving it from rounding artifacts of the liquidator-controlled `debt-amount`, and require that the extra collateral swept in this case still be charged against the debt or explicitly capped to a negligible bonus. Recompute `debt-to-repay`/`scaled-to-remove` consistently whenever `coll-final` is escalated so the debt actually repaid matches the collateral actually seized.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower has collateral C and debt D such that the position is partially liquidatable.
2. Liquidator computes, from public price/penalty/index data, a `debt-amount` value X such that `coll-final-raw = calc-liq-collateral-repay(debt-to-repay, liq-penalty)` leaves a `coll-remaining` whose USD value, after `div-bps-down(rem-coll-usd, BPS+liq-penalty-max)` and the subsequent `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` conversions to scaled debt units, rounds down to `u0`.
3. Liquidator calls `market.liquidate(borrower, collateral-ft, debt-ft, X, 0, none, none)`.
4. In `v0-4-market.clar`, `remaining-debt-to-repay` evaluates to `u0`, so `coll-final` is set to `user-coll-balance` (all of the borrower's collateral), while `debt-to-repay`/`scaled-to-remove` remain bounded to the much smaller `debt-final` computed from X.
5. Liquidator receives the borrower's full collateral balance for a fraction of the debt plus the normal liquidation penalty, an outcome strictly worse for the borrower than the intended proportional liquidation-penalty formula, and better for the liquidator than any legitimately configured `liq-penalty-max`.

;; Scale debt for storage and calculate final execution amounts
;; Converts to scaled units, caps at current debt, calculates final collateral
;; Returns: { scaled-to-remove: uint, debt-to-repay: uint, coll-final: uint }
(define-private (scale-debt-for-liquidation
  (debt-final uint)
  (coll-actual uint)
  (curr-scaled uint)
  (asset-id uint))
  (let (;; convert debt amount to scaled units for storage
        (borrow-index (get index (unwrap-panic (get-cached-indexes asset-id))))
        (scaled-debt (mul-div-down debt-final INDEX-PRECISION borrow-index))
        ;; cap at current debt (prevent over-repayment)
        (scaled-to-remove (if (> scaled-debt curr-scaled) curr-scaled scaled-debt))
        (debt-to-repay (mul-div-up scaled-to-remove borrow-index INDEX-PRECISION))
        ;; If debt was capped, scale collateral proportionally
        (coll-final (if (< scaled-to-remove scaled-debt)
                        (mul-div-down coll-actual scaled-to-remove scaled-debt)
                        coll-actual)))
    {
      scaled-to-remove: scaled-to-remove,
      debt-to-repay: debt-to-repay,
      coll-final: coll-final
    }))
