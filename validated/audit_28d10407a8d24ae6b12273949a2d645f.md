### Title
Debt-value overflow in index accrual permanently freezes a high-utilization market — (File: common/src/rates/compound.rs)

### Summary
An unprivileged supplier/borrower pair can push a market into a state where the next interest-accrual step panics with `MathOverflow` before the borrow-index cap engages. Because every controller verb (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, flash paths) accrues the market first through `global_sync`, the panic bricks the market permanently: no repayment, no withdrawal, no liquidation, no bad-debt cleanup. All supplier funds in that market are frozen forever.

### Finding Description
The CVE class is "reachable assertion/panic that aborts execution". In XOXNO Lending the analog is the fixed-point accrual engine:

- `accrue_step` (called per `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` chunk by `accrue_chunk` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39`) computes the debt value as `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — an `i128`-bounded `Ray` multiplication in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs` and `common/src/math/fp.rs`.
- `compound_interest` in `common/src/rates/compound.rs:36-42` explicitly `panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow)` when the scaled exponent or the debt value does not fit `i128`.
- `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs before every mutating op via `Cache::load`, so a market whose `borrowed * borrow_index` product overflows `i128` panics on every subsequent call.
- The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360`) demonstrates this: a billion-unit-position market at 98% utilization on the XLM rate curve reaches the RAY-value ceiling within ~40 years of accrual, `update_indexes` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the test then proves `withdraw` and `repay` revert with the same error — while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, i.e., the index cap that is supposed to stop growth never engages because the value conversion panics first.

The state is reachable without privilege: an attacker supplies a large principal to a high-decimal market (`supply`), borrows near the cap (`borrow`), then waits (or the market simply accrues over time) until a single unprivileged `update_indexes` call crosses the `i128` debt-value bound. From that point the panic is self-sustaining — accrual is always the first step and always overflows.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds the representable RAY value, `global_sync` panics unconditionally, so:

- suppliers cannot `withdraw` (the test asserts `try_withdraw_raw` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`);
- borrowers cannot `repay` (same assertion), so positions can never deleverage;
- liquidators cannot `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt`, so even a distressed market cannot be wound down;
- `recapitalize` and `claim_revenue` accrue first and fail identically.

All tokens held by the pool for that `(hub, asset)` book — supplier principal and unclaimed revenue — are locked permanently. This is theft-equivalent loss for every supplier in the market and qualifies as permanent freezing of funds / protocol-level insolvency for that book.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-to-high over a market's lifetime:

- The trigger requires only unprivileged actions: a large `supply` and a large `borrow` on an 18-decimal market, then time. No admin, oracle, or governance cooperation is needed.
- The rate curve (`xlm_curve`) is designed to spike toward `MAX_BORROW_RATE_RAY` (2 RAY APR) at high utilization, which is exactly the regime that drives the index up ~170x per the test's measurement. High utilization is also the normal state for a heavily borrowed market, not an exotic configuration.
- The overflow is not gated by the borrow-index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36 raw RAY): the `scaled_to_original` value computation overflows `i128` well before the index reaches its cap, so the safeguard that should bound accrual never fires (`large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:350-353`).
- Anyone can then keep the market in the panic state trivially — every accrual attempt reverts, and nothing can reduce `borrowed` because `repay` itself accrues first.

### Recommendation
Bound the *debt value*, not just the index, inside `accrue_step`:

- Cap accrual (or clamp the effective rate to zero) when `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` approaches the `i128` RAY ceiling — i.e., check the debt value in `I256` and skip further interest accrual once it exceeds a safe bound, so `update_indexes`/`withdraw`/`repay` still succeed even though no new interest accrues.
- Alternatively, accrue in `I256`/`U256` throughout `accrue_step` and `compound_interest`, saturating the stored `borrow_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` rather than panicking on the intermediate value computation.
- Ensure `repay`, `withdraw`, and `liquidate` degrade gracefully in an overflowed market: worst case, allow accrual to be a no-op at the cap so exits and deleveraging remain possible.

### Proof of Concept
The repository's own test is a complete PoC (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`):

```rust
let mut t = LendingTest::new()
    .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
    .with_market(col())
    .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
    .build();
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);          // unprivileged supply
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);             // unprivileged 98% borrow

// advance years; eventually:
assert_contract_error(t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
// market permanently frozen — every verb accrues first:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

Root cause chain: `borrow`/`supply` → `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20`) → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original` and `compound_interest` (`common/src/rates/compound.rs:31-51`), which panics with `MathOverflow` when `borrowed * borrow_index` no longer fits `i128` — permanently, because `repay` cannot run to reduce `borrowed` without first running the same accrual.
