### Title
Vote weight in `countVotes()` is based on balance held at count time, not at vote time, enabling flash-transfer manipulation of `op_list` elections - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
`countVotes()` tallies votes for protocol-level subjects (e.g. `op_list`, the order-provider/witness list) by looking up the *current* stable balance of every address that has ever cast a `system_vote`, rather than the balance that address actually held while its vote was active. An address only needs to hold coins momentarily at the exact moment the tally query runs; it does not need to hold them for the "since" period used to decide which votes are still counted.

### Finding Description
`countVotes()` first selects the set of voting addresses for a subject and the time window during which their votes are considered valid: [1](#0-0) 

It then computes each address's balance from the *live* set of unspent, stable, good outputs — i.e. whatever the address holds right now, at the moment the MCI stabilizes and the tally runs: [2](#0-1) 

This `voter_balances` table is joined against the vote records purely by address and vote timestamp (`timestamp>=since_timestamp`), with no constraint tying the counted balance to the balance the address held when it voted or continuously since then: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This is the structural equivalent of the reported ERC20-snapshot flashloan bug: a governance decision (there, unlocking capital in a protection pool; here, electing the `op_list`/witness set that other nodes use for main-chain stability) is decided using a balance value that is captured at a single point in time that is decoupled from the actual commitment period of the voter. Because `outputs` balances are transferable atomically between addresses/units on the DAG, an address can:
1. Post a small `system_vote` for `op_list` early enough to satisfy the `since_timestamp` window (the vote signal itself is cheap and persists).
2. Shortly before the mci that will trigger `countVotes()` (vote-counting mci boundaries are periodic/deterministic based on protocol constants), receive a large transfer of GBYTE into the voting address.
3. As soon as that unit becomes stable and `countVotes()` runs, the address's current balance — not its historical holding — is added to the vote tally for whichever `op_address` it voted for.
4. Immediately move the coins onward (e.g., to fund the next colluding address, or sell them), since nothing in `countVotes()` requires the balance to persist afterward.

Because `voter_balances` is rebuilt from scratch on every call using only currently-stable UTXOs, the same underlying GBYTE can be walked through a chain of pre-registered voter addresses, one per counting cycle, "renting" enormous voting weight to each colluding vote in turn without ever permanently locking capital — functionally identical to flash-loaning voting power.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` determines the set of order providers/witnesses that all nodes use to compute main-chain stability. If vote counting can be manipulated by capital that is only present for an instant, an attacker with a comparatively small amount of GBYTE that they can move quickly between addresses (or even loop through a single, well-timed transfer) can capture control of the elected `op_list`, causing network-wide disagreement about validity/stability of units, or entrenching malicious order providers who can stall stabilization — a direct hit on the "node disagreement on validity or stability" / "network unable to confirm new units" impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Vote-counting mci boundaries are deterministic (derived from protocol constants and mainchain progression, as seen from the `mci`, `subject`, `is_emergency` parameters and constants such as `constants.pemCurvesFixMci`), so an attacker can predict when a tally will execute and time a transfer to land exactly before it. No special privilege is required — this is reachable by any unprivileged unit poster/wallet that can send a `system_vote` message and subsequently move GBYTE between its own addresses, which matches the "AA trigger sender / unit poster" reachability requirement.

### Recommendation
Use a balance that is committed for the entire voting period rather than the instantaneous balance at tally time, e.g.:
- Snapshot each voter's balance at the time the `system_vote` unit itself becomes stable (its `last_ball` state), and use that value for the entire "since" window, similar to how `var[]`/`balance[]` opcodes in AAs are evaluated against `last_ball_unit` (see `formula/evaluation.js:1510-1528`) rather than "live" chain tip state.
- Alternatively, require that the balance used for counting be the *minimum* balance held by the address continuously since its vote was cast (or since `since_timestamp`), rather than a single point-in-time read, to prevent transient balance from being credited.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker controls address `V`. `V` posts a `system_vote` unit for `op_list` naming a malicious `op_address`, satisfying the earliest allowed `timestamp` for the `since_timestamp` window used in `main_chain.js:1807-1821`.
2. Attacker predicts the next `countVotes()` trigger mci (network-wide, periodic) and, in the unit that stabilizes right before that mci, transfers a very large amount of GBYTE (borrowed/aggregated from elsewhere) into `V`.
3. `countVotes()` executes: `bal_rows` query (`main_chain.js:1757-1777`) reads `V`'s balance as the freshly received large amount and credits it fully to `V`'s previously cast vote for the malicious `op_address` (`main_chain.js:1850-1862`).
4. In the very next unit, attacker moves the GBYTE out of `V` to repeat the same trick with a different pre-registered voting address for a subsequent tally, or simply cashes it out — the large balance was only needed for the instant the tally query executed.
5. Result: the malicious `op_address` accumulates disproportionate vote weight and can be elected into `op_list`, without the attacker ever permanently committing the capital that was credited to the vote.
