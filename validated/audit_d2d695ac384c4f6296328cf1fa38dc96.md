### Title
Supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped suppliers' claims, letting them drain fresh deposits after bad-debt socialization - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by scaling `supply_index` down, but clamps the result UP to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) when the write-down would push the index below the floor. Because scaled supply shares are never burned in proportion, this creates a persistent inconsistency between the share count and the index — the same class as the CVE hint (inconsistent cached/stored state producing an exploitable result). Surviving suppliers retain claims worth more than the market's backing and can withdraw them against the next depositor's cash.

### Finding Description
`interest.rs:73-89` computes `new_supply_index = supply_index * (1 - capped/total_supplied_value)` and then applies `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, the index should be ~0 but is instead forced to `RAY/1000`. Every scaled supply share still held (suppliers whose shares were not burned — the cleanup reclassifies collateral to revenue but does not zero all `supplied` shares) keeps a claim of `scaled * RAY/1000`, which is strictly positive even though the market absorbed a 100% loss. The pool tests pin exactly this: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:316-428` show the stranded claim paying out a fresh depositor's full deposit. The withdrawal path (`Cache::resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` against `cash`) imposes no supply-side solvency guard, so the phantom claim settles whenever `cash` covers it.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a wipeout-grade `clean_bad_debt`, any holder of stranded scaled shares (including an attacker who supplied the wiped market themselves) can call `controller::withdraw` and extract tokens deposited later by honest suppliers. The test demonstrates a 1:1 drain: `gross == fresh deposit`, leaving `cash < honest claim`. `backing_shortfall`/recapitalization can refill the gap, but the stranded claims race the recapitalizer and are paid first-come.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address:
1. Attacker supplies a small amount into a thin market and borrows near the max against cheap collateral (or engineers insolvency on their own account via `swap`/`borrow` plus a price move; self-liquidation is allowed).
2. Once `total_debt > total_collateral` and collateral ≤ `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5), anyone calls permissionless `clean_bad_debt`, which writes off the debt via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. On a market where bad debt ≥ supplied value, `supply_index` clamps to `RAY/1000`.
3. Any scaled supply shares surviving the cleanup (e.g., a second supply position the attacker opened, or other suppliers' shares not burned) hold a phantom claim of `scaled * RAY/1000`.
4. A victim supplies fresh cash into the empty market (`cash` now covers the phantom claim).
5. Attacker calls `withdraw`; `resolve_withdrawal` converts the scaled shares at the floored index and `require_reserves` passes against the fresh deposit — draining it.

The precondition (bad debt ≥ total supplied value) requires an extreme loss on a small/young market, so Medium likelihood; the payout is a direct theft of a subsequent depositor's funds.

### Recommendation
When `remaining == 0` (write-down caps at total supplied), zero out or proportionally burn the stranded scaled supply shares, or floor the index only when `remaining > 0`. Alternatively, gate withdrawals on `resolve_withdrawal` such that claims valued at the artificial floor index cannot settle against cash that arrived after the write-down — e.g., record a per-market "floor-claim" watermark at clamp time and cap withdrawals of pre-clamp shares at the post-socialization backing ratio. At minimum, add a supply-side invariant (`supplied_claim_floor ≤ cash + debt_ceil`) to the withdrawal path rather than relying on `require_reserves` alone.

### Proof of Concept
Pinned by the pool's own tests (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs`):
- `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (lines 372-428): seeds `supplied = 1000*RAY` shares, applies bad debt of `5000*RAY` value, observes `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, then shows `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` and a `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` paying out exactly a fresh supplier's deposit (`gross == fresh_cash`), leaving `cash < bob_claim`.
- `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (lines 316-370): same pattern where a wiped user's residual drains `userB`'s deposit to `cash == 0`.
- Controller-level reachability: `clean_bad_debt` (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:163`) is permissionless under the dust cap (`is_socializable_bad_debt`, `curve.rs:25-27`); `withdraw` is the settlement leg.
