### Title
Unchecked i128 overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market — all verbs panic before the index cap engages - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
The external report describes malformed input triggering an unhandled exception that leaves a service permanently unavailable until manual intervention. The on-chain analog in XOXNO Lending is the accrual path's unchecked RAY-domain value computation: once `borrowed × borrow_index` (or `supplied × supply_index`) overflows `i128`, `scaled_to_original` panics inside `accrue_step`, and because every market verb runs `global_sync` first, the market is permanently bricked — no repay, withdraw, borrow, supply, or liquidation can ever execute again. The borrow-index cap that should bound growth never engages because the value overflow occurs first. The repo's own harness test proves the freeze end-to-end.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:51-94`) computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` at lines 60–61 before applying the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap inside `update_borrow_index` (line 66). `scaled_to_original` multiplies the scaled share amount by the index and divides by RAY; the intermediate product `shares × index` must fit `i128`. Per `docs/reference/formulas.md:434-437`, "Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first."

`global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) runs `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` at the head of every market mutation (`Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate` per `contracts/pool/README.md:159-168`). Once `borrowed × borrow_index` crosses the `i128` RAY-product ceiling, `accrue_step` panics with `MathOverflow` (error 33) on every subsequent call, for any caller, forever — panic state is not transient, it is a persistent property of stored `borrowed`/`borrow_index`/`supplied`.

The test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`) demonstrates exactly this: after the cliff, `try_update_indexes`, `try_withdraw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` confirms the cap never engaged.

An unprivileged attacker reaches this via `supply` + `borrow`: supplying the maximum admitted amount (caps admit up to `i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)`, ~170 billion whole tokens per `formulas.md:429`) and borrowing at high sustained utilization drives `borrow_index` growth until the product overflows. No privileged action, oracle manipulation, or malformed calldata beyond ordinary large amounts is required.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: all suppliers' deposits and all collateral backing the frozen market become unreachable — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, and `clean_bad_debt` all hit the same accrue-first panic. Borrowers can never repay (their debt keeps nominally growing while the market is bricked), suppliers can never exit, and bad debt can never be cleaned. For a hub/spoke market this also poisons `Context`-cached index simulation used by account views. This is materially worse than the report's restartable DoS — there is no recovery path short of a privileged upgrade.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires a market near the RAY-domain value ceiling: a token supply near the admitted cap (~170 billion whole-token units for an 18-decimal asset) combined with high utilization sustained long enough for the index product to overflow — the harness test shows the cliff reachable at 98% utilization on a steep curve, with the overflow occurring before `borrow_index` reaches 10× its initial value. The test comment at line 340 notes the documented bound in `formulas.md` understates reachability ("the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"). Not instant, but achievable by an unprivileged whale on any sufficiently large market and irreversible once triggered.

### Recommendation
Cap `borrow_index`/`supply_index` growth *before* the value-overflow domain, or make `scaled_to_original` saturating inside `accrue_step` so accrual degrades to the index cap instead of panicking. Concretely: clamp `new_borrow_index` such that `borrowed × new_borrow_index / RAY` stays within `i128` (a per-market ceiling derived from current `borrowed`/`supplied`), so the market reaches a "no further interest" steady state rather than an unrecoverable panic. Alternatively, admit caps and token-decimals domains such that `max_cap × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY / RAY` cannot overflow — currently only the cap→ray rescale is bounded (`require_cap_within_asset_domain`), not the cap×index product.

### Proof of Concept
Reachable path, all unprivileged:

```text
1. supply(caller, account, spoke, payments=[(hub, BIG_TOKEN, ~cap_max)])
   → controller::supply → pool::supply mints scaled shares near i128 RAY ceiling.
2. supply collateral on a second market; borrow(caller, account,
   payments=[(hub, BIG_TOKEN, ~98% of supplied)]) → borrowed scaled shares large.
3. Wait / advance time while utilization stays high (attacker can keep their
   own borrow open indefinitely).
4. Any subsequent call — update_indexes(caller, [hub_asset]) — enters
   pool ops → Cache::load → interest::global_sync → accrue_chunk →
   accrue_step → scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index) →
   borrowed * borrow_index overflows i128 → panic MathOverflow (33).
5. All further supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate/clean_bad_debt calls on
   that market revert identically forever; deposited funds are frozen
   permanently.
```

Demonstrated by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360` which asserts `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` after the cliff.
