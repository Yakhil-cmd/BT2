### Title
Governance vote weight excludes GBYTE balances held inside AAs, allowing minority-stake control of `op_list`/system parameters - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` computes a voter's weight for `system_vote`-based governance (which determines `op_list`, i.e. the witness/order-provider list, and other consensus parameters like `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) purely from the voter's own base-asset (`asset IS NULL`) output balance at the voting address. This mirrors the reported bug class: value that a user still economically controls, but that currently sits "elsewhere" (inside an Autonomous Agent) rather than directly at their address, is silently excluded from voting weight, fragmenting total voting power away from actual token control and enabling governance capture by whoever keeps GBYTEs unlocked at voting addresses.

### Finding Description
`countVotes()` restricts the voter-balance query to `outputs.asset IS NULL` and `outputs.address IN (...)`, summing only the plain on-chain output balance of addresses that have historically cast a `system_vote`: [1](#0-0) 

This balance snapshot is inserted into a temporary `voter_balances` table and used directly to weight both `op_list` votes and numerical parameter votes: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

However, GBYTEs deposited into any Autonomous Agent (very common — DEX AAs, bonding-curve/staking AAs, lending AAs, etc.) are moved out of the depositor's `outputs` and tracked instead in the AA's own `aa_balances` table, keyed by the AA address, not the depositor: [5](#0-4) 

AA addresses have no private key and can never author a unit, so they can never appear in `system_votes`/cast a `system_vote` message, meaning any balance parked inside an AA permanently disappears from every human depositor's counted voting weight while it remains deposited — even though the depositor retains full economic control and can withdraw/trigger the AA at will. This is architecturally identical to the reported bug: a fungible token's economic value survives being "elsewhere" (a follower chain in the original report; an AA balance here) but its governance weight does not follow it, so total counted voting power becomes a fraction of the real economically-controlled supply.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` is the single most security-critical governance subject in ocore: it selects the `COUNT_WITNESSES` order providers that anchor main-chain stability and unit finality for the whole network: [6](#0-5) 

Because voting weight is computed only from balances that stayed as plain outputs at voting addresses, an attacker who monitors how much GBYTE supply is currently locked in popular AAs (DeFi/DEX/staking) can time an `op_list` vote (or a `threshold_size`/`tps_*` numeric vote) to occur when a large share of supply is deposited in AAs. The attacker then only needs a majority of the comparatively small remaining "liquid, at-address" balance among addresses that actually vote to steer the witness list or fee/threshold parameters — a governance decision that affects DAG stability determination and fee economics for the entire network, while controlling only a minority of the real GBYTE supply.

### Likelihood Explanation
Depositing bytes into AAs is an ordinary, extremely common action available to any unprivileged user (DEX trades, liquidity provision, staking, curve/bond AAs), so the "fragmentation" condition arises naturally and continuously without any special privilege or attack setup — the attacker merely needs to time a vote/vote-count relative to normal, expected AA usage patterns. `op_list` votes are also periodic (`system_vote_count`), giving repeated opportunities to exploit favorable timing.

### Recommendation
- Short term: When computing `voter_balances` in `countVotes()`, also attribute to a voter any GBYTEs they have deposited into AAs that are attributable back to them (e.g., via AA-tracked per-depositor state, where available), or at minimum surface a warning in wallet/documentation that funds deposited into AAs lose governance voting weight until withdrawn.
- Long term: Redesign vote-weight computation to account for the full economically-controlled GBYTE supply, including funds temporarily held by AAs on behalf of users, rather than only counting balances sitting directly in a voter's own address outputs.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes (via public DAG data) that a large fraction of circulating GBYTE is currently deposited in a popular DEX/staking AA (normal, unprivileged activity by many users).
2. Attacker accumulates/controls a modest amount of GBYTE kept as plain address outputs (not deposited in any AA) and casts `system_vote` messages for `op_list` (or a numeric subject) from those addresses, followed by `system_vote_count`.
3. When `countVotes()` runs at MCI stabilization, it sums only `outputs.asset IS NULL` balances of voting addresses — excluding all GBYTE currently sitting in `aa_balances` for the depositors who chose not to vote (or whose funds are simply unreachable for voting while deposited).
4. Because the "liquid, voting-eligible" balance pool is much smaller than total supply, the attacker's minority stake becomes a majority of counted votes, letting them dictate the new `op_list` (witnesses) or consensus parameters despite controlling only a small fraction of total GBYTE supply.
