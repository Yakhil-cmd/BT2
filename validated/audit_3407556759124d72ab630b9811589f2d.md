### Title
Third-party dust `supply` force-restamps a victim's collateral risk parameters (LTV/LT/bonus/fees) - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
`process_supply` allows any unprivileged caller to top up an existing supply leg of an account they do not own (`require_third_party_existing_supply`, supply.rs:78-97). Every supply leg merge calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple` (supply.rs:288-296), which unconditionally restamps the position's stored LTV and — gated only by an HF ≥ 1.05 check that favors the liquidator — restamps the stored liquidation threshold, bonus, and fee to the current spoke listing. An attacker spending a dust amount can therefore force a victim's collateral onto newly listed, strictly worse risk terms at a time of the attacker's choosing, even though the design intends existing positions to keep their stamped terms until the owner acts. This mirrors the Sense `transferFrom(amount=0)` → forced `collect()` bug: a valueless permissionless call triggers an unwanted state-changing action on a victim's position.

### Finding Description
Supply positions store their own LTV, LT, base bonus and liquidation fee, copied at creation and meant to refresh only through three paths: a supply into the leg (`merge_supply_leg`), a non-liquidation withdrawal, or `update_account_threshold(has_risks=true)` (docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md:376-389). `merge_supply_leg` runs `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `FullTuple` unconditionally (supply.rs:288-296). Because third-party supply is restricted only to *existing* legs — not to owner/delegate callers — `require_third_party_existing_supply` (supply.rs:86-96) lets any attacker pick the moment a victim's leg is restamped.

The gate `apply_gated_liquidation_params` blocks a liquidator-favoring tuple change only when the account's HF computed under the *new* LT is below 1.05 (`THRESHOLD_UPDATE_MIN_HF_RAW`, params.rs:229-233). Consequently:

- **LTV restamps unconditionally** — even below the HF floor. Confirmed by `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:507-532): after governance lowers USDC LTV to 5000, Bob's 1-unit supply forces Alice's stored LTV to 5000 and her subsequent borrow reverts `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`.
- **LT/bonus/fee restamp whenever HF(new LT) ≥ 1.05** — i.e., precisely the window where a lower LT still leaves the account solvent but materially closer to liquidation. After `editAssetInSpoke` lowers LT, an attacker can force-apply it to any healthy victim, raising the price at which the victim becomes liquidatable and raising the max liquidation bonus `M` derived from stored LT (liqvid-listing-params.md:368-371). `update_account_threshold` cannot do this (it reverts below 1.05 and is meant for keepers), and `update_account_threshold` additionally re-checks HF post-refresh — the supply path does neither beyond the gate.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / premature liquidation. A position whose stamped LT kept it unliquidatable can be forced onto a lower listed LT by a dust supply; once restamped, a smaller price drop (or interest accrual) pushes HF below 1 and the attacker liquidates with the higher bonus implied by the new LT. The victim loses the collateral discount (bonus + seized collateral) earlier than the intended design where "an open position keeps its stored tuple" until the owner or keeper touches it. For debt-free or LTV-affected accounts the impact is loss of borrowing power (temporary freezing of funds): a forced LTV cut blocks borrows/withdrawals that were permitted under the stamped terms.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance listing change that worsens LTV/LT/bonus/fees for an asset the victim supplies — a normal operational event (e.g., the documented LT 6000→5300 move). After such a change the attack is permissionless, costs only a dust deposit (which the victim keeps, and which even slightly raises victim collateral), and is repeatable across every victim account holding that leg. Timing control is the attacker's entirely — matching the reported bug's "the `from` might not want the action at that point in time" concern.

### Recommendation
Differentiate a third-party top-up from an owner-initiated supply inside `process_supply`/`merge_supply_leg`, analogous to differentiating `transferFrom` from `collect` in the Sense fix: when the caller is neither the NFT owner nor an active delegate, credit the measured shares but skip `refresh_supply_risk_params` (or refresh with a scope that leaves the stored risk tuple untouched), so only owner/delegate actions or `update_account_threshold` restamp risk terms.

### Proof of Concept
Existing harness tests demonstrate both legs of the attack:

1. `poc_third_party_top_up_force_restamps_ltv` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/security_audit.rs:507-532): Alice supplies 10,000 USDC; governance lowers LTV to 5000 and LT to 5500; Bob calls `supply(bob, alice_account, spoke, [(USDC, 1 unit)])`; Alice's stored LTV is forced to 5000 and her `borrow` now fails `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`.
2. `test_third_party_supply_cannot_force_low_threshold_update` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/supply.rs:265-288) shows the guard covers only HF < 1.05 — for a victim above the floor the same dust supply restamps LT/bonus/fee fully. Steps: victim supplies USDC and borrows to HF ≈ 1.10; governance lowers LT; attacker supplies 1 unit to the victim's leg; stored LT drops (HF under new LT ≥ 1.05 passes the gate); the position becomes liquidatable on a smaller NAV move and the attacker liquidates at the restamped bonus.
