### Title
Sustained accrual on a large-utilization market overflows `i128` inside `accrue_step` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing every verb on the market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2022-2520 is a crash on attacker-influenced data (`tiffcrop` aborts on a `sysmalloc` assertion when rotating a crafted image). The analog in XOXNO Lending is a market-state-dependent panic in interest accrual: once a (hub, token) book's scaled debt value `borrowed * borrow_index` crosses the `i128` ceiling, `interest::global_sync` panics, and because every pool verb accrues first, the market is permanently bricked — no `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, or `update_indexes` ever succeeds again. All supplier and borrower funds in that market are frozen forever. The condition is reachable purely through unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` calls plus the passage of time.

### Finding Description
Accrual runs through `global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` at `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`. Inside `accrue_step`, utilization is computed from unscaled values via `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), and the interest split in `calculate_supplier_rewards` computes `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)` and `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`). `Ray::mul` routes to `mul_div_half_up`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the product `scaled * index` exceeds `i128::MAX` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`).

The protective clamp `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` only bounds `new_index` *after* `old_index.mul(interest_factor)` in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`); it does not bound the *value* product `borrowed * borrow_index`. For a large book (e.g., ~10^9 whole tokens at 18 decimals ⇒ `borrowed` ≈ 10^36 raw RAY), the `i128::MAX` ≈ 1.7×10^38 ceiling is hit at a borrow index of only ~170× — far below the 10^36-raw index cap — and at high utilization on a steep curve the index reaches that multiple in a few years. Since `global_sync` runs before every market mutation (`contracts/pool/README.md:159-168`), the panic bricks the entire market, not just one transaction shape. This is demonstrated end-to-end by `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-361`), which shows `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all reverting with `MATH_OVERFLOW` once the cliff is crossed.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every participant in the affected (hub, token) book: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot liquidate — even an account that goes deeply underwater is unseizable because `liquidate` accrues first. Protocol revenue in that market is also unclaimable. The state is unrecoverable through any unprivileged or privileged entrypoint short of a contract upgrade, since every path touching the market runs the panicking accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger needs a large book at sustained high utilization for a long period. Position sizes are bounded by per-spoke-asset supply/borrow caps, but `max_cap_for_decimals` explicitly permits caps up to the 18-decimal domain where the cliff lives, and the freeze can also arise organically from honest whale-scale usage plus interest drift — the fuzz/docs note utilization drifts upward on its own because debt compounds faster than supply. No attacker privileges, oracle manipulation, or special transaction is needed: `supply`, `borrow`, and time are sufficient. The horizon and size requirements keep this at Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Make accrual saturating rather than panicking at the value ceiling. In `calculate_supplier_rewards` and the utilization path, use `mul_div_floor_saturating`-style value computation (or clamp `borrowed`/`supplied` to `i128::MAX / index` before multiplying) so that once the book hits the representable ceiling, accrual becomes a no-op instead of an abort — mirroring how `mul_div_floor_saturating` is already used for `update_supply_index` growth and `protocol_fee_shares`. At minimum, gate `accrue_chunk` to skip accrual when `borrowed * borrow_index` would overflow, so exits and repayments remain possible at the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`: list a high-decimals market, `supply` ~10^9 whole tokens, `borrow` ~98% of it, advance ledger time year-by-year calling the permissionless `update_indexes`; accrual eventually reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert identically.
