### Title
Scaled RAY value overflow bricks a market before the index cap can engage - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
A single funded caller can drive a `(hub_id, asset)` market into a state where `scaled * index` no longer fits `i128`. The next accrual panics inside the fixed-point unscale path before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` / `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` clamp anything, and because every pool mutation syncs interest first, later repay, withdraw, liquidation and cleanup calls for that market keep reverting. This is a permanent market freeze, not a one-off failed transaction.

### Finding Description
`controller::update_indexes(caller, assets)` is permissionless and forwards the list to the pool (`contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:118-125`). The pool’s `update_indexes` calls `ops::market::accrue`, which loads each `Cache` and runs `interest::global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/market.rs:65-72`, `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`). All normal pool legs do the same through `ops::synced_market` / `load_leg` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs:29-47`).

The overflowing products are the market’s scaled RAY values: utilization computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:19-27`), while accrual computes `borrowed * old/new_borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` and `update_supply_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:73-88`, `common/src/rates/index.rs:29-45`). `scaled_to_original` is just `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:12-16`), and `update_borrow_index` applies the cap only after `old_index.mul(interest_factor)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:11-19`). Once `borrowed * borrow_index` or `supplied * supply_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, these paths raise `MathOverflow` before the index ceiling or supply-index floor can repair the book.

The in-tree stress test states the same mechanism and impact directly: a 1e9-token 18-decimal book is `1e36` scaled RAY, the value ceiling is `i128::MAX`, and after the index passes ~170x “the next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original`”; it then asserts `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-356`).

### Impact Explanation
All suppliers’ principal and yield in the affected market become permanently frozen: withdrawals accrue first and hit the same overflow. Borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize or socialize the position, `recapitalize` still routes through pool state that requires accrual/backing reads, and bad debt cannot be cleaned. Because ledger time only increases and the stored scaled totals/index remain above the product ceiling, every later entrypoint repeats the same panic; this matches the accepted impact classes of permanent freezing of funds and a market unable to operate.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger needs an extreme but representable book: governance cap validation only bounds caps by `max_cap_for_decimals`, i.e. whether `cap` rescales to RAY, not whether `scaled_total * future_index` stays below `i128::MAX` (`common/src/validation.rs:48-70`). For 18 decimals the domain still admits roughly `i128::MAX / 1e9` base units, far above the ~1e9-whole-token scale used by the test. A whale can supply the target asset, post collateral in another listed market, borrow near the cap, keep utilization high on a steep rate curve, then let interest accrue until the scaled value crosses the ceiling; in a shared market the attacker only needs to push aggregate `supplied`/`borrowed` over the edge, so unrelated suppliers are frozen too. The capital and configuration requirements keep this below Critical/High, but it is reachable without privileged keys when caps and utilization limits are configured near the admitted domain.

### Recommendation
Bound market caps by post-index value, not just by `Ray::from_asset(cap)`: enforce `cap_ray <= i128::MAX / MAX_*_INDEX_RAY` at listing/edit time and re-check on accrual. Make accrual use widened/saturating total-value math for utilization and index growth, clamp indexes before multiplying by scaled totals, and avoid ever requiring `supplied * supply_index` or `borrowed * borrow_index` to fit `i128` to keep a live market operable. Add a governance runbook to lower caps before large books approach the bound.

### Proof of Concept
Use the existing deterministic reproduction as the template: create an 18-decimal market on the steep `xlm_curve`, lift supply/borrow caps to `max_cap_for_decimals(18)` and disable the utilization ceiling, `supply` 1_000_000_000 whole tokens, post collateral and `borrow` 98% of it, then advance ledger time in yearly chunks while calling permissionless `controller.update_indexes(caller, [hub_asset])`. The loop ends when `try_update_indexes` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index` still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls also return `MATH_OVERFLOW`, demonstrating the market is bricked (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`).
