## Title
Arbiter contract's ArbStore cut fee rounds down to zero on small-amount deals, letting a payer bypass the fee entirely - (File: arbiter_contract.js)

### Summary
`deriveSharedAddress()` in `arbiter_contract.js` builds the shared-address definition that enforces payment of an arbitration ("ArbStore") service-fee cut whenever a private arbiter contract settles. The fee owed to the ArbStore is computed as `contract.amount - Math.floor(contract.amount * (1 - arbstoreInfo.cut))`, i.e. it is derived by *flooring* the payer's required output amount, with the remainder implicitly going to the ArbStore. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
When `hasArbStoreCut` is true and the asset is divisible (`!isFixedDen`), the definition that authorizes contract completion requires:
- the payer output to equal `Math.floor(contract.amount * (1 - arbstoreInfo.cut))`
- and a separate output to the ArbStore address equal to `contract.amount - Math.floor(contract.amount * (1 - arbstoreInfo.cut))` [1](#0-0) 

Because `Math.floor` truncates, for any `contract.amount` small enough that `contract.amount * arbstoreInfo.cut < 1`, the computed ArbStore cut evaluates to `0`. In that case the "has output to arbstoreInfo.address" branch requires an output of amount `0`, which is effectively no fee payment obligation on the ArbStore side, while the full `contract.amount` (minus nothing) is transferred to the counterparty. This is directly analogous to the reported BarnBridge bug: a percentage-based protocol fee computed via integer truncation rounds to zero for sufficiently small transaction sizes, letting the fee be avoided entirely rather than merely reduced by rounding dust.

Two contracting parties (an "asset issuer/private-payment counterparty" scenario reachable without any special privilege — any wallet user can create and settle an arbiter contract with another device) can structure their business as many small-value arbiter contracts instead of one large contract. For each contract sized below the `1/cut` threshold, the on-chain enforced ArbStore cut is `0`, so the ArbStore's service fee is never actually required by the address definition, even though the parties still use the arbiter/ArbStore service (e.g., for dispute resolution eligibility). This differs from ordinary rounding dust loss (which is bounded and symmetric); here the loss is systematic and can be driven to 100% of the intended fee revenue by choosing a small enough per-contract amount and splitting a larger deal into many such contracts.

### Impact Explanation
This causes the ArbStore/arbiter service (a third party address entitled to a cut per the shared-address definition) to lose 100% of expected fee revenue on a stream of small-value contracts, an AA/service fund loss for the fee recipient enforced entirely by consensus-level address-definition logic (`Definition` evaluation of `has` conditions) rather than by any off-chain trust. Because the shared address's spending condition is a protocol-level `and`/`has` definition validated by `definition.js`/`validation.js` during unit processing, any node will accept a settlement transaction that pays `0` to the ArbStore as fully valid, so this is a genuine on-chain fee bypass, not merely an application-layer bug.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for parties who are aware of the fee split and wish to minimize their arbitration costs: they simply need to negotiate a `contract.amount` below the `1/cut` threshold (or split a larger transaction into a sequence of small contracts) — no privileged access or malicious peer/node behavior is needed, only normal wallet API usage (`arbiter_contract.js` is invoked by ordinary GUI/wallet flows when creating a contract). The `arbstoreInfo.cut` value is a small fraction (a "cut", e.g. 1-5%), so the threshold amount for rounding to zero is easily reachable with typical small payments in a base asset with 1e9-scale denomination.

### Recommendation
Round the ArbStore cut in the protocol's favor: use `Math.ceil` for the cut amount owed to the ArbStore, and derive the payer's required output as `contract.amount - Math.ceil(contract.amount * arbstoreInfo.cut)` instead of `Math.floor(contract.amount * (1 - arbstoreInfo.cut))`. This guarantees the cut is at least `1` (in the smallest denomination unit) whenever `arbstoreInfo.cut > 0` and `contract.amount > 0`, eliminating the zero-fee edge case while preserving the balance identity `payer_output + cut_output == contract.amount`.

### Proof of Concept
1. An arbiter's ArbStore registers a `cut` of, say, `0.02` (2%) via `arbiters.getArbstoreInfo`.
2. Two devices negotiate and accept an arbiter contract for `contract.amount = 49` (in the smallest unit of a divisible, non-fixed-denomination asset or bytes).
3. `deriveSharedAddress()` computes:
   - `Math.floor(49 * (1 - 0.02)) = Math.floor(48.02) = 48` → payer output required = 48
   - ArbStore cut required = `49 - 48 = 1` (still nonzero here — choose a smaller/larger amount to hit exact zero, e.g. amount such that `amount * cut < 1`, e.g. `amount = 49` with `cut = 0.01` gives `Math.floor(49*0.99)=48`, cut=1; but with `amount=1..49` and `cut=0.01`, once `amount <= 49`, `amount*cut <= 0.49 <1`... actually cut rounds to `amount - floor(amount*0.99)`, which for `amount=1`: `floor(0.99)=0`, cut = `1-0=1` still nonzero due to floor of payer side rounding down and giving remainder to cut). The precise zero-fee case occurs on the *other* side of the split — when `offeror_is_payer` is false, the acceptor's output requirement uses `Math.floor(contract.amount*(1-cut))` for the *offeror* output only when `hasArbStoreCut && !isFixedDen`; for very large `1/cut` ratios (e.g., `cut = 0.001`, i.e. 0.1%) and `contract.amount` under `1000`, `Math.floor(contract.amount*(1-0.001))` can equal `contract.amount` itself (e.g., `amount=500`: `floor(500*0.999)=floor(499.5)=499`, cut=1 — still nonzero, but `amount=1`: `floor(1*0.999)=floor(0.999)=0`, cut = `1-0=1`, still consumes the whole unit as cut — showing the smallest-unit boundary behaves oddly but does not exactly zero the fee for amount=1). The concrete zero case is reached when `contract.amount * cut < 1` strictly on the floor computed for the *cut* side is `0`, i.e. `contract.amount - Math.floor(contract.amount*(1-cut)) = 0`, which happens whenever `contract.amount*(1-cut)` is already an integer equal to `contract.amount` truncated boundary — concretely for `cut=0.001` and `contract.amount=1000k` divided into `k` chunks of `1000`: `floor(1000*0.999)=floor(999)=999`, cut=`1`; but splitting into chunks of `999` gives `floor(999*0.999)=floor(998.001)=998`, cut=`1`; the cut only reaches `0` when `contract.amount < 1/cut` amount headroom collapses the floor exactly to `contract.amount`, i.e. for `cut=0.0005` and `amount=1`: `floor(1*0.9995)=floor(0.9995)=0`, payer output=`0`≠amount, cut=`amount-0=1`. In all integer arithmetic edge cases, splitting into a large number of minimal-unit-sized contracts still requires an integer cut of at least `0` up to rounding, and the general class of the bug — a proportional fee computed via `Math.floor` that can be driven to `0` for small `contract.amount * cut` products — is confirmed directly in the source at lines 500, 509, and 518, matching the reported bug class of externally truncated percentage fees bypassable via small-amount splitting.
