### Title
Unchecked aggregate debt multiplication can permanently freeze a market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
**Severity: Medium.** `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies the aggregate scaled debt by both the old and new borrow indexes before subtracting the two totals, without first bounding the aggregate result to `i128` [1](#0-0) . Although `update_borrow_index` caps the index itself, that cap does not prevent `borrowed * index` from overflowing [2](#0-1) . Any caller can reach the vulnerable accrual through `Controller::update_indexes(caller, assets)`, which forwards the requested markets to the pool [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description
Every accrual runs `accrue_chunk`, computes the borrow/supply step, updates both indexes, and books protocol revenue [4](#0-3) . The interest step calls `calculate_supplier_rewards`, which computes `borrowed * old_borrow_index` and `borrowed * new_borrow_index` as raw `i128` fixed-point products [1](#0-0) . For a sufficiently large market, either product can exceed `i128::MAX` even while `new_borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` [5](#0-4) . The resulting `MathOverflow` panic aborts the accrual rather than capping or chunking the aggregate debt value [6](#0-5) .

### Impact Explanation
Once total scaled debt and index growth cross the `i128` aggregate-value boundary, `update_indexes` cannot complete for that market. User-facing `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, and `clean_bad_debt` all depend on pool market accounting and therefore cannot safely proceed while the market cannot accrue [7](#0-6) . The pool also accrues before replacing a rate model, so a routine governance parameter update cannot bypass the overflow [8](#0-7) . This permanently freezes supplier withdrawals, borrower repayments, liquidations, and protocol revenue in the affected market absent a contract upgrade or equivalent privileged recovery [9](#0-8) .

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger is economically expensive but does not require privileged access, oracle manipulation, leaked keys, or malformed parameters. One account can supply the target asset, supply collateral in another listed asset, and borrow the target asset at high utilization through the ordinary `supply` and `borrow` entrypoints [10](#0-9) . Existing suppliers reduce the amount the attacker must contribute, and caps rather than user permissions determine whether the required market size is reachable. The deterministic harness reproduces the condition with a billion whole 18-decimal tokens at 98% utilization and confirms that index growth overflows before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages [11](#0-10) .

### Recommendation
Bound aggregate value, not only the index, before calculating rewards. `calculate_supplier_rewards` should use saturating or wider arithmetic for `borrowed * index`, detect that the representable debt ceiling has been reached, and clamp both totals to a common representable value rather than panic. Alternatively, cap `borrow_index` as a function of aggregate `borrowed` so `borrowed * borrow_index <= i128::MAX` is an explicit invariant. Add a regression test covering `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, and `liquidate` after the capped state is reached [1](#0-0) .

### Proof of Concept
1. An attacker calls `Controller::supply` with `account_id = 0`, supplying a very large amount of an 18-decimal market asset plus sufficient collateral in another listed asset [12](#0-11) .
2. The attacker calls `Controller::borrow` on the created account and borrows approximately 98% of the supplied target asset [13](#0-12) .
3. After sustained high-utilization interest growth, any address calls `Controller::update_indexes(attacker, vec![target_hub_asset])` [3](#0-2) .
4. Accrual invokes `calculate_supplier_rewards`; `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, so the call reverts with `MathOverflow` before the index cap can protect the market [1](#0-0) .
5. Subsequent `repay` and `withdraw` attempts revert for the same reason, leaving borrowed tokens, supplier claims, liquidation proceeds, and revenue inaccessible [11](#0-10) .

    env: &Env,
    params: &MarketParams,
    borrowed: Ray,
    new_borrow_index: Ray,
    old_borrow_index: Ray,

    let old_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index);
    let new_total_debt = borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index);

/// Applies one compound step of `delta_ms` to indexes and protocol revenue.
///
/// The arithmetic lives in [`accrue_step`], shared with the read-only
/// `simulate_update_indexes` so the view and the mutator cannot drift.
fn accrue_chunk(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, delta_ms: u64) {
    let step = accrue_step(
        env,
        cache.params(),
        cache.borrowed(),
        cache.supplied(),
        cache.borrow_index(),
        cache.supply_index(),
        delta_ms,
    );

    cache.set_borrow_index(step.borrow_index);
    cache.set_supply_index(step.supply_index);
    cache.accrue_revenue(step.revenue_shares);
}

fn checked_add_raw(env: &Env, a: i128, b: i128) -> i128 {
    a.checked_add(b)

/// The cliff. A billion whole tokens is `1e36` raw ray; the value ceiling is
/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
