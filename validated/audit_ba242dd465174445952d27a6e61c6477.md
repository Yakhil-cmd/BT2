### Title
Attacker-planted unpriceable dust supply leg permanently bricks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup, leaving bad debt stuck in the pool - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
`liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` both compute account risk via `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals`, which requires a valid price for **every** supply and debt position on the account. Because `supply` accepts a position in any listed market — including one whose oracle feed is or becomes unpriceable — an unprivileged borrower can attach a worthless dust leg priced by a stale/failing feed. Any subsequent `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt` call on that account reverts with `PriceFeedStale`, so the account's debt can never be repaid by liquidators nor socialized, while interest keeps accruing. This is the MySQL CVE-2024-21060 "complete DOS" class mapped onto XOXNO's liquidation-liveness surface, reachable entirely by an unprivileged address.

### Finding Description
- `process_liquidation` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:36`) plans seizure over all collateral legs after computing full account risk; `socialize_bad_debt` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:222-227`) calls `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals` over both position books before the dust gate.
- `calculate_account_risk_totals` fetches strict prices for every leg, so one stale feed aborts the whole call.
- `supply` admits a dust position even when the market's price feed is already stale — the harness test `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` proves supply succeeds with a stale WBTC feed, and that afterward both `try_liquidate` and `try_clean_bad_debt` revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed recovers (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:26-47`).
- The attacker picks a dust collateral whose oracle (e.g., a thin Reflector pair) is liable to stop updating — an asset-quality choice the attacker controls, not an assumption of oracle honesty. Docs confirm cleanup "requires valid required prices" and "listing flags and global pause do not waive pricing" (`docs/reference/runbooks/force-socialize-bad-debt.md:34-35`), so even the governed `force_socialize_bad_debt` path is blocked. ### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / protocol insolvency. Once the feed goes stale, the attacker's account is unliquidatable and uncleanable: `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `force_socialize_bad_debt` all revert. The debt keeps accruing interest; when collateral value no longer covers it, the shortfall can never be written down through `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, so solvent suppliers' claims are stranded — withdrawals fail for lack of pool cash reserved against the frozen debt. Unlike a fail-closed pause, the funds are permanently locked for as long as the chosen feed stays dead, and the attacker chose a feed they expect to die.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires (a) a listed market whose oracle feed can stop updating, and (b) the attacker's position to become insolvent — which the attacker can engineer by borrowing aggressively and waiting for adverse price movement, then supplying the dust leg beforehand since `supply` accepts stale-feed markets. Cost is only the dust amount and gas. Recovery depends on an off-chain feed operator resuming, which is outside the protocol's control.

### Recommendation
Decouple liquidation/cleanup liveness from collateral-leg pricing for legs whose liquidation value is negligible: either (a) skip supply legs valued below the dust threshold when computing risk for `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`, (b) reject `supply` when the market's price feed fails a freshness check, or (c) treat an unpriceable dust collateral leg as zero value in the risk total so cleanup can proceed. At minimum, listing review should refuse markets whose oracle source can permanently stall.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:3-52`):

1. Borrower supplies 10,000 USDC, borrows 3 ETH.
2. WBTC's Reflector feed is backdated 3,600s (stale); `t.try_supply(borrower, "WBTC", 0.001)` succeeds — supply does not check feed freshness.
3. USDC price is dropped so HF < 1.
4. `try_liquidate(LIQUIDATOR, borrower, "ETH", 1.0)` → `PRICE_FEED_STALE`.
5. `try_clean_bad_debt_by_id(borrower_id)` → `PRICE_FEED_STALE`.
6. Only after `set_price("WBTC", …)` restores the feed does the identical liquidation succeed — confirming the dust leg alone bricked every recovery path.
