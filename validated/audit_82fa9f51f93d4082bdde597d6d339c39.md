### Title
Governance vote weight uses current voter balance instead of balance-at-vote-time, allowing manipulation of `op_list` and system parameters - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` tallies `system_vote` messages (used for electing the order-provider/witness list `op_list` and for numerical protocol parameters such as `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) by weighting each voter's ballot with that address's **current** stable balance, queried at the moment the count runs, rather than the balance the address actually held when it cast the vote.

### Finding Description
`countVotes` first collects every distinct address that has ever posted a `system_vote` for the subject being tallied [1](#0-0) , then computes each address's balance from the **live** stable-good UTXO set at the time of the call [2](#0-1) . That live balance is stored into a `voter_balances` temp table and joined against the `system_votes`/`op_votes`/`numerical_votes` tables, which record the address and the vote timestamp but not the balance held at vote time [3](#0-2) . The tally is then done purely by summing this current balance over addresses whose vote timestamp falls in the accepted window [4](#0-3) .

This is structurally the same defect described in the Babylon report: voting/reward weight is computed from an entity's **current** stake rather than the **historical** stake that was actually committed at the decision point being evaluated. Because the ledger records only the vote (address + timestamp), and weight is recomputed from whatever balance exists in that address at count time, any balance change after voting (spending, receiving, or shuffling funds) retroactively changes the influence of a past vote.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` determines the set of order providers (witnesses) that stabilize the DAG, and `threshold_size` / `base_tps_fee` / `tps_interval` / `tps_fee_multiplier` are core consensus/fee parameters. Both are decided entirely by this current-balance tally:
- A holder who voted while owning a large balance and later spends/moves the funds keeps full voting weight attributed to their vote as long as the address is queried before they empty it, but if they spend before the count executes, their vote is silently diluted or nullified even though it was validly cast with real committed stake at the time.
- Conversely, an address that voted with a small balance can have its vote weight inflated to any amount by receiving funds into that same address just before the (deterministic, height-triggered) count executes, then moving the funds elsewhere afterward. Since the count is triggered deterministically off finalized ledger state (at specific MCIs / vote-count commands), an attacker can time a large but transient balance to land in a voting address exactly when the tally runs, letting a small amount of committed stake be leveraged into outsized influence over who becomes an order provider or what the network-wide fee parameters are — a form of "stake" that was never actually locked in at vote time.

Because this directly decides the operator/witness set and global fee parameters used by the whole network (not merely a single AA or address balance), a manipulated outcome can shift protocol-level governance (e.g., installing operators favorable to an attacker, or setting fee parameters that degrade network throughput/availability) — i.e. it can produce a network-wide disagreement about legitimate governance outcomes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires only posting ordinary, unprivileged `system_vote` messages (any address can vote) and controlling the timing of a temporary balance in the voting address around the deterministic vote-count trigger. No special node/hub/peer privilege is needed — this is reachable purely by an unprivileged unit poster manipulating their own address's balance and vote timing, which is a realistic and repeatable attack surface, though it does require some precision in timing the balance around the count execution (e.g., MCI-triggered `vote_count_mci` or a `system_vote_count` command).

### Recommendation
Record the voter's balance (or equivalent committed stake) at the time the `system_vote` unit is included/stabilized, and persist that snapshot alongside the vote (similar to how `headers_commission_contributions`/`witnessing_outputs` capture amounts at a fixed historical point) rather than re-deriving weight from the live balance table at tally time in `countVotes`. Alternatively, require votes to reference/lock the balance as of the vote unit's `last_ball_mci`, and re-validate that the weight used in `countVotes` is drawn from that historical snapshot instead of `SELECT ... FROM outputs ... WHERE units.is_stable=1` computed at count time [2](#0-1) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Address `V` posts a `system_vote` for `threshold_size=X` while holding a negligible balance (e.g., dust). This is recorded in `system_votes`/`numerical_votes` with only `address` and `timestamp`, no balance snapshot [5](#0-4) .
2. Knowing that `countVotes` for this subject will be triggered at a specific, deterministic point (e.g., a `system_vote_count` command unit reaching stability), the controller of `V` arranges for a large, temporary balance to be stable in `V` exactly at that MCI (e.g., via a self-payment finalized just before the triggering unit stabilizes).
3. When `countVotes` executes, it computes `V`'s weight from its current stable balance [6](#0-5) , giving `V`'s stale vote for `X` outsized weight in the `GROUP BY value` tally [7](#0-6) , potentially tipping the majority outcome to `X` even though `V` never actually held that stake when the vote was cast.
4. Immediately after the tally, the controller moves the funds out of `V`; the network parameter (or, analogously, an `op_list` slot) is now committed to `X`/the attacker's preferred outcome based on transient, uncommitted balance rather than the stake genuinely backing the vote.
