## Analysis

The stETH report describes a classic "direct balance donation inflates a computed price/ratio" bug: an unprivileged actor sends value straight to a contract, the contract computes a price/share ratio from a *raw* balance read (not an internally tracked accounting value), and that manipulated ratio damages other users of the pool.

Ocore's oscript layer has the exact analogous primitive: the `balance[asset]` / `balance[address][asset]` formula operator, which returns the AA's raw on-chain balance as tracked in the `aa_balances` table, populated automatically from **any** output sent to the AA's address [1](#0-0) . This is the oscript equivalent of Solidity's `STETH.balanceOf(address(this))` — it is not an "accounted"/internal ledger value, it is whatever bytes/asset units have physically landed at the address, and AA authors are explicitly encouraged (in ocore's own shipped sample AAs) to use it directly for computing share-issuance and pay-out ratios.

`test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` is ocore's official example of a share/vault-style AA and it computes investor share issuance and divestment payouts directly from `balance[$asset]` / `balance[base]`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

`test/samples/51_attack_game.oscript` similarly sets `var['total'] = balance[base]` to compute a payout ratio for winners [4](#0-3) .

Crucially, the protocol itself provides a free, repeatable way for an unprivileged AA trigger sender to inflate this raw balance without minting any shares: when a trigger unit's data doesn't satisfy any `case`/condition (or otherwise fails), the AA bounces, refunding `trigger.outputs - bounce_fees`, but **permanently keeps `bounce_fees[asset]`** in its own `aa_balances`: [5](#0-4) 

That retained bounce fee is added straight into `aa_balances`/`objValidationState.assocBalances` via `updateInitialAABalances`, which unconditionally credits every asset in `trigger.outputs` to the target AA before any case-matching logic runs [6](#0-5) .

## Assessment

This maps to the reported bug class:
- An unprivileged AA trigger sender can repeatedly send tiny triggers that don't match any `if` case in a vault/MM-style AA, causing bounces that leave `bounce_fees.base` permanently in `aa_balances`.
- The AA's own logic (as shown in ocore's official `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` sample) then reads that inflated `balance[base]`/`balance[asset]` directly when computing `$issue_amount` / `$investor_share`, exactly like the stETH `balanceOf()` read inflating share price.
- A legitimate depositor's share calculation (`round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * var['mm_asset_outstanding'])`) can round down to 0 once the balance is sufficiently inflated relative to `mm_asset_outstanding`, while their deposited funds remain in the AA and become claimable by the existing/attacker shareholder on divest.

However, per the validation rules, this requires the *AA's own oscript logic* to trust `balance[]` for pricing — the root "donation" primitive (bounce-fee retention inflating `aa_balances`) lives in core (`aa_composer.js`, `formula/evaluation.js`), but the actual fund-loss impact only manifests in AA scripts written to use `balance[]` naively for share pricing, as ocore's own documentation/sample AAs do. This is a real, reachable core-level design gap (raw, unprotected balance primitive) that produces concrete fund-loss/dilution impact in the officially-demonstrated usage pattern, satisfying the "AA fund loss" acceptance criterion.

### Title
Unaccounted `balance[asset]` Inflation via Retained Bounce Fees Enables Share-Price Manipulation in Balance-Based AAs - (File: `aa_composer.js`, `formula/evaluation.js`)

### Summary
The oscript `balance[asset]` primitive returns the AA's raw, protocol-tracked `aa_balances` value, which any unprivileged sender can inflate for free by repeatedly triggering the AA with data that fails all its `if` cases, causing a bounce that permanently retains `bounce_fees[asset]` in the AA's balance. AAs that use `balance[]` directly to compute share-issuance ratios or payouts (as demonstrated by ocore's own official sample `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`) inherit a donation/inflation vulnerability analogous to the reported stETH `balanceOf()` issue.

### Finding Description
`readBalance()` in `formula/evaluation.js` returns whatever is currently recorded in `aa_balances` for the address/asset, with no distinction between funds credited through a "legitimate" flow (e.g. an `invest` case that also mints a share asset) and funds credited incidentally [1](#0-0) .

`updateInitialAABalances()` unconditionally adds every `trigger.outputs[asset]` to `aa_balances` for the target address before any AA-specific `if`/`case` logic is evaluated [6](#0-5) .

If the trigger fails to match any `if` case, `bounce()` refunds `trigger.outputs[asset] - bounce_fees[asset]` but leaves `bounce_fees[asset]` permanently in the AA's balance [5](#0-4) . An attacker can invoke this repeatedly and cheaply (cost ≈ `bounce_fees.base`, default `constants.MIN_BYTES_BOUNCE_FEE`, per attempt), each time incrementing `balance[base]` without triggering any of the AA's minting logic.

Because ocore's own documented sample AA (`uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`) computes share issuance and divestment payouts directly from `balance[$asset]`/`balance[base]` [2](#0-1) , an inflated balance directly distorts `$current_ratio`, `$issue_amount`, and `$investor_share` for all future interactions with the AA, exactly mirroring the stETH `balanceOf()`-based share-price inflation reported upstream.

### Impact Explanation
Once `balance[base]`/`balance[$asset]` is inflated relative to `var['mm_asset_outstanding']`:
- New investors' `$issue_amount = round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * var['mm_asset_outstanding'])` can round down to zero for normal-sized deposits, causing the AA to silently keep their funds (the zero-amount payment output is filtered out, so nothing is returned) — a direct loss of funds for the depositor.
- Existing/attacker shareholders divesting via `round($investor_share * balance[$asset])` can extract a disproportionate share of the pool, including the funds effectively donated via retained bounce fees and any subsequently trapped victim deposits.
- Exchange-rate formulas (`exchange bytes to asset` / `exchange asset to bytes` cases) are similarly skewed, allowing profitable arbitrage against the manipulated price.

This constitutes AA fund loss/freezing for legitimate depositors and unfair value extraction for the attacker, meeting the Medium/High impact bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires no special privilege — any address can post a trigger unit to the target AA. The cost per inflation step is small (a single bounce-fee amount, on the order of the network's minimum bounce fee), and it can be repeated arbitrarily many times before a victim deposit, or interleaved with monitoring the target AA's balance/state. The precondition (an AA that prices shares from raw `balance[]` rather than an internally-tracked "accounted" state variable) is exactly the pattern ocore's own reference examples teach AA authors to use, making this a realistic, systemic risk across balance-based AAs deployed on the network, not merely a contrived edge case.

### Recommendation
- Document prominently (and update the official sample AAs, e.g. `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`) that `balance[asset]` reflects the *raw* tracked balance and can be inflated by bounce-fee retention or other unaccounted inflows; AA authors should instead track pool reserves in dedicated state vars (`var['reserve_base']`, `var['reserve_asset']`) updated only inside the minting/burning logic, analogous to the report's own recommendation to track balances independently rather than trusting `balanceOf()`.
- Consider whether `bounce()` should refund the entire `trigger.outputs` amount (net of actual network fees) rather than unconditionally retaining the full configured `bounce_fees`, or clearly separate "AA-owned fee revenue" balance from balances an AA's oscript exposes via `balance[]` for pricing purposes.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy an AA modeled on `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` (or any AA that reads `balance[base]`/`balance[asset]` to compute share/pool ratios).
2. Attacker becomes first depositor with a minimal `invest` trigger, receiving the sole `mm_asset` shares, setting `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` to a small value.
3. Attacker repeatedly sends trigger units whose `data`/`outputs` satisfy none of the AA's `if` cases (e.g., a tiny base-byte payment with no asset and no matching data), each of which bounces via `aa_composer.js`'s `bounce()` and permanently retains `bounce_fees.base` in `aa_balances` [7](#0-6) .
4. After enough repetitions, `balance[base]` is inflated far beyond `var['mm_asset_outstanding']`.
5. A victim deposits a normal amount via the legitimate `invest` case; `$issue_amount` rounds to 0 due to the inflated `$bytes_balance` denominator, so the victim receives no `mm_asset` and their bytes remain trapped in the AA's balance.
6. The attacker divests their original shares via the `divest` case, receiving `round($investor_share * balance[$asset])`/`balance[base]`, capturing both their own initial deposit and the victim's trapped funds.
