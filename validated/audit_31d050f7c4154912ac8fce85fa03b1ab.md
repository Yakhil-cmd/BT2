### Title
Whale-scale market reaches the i128 ray-value ceiling before the borrow-index cap, permanently freezing all operations on that market - (File: tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs)

### Summary
The analog of BIT-mariadb-2021-2174 (a reachable condition that turns into a repeatable crash → complete loss of availability) is the ray-value overflow cliff in the pool's accrual path. Every state-changing verb on a market runs `Cache::load → interest::global_sync` first, and interest accrual unscales scaled shares/debt by the borrow index (`scaled_to_original`-style `shares * index` in RAY). When `scaled_amount * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics with `MathOverflow` — and it panics again on every subsequent call, so the market never recovers. The dedicated test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` drives exactly this cliff: at ~98% utilization on the steep XLM curve, the RAY value of the position overflows `i128` while `borrow_index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, i.e., the safety cap that was supposed to bound index growth never engages.

### Finding Description
- Flow: every mutation accrues first — "Each mutation of an existing market runs: entrypoint → Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate → guards → commit" [1](#0-0) 
- The accrual unscales debt by the index; the ray value `scaled_amount * index` is bounded by `i128`, and a billion-token (18-decimal) book at sustained high utilization crosses that bound years before `borrow_index` reaches `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` [2](#0-1) 
- Once over the cliff, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MathOverflow` (33) — "the market freezes: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation... The index cap never engages" [3](#0-2) 
- The panic is reachable through the permissionless entrypoints `supply` / `borrow` (to build the position) and `update_indexes` / any later verb (to trip the accrual). No admin action, oracle manipulation, or pause is involved — it is purely the interest math overflowing inside the normal flow.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds on the affected market: once `scaled_amount * borrow_index` overflows `i128`, every call that touches the market panics in accrual before any mutation runs. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and bad-debt cleanup (`clean_bad_debt`) also accrues first. Because the borrow index is monotone non-decreasing [4](#0-3) , there is no path back under the cliff — all cash held by the pool for that market is locked in the contract indefinitely, matching the report's "complete DOS" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses but capital- and time-intensive. It requires a position whose ray value approaches `i128::MAX / index` — on the order of a billion whole tokens at 18 decimals — plus sustained near-max utilization for enough accrual time to grow the index past the overflow threshold. Supply and borrow caps (`lift_caps` in the test) must also permit the size, so it is most plausible on a market with high/zero caps and a steep rate curve. Once the book is large enough, no attacker action is even needed: ordinary compounding pushes the market over the cliff on its own, and any single unprivileged `update_indexes` call seals it. Medium likelihood at best for low-cap markets; the test bounds the cliff within 40 years at 98% utilization on the steepest curve.

### Recommendation
Cap `scaled_amount * index` arithmetic explicitly rather than relying on `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`: either (a) enforce `borrow_index` growth to stop accrual (clamp the index instead of overflowing) once the unscaling product approaches `i128::MAX`, or (b) bound per-market scaled supply/debt caps such that `scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY < i128::MAX` — i.e., derive `supply_cap`/`borrow_cap` from the index ceiling at listing time, not just token decimals. At minimum, make `update_indexes` able to clamp the index at the point of overflow so repay/withdraw paths remain usable instead of panicking forever.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` [5](#0-4) : supply 1e9·10^18 of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98%, advance time; the first `update_indexes` that overflows returns `MathOverflow`, and subsequent `withdraw(BOB, 1)` and `repay(ALICE, 1.0)` revert with the same error while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. All attacker steps are permissionless `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes` calls.

```rust
/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    std::println!(
        "ray-value cliff reached after {years} years at 98 percent utilization on the XLM curve; last index x{:.1}",
        last.borrow_index as f64 / RAY as f64
    );
```
