### Title
`claim_revenue` pays protocol revenue out of cash that backs underwater supplier claims — no backing check lets the protocol drain residual funds after bad debt - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs)

### Summary
The pool tracks a single `cash` reserve per market that backs **all** supply claims (suppliers plus protocol revenue shares). `claim_revenue` burns revenue shares and pays out `min(cash, floor(revenue_value))`, but unlike `supply` it never checks `backing_shortfall`/`require_backed_market`. After a bad-debt write-down leaves residual supplier claims exceeding `cash + debt` (a state the protocol itself documents as reachable because of the non-zero supply-index floor), any caller can invoke `claim_revenue` to extract the remaining cash as protocol revenue, permanently stranding supplier funds that the cash was owed to.

### Finding Description
`revenue::accounting` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs:39-55` performs only two guards after burning claimable revenue: `require_utilization_below_max` and `require_supply_for_debt`. There is no `require_backed_market` call — the backing shortfall check exists in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:52-66` but is only wired into the supply path (INV-ACCT-04).

The payout amount is computed in `burn_claimable_revenue` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:54-74`):

```rust
let treasury_actual = self.unscale_supply_floor(self.revenue);
let amount = self.cash.min(treasury_actual);
```

`cash` here is the market's entire tracked reserve — the same reserve that `debit_cash`/`require_reserves` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs:15-41`) makes the sole liquidity source for supplier `withdraw`. Nothing distinguishes "cash owed to suppliers" from "cash available for revenue."

The exploitable state is reached through the documented bad-debt path:

1. `seize_positions` on the borrow side calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and burns the debt (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`). The index is floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`), and the docs explicitly state "the non-zero supply-index floor can leave residual claims requiring recapitalization" — i.e., `backing_shortfall > 0` with `borrowed == 0`.
2. In that state, both post-burn guards are vacuous: `require_utilization_below_max` returns early when `borrowed == 0` (`guards.rs:25-27`), and `require_supply_for_debt` only fires when `borrowed != 0` (`guards.rs:69-73`).
3. Revenue shares retain positive floor value (revenue shares were minted by interest/flash/liquidation fees via `accrue_revenue`, `shares.rs:36-39`, and can be inflated further by deposit-side seizure via `absorb_supply_as_revenue`, `shares.rs:45-48`, which converts user supply into revenue while its backing cash stays pooled).
4. `controller::claim_revenue` (`contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:129-138`) is callable by any authorized caller (only `require_authorized_caller`, i.e. self-auth) and forwards the measured receipt to the accumulator (`markets.rs:168-208`).

So an unprivileged caller burns revenue shares for `min(cash, revenue_value)`, and the pool transfers out cash that is the *only* remaining backing for residual supplier claims (`debt == 0` after cleanup). Subsequent supplier `withdraw` calls hit `require_reserves`/`debit_cash` and revert with `InsufficientLiquidity`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft/permanent freezing of user funds: cash that is owed to suppliers (their residual post-write-down claims) is paid out to the protocol accumulator instead. Because the market's debt was burned during cleanup, no future repayments will replenish cash; only `recapitalize` — which fills at most `backing_shortfall` and mints no shares — could restore it, and nothing forces anyone to recapitalize. Suppliers' residual claims become unpayable.

### Likelihood Explanation
**Medium.** Requires a bad-debt cleanup that leaves a positive `backing_shortfall` (the supply-index floor makes this reachable whenever the written-down index would need to go below `RAY/1000`), plus outstanding revenue shares. Once that state exists, a single permissionless `claim_revenue` call drains the remaining cash — no timing, no privileged role, no market conditions needed.

### Recommendation
Add `guards::require_backed_market(env, &cache)` (or an explicit check that post-claim `cash + ceil(debt) >= floor(supplied_claim)`) in `revenue::accounting` after `burn_claimable_revenue`, mirroring the supply-path backing guard. Alternatively, cap the claim at the surplus `max(0, cash + debt_value − supplied_claim_excluding_revenue)` so revenue can never be paid out of supplier-backed cash.

### Proof of Concept
1. Suppliers deposit USDC; a borrower draws debt. Interest accrues, minting revenue shares (`accrue_revenue`).
2. Collateral crashes; `liquidate` then `clean_bad_debt` → pool `seize_positions` borrow side: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` scales `supply_index` down (clamped at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`) and `burn_debt` zeroes `borrowed`. Result: `backing_shortfall > 0`, `cash > 0`, `revenue > 0`.
3. Anyone calls `controller.claim_revenue(hub_asset)` → `pool.claim_revenue` → `burn_claimable_revenue` pays `min(cash, revenue_value)`; both utilization and supply-for-debt guards pass vacuously; cash is transferred to the accumulator.
4. A supplier calls `withdraw` → `require_reserves(net_transfer)` fails on the drained cash → `InsufficientLiquidity`. With `borrowed == 0`, no repayment can ever refill the reserve; the supplier's claim is permanently unpayable.

Caveat: I verified the guard wiring and share mechanics directly, but did not trace `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` to confirm the exact magnitude of residual claims the index floor can leave; the documented invariant text confirms the shortfall state is reachable, though the extracted amount may be bounded by the size of accrued revenue and the floor residual.
