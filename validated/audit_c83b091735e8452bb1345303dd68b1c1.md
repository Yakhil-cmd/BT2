### Title
RAY value overflow permanently freezes an overgrown market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Scaled RAY totals are multiplied by their indexes without first enforcing that the resulting market value remains representable in `i128`. Once a sufficiently large market reaches a large borrow index, `accrue_step` panics inside `scaled_to_original`, and every subsequently attempted accrual fails before repayment, withdrawal, or liquidation logic runs.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` is executed before market mutations and repeatedly calls `accrue_step` until the market is synchronized with the current timestamp. [1](#0-0) 

For each step, `accrue_step` computes the total borrowed value as `borrowed * borrow_index` and the total supplied value as `supplied * supply_index`. [2](#0-1) 

`scaled_to_original` performs the multiplication through the panicking fixed-point helper, so a total whose exact RAY value exceeds `i128::MAX` raises `MathOverflow`. [3](#0-2) 

The index ceiling does not prevent this condition because the debt-value multiplication is evaluated before the next index is computed and clamped. [4](#0-3) 

The repository’s harness demonstrates that `update_indexes` first reaches this failure and that subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent freezing of funds and market functionality for the affected asset because the failing multiplication runs during the mandatory accrual prelude rather than in an optional view or privileged maintenance path. [6](#0-5) 

All suppliers in the market are affected: neither ordinary exits nor repayment can advance the market past the overflowing step. [7](#0-6) 

The regression test models a billion-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization and demonstrates that the borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` when the market becomes stuck. [8](#0-7) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The issue requires a very large market and sustained high utilization, so it is not a small-input or immediate trigger. [9](#0-8) 

A single unprivileged whale can still construct the state without exploiting a privileged verb when the market’s configured caps and collateral limits admit the position: supply the debt asset, supply sufficient collateral, borrow enough of the debt asset to keep utilization near the steep part of the curve, and let accrual remain idle until the total-debt value crosses the `i128` ceiling. [10](#0-9) 

The configured index and amount domains do not independently guarantee that their product remains representable, which makes the accrual-time value ceiling an additional invariant that entry checks do not enforce. [11](#0-10) 

### Recommendation
Enforce an index-aware ceiling on total scaled supply and scaled debt when minting positions, such as `scaled <= i128::MAX / MAX_INDEX`, or maintain and persist a safe accounting representation that cannot make the required accrual multiplication overflow. [12](#0-11) 

Alternatively, change accrual to detect the value overflow before calling the panicking multiplication, clamp the affected index or accrual step to the maximum representable value, and preserve a recovery path for repayment and withdrawal. [13](#0-12) 

For existing markets, add an emergency-capable but bounded accounting path that can advance or settle positions when `borrowed * borrow_index` or `supplied * supply_index` no longer fits `i128`; otherwise the same panic remains part of every subsequent mutation. [14](#0-13) 

### Proof of Concept
Assume an active 18-decimal market whose configured caps admit `1e36` base units and a separate collateral market large enough to support a 98% draw.

1. Attacker calls `supply(hub_id, spoke_id, [{ asset: debt_asset, amount: 1_000_000_000 * 10^18 }])`, creating `1e36` RAY-normalized supply before the index component.
2. Attacker calls `supply(...)` on the configured collateral market with enough collateral to authorize the borrow.
3. Attacker calls `borrow(account_id, [{ asset: debt_asset, amount: 980_000_000 * 10^18 }])`, leaving utilization at approximately 98%.
4. Time advances through enough high-utilization accrual chunks that `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`; the borrow index can still be below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Any unprivileged caller invokes `update_indexes([debt_asset])`.
6. `global_sync` invokes `accrue_step`, `scaled_to_original` evaluates the oversized product, and the transaction panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`.
7. `withdraw(account_id, [{ asset: debt_asset, amount: 1 }])`, `repay(account_id, [{ asset: debt_asset, amount: 1 }])`, and liquidation flows that synchronize the market fail on the same multiplication.

This behavior is reproduced by `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`, which supplies `1e36` base units, borrows 98%, advances time until `try_update_indexes` fails, and then verifies that withdrawal and repayment also fail. [8](#0-7)

/// The cliff. A billion whole tokens is `1e36` raw ray; the value ceiling is
/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);

/// Mints scaled debt for `amount` of underlying and enforces max utilization.
///
/// Requires positive amount, sufficient cash reserves, and that the draw
/// leaves the liquidation buffer intact. Panics if the scaled mint rounds to
/// zero shares.
pub(crate) fn mint_debt(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, position: &mut Ray, amount: i128) {
    require_positive_amount(env, amount);
    cache.require_reserves(amount);
    guards::require_liquidation_buffer(env, cache, amount);

    let minted = cache.calculate_scaled_borrow(amount);

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        minted.raw() > 0,
        GenericError::BorrowRoundsToZeroShares
    );

    *position = position.checked_add(env, minted);
    cache.mint_debt(minted);
    guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
}
