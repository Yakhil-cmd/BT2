### Title
i128 overflow in index accrual permanently freezes an entire market before the index cap can engage - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The interest-accrual path multiplies total scaled shares by the index in `i128` (`Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up`). On a large market, `borrowed * borrow_index` can exceed `i128::MAX` (≈1.7e38) while `borrow_index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (1e36 raw, i.e. x1e9). The multiplication panics with `MathOverflow` inside `calculate_supplier_rewards`, which runs on every accrual chunk in `accrue_chunk`. Since every user-facing verb calls `global_sync` first, the overflow bricks the market permanently: no supply, borrow, repay, withdraw, liquidate, bad-debt cleanup, or index update can ever execute again. The repo's own stress test demonstrates this end-to-end.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (called per chunk from `accrue_chunk` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-53`, reached via `global_sync` at lines 20-33) computes accrued interest in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:73-89`) as `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` minus `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)`. `Ray::mul` uses `fp_core::mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp.rs:50-52`), which widens the intermediate product to `I256` but panics with `MathOverflow` when the *result* `borrowed * index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`.

The borrow index is capped by `update_borrow_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` raw (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`, `common/src/constants/pool.rs:19`), i.e. a multiplier of 1e9. But the *value* overflow occurs at a much lower index whenever `borrowed` shares are large: 1e9 whole tokens at 18 decimals is `1e27` base units = `1e36` ray of scaled shares, so `shares * index` overflows `i128::MAX` once the index reaches ≈x170 — more than five orders of magnitude before the x1e9 cap. The cap therefore cannot act as a bound; accrual traps first.

The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) proves exactly this: after compounding a 98%-utilization, billion-token market on the XLM curve, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequently both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with the same error because every op accrues first.

Reachability: an unprivileged actor supplies (`controller::supply`), a second unprivileged actor supplies collateral and borrows (`controller::borrow`) to pin utilization at the curve's steep segment, and anyone calls `update_indexes` over time. No governance action, oracle manipulation, or privileged state is required — the curve parameters, caps, and decimals needed are ordinary market configuration, and `max_utilization` at `RAY` (as in the tested curve) permits the sustained high utilization.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for the whole market. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX`, `global_sync` traps before any state is written, so every entry point that accrues — supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, clean_bad_debt, flash_loan, flash_position, recapitalize — reverts unconditionally and forever. Suppliers' deposited principal and accrued interest are locked in the pool; borrowers' collateral backing the frozen debt is likewise unrecoverable through liquidation or repayment. This is the "permanent freezing of funds" impact class, and there is no recovery path short of a contract upgrade because the panic is deterministic on every subsequent call.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. The attack needs a very large market — on the order of a billion whole tokens supplied at 18 decimals (or equivalently large scaled-share totals at other decimals) — and the borrow index must grow past the overflow multiplier, which requires sustained near-max utilization compounding at the curve's steep segment for an extended period. These are plausible market conditions on a high-APY, high-decimals asset rather than exotic ones, and no rational-actor assumption is needed: utilization drift is automatic once a market is left near the optimal point (debt compounds faster than supply, pushing utilization upward over time, per the test file's own note). The bug is deterministic once reached — it does not depend on timing races or oracle behavior — and is proven by the in-repo test. The capital requirement and the multi-year compounding horizon are the mitigating factors.

### Recommendation
Break the `i128` value ceiling before it can trap accrual. Options:
- Compute accrued interest via the increment rather than the total: `accrued = borrowed * (new_index - old_index) / RAY` uses `index_delta`, which is bounded by the per-chunk interest factor and stays representable far longer than `borrowed * index`. This keeps the same economics while shrinking the operand that overflows.
- Alternatively, cap the borrow index at `i128::MAX / max_scaled_shares` per market (a dynamic ceiling derived from total borrowed shares) instead of the fixed `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so the index saturates — freezing only further interest growth — rather than the arithmetic trapping and freezing all operations.
- Whichever fix is chosen, add the harness scenario at `large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361` as a regression test asserting accrual saturates gracefully and exits still succeed, rather than asserting `MATH_OVERFLOW`.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`:

1. Create a market `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM curve with `max_borrow_rate = 1.75` RAY, `max_utilization = RAY`) plus a collateral market `COL`; lift supply/borrow caps to `max_cap_for_decimals`.
2. BOB calls `supply("BIG18", 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)` — `1e27` base units, `1e36` ray scaled shares.
3. ALICE supplies collateral and calls `borrow("BIG18", 98% of principal)`, pinning utilization at 98% on the steep segment.
4. Advance ledger time one year at a time and call `update_indexes`. After the index compounds past ≈x170 (still << `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` = x1e9), `update_indexes` reverts with `MathOverflow` from `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards`.
5. `withdraw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `repay(ALICE, "BIG18", ...)` both revert with `MathOverflow`, permanently locking all supplied principal, collateral, and accrued interest in the market.
