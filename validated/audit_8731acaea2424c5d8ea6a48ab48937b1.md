### Title
Attacker-planted Aquarius LP dust leg makes an underwater account unliquidatable by self-inflicting `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` - (contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
`supply` needs no price, so any borrower can add a dust supply position in a collateral priced from an Aquarius LP pool. The Aquarius LP provider fails closed when pool value drops below `min_pool_value_wad`, and all liquidation-related paths load prices strictly for every collateral leg of the account. A borrower who also provides liquidity to that pool can withdraw their own LP share, push the pool value below the floor, and brick `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and their own `withdraw` — shielding a deteriorating position from liquidation until they choose to restore liquidity. This is documented in the threat model (DoS.1) and reproduced by the existing audit tests; the bug class is the same unauthenticated, repeatable denial of service as CVE-2017-3450.

### Finding Description
Three properties combine into a self-inflicted price outage:

1. `supply` accepts a new collateral leg without loading its price — `Context::load_markets`/`fetch_prices` is only invoked where valuation is needed, and supply intentionally does not require a price (threat-model DoS.1: "Supply needs no price"). `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:26-30` confirms `try_supply` succeeds even when the feed is already unusable.

2. `providers/aquarius.rs::read` returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` whenever the computed pool value is below `lp.min_pool_value_wad` (`aquarius.rs:118-122`). An unprivileged LP holder can trigger this at will by withdrawing liquidity from the pool — an "own trade on Aquarius" within scope.

3. `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `withdraw` load prices for all of the account's legs through `Context::fetch_prices`/`cached_price` (`context.rs:142-160`), which panics on any unusable feed. The audit tests show both `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` reverting (`audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:36-40`), and even the victim's `withdraw` of the poisoned leg reverts (`audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:57-58`), so the leg cannot be removed to recover.

### Impact Explanation
A borrower keeps a large underwater (or soon-to-be-underwater) debt position, plants a dust-sized supply leg of an Aquarius LP-priced collateral, and withdraws their own liquidity from that LP pool. Every `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` call against the account reverts with an oracle error until the attacker re-adds liquidity or the leg otherwise becomes priceable. During the shielded window the debt accrues interest while collateral cannot be seized, converting a liquidatable position into bad debt that is socialized onto suppliers via index write-down. Impact: temporary freezing of funds and blocked liquidation leading to protocol insolvency risk — a reachable DoS against a core protocol function by an unprivileged address.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully permissionless and repeatable: the attacker controls both the leg (via `supply`) and the outage (via their own LP withdrawal). It requires a listed collateral whose price source is an Aquarius pool thin enough for one LP to push below `min_pool_value_wad`; the repo's own `audit_*` tests demonstrate the mechanism deterministically. The attacker bears only the opportunity cost of temporarily parking LP liquidity. Severity: Medium — the outage is reversible by anyone re-adding liquidity, and `min_pool_value_wad` sizing bounds feasibility, but no privileged action is needed to trigger or sustain it.

### Recommendation
Do not fail liquidation/cleanup on legs that contribute zero or dust value: skip or floor-to-zero collateral legs whose valuation is unavailable when computing health factor, rather than aborting the whole call. Alternatively, treat `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` for a collateral leg as `price = 0` in strict risk computation (conservatively zero collateral value keeps the account liquidatable), and let `clean_bad_debt` ignore unpriceable supply legs. Longer term, decouple per-leg price failures so one poisoned asset cannot veto the account's entire risk evaluation.

### Proof of Concept
- `t.supply(attacker, "USDC", N)`; `t.borrow(attacker, "ETH", …)`; `t.supply(attacker, "<AquariusLP-share-token>", dust)` — succeeds with no price check.
- Attacker calls `withdraw_liquidity` on the Aquarius pool until `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad` (`aquarius.rs:120`).
- `liquidate(liquidator, attacker_id, …)` reverts on `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`; `clean_bad_debt(attacker_id)` reverts identically — matching `audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:36-40` and `audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:51-58` (same mechanism via stale feed rather than LP drain).
- Attacker re-deposits LP liquidity to restore the price at will, or lets the debt accrue into bad debt.
