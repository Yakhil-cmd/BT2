### Title
Whale-scale deposit permanently freezes a market via `i128` overflow in `scaled_to_original` during accrual - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2019-5795 (integer overflow → memory corruption) here is an `i128` multiply-divide overflow in the share-unscaling math. `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` calls `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), which computes `scaled.mul(env, index)`. When the ray-valued `supplied * supply_index` product exceeds `i128::MAX`, `mul` panics with `MathOverflow` instead of saturating (only `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates, per `scaling.rs:18-25`). Because every mutating verb accrues first — the pool's `interest::global_sync` runs `accrue_step` before any supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate accounting — once a market crosses the ray-value cliff, no user can ever touch that market again. The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` proves exactly this: after the panic, `try_withdraw_raw`, `try_repay`, and `update_indexes` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the index cap never engages.

### Finding Description
`calculate_scaled_supply` (`scaling.rs:37-39`) mints scaled shares as `Ray::from_asset(amount, decimals).div_floor(supply_index)`. A direct `supply` of ~170 billion whole tokens (the token-to-RAY maximum per `docs/reference/formulas.md`) pushes `supplied` into the ~1e38-ray domain. The very next accrual — triggered by anyone's `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, or `update_indexes` on that market — evaluates `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` and overflows `i128`, panicking with `MathOverflow`. No bound is enforced at entry: `update_supply_index` and `calculate_supplier_rewards` also assume `borrowed * new_index` and `supplied * supply_index` fit i128, and caps/listing validation only bound asset-unit amounts, not the resulting ray domain (`certora` spec comments at `rates_rules.rs:316-320` explicitly acknowledge the panic site). Deposits can also be accumulated across many `supply` calls by one or a few unprivileged addresses.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every participant in the affected `(hub, token)` market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay or be liquidated, and bad-debt cleanup cannot run, because each of those paths accrues first and hits the same `MathOverflow` panic. Liquidation stalling additionally leaves undercollateralized debt frozen, trending the market toward insolvency. This is a user-reachable value-overflow cliff well before the documented `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address reaching it requires only `supply` with a very large token balance — no privileged role, no price manipulation, and no waiting once the ray-domain product is near the limit (high utilization merely accelerates it). The attacker's own deposit is also frozen, so the cost is capital lock-up rather than principal loss; the griefing footprint covers all other suppliers and borrowers in that market.

### Recommendation
Enforce the ray-domain bound at entry: after computing the new scaled `supplied`/`borrowed` totals in the pool's supply/borrow paths, reject (with a protocol error rather than relying on `MathOverflow`) when `scaled * current_index` would exceed `i128::MAX`, or cap `supplied`/`borrowed` scaled values at `i128::MAX / MAX_*_INDEX_RAY`. Alternatively, make `scaled_to_original` used in `accrue_step` saturate like `calculate_scaled_cap` does, so accrual degrades gracefully instead of freezing all market verbs.

### Proof of Concept
1. On an 18-decimal listed market with caps lifted (`lift_caps` in the harness mirrors admissible caps), attacker calls controller `supply` with ~1 billion whole tokens (`1e9 * 10^18` base units), minting scaled shares whose ray value approaches the i128 ray-domain limit.
2. Attacker (or anyone) calls `borrow`/`update_indexes` on that market; `interest::global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` overflows `i128` and panics `MathOverflow`.
3. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `update_indexes` calls for the market all revert identically. This is reproduced verbatim by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`, which asserts `MATH_OVERFLOW` on `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` after the cliff.
