### Title
Accrual `MathOverflow` permanently freezes a large high-utilization market — every pool verb panics on the debt-value multiplication - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2018-11782 is a "well-formed request produces a particular answer that crashes the server" bug: a legitimate read-only operation triggers a fatal exit on a specific computed result. The analog here is the pool's interest accrual: a legitimate, unprivileged state (large debt at sustained high utilization) makes the next accrual compute a debt value that exceeds `i128`, panicking inside `scaled_to_original` with `MathOverflow`. Because every pool and controller mutation accrues first, the panic permanently bricks the market — no supply, borrow, repay, withdraw, liquidation, bad-debt cleanup, or revenue claim can ever execute again on it.

### Finding Description
Accrual runs through `interest::global_sync` on every market mutation (`Cache::load → global_sync → mutate → guards → commit`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/market.rs:65-72` and the documented flow in `contracts/pool/README.md:161-167`). The borrow index itself is capped and monotone (`docs/reference/invariants.md:229-236`), but computing borrower interest requires unscaling `borrowed_shares * borrow_index` via `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:85-87`), which uses checked `i128`/`I256` arithmetic and raises `MathOverflow` when the RAY-valued debt no longer fits `i128`.

The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-362`) proves this concretely: on an 18-decimal market with ~1e27-raw-unit principal at 98% utilization on the steep segment of the rate curve, `update_indexes` eventually reverts with `MathOverflow` before the borrow-index cap engages, and the test explicitly demonstrates that `withdraw` and `repay` then fail with the same error because they accrue first (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:354-356`). The docs acknowledge the same gap: "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached" (`docs/reference/invariants.md:235`).

An unprivileged user reaches this purely through `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes`: supply a large amount of a high-decimal asset, borrow near `max_utilization`, and let time accrue. No privileged call, bad parameter, or external dependency is required — the freeze is a function of market size and elapsed time.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the debt-value multiplication overflows, every entrypoint that touches the market — `withdraw`, `repay`, `supply`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize` — panics in `global_sync` before any state change. Suppliers' deposits, borrowers' collateral backing (on the collateral side via shared hub books), and accrued revenue for that `(hub, asset)` book are locked forever. There is no governance escape hatch that skips accrual: even `recapitalize` and `update_params` accrue first (`contracts/pool/src/ops/market.rs:52-58`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a very large market (debt RAY value approaching `i128::MAX`, i.e. ~1e38-scale raw debt) at sustained high utilization on a steep rate curve — the test needs ~1e27 raw units of an 18-decimal asset for multiple years. It cannot be triggered on small markets quickly, but nothing in the protocol bounds supply/debt caps below the cliff: `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates at `i128::MAX` rather than capping it (`docs/reference/formulas.md:405-417`), so a listed high-decimal asset can be configured to admit a book large enough to reach the cliff. An attacker holding or willing to post whale-scale collateral can deliberately drive a market toward this state; once debt value crosses the representable bound mid-accrual, the freeze is irreversible. Medium likelihood, High impact.

### Recommendation
Saturate or clamp the debt-value computation inside accrual instead of raising `MathOverflow`: e.g., detect when `borrowed * borrow_index` would exceed `i128::MAX` and cap `borrow_index` growth at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` with a saturating valuation step (consistent with the existing `div_floor_saturating` pattern used for health factor in `certora/shared/summaries/mod.rs:91-95`), so the index cap engages before the value multiplication traps. Alternatively, enforce market supply/borrow caps at listing time that keep `scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` representable, or add a non-accruing emergency exit path (withdraw/repay against the stored index) so funds remain recoverable after the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
Already demonstrated in-repo by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`:

1. Create an 18-decimal market (`BIG18`) with a steep curve (`xlm_curve()`), lift caps.
2. `supply` ~1e9 * 10^18 raw units; `borrow` ~98% of it against collateral on another market (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:329-333`).
3. Advance ledger time year by year calling `update_indexes`; eventually it returns `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engaged).
4. Subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with `MathOverflow` — market frozen, suppliers' ~1e27-raw-unit deposits unrecoverable.
