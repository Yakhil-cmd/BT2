### Title
Unprivileged freezing of an entire market: accrual-time `scaled_to_original` overflows i128 RAY value before the borrow-index ceiling, permanently blocking every verb - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The heap-buffer-overflow class maps here to an out-of-range arithmetic write: every state-mutating pool operation first runs `interest::global_sync`, which calls `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`. That step values the stored `borrowed`/`supplied` scaled shares by `scaled_to_original` (`scaled.mul(index)`), a checked i128 multiply-divide in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`. On a market holding a large book, accrued index growth multiplies the RAY-scaled totals past `i128::MAX` **before** `update_borrow_index` can clamp at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36), because the raw value product `supplied × supply_index` (or `borrowed × borrow_index`) overflows first. Once `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow` inside accrual, every subsequent `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `net_settle`, `claim_revenue`, and `recapitalize` on that `(hub, token)` book reverts at the same line — the market is permanently frozen, since nothing can run without accruing first.

### Finding Description
`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs `accrue_chunk` unconditionally at the top of every mutating path. `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)`. `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), which raises `GenericError::MathOverflow` on an unrepresentable result — consistent with `docs/reference/formulas.md:20-24` ("Unrepresentable results raise `MathOverflow`"). The panic propagates out of `global_sync`, so no accounting mutation on that market can ever commit again. The index ceiling in `update_borrow_index` cannot prevent it: the overflow happens in the *value* multiplication inside `accrue_step`, not in the index update, so a book whose `borrowed × borrow_index` exceeds ~`i128::MAX` (about 1.7e38 RAY-units, ≈170e9 whole-token-equivalents at index 1) bricks itself — the borrow index cap of 10^36 is a per-index bound that says nothing about the `shares × index` product.

The repo's own test pins this end to end: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` builds an 18-decimal market with ~1e9 whole tokens supplied at ~98% utilization on the XLM rate curve, advances simulated years, and observes `update_indexes` fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — then confirms `withdraw` and `repay` on that market also panic with `MATH_OVERFLOW` for the same reason. `docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437` acknowledges the bound ("Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first"), and `docs/explanation/threat-model.md:319-324` registers it as `Tamper.6`, but no code change, early-detection hook, or governance circuit-breaker prevents the state: the saturation used for `calculate_scaled_cap` (`mul_div_floor_saturating`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:26-33`) is deliberately not applied to position/value accounting.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and contract unable to operate: once the `shares × index` product crosses the i128 RAY boundary, all suppliers' tokens, all revenue, and all collateral claims booked in that `(hub, token)` market are unrecoverable — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize`, and `clean_bad_debt` all route through `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` and revert identically. Borrowers cannot repay (frozen debt), suppliers cannot exit (frozen supply), liquidators cannot touch positions whose market is frozen (liquidation calls `Context` market indexes that simulate the same accrual), and protocol revenue in that market is locked forever. This matches the CVE's class (out-of-bounds/corruption from unchecked range) mapped onto Soroban: a bounded-integer overflow in a checked-arithmetic hot path rather than a memory write.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged callers through `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes` alone: an attacker (or merely organic whale activity) supplies a very large book — the documented ceiling is roughly 170 billion whole tokens at index 1 (`docs/reference/formulas.md:429`) — and borrows to high utilization; sustained accrual then pushes `borrowed × borrow_index` or `supplied × supply_index` over the limit. The harness test reaches the cliff in simulated years at 98% utilization on a steep curve. Prerequisites are heavy (a dominant book and a long high-utilization period), which lowers likelihood, but no privileged action, oracle manipulation, or code upgrade is required, and once crossed the condition is irreversible — there is no entrypoint that rewinds `last_timestamp` or shrinks stored shares. Severity: **Medium** (permanent freeze, but requires an extreme book and extended accrual horizon).

### Recommendation
Add an explicit headroom check in `accrue_step` (or a saturating value computation): before `scaled_to_original`, verify `borrowed × borrow_index` and `supplied × supply_index` stay within i128 with margin, and clamp accrual (e.g., pin `borrow_index`/`supply_index` at the last safe value, mirroring the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp in `update_borrow_index`) instead of panicking — a market that stops accruing keeps `repay`/`withdraw`/`liquidate` live, which is strictly better than a frozen book. Alternatively, gate `supply`/`borrow` on projected accrual headroom so the book can never be sized to overflow. Whichever is chosen, the panic path should be reachable only as a defensive invariant, not as the de-facto market halt.

### Proof of Concept
Already reproduced in-repo by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`:

1. List an 18-decimal market (`BIG18`) with the XLM rate curve and disabled `max_utilization`; lift supply/borrow caps (`lift_caps`).
2. `BOB` supplies `1e9 × 10^18` base units; `ALICE` supplies collateral and borrows ~98% of the book.
3. Repeatedly `advance_time(YEAR_SECS)` and call `update_indexes` — accrual chunks (`MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS`) compound the borrow index.
4. At the year where `borrowed × borrow_index` (RAY domain) exceeds `i128::MAX`, `update_indexes` reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the index cap never engaged).
5. `withdraw(BOB, BIG18, 1)` and `repay(ALICE, BIG18)` both revert with the same `MathOverflow`, confirming the market — and all funds booked in it — is permanently frozen.

Root cause chain: `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` (`global_sync` on every verb) → `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` (`scaled_to_original` on `borrowed`/`supplied`) → `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16` (`mul` raises `MathOverflow` on i128 overflow).
