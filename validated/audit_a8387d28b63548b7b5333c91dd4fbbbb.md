### Title
Whale-scale debt makes index accrual overflow `i128` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages, permanently freezing the market - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
The bug class in CVE-2016-5359 is an integer overflow that produces a denial of service. The same class exists in the pool's interest accrual: the RAY-valued product `borrowed * borrow_index` (and `supplied * supply_index`) can exceed `i128::MAX` and panic with `MathOverflow` *before* the borrow index ever reaches its `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap. Because every pool verb (supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, flash, recapitalize) accrues the index first, the market is permanently bricked and all supplier/borrower funds are frozen.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` clamps the new index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), and `update_supply_index` clamps at `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:43-44`). Those caps bound the *index*, but the overflow happens on the *value* products: `supplied.mul(old_index)` at `common/src/rates/index.rs:34`, `borrowed.mul(...)` at `common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`, and `scaled_to_original` calls in `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-24` (used by `calculate_utilization`, which every op runs after accruing). `Ray::mul` panics on `i128` overflow rather than saturating, so once `scaled_shares * index` crosses ~`i128::MAX`, `accrue` reverts forever — the index is never written, and the cap never gets a chance to engage.

This is not hypothetical: the characterization test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`) drives a 98%-utilization market on the XLM rate curve until `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, then confirms `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (cap never engaged) and that subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert with the same error. The test itself notes the bound given in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the value product exceeds `i128::MAX`, no unprivileged entrypoint on that market can execute: withdraw, repay, liquidate, flash loan, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize` all accrue first and panic. Every supplier's deposit and every borrower's collateral claim in that (hub, token) book is irrecoverable, and `recapitalize` cannot rescue it because it too accrues the index. The attacker only needs to be the whale supplier/borrower themselves (own supply + own borrow at high utilization on a steep rate curve); time does the rest.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires a very large book (scaled share counts near `i128::MAX / index`, i.e. whale-scale supply) and sustained high utilization on a steep rate curve, and accrual takes years of compounding in the demonstrated scenario. It is fully permissionless — `supply` and `borrow` are open to any address — but the capital requirement and the rate-curve dependence make this a Medium-severity analog rather than a cheap attack. Interest-bearing index growth is monotonic, so once the cliff is crossed there is no recovery path.

### Recommendation
Make accrual overflow-safe before the index cap becomes moot:
- Use the saturating/widened mul-div helpers (`mul_div_floor_saturating` / I256 paths already in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`) for `supplied * index`, `borrowed * index`, and `scaled_to_original`, or
- Clamp the index *and* the scaled totals inside `update_borrow_index`/`update_supply_index` before forming the RAY value product, and/or enforce a `MAX_TOTAL_VALUE_RAY`-style cap at `supply`/`borrow` time so `scaled * index` can never reach `i128::MAX`.
- Correct the bound claimed in `docs/reference/formulas.md`, which the existing test shows is wrong.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`. An unprivileged actor supplies `BILLION * 10^18` units of an 18-decimal market, a second account supplies collateral and borrows 98% of it, then time is advanced year-by-year calling `update_indexes` until it reverts with `MathOverflow`. The assertion `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` proves the cap never engaged; subsequent `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both revert, demonstrating permanent freeze of all positions in that market.
