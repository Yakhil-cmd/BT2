### Title
System governance vote weight uses instantaneous spendable balance with no lock-up, enabling "flash-vote" manipulation of `op_list`/numeric parameters - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
Obyte's on-chain governance for system parameters (`op_list`, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) is the analog of the Aragon-based `veFXS` voting flagged in the report. Just as the report criticizes Aragon voting for lacking protection against "quick vote and withdraw" strategies and for having no cost-of-participation lock-up, `countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` computes a voter's weight from their **current** stable, unspent byte balance at the moment the vote tally runs, not from a balance that was locked or held at the time the vote was cast.

### Finding Description
`countVotes()` selects distinct voter addresses from `system_votes` and then computes weight purely from currently unspent, stable, `sequence='good'` outputs: [1](#0-0) 

This balance is used to weight votes going back up to multiple years via the `since_timestamp` expansion loop: [2](#0-1) 

For `op_list`, the aggregated weight per candidate OP determines the next Order Provider list: [3](#0-2) 

Casting a `system_vote` is cheap: it is a single inline message validated with minimal constraints (a sorted/valid OP array or a bounded numeric value) and no requirement that the voter's balance be locked, escrowed, or held for any duration: [4](#0-3) 

Because the weight is read from the *live* balance at tally time (which happens whenever a new stable MCI recount is triggered, including the emergency path), an address can:
1. Cast a `system_vote` once (cheaply, at any point, even long ago — old votes remain valid for up to multiple years per the `since_timestamp` loop).
2. Acquire a large byte balance only momentarily (e.g., via a temporary large transfer, an intra-day balance from an exchange, or coordinated fund concentration) exactly around the point a recount will execute.
3. Have that balance counted at full weight in `countVotes()`.
4. Immediately move/spend the balance afterward, since nothing prevents it — the outputs table is only checked for "currently unspent" at query time, with no historical snapshot tied to the vote-casting time.

This is the direct on-chain analog of the reported "quick vote and withdraw" weakness: voting power is decoupled from sustained economic commitment, letting a well-funded actor swing consensus-critical governance (the OP/witness list feeding into stability determination, or fee/TPS parameters) without permanently locking capital.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` determines the Order Providers, which are central to unit ordering/stability determination in this fork of the DAG consensus. Manipulating it transiently (analogous to Eve seizing minting control in the report) could let an attacker with temporarily large balance push in adversarial or colluding OP addresses, undermining network agreement on validity/stability of units, or push through favorable/malicious `threshold_size`, `tps_interval`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_fee_multiplier` values that degrade network throughput/fee economics. This is a High-impact governance/consensus-parameter integrity issue.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires only:
- A cheap `system_vote` message (already cast, possibly long in the past).
- Temporary access to a large byte balance at the exact moment a recount is triggered.

Recount triggers are frequent — every new incoming vote for a subject reruns `countVotes` at the next `checkAndCountVotes` cycle referenced by `applyEmergencyOpListChange` and the normal MCI-stabilization path — so an attacker can time the balance acquisition to coincide with a recount window. No lock-up, vesting, or minimum holding period is enforced anywhere in the vote-casting (`validateInlinePayload` "system_vote" case) or vote-counting (`countVotes`) logic.

### Recommendation
- Require voting weight to be based on a balance that was held continuously (or locked) from the time the `system_vote` was cast through the counting time, rather than the balance at counting time only (e.g., snapshot balance at vote-cast MCI and require it remain unspent, or require an explicit token lock similar to `veFXS`-style time-locking).
- Consider a minimum holding/vesting period before newly-cast votes contribute full weight, mirroring standard anti-flash-voting mitigations.
- Add rate/spam limits on frequency of vote changes per address impacting `op_list` and other consensus parameters, and audit the `since_timestamp` expansion logic for potential replay/stale-vote issues.

### Proof of Concept
1. Address A casts `system_vote` for `op_list` (candidate OP set favorable to attacker) at any past point — the message is cheap and valid per `validation.js:1844-1911`.
2. Attacker times a large temporary transfer of bytes into address A immediately before an OP-vote recount executes (recounts run on each `countVotes` invocation, e.g., via `applyEmergencyOpListChange` in `main_chain.js:1936-1946` or during normal stabilization).
3. `countVotes()` reads address A's current unspent balance (`main_chain.js:1757-1773`) and includes it at full weight in the OP-list tally (`main_chain.js:1850-1863`).
4. Once the vote count and `system_vars` update complete (`main_chain.js:1910`), attacker moves/spends the temporary balance away — no lockup or clawback prevents this, since only the live snapshot at tally time was consulted.
