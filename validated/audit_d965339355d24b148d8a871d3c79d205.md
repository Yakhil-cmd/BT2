### Title
Asset attestor list can be revoked unilaterally and without limit by the asset definer, freezing all previously-issued outputs of `spender_attested` assets - ([File: validation.js])

### Summary
For assets defined with `spender_attested: true`, the asset definer (playing the same "coordinator" role as in the Perennial report — a party with elevated but bounded, non-owner authority over a shared financial object) can post an `asset_attestors` update message at any time, with no limit on frequency, no timelock, and no restriction preventing the attestor list from being emptied or replaced with addresses that no longer cover existing holders.

### Finding Description
`asset_attestors` messages are accepted for validation with only structural checks — a non-empty attestor array and "one update per asset per unit" — and no constraint that the new list must be a superset of, or otherwise compatible with, the previous list: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Once accepted, the current attestor list directly gates every subsequent transfer of the asset. `validatePayment` requires that at least one author be attested, and for issuance the issuer itself must be attested: [3](#0-2) 

For payment outputs, `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` additionally requires that every output address be attested under the *current* (live) attestor list before the payment is even valid: [4](#0-3) 

Because this check is evaluated against the attestor list as of `last_ball_mci` at the time of each new transfer (via `storage.loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors` / `filterAttestedAddresses`), any prior attestation status can be revoked at will by simply publishing a new `asset_attestors` message that drops the attestor(s) who had certified the holder. There is no timelock, no minimum-notice period, and no floor preventing the definer from reducing the effective attestor set to zero or to attestors who will attest no one, unlike the numeric floor Perennial at least applies to margin ratio (their bug is about the *absence of an upside* limit; here there is neither a temporal nor a compositional limit on the definer's control of the gating condition).

### Impact Explanation
This mirrors the H-2 root cause exactly: a privileged-but-not-owner role (asset definer / "coordinator") has unbounded, un-timelocked control over a parameter that determines whether existing holders' funds remain spendable. A malicious or compromised asset definer can:
1. Wait until users accumulate holdings of the asset (analogous to open positions in the market).
2. Publish an `asset_attestors` update that removes/replaces the attestors that had certified those holders.
3. Instantly render every previously-issued, unspent output of that asset unspendable for the affected holders (since `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` will reject any output whose address is not attested under the new list), while the definer's own address (freshly attested by itself, if `cosigned_by_definer`/self-attestation patterns are used) can continue to transact.

This is a concrete "AA/user fund freezing" outcome reachable purely through in-protocol messages authored by the asset definer — no node, hub, or peer compromise is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is limited to assets that opt into `spender_attested: true`, and requires the definer role to act maliciously (or be compromised) — the same precondition as the Sherlock report (malicious coordinator). Given that `spender_attested` is a documented, actively-used asset feature (whitelisted/KYC assets), and updating the list is a single, unrestricted message, the barrier to executing the attack is low once a party controls the definer key.

### Recommendation
- Add a timelock/notice period between publishing an `asset_attestors` update and it taking effect for already-attested addresses, so holders have advance warning (mirrors the report's primary recommendation).
- Consider requiring that attestor-list updates cannot retroactively de-attest addresses that were valid as of an already-confirmed output, or provide a grace period for existing holders to move funds before a revocation becomes enforceable.
- Optionally, add sanity bounds (e.g., minimum attestor set size, cool-down between successive updates) analogous to the "sanity upside limit" suggested for margin/maintenance ratios in the source report.

### Proof of Concept
1. Definer issues asset `A` with `spender_attested: true` and an initial attestor `X`.
2. `X` attests address `H` (holder). `H` receives a payment output in asset `A` — validated successfully because `H` is in `arrAttestedAddresses` per `validation.js:2632-2641`.
3. Definer publishes `asset_attestors` for asset `A` removing `X` (or replacing it with an attestor that will never attest `H`) — accepted per `validation.js:2033-2041` with no limit or timelock.
4. `H` attempts to spend the previously-received, fully valid output. `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` now rejects the transfer because `H` is no longer in the (recomputed, live) attested-address set — funds are frozen despite being legitimately owned and previously valid.
