Stale secondary oracle prices contaminate strict collateral valuation and bypass borrow limits - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary

The dual-source Reflector-primary/RedStone-anchor pricing path treats a nearly day-old anchor observation as a current quote when it remains within its independent staleness and tolerance bounds. An unprivileged borrower can supply collateral, let a stale secondary quote suppress a fresh primary-price movement, and borrow an amount that the same position cannot support when the anchor is fresh. [1](#0-0) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description

`Controller::supply` and `Controller::borrow` are caller-authorized entrypoints; after pool debt settlement, `process_borrow` enforces solvency using the controller's cached price data. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The reproduced market uses Reflector as the primary source and RedStone as the anchor, with a 10% tolerance and an 86,400-second anchor staleness limit. After Reflector moves from `$1.00` to `$0.91`, the RedStone observation remains `$1.00` with a timestamp 82,800 seconds old; the 9% divergence and 23-hour age are both inside the configured bounds. [1](#0-0) 

Instead of rejecting the stale secondary observation or valuing the asset from the fresh primary observation, the pricing path blends it into the strict collateral value. The resulting valuation is more than 4% higher than the fresh-anchor control, and a `70,000` USDC borrow succeeds while the fresh-anchor control fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [6](#0-5) 

This is analogous to the `SuppressedError` escape: a secondary/error-handling object or data channel is allowed to cross a trusted boundary and override the primary security decision. Here, the secondary oracle observation suppresses the fresh primary price signal instead of merely constraining it. [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation

Borrowed assets are paid to the caller-selected recipient before the post-operation solvency check finalizes the account. A stale-anchor valuation can therefore remove real pool liquidity on collateral whose current price no longer supports the configured borrow capacity. [7](#0-6) 

On a sufficiently high-LTV market, the inflated blended valuation can permit debt exceeding the collateral's realizable value. Once the stale anchor refreshes or liquidation valuation catches up, the account can contain bad debt and leave suppliers with a protocol shortfall. The committed control demonstrates that the same borrow is correctly rejected when the anchor is fresh. [3](#0-2) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attacker needs no privileged role, oracle signer key, leaked credential, callback, or malicious token. They only need a listed dual-source collateral, enough pool liquidity, and a natural price movement that remains below the configured cross-source tolerance while the secondary observation is inside its stale-time allowance. [8](#0-7) 

The exploit does not rely on the third-party oracle lying: the anchor reports a truthful historical observation and timestamp, but the protocol gives that stale observation independent weight in the strict risk price. [9](#0-8) 

### Recommendation

Do not blend a materially stale secondary oracle observation with a fresh primary observation for strict collateral valuation. pub(crate) fn process_borrow(
    env: &Env,
    caller: &Address,
    account_id: u64,
    borrows: &Vec<HubPayment>,
    to: Option<Address>,
) {
    validation::require_authorized_caller(env, caller);

    let mut account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);
    require_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, caller, &account.owner);

    let recipient = to.unwrap_or_else(|| caller.clone());
    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    require_external_recipient(env, &mut cache, &recipient);
    let aggregated = payments::aggregate_positive_payments(env, borrows);

    validate_position_entry_gates(
        env,
        &account,
        &aggregated,
        &mut cache,
        AccountPositionType::Borrow,
    );
    settle_borrow(env, &mut account, &recipient, &aggregated, &mut cache);

    let restamped = enforce_post_pool_solvency(env, &mut cache, &mut account);
    let sides = if restamped {
        PositionSides::Both
    } else {
        PositionSides::Debt
    };
    finalize_position_flow(env, account_id, &account, &mut cache, sides, false);
