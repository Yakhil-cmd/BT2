### Title
Balance-weighted OP-list vote counting lets a voter swing the tie-break/ranking with a transient balance that is withdrawn immediately after the count - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` decides the network's Order-Provider (witness) list by summing each voter's *current spendable balance* at the moment a `system_vote_count` message stabilizes, then ranking candidate OP addresses by `total_balance DESC, op_address` as a tie-breaker. Just like DSChief's `swap`/`drop`, which sort finalists by weight and break ties by insertion order while allowing `lock`/`free` to transiently inflate weight, ocore's OP-list election allows a voter's weight (its stable, unspent balance) to be transiently inflated right before the counting snapshot and withdrawn immediately afterward, permanently changing the ranked outcome (which OP gets a seat, and who wins ties) without any lasting economic commitment.

### Finding Description
`countVotes(conn, mci, subject)` computes voter weight as the *live* stable balance of every address that has ever cast a `system_vote` for the subject: [1](#0-0) 

For `subject === 'op_list'`, the candidates are ranked purely by this instantaneous balance sum, with ties broken lexicographically by address: [2](#0-1) 

Crucially, nothing about `system_vote` requires locking or staking bytes — casting a vote is a free message, validated only for format/target address, with no balance or fund transfer involved: [3](#0-2) 

The weight used for tallying is recomputed fresh from whatever stable, unspent outputs the voting address happens to hold at the exact moment the `system_vote_count` unit stabilizes, not from bytes deposited/locked into any voting mechanism: [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally identical to the DSPrism bug class: DSPrism's finalist ranking is supposed to reflect a durable commitment of locked voting tokens, but `lock`/`free` let a caller transiently inflate weight to win a strict-inequality tie-break and then withdraw the excess, leaving the *permanent* list state (finalist replaced, order changed) inconsistent with the *actual* long-term weights. In ocore, the OP list is supposed to reflect the durable stake of the community behind each candidate witness, but because voter weight is a snapshot of current UTXOs rather than a locked/staked amount, an address can:
1. Pre-register a `system_vote` for its preferred (or colluding) OP-list candidate at any earlier time (cheap, no funds needed).
2. Shortly before triggering (or anticipating) the stabilization of a `system_vote_count` unit, receive a large payment into the voting address and let that output become stable.
3. Let/force the vote count to execute while holding this inflated balance, swinging `SUM(balance)` for its candidate above a rival candidate — deciding both the ranking (`ORDER BY total_balance DESC`) and, in the case of an exact tie, the tie-break (`op_address` ascending), directly mirroring Exploit Scenario 1/2 of the report.
4. Immediately spend/move the funds elsewhere once the vote is counted and `system_vars.op_list` (a durable, protocol-critical piece of consensus state, cf. `storage.systemVars`) has been permanently updated.

Because `op_list` values (`storage.systemVars.op_list`) determine the Order Providers used throughout witnessed-level computation, best-parent selection and stability determination (`storage.determineBestParent`, `storage.getOpList`), corrupting this ranking has consensus-wide consequences, unlike DSPrism's finalist list whose significance the original auditors themselves considered implementation-dependent.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker (any unprivileged holder of bytes, not an operator or hub) can use a transient balance spike — comparable to a flash-loan style trick — to tip a marginal OP-list election in favor of an address they control or collude with, with no lasting stake in the outcome. Because `op_list` directly seeds `storage.getOpList()` used by `determineBestParent`, `determineWitnessedLevelAndBestParent`, and MC/stability computations across `main_chain.js` and `storage.js`, installing or displacing an Order Provider through weight manipulation can degrade the trust assumption behind stability/finality (nodes rely on OPs' honest majority), enabling downstream node disagreement on validity/stability or a network unable to reliably confirm units if a colluding minority OP is elected this way. This satisfies the "node disagreement on validity or stability" / "network unable to confirm new units" impact bar from the rules once repeated across successive elections.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to time a large, temporary balance around the exact mci at which a `system_vote_count` for `op_list` stabilizes. The attacker does not control this mci precisely (it is determined by consensus stabilization of whichever unit carries the `system_vote_count` message), but an attacker can post the triggering `system_vote_count` unit themselves and reasonably predict/target the stabilization mci, then time the balance inflation accordingly. The `since_timestamp` expansion logic (searching back for enough voter participation) adds some friction but does not require balance to be held over that whole window — only at the single moment `countVotes` executes its `bal_rows` query. This makes the attack technically demanding but realistically executable by a well-resourced, single unprivileged holder, especially near close elections/ties where only a modest transient balance is needed to flip the outcome — exactly the scenario the report calls "Difficulty: Undetermined."

### Recommendation
- Require voting weight to be based on a time-averaged or minimum-held balance (e.g., balance that has been continuously stable for N mci prior to the count), rather than an instantaneous snapshot, so transient balance spikes cannot swing the tally.
- Alternatively, require voters to lock/stake bytes into a dedicated system-vote UTXO/contract for the vote's duration (mirroring true "locked" governance weight), only released after the counting mci has passed and is itself stable beyond reorg risk.
- Document explicitly, if this behavior is intentionally accepted, that `op_list` and other balance-weighted votes are snapshot-based and can be manipulated by transient balance holders, and that election outcomes near a tie should not be treated as reflecting durable community stake.

### Proof of Concept
1. Address A casts `system_vote` for `subject: "op_list"` naming a candidate list that includes a witness address `X` (attacker-controlled or colluding), at negligible cost, per `validation.js` `system_vote` handling.
2. A monitors the OP-list vote tally; suppose without intervention `X` would rank one seat short of `constants.COUNT_WITNESSES` (i.e., losing a close race, or tied by `op_address`).
3. Shortly before/при posting a `system_vote_count` unit for `op_list`, A receives (e.g., from a cooperating party or via a large legitimate incoming payment) a large sum of bytes to the voting address and waits for that output to stabilize.
4. A posts the `system_vote_count` message; when its containing unit stabilizes, `countVotes()` executes the `bal_rows`/`op_rows` queries and sums A's now-inflated balance toward `X`, pushing `X` above the threshold or winning the `op_address` tie-break, per the logic at `main_chain.js` lines 1850-1863.
5. `storage.systemVars.op_list` and the on-chain `system_vars` row are permanently updated to include `X`.
6. A immediately spends/transfers the large balance elsewhere; A's genuine long-term holdings never actually supported `X`'s election, yet `X` remains an Order Provider until the next vote count. [2](#0-1) [1](#0-0) [3](#0-2)
