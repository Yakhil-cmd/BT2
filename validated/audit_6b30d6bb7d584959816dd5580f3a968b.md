## Title
AA responses that bundle multiple payment messages (e.g. an asset payout plus a bytes payout) bounce entirely — and lose the bounce fee — when only one of the assets has insufficient AA balance, even though the other output was fully payable ([File: aa_composer.js])

### Summary
`aa_composer.js`'s `sendUnit()` builds the AA's response unit by iterating over all `payment` messages produced by the oscript formula and completing each one independently. If any single non-base asset payment cannot be funded from the AA's own balance (plus what it can self-issue), the whole unit construction aborts with an error, and the trigger is bounced — undoing every other output in the same response, including a fully-fundable base-byte or other-asset payment that the user was legitimately entitled to receive. This mirrors the reported Y2K bug class: lack of a balance-sufficiency check for one leg of a compound transfer blocks a receiver from getting the other leg it was already entitled to.

### Finding Description
In `handleTrigger`'s `sendUnit()`, each `payment` message in the AA's response is completed via `completePaymentPayload()` inside an `async.eachSeries` loop: [1](#0-0) 

For a non-base asset payment, if the AA does not hold enough of that asset among its stable/unstable outputs and cannot self-issue it, `completePaymentPayload` reports an error such as `'not enough funds for ... of asset ' + asset`: [2](#0-1) 

That error is bubbled up to the `async.eachSeries` completion callback, which immediately calls `bounce(err)` — discarding the entire response unit, including any other payment message in the same set (e.g., a base-byte payout or a different asset payout) that had sufficient balance and would otherwise have succeeded: [3](#0-2) 

`bounce()` then not only fails to deliver any of the intended outputs, it also keeps the AA's configured `bounce_fees` out of what is returned to the trigger's sender, and only refunds `trigger.outputs - bounce_fees`: [4](#0-3) 

This is the direct analog of the reported issue: in Carousel's `withdraw()`, a missing balance check on `emissionsToken` reverted the whole call — including the successful `entitledShares` transfer — even though the underlying asset had enough balance. In ocore, an oscript author who composes a multi-message payout (e.g., a bank/vault-style AA that pays back a principal asset plus an incentive/emission asset, exactly the pattern used in `test/samples/a_bank_without_percent.oscript` and `order_book_exchange.oscript`) will have the entire trigger response — including the perfectly payable leg — bounced whenever the AA's balance of the second asset temporarily runs short (e.g., due to a preceding withdrawal by another user draining that asset first).

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged unit poster (trigger sender) who is entitled to withdraw funds from an AA can have that withdrawal denied/frozen purely because of a balance shortfall in an unrelated, secondary asset bundled in the same response message set — even though the primary asset the user actually wants is fully available. Additionally, the sender loses `bounce_fees` (denominated in bytes and/or other assets) on every such failed attempt, since `bounce()` withholds those fees from the refunded amount. Repeated legitimate withdrawal attempts against an AA whose secondary-asset balance is drained (accidentally or by a griefing prior user) result in real, retained fund loss and denial of otherwise valid fund access — matching "AA fund loss or freezing."

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no privileged access: any AA definition that emits more than one `payment` message per response (a common oscript pattern for combined principal + reward/emission payouts, seen directly in the shipped sample oscripts) is affected. The condition is triggered purely by ordinary usage — one asset's AA balance being temporarily lower than the other's — which is easily reachable by a normal sequence of deposits/withdrawals from multiple unprivileged users, not by any malicious-peer/network behavior.

### Recommendation
Before emitting a payment message for a secondary asset in a compound AA response, the oscript logic (or `completePaymentPayload`) should be able to independently gate/omit that specific output when the AA's balance for that asset is insufficient, rather than causing the entire unit (and all its other, fundable payment messages) to bounce. Concretely, `sendUnit()` could support treating an underfunded non-base payment message as omittable (similar to how a `0`-amount output is already filtered out) rather than propagating its failure as a hard error that aborts the whole response, or oscript authors should be given/encouraged to use `var[]`/`balance[]` checks combined with conditional message inclusion (`if` per message) so a shortfall in one asset does not block the other.

### Proof of Concept
1. Define an AA whose formula, similar to `test/samples/a_bank_without_percent.oscript`, on `trigger.data.withdraw` emits two `payment` messages in the same response: one in `base` bytes and one in a custom asset (e.g. an "emissions"-style reward asset), both addressed to `trigger.address`.
2. Have the AA's balance of the custom asset become smaller than what a legitimate withdrawer is currently entitled to (e.g., because another user withdrew first, or the AA never received enough of that asset), while its `base` balance remains more than sufficient.
3. A user triggers a legitimate withdrawal for the amount they are entitled to in both assets.
4. In `sendUnit()`, `completePaymentPayload` for the custom asset fails with `'not enough funds for ... of asset ' + asset` (`aa_composer.js:1238`-`1239`), the `async.eachSeries` callback receives this error and calls `bounce(err)` (`aa_composer.js:1346`-`1349`), discarding the fully-payable `base` payment as well, and the user is refunded only `trigger.outputs - bounce_fees` (`aa_composer.js:928`-`944`), losing the bounce fee and being denied the base-byte withdrawal they were otherwise entitled to.
