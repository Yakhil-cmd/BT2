### Title
Borrow-index growth overflows `i128` in `accrue_step` before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap engages, permanently freezing a market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
The CVE class is "a missing bound/overflow check on an iterated quantity turns into a denial of service." XOXNO Lending's analog: `update_borrow_index` caps the borrow index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but accrual first computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — a `Ray::mul` that panics with `MathOverflow` when the product exceeds `i128::MAX`. On a whale-sized market at sustained high utilization, the ray *value* of the debt crosses `i128::MAX` long before the index cap is reached. Since every state-changing verb accrues first, the market then freezes permanently: no withdraw, no repay, no liquidation, no `clean_bad_debt`.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-66`) unscales the scaled debt before the index update:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);   // can panic
let util = utilization(env, borrowed_original, supplied_original);
let borrow_rate = calculate_borrow_rate(env, util, params);
let interest_factor = compound_interest(env, borrow_rate, delta_ms);
let new_borrow_index = update_borrow_index(env, borrow_index, interest_factor);
```

`scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), which panics on `i128` overflow via `MathOverflow`. The cap in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) only clamps the *index*, not the product `borrowed × index`, so it cannot prevent the panic. The same panic also occurs in `update_supply_index` at `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:34`).

The repository's own stress test demonstrates the failure: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) shows that with a ~1e9 whole-token 18-decimal market at ~98% utilization on the steep XLM rate curve, `update_indexes` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and confirms `withdraw` and `repay` hit the same panic — "The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic. The index cap never engages."

Note the drift is self-reinforcing: debt compounds faster than supply, so even a book left near mid utilization migrates toward the steep segment over time (per the test module doc, `large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:8-13`).

### Impact Explanation
Once the panic threshold is crossed, every controller/pool entrypoint that touches the market (`update_indexes`, `supply`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash` paths, router strategies) calls accrual and reverts. Supplier principal and borrower collateral in that market are permanently frozen, underwater positions can never be liquidated, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` cannot run, leaving bad debt unsocializable. This is permanent freezing of user funds plus an insolvency amplifier.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged calls: an attacker (or ordinary whale) supplies a very large position (`supply`) and borrows to high utilization (`borrow`) on a listed market with a steep rate curve and lifted/hard caps. From there, elapsed time alone — not any further attacker action — pushes `borrowed × borrow_index` past `i128::MAX`. The required scale is enormous (billions of whole tokens, multi-year accrual at extreme utilization per the harness test), and caps/`max_utilization` plus normal liquidation cadence make it unlikely in practice — hence Medium rather than High — but the protocol provides no recovery path once crossed.

### Recommendation
Apply the overflow check where the iterated quantity actually lives, mirroring the upstream fix: bound `borrowed`/`supplied` scaled shares or detect when `borrowed × borrow_index` is within a safety margin of `i128::MAX` and clamp growth (e.g., cap `new_borrow_index` at `min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX / borrowed_raw * RAY)`), or saturate `scaled_to_original` inside accrual and short-circuit further compounding once the ceiling is hit. A cheaper mitigation is enforcing a per-market supply cap in ray terms (`cap × 10^(27−d) × MAX_INDEX < i128::MAX`) so the cliff is unreachable for admitted configurations.

### Proof of Concept
Existing reproduction: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`.

```rust
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

loop {
    t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
    if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) { break e; }
}
// errors::MATH_OVERFLOW while borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

All calls are unprivileged (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, `repay`); the freeze requires no privileged role.
