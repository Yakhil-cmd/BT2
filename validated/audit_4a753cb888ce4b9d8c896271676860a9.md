### Title
Unfunded repay overpayment is refunded out of pool custody because the refund is derived from the declared amount, not a verified inbound transfer - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs])

### Summary
Analogous to `extract_cc_from_data` trusting `atom_length` without a bounds check and then subtracting a constant (producing a wrapped, oversized `cclen`), `ops::repay` trusts the declared `action.amount` without verifying that any tokens were actually transferred in, computes `overpayment = amount - actual_debt`, and transfers that "excess" back out of real pool custody. When the outstanding debt is zero, the entire declared amount is classified as overpayment and paid out of other users' funds.

### Finding Description
`repay::accounting` resolves the repay into `burned` shares and `overpayment` purely from the declared `action.amount` and the stored debt position:

- `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:44-47`: `cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` yields `(burned, overpayment)` and `net_repay = amount - overpayment`.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:48-52`: the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` guard is satisfied on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, so a zero-debt market passes.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:32`: `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` sends the overpayment from pool custody.

No step measures the pool's actual token balance delta or requires that an inbound transfer of `amount` occurred. The identical gap exists in `recapitalize::accounting` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52-55`), where `refund = amount - applied` is refunded out of custody. The codebase's own regression test documents the drain: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611` repays `custody_before` against a market with `borrowed == 0`, receives `credited == 0`, and asserts the whole custody balance was paid to the payer (`assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody`), with the comments at lines 3578-3588 explicitly stating the refund "is derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book" and "the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched."

### Impact Explanation
If the pool `repay`/`recapitalize` entrypoints are reachable by an unprivileged payer without the controller having first pulled `amount` tokens into the pool (the direct pool `repay` call is exercised in the test without a preceding transfer), the attacker receives `amount` tokens of real supplier/borrower custody for free — theft of user funds, up to the market's entire cash balance per call, repeatable across markets.

Caveat: I could not fully verify within the available iterations whether `pool::repay` enforces a controller-only caller check in `lib.rs` (the grep matched but line contents were not retrieved). If the controller is the sole caller and always pulls `amount` from the payer first, the refund returns the payer's own funds and the finding is non-exploitable; the in-repo test treats the custody drain as real behavior, which is why it is reported.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires a market with zero (or very small) outstanding debt relative to its cash — trivially satisfiable for any freshly supplied or fully-repaid market — plus one call with `amount = market cash`. No privileged role, timing, or price manipulation is needed; the cost is one transaction.

### Recommendation
- Enforce that the caller of `repay`/`recapitalize` is the controller, and/or make the pool settle on measured receipts (balance delta) rather than the declared `amount`.
- Cap `overpayment`/`refund` by the actually received inbound amount and by the market's cash book; reject a positive refund when `net_repay == 0` and no inbound transfer was recorded.

### Proof of Concept
Provided in-repo: `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590-3611` — supply funds so the market has cash, leave `borrowed == 0`, call `pool.repay(payer, amount = custody_before)` with no inbound transfer; observe `actual_amount == 0` and the payer's balance increase by `custody_before`.
