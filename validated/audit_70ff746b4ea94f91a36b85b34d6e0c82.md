### Title
Position NFT sellers can extract account value before a secondary-market transfer completes - (File: `contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs`)

### Summary
A lending position NFT directly controls the mutable collateral and debt positions stored under the same controller account. Because the NFT remains under the seller’s control until the marketplace transfer executes, the seller can front-run that transfer with `withdraw` and/or `borrow`, pay the proceeds to themselves, and leave the buyer with a materially worse account than the one priced during purchase. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
`PositionNft` defines `account_id == token_id` and makes the token owner the lending-account owner. [2](#0-1)  The controller resolves ownership dynamically through that NFT rather than freezing the position for a pending secondary-market sale. [3](#0-2) 

Before transfer, the seller remains an authorized caller and can invoke `Controller::borrow` or `Controller::withdraw`. `borrow` authenticates the caller, verifies owner/delegate authority, sends pool funds to `to.unwrap_or(caller)`, creates debt on the transferred account, and only requires the resulting account to remain solvent. [4](#0-3)  `withdraw` similarly authenticates the current owner/delegate and sends withdrawn collateral to an arbitrary external recipient before enforcing post-withdrawal solvency. [5](#0-4) 

Neither `PositionNft::transfer` nor inherited `transfer_from` atomically snapshots or locks the referenced account. Once the NFT transfer completes, collateral and debt both remain attached to that account and become the buyer’s economic position. [1](#0-0) 

### Impact Explanation
A buyer can pay for a position based on its observed collateral value, but receive an account whose collateral has been removed or whose debt has been increased. The extracted pool assets are paid to the seller-selected `to` recipient, while the liability remains in the transferred account, causing theft of expected position value and potentially requiring the buyer to repay debt to recover remaining collateral. [6](#0-5) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack only requires the seller to submit normal unprivileged controller calls between the buyer’s observation/signing step and the marketplace’s NFT transfer. Borrowing and withdrawing are authorized until the NFT owner changes, and neither entrypoint has a sale-specific lock or settlement window. [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Use a protocol-aware sale/settlement mechanism that freezes owner-controlled account mutations while the NFT is committed to a sale. A sale escrow should acquire the NFT, verify an expected position snapshot—supply positions, debt positions, health factor, spoke, and mode—at execution, and reject settlement if the snapshot changed. Alternatively, add a controller-level transfer lock that blocks `borrow`, `withdraw`, and account-mutating strategy calls during a bounded sale window, while still allowing `repay` and liquidation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Seller owns account `id = token_id` containing valuable collateral and no debt.
2. Buyer observes `get_account_positions(id)` / `get_health_factor(id)` and submits a marketplace purchase for the NFT.
3. Before that purchase transaction executes, seller calls:
   - `borrow(caller=seller, account_id=id, borrows=[(hub_asset, amount)], to=Some(seller))`; or
   - `withdraw(caller=seller, account_id=id, withdrawals=[(collateral, amount)], to=Some(seller))`.
4. The controller authorizes the seller because the NFT has not yet transferred, pays the borrowed or withdrawn assets to `seller`, and preserves only the protocol solvency floor. [8](#0-7) 
5. The marketplace subsequently transfers `token_id=id` to the buyer. The buyer receives the same account but with reduced collateral and/or newly created debt, while the seller keeps both the sale proceeds and the extracted pool assets. [1](#0-0)
