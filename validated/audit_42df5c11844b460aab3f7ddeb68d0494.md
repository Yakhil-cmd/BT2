### Title
Single-transaction reserve skew on Aquarius stable pools depresses the fair LP price and enables unfair liquidations of LP-collateralized positions - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The Blueberry bug class is "oracle reads live AMM reserves (`getReserves`) / `totalSupply` and turns them into a price an attacker can move inside one transaction." XOXNO Lending does not price a plain token from a reserve ratio, but its `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` sources read the pool's live `get_reserves` and `get_total_shares` at resolution time in `providers::aquarius::read` (contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-114) and feed them into `fair_lp_price_wad` / `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (common/src/oracle/lp.rs, `lp_stable`). The constant-product fair-value formula `2·sqrt(va·vb)/shares` is essentially invariant under trades, but the stable-pool price is `min(price_a, price_b) · D(reserves)/shares`, where the StableSwap invariant `D` shrinks when reserves are skewed away from balance. An unprivileged attacker can therefore push a victim's LP collateral below its liquidation threshold within a single transaction, using the controller's own `flash_loan` / `flash_position` for capital.

### Finding Description
`read` performs fresh cross-contract calls each resolution: `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` → `pool.get_reserves()` and `aquarius_total_shares_call` → `pool.get_total_shares()` (common/src/oracle/providers/aquarius.rs:45-57, 88-93). For `AquariusStableLp` markets the price is `fair_stable_lp_price_wad(env, leg_a, leg_b, supply, amp)` (aquarius.rs:109-114), which computes the StableSwap invariant `D` from the live reserves and multiplies it by `min(pa, pb)` divided by total shares (mirrored in the reference implementation at tests/integration/production_config.py:169-177).

Two facts make the reserves attacker-controlled:
- `D` is maximized when `x == y`. Any skew — created by a large swap or a one-sided liquidity add on Aquarius — strictly lowers `D` relative to the balanced value, lowering the reported LP share price. Unlike the constant-product geometric mean (invariant under trades), the stable invariant is not skew-invariant.
- A single transaction can skew the pool, call the controller's `liquidate` (or `borrow` against freshly deposited-but-mispriced collateral in the other direction), and unwind — funded by the protocol's own `flash_loan` or a `flash_position` callback, and the skew itself can be partially unwound by an arbitrage-free reverse swap at the end of the same transaction.

The protective layers do not close this:
- `min_pool_value_wad` only rejects a shrunken pool (aquarius.rs:118-122); skewing reserves does not lower total pool value below the floor.
- `min_sanity_price_wad`/`max_sanity_price_wad` are deliberately wide bands (e.g., `XAUMUSDC_LP` allows 6–25 WAD around ~13 WAD, configs/mainnet/markets.json:1510-1511); a few-percent `D` reduction needed to push a high-LTV position under its liquidation threshold passes the band.
- Tolerance `upper_ratio_bps`/`lower_ratio_bps` is `0` for LP sources, so there is no second-source cross-check damping the move.
- `update_account_threshold` restamps a position's cached LTV/threshold, but the health factor itself is computed from the Context-cached strict price — the manipulated reading.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: any position whose collateral is an `AquariusStableLp` share (e.g., the PYUSD/USDC-style stable markets in configs/mainnet/markets.json) can be pushed below HF=1 by a same-transaction reserve skew, letting the attacker liquidate it and capture the liquidation bonus — or, with a deep enough skew, push healthy positions into `clean_bad_debt` territory and socialize losses to suppliers via the supply-index write-down.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-High. Requirements: a stable Aquarius pool backing a live lending market as collateral, victim positions near the liquidation threshold, and capital sufficient to move a stable-curve pool — which is expensive precisely because the curve is flat, but the controller's `flash_loan` and `flash_position` entrypoints supply that capital atomically, and Aquarius itself permits same-tx swaps. The cost is bounded by swap fees plus the residual skew after unwind; the payoff is the liquidation bonus on the victim's collateral. Mitigating: reserves revert after the tx, so the attack must land within one transaction and the pool depth (enforced loosely by `min_pool_value_wad`) raises the required capital.

### Recommendation
Use a manipulation-resistant bound for the stable LP price instead of the raw invariant on live reserves — e.g., price the LP at `min(pa, pb) · min(D_est)` where `D_est` is computed under the *balanced* assumption `x = y = (va/pa + vb/pb)/2` in value space (i.e., use the lower of the reserve-implied `D` and the balanced `D` consistent with external leg prices), or adopt the standard conservative bound `n · min(leg_prices) / shares`-style floors. At minimum, read reserves through a TWAP-style or least-recent-block source rather than the instantaneous `get_reserves`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker identifies victim position collateralized by stable-LP token S (hub 3 market) with HF ≈ 1.01.
2. In one transaction: call controller `flash_loan` (or `flash_position`) for token A leg of the Aquarius stable pool; inside the receiver callback, swap the borrowed A for B on the Aquarius pool, skewing `reserve_a >> reserve_b` so the StableSwap `D` falls by δ%.
3. Call `liquidate` on the victim. `read` → `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` returns the skewed reserves → `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` returns `min(pa,pb)·D'/shares`, δ% below fair; the position's HF computed from the Context-cached price drops below 1; the attacker repays debt and seizes S collateral plus the HF-based bonus.
4. Swap B back to A (recovering most capital net of fees), repay the flash loan in the same transaction — settlement is exact-balance so the flash guard is satisfied. Pool and price revert, but the victim's collateral is already seized.
