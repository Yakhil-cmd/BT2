### Title
Free-retry gambling attack via cascading AA-chain bounce/revert - (File: aa_composer.js)

### Summary
The reported LuckLambo104 incident describes a "transaction rollback attack": an attacker's contract calls the gambling contract, inspects the outcome, and conditionally reverts to erase losing bets while keeping winning ones, at near-zero cost. ocore's Autonomous Agent (AA) execution model contains a structurally analogous mechanism: a chain of AA triggers is executed atomically, and if any AA further down the chain bounces, the *entire* primary trigger's state changes are rolled back to a savepoint, while the original bytes sent are refunded to the trigger sender minus a small, self-defined bounce fee.

### Finding Description
When a primary trigger fans out into secondary/tertiary AA calls, `handleTrigger` propagates failures upward: a secondary AA that bounces causes its caller's `handleSecondaryTriggers` error branch to run, and if the caller itself is a secondary AA it also just calls `bounce(err)`; once the failure reaches the *primary* (non-secondary) AA, `revert(err)` is invoked instead [1](#0-0) .

`revert()` clears all accumulated responses/state and issues `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT initial_balances`, undoing every balance and state-variable change made by *every* AA in the chain (including a target "gambling" AA's jackpot/state updates), then calls `bounce(err)` on the primary AA [2](#0-1) .

`bounce()` then refunds the trigger's payment back to the original trigger address, subtracting only `bounce_fees` — an amount fully defined by the *primary* AA's own author (default is the protocol minimum, `constants.MIN_BYTES_BOUNCE_FEE`) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) .

Because AAs can read state of other addresses via `var[address][name]`, `previous_aa_responses`, and `trigger.output[[...]]` inside the same trigger chain [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) , an attacker-authored AA acting as the outer ("wrapper") trigger sender can:
1. Forward a wager to a target gambling/lottery AA as a secondary trigger.
2. Receive the gambling AA's payout/response back as a further secondary trigger (by routing the payout to the wrapper's own address).
3. Inspect the outcome (win/lose amount, RNG result, or state var) inside that secondary invocation.
4. Call `bounce()` deliberately when the outcome is unfavorable.

This deliberate bounce propagates to `revert()` at the primary level, erasing the entire bet (including the gambling AA's own pot deduction/state update) as if it never happened, while the wrapper AA — fully controlled and defined by the attacker — refunds nearly all of the wagered bytes back to itself, losing only its own self-chosen `bounce_fees`.

### Impact Explanation
This lets an attacker eliminate downside risk on any AA-based game whose payout/state-commit logic occurs within the same atomic trigger chain as the bet: losing rounds are costlessly discarded (only the attacker's own minimal bounce fee is lost), while winning rounds are finalized and drain the target AA's pot/jackpot. Over repeated attempts this asymmetrically extracts funds from the target AA, mirroring the "rollback attack" pattern in the original report (persistent free retries until a favorable outcome, draining the contract's balance).

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability requires only posting an AA definition (unprivileged, permissionless) and sending ordinary triggers — no special privileges, and it works against any AA whose game logic and payout are executed as callable secondary AAs of an attacker-controlled wrapper. This is a documented consequence of atomic, revertible AA composition, but many third-party gambling/lottery AAs may not defend against it (e.g., by refusing calls that are themselves AAs, or by settling bets outside the reversible chain), making practical occurrence plausible whenever such contracts are deployed.

### Recommendation
- For AA game authors: reject triggers or payout recipients that are themselves AA addresses (`is_aa`) to prevent conditional-bounce wrapper chains; commit irreversible side effects (e.g., pot debits) using mechanisms not undone by `revert()`, or require the bet resolution to depend on data unknowable at trigger-composition time (e.g., a future oracle-posted value) rather than data revealed within the same atomic chain.
- Document/highlight in ocore's AA design guidance that `bounce()`/`revert()` fully undo all balance and state changes across an entire trigger chain and that the refund cost is bounded only by the *primary* AA's own configurable `bounce_fees`, meaning an attacker who controls the primary AA controls their own retry cost.
- Consider protocol-level guidance or lint checks in `aa_validation.js` warning authors when a gambling/lottery-style AA can be triggered indirectly through another AA and pays out within the same chain.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual (oscript-level) reproduction based on verified mechanics:
1. Attacker deploys `WrapperAA` with `bounce_fees: {base: 10000}` (protocol minimum).
2. `WrapperAA`'s messages forward the trigger's bytes as a payment to `GamblingAA` (secondary trigger) and set `WrapperAA` as the payout recipient (`trigger.address`) inside that secondary call.
3. `GamblingAA` computes an outcome (e.g., via `number_from_seed`) and pays out to `trigger.address`, which is `WrapperAA` — this pays back into `WrapperAA`, invoking it a second time as a further secondary trigger.
4. In this second invocation, `WrapperAA`'s oscript checks `trigger.output[[asset=base]]` (the amount received back from `GamblingAA`, revealing win/loss) and calls `bounce("try again")` when the amount indicates a loss.
5. `handleSecondaryTriggers`/`revert()` unwind the whole primary trigger via `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT initial_balances` [2](#0-1) , and `bounce()` on `WrapperAA` refunds the wager minus 10,000 bytes bounce fee to the original sender [7](#0-6) .
6. Repeating this only "keeps" winning rounds while losing rounds cost the attacker just the fixed bounce fee, draining `GamblingAA`'s balance asymmetrically over time.

Note: This analysis is based on statically reading `aa_composer.js` and `formula/evaluation.js`; I was not able to execute the AA test suite to empirically confirm end-to-end fund flow across three chained AAs (only two-level chains are shown in the existing test `test/aa_composer.test.js:156-253`), so the exact multi-hop reflection scenario (steps 2–3 above) is inferred from the documented mechanics of `handleSecondaryTriggers`, `bounce`, and `revert`, not from a directly observed 3-AA test case.
