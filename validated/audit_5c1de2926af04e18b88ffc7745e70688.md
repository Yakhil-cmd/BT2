### Title
Scaled-supply overflow permanently bricks a market: `accrue_step` panics in `scaled_to_original` once `supplied × supply_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX` - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
The interest-accrual step that every pool mutation runs first computes `supplied_original = scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` via `mul_div_half_up`, which widens to `I256` and then converts back to `i128`, returning `None` — and therefore panicking with `GenericError::MathOverflow` — whenever the RAY-denominated supplied value does not fit `i128` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs`, `try_mul_div_half_up` → `to_i128`). Because `supplied` is attacker-growable through the permissionless `supply` entrypoint up to the listed cap (whose domain ceiling is calibrated only so `cap × 10^(27-d)` fits `i128`, leaving no headroom for `× index / RAY`), an unprivileged supplier can push the market's scaled supply close enough to `i128::MAX` that accrual overflows as soon as `supply_index` drifts above `RAY`. Since `global_sync` runs inside `ops::renewed_market` at the head of every market operation, the panic makes every subsequent call — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `flash_loan`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes` — revert permanently for that market.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` unconditionally evaluates `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` before anything else (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs` line 61). `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` → `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs`). The half-up path computes `(x·y + d/2)/d` exactly in `I256` but then calls `.to_i128()`; a result above `i128::MAX` yields `None`, and `mul_div_half_up` panics `MathOverflow` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs` lines 108–143). The same panic fires in `update_supply_index`, `supply_index_reward_shortfall`, and `protocol_fee_shares`, all of which multiply `supplied` by an index.

The pool's mutating path `interest::global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` invokes `accrue_step` with the stored `supplied` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` lines 39–53), and `renewed_market` runs `global_sync` before every op. Nothing caps `supplied` below `i128::MAX / (index/RAY)`: `protocol_fee_shares` explicitly clamps revenue shares at `i128::MAX − supplied`, acknowledging `supplied` may approach `i128::MAX`. The harness test `supply_at_the_domain_ceiling_succeeds_and_one_unit_more_is_rejected` confirms that a supply at the asset-domain ceiling books successfully while the next unit is rejected only by `MATH_OVERFLOW` — i.e., the ceiling admits scaled supply values whose accrual-time valuation can exceed `i128::MAX` once the index exceeds `RAY`. Once that state is reached, `supply_index` and `borrow_index` only grow (borrow index is monotone, capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36`), so the overflow is irreversible: bad-debt write-down (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`) lowers `supply_index` but is itself unreachable because `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` also go through `global_sync`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate: every entry path that touches the affected market — all pool ops plus controller `supply`/`borrow`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`liquidate`/`flash_position`/`multiply`/`update_indexes` — panics on `MathOverflow` inside `global_sync` forever. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidations cannot run, and the pool's token balance (including donated/stranded cash) is locked. The cost to the attacker is only temporarily locked capital: they supply up to the cap (or a whale-scale amount close to the domain ceiling) in a market where the index will exceed `RAY`, wait for accrual, and the market is bricked.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address via `controller::supply` (or direct pool supply) with an amount at/near the market cap on an asset whose decimals place the domain ceiling near `i128::MAX` in RAY terms; no privileged action, oracle manipulation, or leaked key is needed. Exploitability depends on the configured cap allowing `supplied` within a factor `index/RAY` of `i128::MAX`; for low-decimal assets the `×10^(27-d)` normalization makes this margin small, and `protocol_fee_shares`' `i128::MAX − supplied` clamp confirms the designers anticipated `supplied` approaching the `i128` boundary. Once `supply_index > RAY` (guaranteed by any nonzero borrow activity over time), the first `global_sync` after the threshold crossing permanently DoSes the market.

### Recommendation
Bound `supplied` (scaled RAY shares) at mint time so that `supplied × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY / RAY` (and `× MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX`) always fits `i128` — i.e., enforce `scaled_supply + minted ≤ i128::MAX / MAX_INDEX_MULTIPLE` inside `ops::supply`/`accrue_revenue`, rather than only checking that the asset-unit cap's RAY normalization fits. Alternatively, saturate accrual inputs: clamp `scaled_to_original(supplied, index)` to `i128::MAX` inside `accrue_step` (mirroring the existing `mul_div_floor_saturating` used by `calculate_scaled_cap`) so accrual degrades gracefully instead of panicking, and emit a market-halted event when saturation occurs. Both fixes keep `global_sync` total and prevent an attacker from converting a large-but-legal supply position into a permanent market freeze.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists asset `A` with `decimals = 18` (or low decimals) and a cap at/near the asset-domain ceiling; market starts with `supply_index = borrow_index = RAY`.
2. Attacker calls `controller::supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, [(A_key, cap_amount)])` — succeeds, booking `supplied ≈ floor(cap × 10^9 × RAY / RAY) ≈ i128::MAX` scaled shares (per the domain-ceiling test, this "books exactly").
3. Any borrow activity occurs (attacker can do this on a separate account with collateral in another market) so that after `delta_ms > 0`, `update_borrow_index` yields `borrow_index > RAY` and `update_supply_index` yields `supply_index > RAY`.
4. Next call into the market — e.g., any user's `withdraw` — enters `ops::renewed_market` → `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` → `try_mul_div_half_up` → `I256` product `supplied·index/RAY > i128::MAX` → `to_i128() = None` → `panic MathOverflow`.
5. The panic is permanent: `supply_index` never decreases (bad-debt write-down is unreachable through the same bricked path), so every subsequent `supply`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`liquidate`/`update_indexes`/`flash_loan` on market `A` reverts; all supplier funds and protocol revenue in that market are frozen indefinitely.

Note: the precise margin between the asset-domain cap ceiling and the `i128::MAX` accrual boundary (`supplied ≤ i128::MAX·RAY/index`) was not fully verifiable from the indexed `max_cap_for_decimals` helper; the finding holds wherever the listed cap permits `supplied` to approach that boundary, which the `i128::MAX − supplied` revenue clamp and the domain-ceiling test indicate is a reachable regime.
