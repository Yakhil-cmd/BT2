### Title
Attacker-controlled asset issuance can push an AA's stored balance to the `MAX_BALANCE` ceiling, permanently freezing that AA's ability to accept further deposits of the asset - ([File: aa_composer.js])

### Summary
The reported Connext bug is a class of "resource pushed to its boundary by an attacker, causing all future legitimate operations that rely on headroom in that resource to permanently revert/DoS." In `ocore`, `aa_composer.js` maintains a hard ceiling `MAX_BALANCE` on the per-asset balance an Autonomous Agent (AA) can hold [1](#0-0) . Whenever a trigger (a payment message posted by *any* unprivileged unit poster) would push an AA's balance for an asset above this ceiling, the trigger is bounced with `"balance overflow"` [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . An asset issuer who defines an uncapped, freely-issuable asset (an "asset issuer" role explicitly reachable by an unprivileged user) can mint and send successive maximal-size units to an AA to intentionally saturate that AA's balance close to `MAX_BALANCE`. Once saturated, *any* subsequent trigger — from the attacker or from any other legitimate user — that includes an output of that asset to the AA will always overflow the check and get bounced, permanently denying the AA the ability to receive further deposits of that asset.

### Finding Description
`MAX_BALANCE` is defined as a large but finite ceiling: `2**63 - 1 - MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT * MAX_CAP` [1](#0-0) , using `constants.MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT = 128` and `constants.MAX_CAP = 9e15` [5](#0-4) .

`updateInitialAABalances`, called on every trigger to an AA, adds the trigger's outputs to the AA's current balance for each asset and flags overflow if the new balance exceeds `MAX_BALANCE`: [6](#0-5) 

The db-backed path (used for real, non-dry-run triggers) performs the same check and returns `"balance overflow"` once `pemCurvesFixMci` is active: [7](#0-6) 

This check unconditionally applies to *any* incoming trigger to the AA for that asset — not just the attacker's own units. If an asset has no `cap` (unlimited issuance is a legitimate, supported asset configuration in ocore's `asset` message), its issuer can mint and send arbitrarily many units of up to `MAX_CAP` per output, up to `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT` outputs per unit, repeatedly to the target AA. Since each maximal unit can push the balance up by roughly `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT * MAX_CAP` (~1.152e18), only a handful of such units (~8) are needed to bring the AA's balance for that asset within striking distance of `MAX_BALANCE` (~9.2e18 minus that same margin). From that point on, the ceiling check in `updateInitialAABalances` will reject (bounce) *every future trigger* that adds even a small additional amount of that asset to the AA — the balance can never grow again for that asset unless the AA itself spends the balance back down, which it may not be programmed to do unconditionally.

This mirrors the reported Executor bug's root cause exactly: a state variable (allowance / AA asset balance) is incremented toward a hard limit by an entity that controls the trigger content, and once at/near the limit, an unconditional pre-check on the increment path causes every subsequent legitimate operation to revert, producing a persistent DoS rather than a one-off failure.

### Impact Explanation
Any AA that accepts a particular non-capped, third-party-issued asset as part of its normal operation (e.g., a DEX/AMM/pool AA, a payment-channel style AA, or any AA that tracks `balance[asset]`) can be permanently denied the ability to receive further deposits of that asset once an attacker (the asset's issuer) saturates the AA's tracked balance near `MAX_BALANCE`. This is a concrete "AA fund loss or freezing" condition: users who wish to interact with the AA using that asset can no longer do so — their triggers will always bounce with "balance overflow," and the AA's business logic for that asset becomes permanently unusable. Because the balance ceiling is per-asset and per-address, the blast radius is scoped to AAs that accept the attacker-controlled asset, but for any pool/AMM-style AA that must accept deposits of a specific asset to function (as shown by the market-maker and order-book AA templates in the repo's own examples [8](#0-7) ), this constitutes a lasting denial of service against the contract's core function.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only actions available to an ordinary unprivileged user: defining an asset with no `cap` (or a very large cap) via a standard `asset` message, issuing it to themselves, and sending a modest number (roughly single digits) of maximal-size payment units to the target AA. All of this is achievable through normal DAG unit posting; no special/hub/operator privileges are needed. The main cost to the attacker is standard unit fees for a handful of maximal-size units, which is inexpensive relative to permanently disabling an AA's use of an asset.

### Recommendation
- Scope the overflow protection so that it does not create an unrecoverable "always bounce" state: e.g., reject/limit balances only at asset-definition time (cap enforcement) rather than relying on a global numeric ceiling reachable via unlimited issuance, or allow the AA logic to explicitly evict/reject excess deposits without bouncing the whole trigger permanently.
- Consider bounding per-asset balance growth by the asset's own declared `cap` (when present) instead of, or in addition to, the fixed `MAX_BALANCE`, and require an explicit, sane cap for assets that will be accepted by AAs performing this kind of accumulation.
- Alternatively, treat "balance overflow" purely as a soft cap enforced at the protocol level for uncapped assets (e.g., disallow single addresses, including AA addresses, from holding more than some sane multiple of realistic total-supply for uncapped assets) so that legitimate future deposits are not unconditionally and irreversibly blocked.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker issues asset `X` via an `asset` message with no `cap`, `is_private: false`, `fixed_denominations: false`, `issued_by_definer_only: true` — a normal, supported asset configuration.
2. Attacker issues enough units of `X` to themselves to hold a very large quantity (issuance is free-form when uncapped).
3. Attacker crafts and posts a trigger unit to victim AA `V` containing a `payment` message in asset `X` with up to `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT` (128) outputs to `V`, each up to `MAX_CAP` (9e15) — repeating this for a handful of units (~8) until `V`'s tracked balance for asset `X`, as computed in `updateInitialAABalances` [6](#0-5) , approaches `MAX_BALANCE`.
4. Any further trigger — from the attacker or any other user — that sends any additional amount of asset `X` to `V` now causes `objValidationState.assocBalances[address][asset] > MAX_BALANCE` to evaluate true, setting `bOverflow`, and the trigger is bounced with `"balance overflow"` [4](#0-3) .
5. `V` is now permanently unable to accept further deposits of asset `X`, freezing any functionality of `V` that depends on receiving that asset.

Note: I was not able to fully verify runtime behavior (e.g., whether any AA-side logic could later spend down the balance to un-stick this state) without executing the code; this assessment is based on static reading of `aa_composer.js`'s balance-accounting and overflow-check logic.
