### Title
Permanent market freeze via RAY value-overflow in accrual — an unprivileged supplier/borrower can push a market's debt value past `i128`, bricking every entrypoint - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs](contracts/pool/src/interest.rs))

### Summary
Every mutating pool entrypoint (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `flash_loan`, `seize_positions` via controller liquidation, `net_settle`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue`) first runs `global_sync`, which accrues interest via `accrue_step`. Accrual computes the ceiled debt value `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` in `i128`/`I256`-to-`i128` fixed-point math. When the market's scaled debt times the index exceeds the `i128` RAY-value ceiling (~`1.7e38`), `scaled_to_original`/`mul_ceil` panics with `MathOverflow` (error 33). Because the panic happens inside accrual — before any verb-specific logic — every subsequent call to the market reverts forever. The borrow-index cap (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36`) does not prevent this: the debt-value ceiling is reached first when `borrowed` shares are large, so accrual dies before the cap can clamp the index.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step`, which evaluates `borrowed × borrow_index` in RAY. Per `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`, multiply-divide widens to `I256` only for the intermediate; the final `.to_i128()` yields `None`/overflow when the *result* exceeds `i128`, raising `GenericError::MathOverflow`. `docs/reference/formulas.md:433-437` concedes: "Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first." The in-repo proof is `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`: a whale market with ~10⁹ × 10¹⁸-token supply at 98% utilization on a steep rate curve panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original`, after which `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert identically — and liquidation path `seize_positions` also accrues first, so even cleanup is impossible. Supply caps are the only mitigation, and they are per-market governance-set bounds that can be lifted (`lift_caps` in the test) or simply set high for large-supply assets.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds (accepted impact class). Once the RAY-value ceiling is crossed, the market is bricked: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize, and the protocol cannot recapitalize or clean bad debt — every call panics in accrual. All tokens held as pool cash for that market are locked permanently; open debt positions across other markets remain un-liquidatable in the affected leg, degrading to protocol insolvency risk for cross-collateralized accounts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Triggering requires no privilege — only `supply`/`borrow` on a market whose scaled debt × index product approaches `i128::MAX`. The hurdle is capital scale: the attacker (or organic growth) must push the RAY-denominated debt value near the ceiling, which for an 18-decimal asset means on the order of 10²⁶ token units, and sustained high utilization at a steep curve segment to grow the index. For low-decimals/high-price assets or markets with lifted/loose supply caps the bound is materially easier to reach; the ceiling admits ~170 billion whole tokens of input and the index multiplies that further. It is a deterministic, permanent, one-shot trap rather than a probabilistic exploit, and once crossed it cannot be undone by any governance action short of upgrade.

### Recommendation
Cap the accrual safely instead of overflowing: in `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, clamp the computed debt value (or clamp `borrowed` shares used for interest valuation) to `i128::MAX` rather than panicking, matching the already-saturating `revenue_shares = min(..., i128::MAX − supplied)` pattern in `simulate.rs`. Alternatively, enforce a hard `MathOverflow`-free domain by rejecting `supply`/`borrow` amounts that would push `borrowed × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` near the `i128` ceiling — a derived per-market share cap enforced in `calculate_scaled_borrow`/`calculate_scaled_supply` — so the overflow state is unreachable rather than merely documented.

### Proof of Concept
The repo contains the executable PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321` — `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`. Steps: (1) `supply` ~10⁹·10¹⁸ units of an 18-decimal asset (unprivileged); (2) `borrow` 98% of it (unprivileged, caps lifted as they can be by governance or for uncapped listings); (3) advance ledger time; (4) `update_indexes` reverts with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; (5) `withdraw(1)` and `repay(1)` revert identically — market permanently frozen, all cash locked.
