### Title
Stale anchor leg inflates dual-source prices and enables undercollateralized borrowing - (File: `contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs`)

### Summary

A two-source oracle accepts each configured source until its own `max_stale_seconds` boundary and then prices the asset at the midpoint of both readings, even when the two observations are many hours apart. An unprivileged borrower can therefore supply collateral while a slow “anchor” feed still reports an obsolete high price, causing the blended collateral price and LTV borrowing capacity to exceed the fresh market value.

### Finding Description

For a two-source configuration, `compose` reads both configured sources and `blend` accepts them unless either leg is individually stale or the applicable market-leg age spread exceeds the limit. The code nevertheless assigns the accepted price as `midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor.price_wad, primary.price_wad)` rather than preferring the fresh conservative leg. [1](#0-0)  Source freshness is independently bounded by `FeedSource.max_stale_seconds` and the oracle-wide `max_price_stale_seconds`, allowing a correctly configured secondary feed to remain usable after hours without an update. [2](#0-1) 

The controller turns this inflated blended price directly into borrowing power: collateral legs are valued with `position_value_floor(scaled_amount, supply_index, price)` and weighted by the stored effective LTV. [3](#0-2)  `borrow` then pays out through the pool before enforcing post-mutation solvency against those same cached prices. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation

A user can withdraw more debt than the collateral is worth at the current market price. Once the stale feed updates or is excluded after crossing its stale boundary, the account can remain undercollateralized, shifting the loss to liquidators, suppliers, or the protocol through bad debt.

### Likelihood Explanation

The path is permissionless for an account owner: call `supply` to establish collateral and then `borrow` while the old anchor observation remains inside its configured freshness window. The test harness demonstrates this concrete condition using an anchor price of `$1.00`, a fresh primary price of `$0.91`, a 10% tolerance, and an anchor age of 82,800 seconds; the blended collateral valuation is inflated by over 4% and a borrow that should revert succeeds. [5](#0-4) 

### Recommendation

Do not blend materially asynchronous observations into an actionable strict price. Bound the timestamp spread between every required leg—not only between two `Market` legs—to a value appropriate for the fastest oracle heartbeat, and reject an anchor leg after that shorter synchronization window. Where a secondary feed is intended as a sanity anchor rather than a market quote, use the more conservative acceptable price for collateral valuation or fail closed when the legs disagree because of age.

### Proof of Concept

1. Configure collateral `X` with a fresh primary feed and an anchor feed whose `max_stale_seconds` is `86,400`.
2. Initially set both feeds to `$1.00` and supply 100,000 `X`.
3. Update the primary feed to `$0.91` while leaving the anchor observation at `$1.00` from 82,800 seconds earlier.
4. Both readings remain inside tolerance and freshness limits, so the strict blended price becomes approximately `$0.955`.
5. Call `borrow` for an amount justified by the blended collateral value but not by the fresh `$0.91` valuation.
6. The harness test confirms that the stale-anchor execution completes while the equivalent fresh-anchor control execution reverts with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [6](#0-5)

/// source's own component-level staleness with staleness computed against

    key: &PriceKey,
    oracle: &AssetOracle,
    source: &PriceSource,

    };

            timestamp,

/// Uses each position's stored `min(LTV, liquidation_threshold)`.
pub(crate) fn calculate_ltv_collateral_wad(
    env: &Env,
    cache: &mut Context,
    supply_positions: &Map<HubAssetKey, AccountPositionRaw>,
) -> Wad {
    cache.load_markets(&supply_positions.keys());

    let mut ltv = Wad::ZERO;
    for (hub_asset, position) in iter_typed_positions(supply_positions) {
        let feed = cache.cached_price(&hub_asset.asset);
        let market_index = cache.cached_market_index(&hub_asset);

        let value = position_value_floor(
            env,
            position.scaled_amount,
            market_index.supply_index,
            feed.price,
        );

        let effective_ltv = position.loan_to_value.min(position.liquidation_threshold);
        ltv = ltv.checked_add(env, effective_ltv.apply_to_wad_floor(env, value));

    let mut account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

        &account,

        PositionSides::Both
    } else {
