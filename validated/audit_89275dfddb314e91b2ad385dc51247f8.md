### Title
RAY debt-value overflow permanently freezes market operations - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
Interest accrual evaluates total debt as `borrowed * borrow_index` before applying the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap. For a sufficiently large market, the index can remain below its cap while the unscaled debt value no longer fits `i128`, causing every subsequent market operation to panic with `MathOverflow`. An unprivileged borrower can establish the required state through `Controller::supply`, `Controller::borrow`, and later trigger the overflow through `Controller::update_indexes`.

### Finding Description
Every pool mutation loads its market through `synced_market`, which calls `interest::global_sync` before the operation proceeds. `global_sync` splits elapsed time into chunks and invokes `accrue_chunk` for each interval. `accrue_chunk` delegates the state transition to `accrue_step`.

Inside `accrue_step`, utilization is computed by first converting scaled debt to its current RAY value with `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)`. `scaled_to_original` is a non-saturating `Ray::mul`; its underlying `mul_div_half_up` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the exact result does not fit `i128`.

The borrow index is capped only after this unscaled debt valuation occurs. Therefore, the borrow index may remain below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` even though `borrowed * borrow_index` has already exceeded the representable RAY domain. Once the market reaches that state, all operations that sync the market—borrow, repay, withdraw, liquidation-related pool calls, recapitalization, revenue claims, and direct index updates—execute the same failing accrual path.

`Controller::update_indexes` exposes the trigger to any authorized caller: it calls `require_authorized_caller` and then invokes the pool's `update_indexes` owner entrypoint. The user's market position itself can be established without privileged control through public `supply` and `borrow` entrypoints, subject to the market's configured caps, collateral, liquidity, and rate model.

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent freezing-of-funds condition for the affected `(hub_id, asset)` market. Once the accrued debt-value calculation overflows, suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot process positions touching that market, and recapitalization cannot recover it because those flows accrue interest before performing their mutation.

The failure is not a bounded fail-closed input rejection: it is a persistent state in which every subsequent valid market operation panics. Existing supplier collateral and any collectible debt become inaccessible through the protocol surface. The in-repo harness confirms this concrete outcome: after the overflow, both a one-unit withdrawal and a repayment revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, while the stored borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends on admitted market scale and sustained utilization. The RAY asset domain must exceed `i128::MAX`; for an 18-decimal asset, the documented fixture uses roughly 170 billion whole tokens of represented debt-side value. At 98% utilization on the tested curve, repeated yearly accrual reaches the overflow before the borrow-index cap engages.

A single attacker can provide the required collateral to their own account, borrow the large position, and later call `update_indexes`. Other suppliers' funds are trapped if they supplied the same market. The attack requires a market whose caps admit the required scale and enough time or index growth for the debt value to exceed the `i128` RAY bound; markets with lower caps or different rate curves may not be susceptible.

### Recommendation
Make accrual bounded by representable market value, not only by `borrow_index`.

- Before mutating `borrow_index`, calculate whether `borrowed * new_index` fits the RAY domain.
- If it would overflow, clamp the index to the largest value that keeps `borrowed * borrow_index` representable, rather than reverting.
- Alternatively, cap admitted scaled debt below `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, accounting for revenue-share growth and subsequent bad-debt/index changes.
- Add a market-level invariant test asserting that `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` remain representable after every accrual step.
- Ensure repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and recapitalization continue to work when an index reaches its bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. In an 18-decimal market, supply approximately `1e9 * 10^18` base units.
2. Supply sufficient separate collateral to the attacker's account.
3. Borrow approximately 98% of the supplied asset through `Controller::borrow`.
4. Advance ledger time until the next accrual would push `borrowed * borrow_index` above `i128::MAX`.
5. Call `Controller::update_indexes` for that `HubAssetKey`.
6. The pool's `accrue_step` evaluates `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and panics with `MathOverflow`.
7. Subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls panic for the same reason before reaching their withdrawal or repayment logic.

The in-repository test demonstrates the sequence and resulting freeze:
- `borrowed` and `supplied` are passed into `accrue_step`.
- `scaled_to_original` multiplies scaled debt by the current index.
- The checked result overflows during accrual.
- Both withdrawal and repayment revert afterward with `MATH_OVERFLOW`.
