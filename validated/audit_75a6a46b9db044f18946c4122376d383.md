### Title
Residual dust supply shares keep `supplied > 0` after full value withdrawal, diverting borrower interest from protocol revenue - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The pool's accrual and solvency logic uses the raw scaled-share total `supplied` (and `borrowed`) as the "does anyone have a position" signal, the same role `unstreamed` plays in the reference report. `resolve_withdrawal` treats a request below the half-up displayed balance as partial, burning `ceil` shares but paying the floor-valued amount, which can leave a dust share residual on a position whose entire real balance was paid out. Once every supplier exits this way, `supplied != Ray::ZERO` even though essentially all deposited tokens were withdrawn, so `accrue_step` keeps distributing `supplier_rewards` into the supply index over the dust instead of booking the full reward as protocol revenue via `supply_index_reward_shortfall`, and utilization is computed against a near-zero supply value, pinning the borrow rate at its cap.

### Finding Description
Two zero-checks gate on the share counter rather than on value:

1. `Cache::calculate_utilization` returns `Ray::ZERO` only when `self.supplied == Ray::ZERO`, then computes `utilization(total_borrowed, total_supplied)` on the scaled values — `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:19-27`.
2. `update_supply_index` distributes `rewards_increase` over `supplied` shares; it early-returns only when `supplied == 0` or the RAY-valued `total_supplied_value` rounds to zero — `common/src/rates/index.rs:29-44`. The residual reward that the index cannot absorb is recovered by `supply_index_reward_shortfall` and credited to protocol revenue in `accrue_step` — `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:71-87`.

Per `docs/reference/formulas.md` and `skills/xoxno-lending/math.md:55`, a withdrawal request below the half-up displayed balance is resolved as partial: it pays the requested amount and burns `ceil` shares, and "a request sized from the floor balance is partial and leaves dust shares." Nothing forces a full burn — only `withdraw(0)`/requests ≥ the half-up balance do that. `require_supply_for_debt` also only panics when `supplied == 0` while `borrowed != 0` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:69-73`), so dust shares additionally keep a drained market "backed" and avoid the insolvency halt.

The exploitable configuration: a dust share residual whose RAY-valued supply `supplied * supply_index` is just above zero (so `update_supply_index` does not early-return) but whose token value is ~0 (so the user has already received their full payout). With outstanding debt, utilization = debt / dust ≈ max, `calculate_borrow_rate` hits the curve cap, and `supplier_rewards` — which for a truly empty market would flow entirely into `supply_index_reward_shortfall` → protocol revenue — are instead spread over the dust shares, inflating the supply index on them.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of unclaimed yield / protocol revenue. When all real supply is withdrawn, correct accounting (the `supplied == 0` path) routes the full `supplier_rewards` portion of borrower interest to protocol revenue as shortfall. The dust residue instead credits that interest to the dust positions, which their owners can later withdraw once borrowers repay (repay is not gated by `require_utilization_below_max`, which exits skip anyway). The attacker captures yield on a zero-value stake, and `require_utilization_below_max` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-33`) simultaneously rejects new borrows, further withdrawals, and `claim_revenue` because `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied)` exceeds `max_utilization` — freezing revenue collection while the dust accrues.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged user via `controller.withdraw` with a partial amount (or the floor-balance amount), requiring no special permissions. The attacker needs outstanding third-party (or self-created via a second account) debt on the market to generate interest. Positioning the dust so that `supplied * supply_index` stays nonzero in RAY while representing a fraction of a base unit is straightforward since shares are RAY-scaled and withdrawals burn `ceil` shares. The protocol's own documentation acknowledges dust shares as a normal outcome, so this arises from ordinary flows, not only adversarial ones.

### Recommendation
Mirror the report's fix: gate accrual and utilization on value, not on the share counter. In `Cache::calculate_utilization` and `accrue_step`/`update_supply_index`, treat the market as empty when the floored token-denominated supply value (e.g. `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) == 0`) is zero, not merely when `supplied == Ray::ZERO`. Alternatively, in `resolve_withdrawal`, treat a request ≥ the floor-valued balance as a full close and burn all shares, eliminating dust residuals.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market has supply index `S`, borrow index `B`, `asset_decimals = 7`, `max_utilization < RAY`.
2. Attacker calls `controller.supply` for 1,000 USDC; shares minted at `floor`.
3. Bob borrows most of the liquidity (any borrower; the attacker can borrow from a second account with collateral on another asset).
4. Attacker calls `controller.withdraw` with `amount = unscale_supply_floor(position)` (the floor-valued balance). `resolve_withdrawal` treats it as partial, burns `ceil` shares, leaves dust shares `d > 0` on the position; pool `supplied = d`, cash ≈ 0.
5. `global_sync`/`accrue_step` runs: `supplied != 0` and `d * S > 0` in RAY, so utilization ≈ debt/dust → borrow rate at cap; `supplier_rewards` are folded into the supply index over `d` instead of becoming `shortfall` revenue.
6. Bob later `repay`s principal + capped-rate interest. Attacker calls `withdraw(0)`; dust shares, now valued at the inflated index, pay out yield funded by Bob's interest. `claim_revenue` collected nothing during the window (reverts or books zero) even though the market was economically empty of real suppliers.
