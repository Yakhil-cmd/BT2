### Title
Bad-debt write-down revives wiped supplier claims through the supply-index floor - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When bad debt is greater than or equal to the market’s total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes zero remaining backing but then clamps the supply index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. This leaves every pre-wipeout supply share with a positive withdrawal claim even though the debt write-down was intended to consume all supplier value. Subsequent recapitalization creates withdrawable cash that stale shares can drain, preventing the market from being safely restored.

### Finding Description
Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` requires only caller authorization and invokes `clean_bad_debt_standalone` when the account is insolvent and its collateral is at or below the dust threshold. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Cleanup sends every remaining supply and debt position to `pool_seize_positions_call` and then deletes the account. [3](#0-2) 

For a borrow-side entry, the pool converts the debt shares to an asset-denominated `bad_debt`, applies it to the supply index, and burns the debt. [4](#0-3) 

The vulnerable calculation caps the loss at total supplied value, computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped`, and derives a reduction factor. [5](#0-4) 

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero and `new_supply_index` is zero, but `new_supply_index.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` raises it to `RAY / 1000` instead of making all old supply shares worthless. [6](#0-5) 

The existing supply positions remain stored in other accounts, while the pool’s `supplied` aggregate also remains nonzero; only the conversion index was reduced. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

The project’s own test demonstrates the consequence: after a wipeout write-down, an old scaled position still unscales to a positive amount, and once cash is introduced that stale position withdraws it. [9](#0-8) 

New ordinary supply is initially blocked because the phantom claims create a backing shortfall, but permissionless `recapitalize` exists specifically to add cash against that shortfall. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

After recapitalization credits cash, withdrawal resolves the stale shares into a positive gross amount, checks only available reserves and post-withdrawal guards, debits cash, and transfers tokens. [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
A completely written-down market retains unbacked supplier and revenue-share claims instead of deleting or disabling them. Those claims make the market permanently under-backed, so ordinary suppliers cannot enter until someone recapitalizes it. Any recapitalized cash can then be withdrawn by holders of pre-wipeout shares, including revenue-share claims, causing the recapitalizer to lose funds and leaving the market insolvent again.

This is permanent protocol insolvency and theft of funds contributed through `recapitalize`; it also prevents normal market operation because `supply` rejects an under-backed book while the phantom claims remain. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) 

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged caller can reach the vulnerable path through `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` once ordinary price movement makes a borrower’s collateral at most the configured dust threshold and below its debt. [15](#0-14) 

The floor case requires the written-down debt to be at least the debt market’s total supplied value. This can arise after a highly utilized market accrues borrower interest faster than supplier claims, particularly with a nonzero reserve factor, before the collateral crashes into the dust-gated cleanup range. [16](#0-15) 

No privileged action, oracle misbehavior, external callback, or malformed token is required; the exploit only uses ordinary borrow, price deterioration, permissionless cleanup, recapitalization, and withdrawal.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp the index upward after a total supply wipeout. When `capped == total_supplied_value`, the write-down must make all pre-wipeout supply and revenue shares non-withdrawable rather than leaving `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` value behind.

A safe design should explicitly represent a wiped market—for example, a write-off epoch or market reset—and ensure that:

- all supply and revenue shares existing before the wipeout have zero claim;
- no old controller position can later withdraw recapitalized cash;
- new supply begins only under a fresh share epoch or after all stale positions are invalidated;
- `recapitalize` cannot make pre-wipeout shares payable;
- tests cover `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value` through the public `clean_bad_debt` path, followed by `recapitalize` and attempted withdrawal.

### Proof of Concept
1. BOB supplies `X` units of `DEBT_ASSET` through `supply(caller=BOB, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, assets=[(debt_hub_asset, X)])`.

2. ALICE supplies collateral in another asset and borrows as much `DEBT_ASSET` as permitted through `borrow(caller=ALICE, account_id=A, borrows=[(debt_hub_asset, B)], to=None)`, leaving the debt market highly utilized.

3. Let the market accrue until `B * borrow_index >= total_supplied * supply_index`, then let the collateral price fall so ALICE has `total_debt > total_collateral` and `total_collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`; no oracle misconduct is needed.

4. Any caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(caller=KEEPER, account_id=A)`. The cleanup sends ALICE’s borrow shares to `seize_positions`, where `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` calculates zero remaining supplier value but stores `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of a zero-value write-off. [3](#0-2) [17](#0-16) 

5. BOB’s stored supply shares remain present. Their floor-scaled value is positive even though the write-down should have consumed their full claim, matching the repository’s raw-cache regression test. [18](#0-17) 

6. A third party calls `recapitalize(payer=CAROL, hub_asset=debt_hub_asset, amount=R)`, crediting real cash toward the apparent backing shortfall.

7. BOB calls `withdraw(caller=BOB, account_id=B_acct, withdrawals=[(debt_hub_asset, 0)], to=None)`. Amount zero requests the full position; the pool resolves BOB’s stale shares to a positive gross amount, debits the newly recapitalized cash, and transfers it to BOB. [12](#0-11) 

8. CAROL’s recapitalization is consumed by a claim that should have been erased, and the market returns to an under-backed state. Repeating recapitalization repeats the drain while the stale shares survive.

/// Requires open debt and the selected insolvency gate, then cleans up the account.
fn socialize_bad_debt(env: &Env, account_id: u64, gate: BadDebtGate) {
    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    let account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        !account.borrow_positions.is_empty(),
        CollateralError::DebtPositionNotFound
    );

    let totals = risk::calculate_account_risk_totals(
        env,
        &mut cache,
        &account.supply_positions,
        &account.borrow_positions,
    );

    let admits = match gate {
        BadDebtGate::DustCapped => {
            is_socializable_bad_debt(totals.total_debt, totals.total_collateral)
        }
        BadDebtGate::InsolventOnly => totals.total_debt > totals.total_collateral,
    };
    assert_with_error!(env, admits, CollateralError::CannotCleanBadDebt);

    bad_debt::execute_bad_debt_cleanup(env, &mut cache, account_id, &account, &totals);
}

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

**`repay`** and **`recapitalize`** refund from pool cash without debiting it,
correct only because the controller transferred the full amount in first.

**`claim_revenue`** is capped by cash. `Cache::burn_claimable_revenue` claims
`min(cash, floor(revenue_value))`, so a fully lent-out market pays out less than
`get_revenue` reports, and pays out zero when `cash` is zero. A partial claim
burns revenue shares with `mul_ratio_ceil`, which burns slightly more shares
than the proportional amount. Nothing is lost: the unclaimed remainder stays as
revenue shares and keeps earning. `require_utilization_below_max` and
`require_supply_for_debt` run even when nothing is claimable. If they pass, the
call returns `actual_amount = 0`, moves no tokens, and emits a market state
snapshot. Always read `actual_amount` from the returned `PoolAmountMutation`;
never assume the full revenue was paid.

/// Maps requested amount and position to shares burned and gross asset amount.
/// Panics if a nonzero gross amount would burn zero shares.
fn resolve_close_or_partial(cache: &Cache, amount: i128, position: Ray) -> (Ray, i128) {
    let (burned, gross_amount) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(amount, position);
    assert_with_error!(
        cache.env(),
        gross_amount == 0 || burned.raw() > 0,
        GenericError::WithdrawRoundsToZeroShares
    );
    (burned, gross_amount)
}

/// Burns `burned` from market supply and returns the user's remaining scaled position.
fn burn_position(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, position: Ray, burned: Ray) -> Ray {
    cache.burn_supply(burned);
    position.checked_sub(env, burned)
}

/// Enforces reserve, utilization, and solvency guards, then debits cash for
/// the net transfer. Liquidations and footprint-only closes skip utilization.
fn gate_and_debit(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, net_transfer: i128, skip_utilization_check: bool) {
    cache.require_reserves(net_transfer);

    if !skip_utilization_check {
        guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
    }
    guards::require_supply_for_debt(env, cache);
    cache.debit_cash(net_transfer);
}
