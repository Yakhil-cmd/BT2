### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps supply index at floor instead of zeroing stranded shares, letting wiped-out suppliers drain future deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The heap-overflow bug class maps here onto an accounting overwrite: when socialized bad debt exceeds the total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the supply index up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) rather than resetting it to zero or burning the residual supply shares. The surviving scaled supply balances therefore retain a non-zero phantom claim. Because the pool is a hub/spoke book over one physical token balance, any fresh deposit into the wiped market credits real cash that those stranded claims can withdraw, producing protocol insolvency and theft of the fresh depositor's funds.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value = supplied * supply_index`, caps `bad_debt` at that value, computes `reduction_factor = remaining / total_supplied_value` and sets `supply_index = supply_index * reduction_factor`, floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), `remaining == 0`, `reduction_factor == 0`, and the index is clamped *up* to `RAY/1000` — strictly positive — while `cache.supplied()` (the aggregate scaled shares) is left unchanged.

In `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`, the borrow side of a seize entry calls `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray` on the position, applies that as bad debt, and only burns `cache.burn_debt(position)` — the supply shares are never burned or zeroed.

The repo's own tests demonstrate the consequence at cache level (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` and `:430-495`): after a wipeout clamps the index, `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` returns a stranded claim; a fresh `calculate_scaled_supply`/`mint_supply`/`credit_cash` deposit is immediately withdrawable in full by the wiped-out holder via `resolve_withdrawal`, leaving `cash < fresh_claim` — the books cannot cover the new supplier. The test names note this is "without supply guard"; at the pool-op level no guard is visible in `seize.rs` — the only brake is `require_reserves(gross)`, which merely defers the theft until new cash exists rather than preventing it.

Entry path: any unprivileged liquidator or keeper calls `controller::liquidate` or `controller::clean_bad_debt` on a position whose collateral cannot cover the debt, causing the controller to submit a `PoolSeizeEntry { side: Borrow, .. }` to the pool; a market where unpaid debt ≥ total supplied value (e.g., a thin spoke or a fast crash through the liquidation threshold) triggers the clamp.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent protocol insolvency and theft of user funds: wiped-out supply shares retain a residual claim equal to `scaled * RAY/1000` against all future cash deposited into that market's book. The first fresh depositor's principal can be fully withdrawn by holders of the stranded shares, and the depositor is left with an unbacked claim. Because hub/spoke books share the pool's physical token balance per token, the loss is realized in real token transfers.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a wipeout event: a borrow position going to full bad debt (collateral seized/worthless) in a market where the socialized loss meets or exceeds remaining supplied value. Thin markets or markets with a single dominant borrower make this reachable. `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless, so triggering the write-down needs no privilege. The secondary exploitation step — a wiped-out share then draining a new deposit — is a plain `supply` + `withdraw` by any address.

### Recommendation
When `remaining == Ray::ZERO` in `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, do not floor the index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while leaving `supplied > 0`. Either (a) burn/rebase all outstanding supply shares (set `supplied` and per-position scaled balances to zero, e.g., via `absorb_supply_as_revenue`-style reclassification), or (b) set the index to true zero and add a `require` in withdraw/settle paths that scaled supply must be zero-claim when the index is at floor. Track the wipeout as recorded socialized loss so `recapitalize` can restore solvency without minting claims.

### Proof of Concept
Sketched from `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` (raw-cache reproduction already present in the repo):

1. Market state: `supplied = 1000 RAY` shares (Alice), `borrowed = 1000 RAY` shares, `supply_index = borrow_index = RAY`, `cash = 0` (Alice's deposit fully borrowed by a defaulted position whose collateral was seized worthless).
2. Keeper calls `controller::clean_bad_debt` on the defaulted position; controller submits `PoolSeizeEntry { side: Borrow, position.scaled_amount = 1000 RAY }`.
3. `seize::apply` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(bad_debt = 1000 RAY value)` → `remaining = 0` → `supply_index` clamps to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alice's `1000 RAY` shares now carry claim `floor(1000e27 * 1e24 / 1e27) > 0` instead of zero.
4. Bob calls `controller::supply` depositing `D` tokens; `calculate_scaled_supply` mints shares at the floored index, `credit_cash(D)` puts real tokens in the pool.
5. Alice calls `controller::withdraw` with `amount = 0` (withdraw-all); `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, 1000 RAY)` returns gross `= D`, `require_reserves` passes (fresh cash exists), pool transfers `D` to Alice.
6. Bob's `unscale_supply_floor` claim exceeds remaining `cash` — his deposit is gone; the market is insolvent.
