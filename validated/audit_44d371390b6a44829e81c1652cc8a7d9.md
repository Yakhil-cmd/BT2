### Title
Instantaneous, un-time-weighted balance snapshot in system-vote counting allows flash-transfer manipulation of `op_list`/fee-parameter governance - (File: `main_chain.js`)

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` determines the outcome of `system_vote` governance (the witness/order-processor `op_list`, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) by summing each voting address's *current* stable, unspent byte balance and multiplying it by whatever vote value that address last cast, with no requirement that the balance be held for any minimum duration before the count. This is the same MEV/flash-loan-style class of bug identified in the stUSDC report: a value/weight distribution is computed atomically from a balance snapshot that a party fully controls the timing of, letting them temporarily inflate their weight right before the snapshot and withdraw immediately after.

### Finding Description
`countVotes(conn, mci, subject, …)` builds `voter_balances` from the addresses that ever cast a `system_vote` for `subject`, using their balance at the moment the vote is tallied (triggered when an `mci` becomes stable), not the balance held at the time each vote was cast: [1](#0-0) 

The vote weight is then simply `SUM(balance)` grouped by chosen value/OP, using this instantaneous balance: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

A `system_vote` unit itself can be posted with any balance (even zero) at cast time, and validation only checks the shape/content of the vote, not the voter's balance or how long they've held it: [4](#0-3) 

`countVotes` is invoked deterministically once per stabilized `mci` for every accumulated subject, right after `system_vote` messages for that mci are persisted: [5](#0-4) 

Because the weight is read at count time and not pinned to the vote's timestamp/mci, an attacker can:
1. Post a `system_vote` unit for `op_list` (or a fee parameter) from address A with any/no balance.
2. Shortly before the main-chain index that will trigger `countVotes` stabilizes, send themselves (or receive from a cooperating/borrowed source) a very large byte balance into address A and let that output become stable+good.
3. Let `countVotes` run, counting A's full current balance as voting weight for their chosen `op_list`/parameter value, potentially dominating or tipping the vote outcome (e.g., choosing malicious/colluding order processors, or manipulating `base_tps_fee`/`tps_interval`/`tps_fee_multiplier`).
4. Immediately move the funds back out (spend them) after the count, since there is no lock, decay, or minimum holding period tied to the balance used in the tally.

This mirrors the report's root cause precisely: yield/voting weight is computed from a point-in-time balance/value snapshot with no drip, timelock, or time-weighting, so whoever can transiently control the largest balance at the exact snapshot moment captures a disproportionate share of the outcome.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` votes determine the set of order processors that provide majority witnessing and stability for the entire DAG; `tps_fee`-family votes determine network-wide fee economics that gate unit acceptance. Because voting weight can be manipulated by a party who only transiently, not durably, holds bytes, an attacker (or colluding set of attackers who can move a large temporary balance through voting addresses) can:
- Force the network to adopt a malicious or colluding `op_list`, undermining consensus over stability/validity and potentially enabling downstream attacks on stability determination.
- Manipulate `base_tps_fee`/`tps_interval`/`tps_fee_multiplier` to values that either freeze normal unit submission (network unable to confirm new units at reasonable cost) or open cheap spam vectors, harming overall network availability.

This satisfies the "node disagreement on validity/stability" and "network unable to confirm new units" impact classes, driven purely by unauthorized manipulation of a protocol-level governance mechanism reachable from ordinary posted units (system_vote messages) — no privileged, malicious-node, or p2p component is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability requires only the ability to (a) post a `system_vote` unit and (b) transiently hold a very large byte balance in the voting address right before the mci that triggers `countVotes` stabilizes, then move it out afterward. Both are ordinary, unprivileged wallet operations available to any user; the difficulty is proportional to how large a balance the attacker can command at the right moment (similar to a flash-loan-style attack), and to correctly timing the transfer relative to main-chain stabilization, which is observable/predictable from the public DAG. No malicious node, hub, or peer collusion is needed, matching the "unprivileged unit poster" reachability requirement.

### Recommendation
Tie voting weight to the balance held continuously since (or before) the vote was cast, rather than the balance at count time — e.g., record and use the balance as of the vote's own mci/timestamp, or require a minimum holding period (time-lock) before a balance change affects vote weight, analogous to the drip/timelock remediation adopted for the stUSDC yield-distribution bug (PR#89/#91 in the referenced report).

### Proof of Concept
1. Address A posts a `system_vote` unit for `subject: "op_list"` (or a numerical subject) with an arbitrarily small/zero balance; the vote is validated and stored via `saveSystemVote` once stable. [6](#0-5) 
2. Shortly before the mci that will next invoke `countVotes` becomes stable, A receives a very large stable-good output (e.g., via a large transfer from another address the attacker controls).
3. `countVotes` runs on stabilization of that mci, computing A's `balance` as the current SUM of unspent stable-good outputs and using it as voting weight for A's already-cast vote. [7](#0-6) 
4. Because A's chosen OP list (or fee parameter) accumulates outsized weight from a balance A never held during the "vote timeframe" it's supposed to represent, the tally in `op_rows`/`rows` skews toward A's choice. [8](#0-7) 
5. A spends/moves the large balance out immediately after the count, retaining no long-term economic stake while having swayed a protocol-critical governance decision.
