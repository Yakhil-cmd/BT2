### Title
Permanent market freeze via `i128` overflow in RAY-value accrual before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The analog of the LibTIFF NULL-pointer dereference (a crash-on-input bug class) in this codebase is an unchecked fixed-point multiplication that panics inside interest accrual. `scaled_to_original` multiplies a scaled `Ray` balance by `borrow_index`/`supply_index` without saturating at the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` index cap. Because every state-changing pool entrypoint accrues first (`Cache::load` → `interest::global_sync`), once `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows `i128` the market permanently reverts: no `repay`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `seize_positions`, or `update_indexes` can ever execute on it again.

### Finding Description
`scaled_to_original` is a thin wrapper over `Ray::mul`, which panics with `MathOverflow` on `i128` overflow (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`). It is called from `unscale_supply`/`unscale_borrow` and from the accrual/allocation path (`calculate_supplier_rewards`, `update_supply_index` in `common/src/rates/index.rs`) that `interest::global_sync` runs on every market mutation. The index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` bounds the index itself, but the overflow happens in the *value* product `borrowed × borrow_index`, which grows ~170× faster than the index alone — the project's own test demonstrates the value ceiling is hit first while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`). An unprivileged user reaches this purely through `controller.supply` and `controller.borrow`: list nothing, call nothing privileged — just open a whale-size position at ~98% utilization on a high-decimal asset and let accrual run.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and a contract path unable to operate. After the cliff, `try_withdraw` and `try_repay` both revert with `MathOverflow` because accrual runs before the mutation (test asserts both revert at lines 355-356). All suppliers' principal and all liquidators' ability to close bad debt in that market are bricked forever — the panic is in shared market state, not per-user, so no caller can route around it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. It requires an 18-decimal market holding on the order of 10²⁸ units at ~98% utilization for multiple accrual years on a steep rate curve; it cannot be triggered quickly. However, it needs no privilege, no oracle manipulation, and no cooperation — one attacker's own supply+borrow pair is sufficient, and any third-party suppliers who joined the market lose their funds alongside. The test confirms the protocol's documented safety bound ("the index cap protects the value product") is false.

### Recommendation
Cap the *value* product, not just the index: inside `update_borrow_index`/accrual, clamp `borrow_index` such that `borrowed * borrow_index <= i128::MAX`, or make `scaled_to_original` use a saturating `mul_div` with an explicit `AccrualSaturated` state that still permits `repay` and `liquidate` (repayment only ever shrinks `borrowed`, so a saturating accrual of zero additional interest is strictly preferable to a permanently bricked market).

Root cause: `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14` `scaled.mul(env, index)` panics on `borrowed * borrow_index > i128::MAX` while `borrow_index` is still under `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
