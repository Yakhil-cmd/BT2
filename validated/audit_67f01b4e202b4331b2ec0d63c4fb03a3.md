### Title
MEV front-run of `socialize-debt` lets a depositor escape a bad-debt write-down and shift the loss onto remaining zToken holders - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar`, applies to all `v0-vault-*.clar` vaults)

### Summary
Every vault (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) exposes `socialize-debt`, which writes down the vault's `lindex` (liquidity index) to spread an unrecoverable-debt loss across all outstanding zToken holders [1](#0-0) . Because this write-down is a normal, mempool-visible transaction (gated only by `check-caller-auth`, i.e. an authorized contract such as the market, not by atomicity with liquidation) [2](#0-1) , any zToken holder can see it pending and front-run it with a `redeem` call, cashing out at the pre-writedown share price and escaping their share of the loss. This is the same class of bug as the Sturdy `distributeYield` finding: an unprivileged actor uses ordering/MEV around a value-redistributing state transition to make other, non-participating pool members bear a cost that should have been shared, i.e. "a socialization charged to all suppliers" that one caller can dodge at another's expense.

### Finding Description
`redeem` prices shares using `convert-to-assets-preview`, which is `total-assets-preview` divided by `total-supply-preview` at the moment of the call [3](#0-2) . `total-assets-preview` computes "interest" purely as `debt - borrowed`, where `debt` is derived from `principal-scaled` and the accrual `index`; it does **not** know that part of that debt is uncollectible [4](#0-3) . Until `socialize-debt` explicitly runs, the vault still reports the defaulted debt as if it were healthy, so any zToken holder redeeming beforehand receives full nominal value.

`socialize-debt` is the only mechanism that reflects the loss: it reduces `principal-scaled`, `total-borrowed`, `assets`, and critically writes the loss into `lindex` proportionally, cutting the redemption value of every outstanding zToken by the same factor [1](#0-0) . Because this is a standalone public transaction with a fixed loss amount (`scaled-amount`/`debt-reduction`) determined *before* the transaction lands, and it is not bundled atomically with the triggering liquidation/default event, it sits in the mempool exactly like Sturdy's `distributeYield` call. An attacker who is a large zToken holder (or acquires zTokens cheaply once bad debt is anticipated) can watch for this transaction and submit `redeem` immediately before it, exiting at the stale, unwritten-down price.

Shared state exploited: `lindex` / `total-assets`, which determines the redemption rate for *every* zToken holder of that vault, not just the caller. Attacker: any address holding (or able to quickly acquire) zTokens who observes the pending `socialize-debt` call. Victim: all zToken holders who do not redeem before the write-down lands - their share of the now-smaller remaining pool must absorb the entire fixed loss amount, since the redeemer(s) who exited early paid nothing toward it.

### Impact Explanation
Without the attacker's front-run, the fixed bad-debt loss is spread across the full pool of zToken holders at write-down time, each losing a proportional (small) amount via the `lindex` cut. With the attacker's front-run, the attacker exits at full pre-writedown value, removing their assets (and their share of `total-supply`) from the base over which the loss is spread; the same absolute loss is then divided among the remaining, smaller pool of holders, increasing the writedown severity/loss borne by each of them. This is a direct transfer of principal loss from the attacker to remaining depositors - not merely unclaimed yield - so it lands on the "theft/permanent loss of user funds at rest" impact class for the remaining suppliers.

### Likelihood Explanation
`socialize-debt` calls are infrequent (tied to defaults/liquidation shortfalls) but are exactly the kind of high-value, predictable event that MEV searchers monitor, mirroring the original Sturdy report's assumption that admin/market calls are visible in the mempool. Any address holding zTokens of the affected vault, or one able to acquire them just-in-time, can execute the front-run with a single `redeem` transaction ordered ahead of the `socialize-debt` transaction (via gas bidding or a searcher relationship), with no special privilege required.

### Recommendation
- Make debt write-down atomic with the event that determines it (e.g., perform the `lindex` cut inside the same liquidation/repayment transaction that discovers the shortfall, rather than as a separate, front-runnable call), or
- Have `total-assets`/`total-assets-preview` account for likely-unrecoverable debt (e.g., via a provisioning/haircut mechanism) continuously, rather than only at the moment `socialize-debt` executes, so price does not have a discrete step that can be gamed by ordering, or
- Submit `socialize-debt` through a private/backrun-protected channel (flashbots-style) and/or add a redemption delay/cooldown so pending redemptions cannot be reordered ahead of a known loss event.

### Proof of Concept
1. The market/DAO detects unrecoverable debt in a vault (e.g., `vault-usdc`) and prepares to call `socialize-debt` with a fixed `scaled-amount` reflecting the loss [5](#0-4) .
2. Attacker observes this pending transaction in the mempool (or infers it is imminent from an on-chain liquidation shortfall event).
3. Attacker submits `redeem` for their full zToken balance, ordered/mined immediately before `socialize-debt`; `redeem` computes `inkind` using `convert-to-assets-preview`, which still reflects the stale, not-yet-written-down price [6](#0-5) .
4. `socialize-debt` then executes, cutting `lindex` proportionally to the loss over the now-reduced `total-supply` (since the attacker's shares were already burned) [7](#0-6) , so remaining holders absorb a larger per-share loss than they would have if the attacker's shares had still been part of the base at write-down time.

(define-public (socialize-debt (scaled-amount uint))
  (let ((scaled-principal (var-get principal-scaled))
        (borrowed (var-get total-borrowed))
        (idx (var-get index))
        (current-assets (var-get assets))
        (current-lindex (var-get lindex))
        (old-total-assets (total-assets))
        (debt-reduction (mul-div-down scaled-amount idx INDEX-PRECISION))
        (principal-reduction (if (> scaled-principal u0)
                                (mul-div-down scaled-amount borrowed scaled-principal)
                                u0))
        ;; Write down lindex proportionally to loss in total-assets
        (new-lindex (if (and (> old-total-assets u0) (> old-total-assets debt-reduction))
                       (mul-div-down current-lindex (- old-total-assets debt-reduction) old-total-assets)
                       u0)))
