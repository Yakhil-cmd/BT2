## Title
Missing sender authorization in the fraud-proof case lets any third party steal the entire payment-channel balance - (`File: test/samples/payment_channels.oscript`)

### Summary
The `payment_channels.oscript` sample Autonomous Agent implements a two-party payment channel between `$addressA` and `$addressB`. Every state-changing case in the AA restricts execution to the two channel parties by checking `$bFromParties` (`trigger.address == $addressA OR trigger.address == $addressB`), **except** the "fraud proof" case, whose `if` condition only checks `trigger.data.fraud_proof AND var['close_initiated_by'] AND trigger.data.sentByPeer` and never verifies that the trigger sender is one of the two channel parties. The payout message then pays out the AA's **entire** base-asset balance to `trigger.address` — i.e., to whoever sent the trigger — rather than to the wronged party.

### Finding Description
Compare the four cases in `test/samples/payment_channels.oscript`: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

each of the first three ("refill", "start closing", "confirm closure") explicitly guard on `$bFromParties` (computed in `init` as `trigger.address == $addressA OR trigger.address == $addressB`) [4](#0-3) .

The fraud-proof case drops this guard entirely: [5](#0-4) 

and its payout message sends the AA's whole base-asset balance ("send all", no `amount` field) to `trigger.address`: [6](#0-5) 

The `sentByPeer` package that satisfies `is_valid_signed_package(trigger.data.sentByPeer, ...)` is a value signed once by one of the two parties and is subsequently published on-chain as `trigger.data` in the "start closing" call (`payment_channels.oscript` lines 31-67), or shared off-chain between the counterparties. Because the fraud-proof branch never checks that `trigger.address` is `$addressA` or `$addressB`, **anyone** who can read/replay this already-signed, already-broadcast package (an on-chain `trigger.data.sentByPeer` value from a previous "start closing" unit, or a copy relayed off-chain) can submit a brand-new trigger unit with `trigger.address` set to their own address and the exact same `trigger.data.fraud_proof=true` / `trigger.data.sentByPeer=...` payload. The AA engine (`aa_composer.js`) validates only the signature inside `sentByPeer` and the `$transferredFromPeerAsClaimedByPeer` comparison — it has no notion of "who is allowed to collect the fraud penalty" beyond what the oscript itself enforces, and here the oscript enforces nothing about the caller. The "send all" payment output mechanism (`{address: '{trigger.address}'}` with no `amount`, resolved by the AA engine's balance bookkeeping in `aa_composer.js`, e.g. the `sendDummyUnit`/balance-deduction logic around lines 947-1005) then transfers the entire AA base balance to that arbitrary caller. [7](#0-6) 

This is structurally identical to the reported D3Proxy issue: a function meant to pay out funds that "belong" to a specific counterparty instead pays out the full pooled balance to whichever address happens to invoke it, because the authorization check on the recipient/caller is missing.

### Impact Explanation
Any unrelated third party who observes a channel's `sentByPeer` signed package (published on-chain during "start closing", or leaked via any off-chain relay) can redirect the entire remaining channel balance to their own address instead of the honest party who should receive the fraud penalty. This is a direct, unauthorized fund-loss vulnerability for AA-held funds — the honest party who was supposed to be compensated for the counterparty's fraud gets nothing, and an unrelated attacker drains the channel.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires only that the attacker obtain a validly-signed `sentByPeer` package proving the counterparty understated the amount spent. Since the very act of initiating channel closure ("start closing") publishes the counterparty's signed package on the DAG as `trigger.data.sentByPeer`, this data is trivially observable by any node/watcher of the AA's trigger units once closing has started, making the attack straightforward to execute by a purely passive, unprivileged observer — no special access or trust relationship with either channel party is required.

### Recommendation
Add the same `$bFromParties` (or a stronger `trigger.address == (the non-cheating party)`) check to the fraud-proof case's `if`/`init` condition, ensuring the payout always goes to the correct channel party rather than to `trigger.address` of whoever submits the trigger. More generally, any AA branch that pays out the AA's pooled/"send all" balance must independently verify that the recipient (`trigger.address`) is authorized to receive those funds, rather than relying solely on the validity of a bystander-replayable signed message.

### Proof of Concept
1. Party A and Party B open and fund a channel via the "refill" case.
2. Party A calls "start closing" including `trigger.data.sentByPeer` (B's signed package understating B's spend). This trigger unit — and therefore B's signed package — is now public on the DAG.
3. Attacker C extracts B's signed `sentByPeer` package from A's public "start closing" unit.
4. Attacker C sends a new trigger unit from their own address `C`, with `trigger.data.fraud_proof=true` and the same `trigger.data.sentByPeer` extracted in step 3.
5. Because the fraud-proof case's `if` condition never checks `trigger.address in {addressA, addressB}`, the AA accepts the trigger, validates B's signature (still valid), and executes the payout message `{app:'payment', payload:{asset:'base', outputs:[{address:'{trigger.address}'}]}}`, sending the entire channel balance to `C` instead of to A.
