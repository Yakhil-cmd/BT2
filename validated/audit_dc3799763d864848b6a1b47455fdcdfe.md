### Title
Sender of an indivisible (fixed-denomination) asset payment can grief a recipient by splitting the payment into many minimal-denomination coins instead of the largest matching one - (File: validation.js / indivisible_asset.js)

### Summary
`ocore` indivisible (fixed-denomination) custom assets are analogous to Party's per-address NFTs: an asset can be defined with multiple denominations and a payer freely chooses which mix of denomination-coins to include in the outputs of a `payment` message, just as `ReraiseETHCrowdfund#claimMultiple` let the caller freely choose `votingPowerByCard`. Validation never enforces that the sender used the minimal number of coins/denominations to reach the target amount for the recipient, so a griefer who controls the payer side (their own wallet, or an AA/asset issuer) can force a victim address to receive many low-denomination coins instead of one high-denomination coin.

### Finding Description
For fixed-denomination ("indivisible") assets, `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` enforces `payload.inputs.length !== 1` per message (one input coin per message) [1](#0-0)  and caps outputs/inputs only by generic anti-spam limits (`MAX_INPUTS_PER_PAYMENT_MESSAGE`, `MAX_OUTPUTS_PER_PAYMENT_MESSAGE`, `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT`) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) , but it does not check that the denomination chosen for a given output is the largest one that satisfies the amount, nor that the number of coin-messages sent to a single recipient is minimal.

The reference (non-malicious) wallet composer, `pickIndivisibleCoinsForAmount` in `indivisible_asset.js`, greedily prefers the largest available denomination (`ORDER BY denomination DESC, ...`) and issues new coins from the largest denomination that fits [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) , and it aborts once it collects too many payloads: `if (arrPayloadsWithProofs.length >= constants.MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT - 1)` [6](#0-5) . This proves that the protocol authors were aware that fragmenting into many small-denomination coins is expensive and default logic avoids it — but this is purely a client-side courtesy, not a validation rule. A hand-crafted unit is free to construct the payment however it wants: as long as each message has 1 valid input (a coin the payer legitimately owns, of any denomination) and 1+ outputs whose amounts are multiples of that denomination, the payment is accepted by consensus rules, regardless of whether a larger, more efficient denomination coin was available to the payer.

Analogous to the C4 report (attacker mints many small NFTs to the victim instead of one large one when the attacker controls the minting transaction on the victim's behalf), here the attacker (as the payer of an indivisible/fixed-denomination custom asset, or as the asset's issuer sending directly to a victim) can:
1. Acquire/issue many minimal-denomination coins of an asset.
2. Send them to the victim address across the maximum allowed number of payment messages per unit (`MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT`, 128) and/or across multiple units, instead of sending one coin of the matching larger denomination.
3. The victim ends up holding a large number of small, fixed-denomination coin-outputs for the same total value that could have been represented by far fewer/larger denomination outputs.

Because fixed-denomination payments are restricted to exactly 1 input per message (`validation.js:2142-2143`), any future consolidation/spend by the victim of that fragmented balance requires one payment message per coin they wish to spend, quickly hitting `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT` (128) and forcing the victim to split their own spend across many separate units, each paying full headers/payload commissions.

### Impact Explanation
This does not break consensus or double-spend directly, but it inflicts a real, unavoidable, and disproportionate cost on the victim: consolidating or spending a fragmented indivisible-asset balance requires many extra units and thus many extra headers/payload commissions (bytes), which are burned/paid to witnesses. For large received amounts split into the smallest denomination, the number of required follow-up units can become large enough to make it economically infeasible (or extremely costly relative to the value received) for the victim to fully liquidate their balance — a fund-freezing-style effect analogous to the original bug's "grief large depositors by forcing many small NFTs, dramatically inflating gas costs." Because the victim cannot opt out (they do not control how the payer structures the payment), this is a Medium severity griefing vector reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who is a payer of a fixed-denomination custom asset (or the asset issuer, if `issued_by_definer_only` allows them to mint many small denominations directly to victims).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (a) a fixed-denomination custom asset with more than one available denomination (`MAX_DENOMINATIONS_PER_ASSET_DEFINITION` allows up to 64 denominations per asset) and (b) the attacker being able to obtain/issue many small-denomination coins, which is straightforward if they are the asset definer/issuer or simply a holder of small-denomination change. No special privilege beyond being a valid payer/issuer of the asset is required, and validation places no obstacle in the way of choosing sub-optimal denominations for outputs.

### Recommendation
Add a validation-layer or convention-layer constraint analogous to the C4 fix recommendation: when constructing/validating an indivisible-asset payment to a third-party recipient, require that the chosen coin(s)/denomination(s) for each output be the minimal number needed to represent the amount (i.e., disallow choosing a smaller denomination when a larger denomination coin owned by the payer could satisfy the output exactly, or cap the number of same-asset payment messages/coins directed at a single new recipient address within a short window). At minimum, document/enforce that wallets and any automated flow (e.g., AAs issuing/transferring fixed-denomination assets) must use `pickIndivisibleCoinsForAmount`'s greedy-largest-denomination selection, and consider adding a protocol-level check that rejects unnecessarily fragmented outputs to a payee who did not consent to receiving them in that form.

### Proof of Concept
1. Define a custom fixed-denomination asset with denominations `[1, 100]` (via an `asset` message, `is_private:false`, `fixed_denominations:true`, with `denominations: [{denomination:1, count_coins:null}, {denomination:100, count_coins:null}]`), permitting non-definer issuance.
2. As the attacker (a valid holder/issuer of this asset), instead of sending the victim one coin of denomination 100 (which fully covers the intended amount of 100), craft a unit containing up to 100 separate `payment` messages, each with 1 input (a denomination-1 coin the attacker owns) and 1 output of amount 1 to the victim's address, i.e. `payload.denomination = 1`, `inputs: [{...}]`, `outputs: [{address: victim, amount: 1}]`, repeated `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT` (128) times per unit (or spread over multiple units for the full 100).
3. `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` accepts every message individually because each has exactly 1 input (`inputs.length === 1`, matching the fixed-denomination rule at `validation.js:2142-2143`) and passes the generic per-message/per-unit limits; nothing forces the attacker to instead use the available/issuable denomination-100 coin.
4. The victim now holds 100 separate denomination-1 coin outputs for the asset instead of a single denomination-100 output. To later spend the full 100 units of value, the victim must construct payment message(s) with 100 separate 1-input messages (again bounded by `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT`), potentially needing multiple units, each incurring full header/payload commissions — a disproportionate cost compared to spending a single denomination-100 coin.
