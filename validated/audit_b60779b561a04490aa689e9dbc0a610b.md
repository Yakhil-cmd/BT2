### Title
Attacker-bricked collateral leg via Aquarius LP liquidity floor makes an account unliquidatable and blocks bad-debt cleanup - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs))

### Summary
The Aquarius LP price source fails closed with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever the underlying pool's total value falls below the configured `min_pool_value_wad` floor [1](#0-0) . Liquidation computes the account's collateral by strictly pricing **every** supply position in the book [2](#0-1) , and seizure planning likewise calls `cache.cached_price` for each leg [3](#0-2) . Because `supply` requires no price, an unprivileged borrower can plant a dust leg of an LP-token collateral, drain the shallow Aquarius pool below the floor with their own liquidity withdrawal, and become unliquidatable — while `clean_bad_debt` reverts on the same price error, so forced socialization is also blocked [4](#0-3) .

### Finding Description
The analog of CVE-2017-7652 is "an attacker-induced exhaustion of an external resource makes a routine operation fail closed." In Mosquitto, fd exhaustion makes the reload fail; here, an attacker exhausts the Aquarius pool's value below `min_pool_value_wad`, which makes the LP leg's price read fail. Since risk totals, seizure planning, and bad-debt cleanup all iterate the full supply-position map and panic on any leg's price error, one dust-priced leg poisons the entire account: `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize`-adjacent valuation all revert. The attacker path is fully unprivileged: `supply` a dust amount of the LP collateral (supply needs no price [5](#0-4) ), `borrow` against the account's other collateral, then withdraw own liquidity from the underlying Aquarius pool (own trades on Aquarius are in-scope) to push `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad`.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency / effective theft of borrowed funds. While the floor breach persists, no liquidator can close the position (every plan prices all collateral legs), and `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize the loss, so the account's debt accrues unbacked. The attacker retains the borrowed principal; suppliers absorb the eventual shortfall through the supply-index write-down once the price recovers — or the freeze is indefinite if the attacker keeps the pool drained, since they control the LP position. The protocol's own alert documents that accounts holding such a leg "become unliquidatable" below the floor [6](#0-5) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a listed collateral whose oracle is `PriceSource::AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp` over a shallow pool, and an attacker who holds or acquires enough LP shares to push pool value under the floor. The attacker's cost is bounded by the pool depth near the floor (they only need to cross it, and they retain the withdrawn underlying). The harness already proves the mechanism end-to-end: a dust leg whose feed fails makes `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` both revert while leaving the debt intact [7](#0-6) . A self-interested LP holder can perform this atomically inside the same window, so no oracle dishonesty or privileged action is needed.

### Recommendation
- Make liquidation pricing tolerant of a failed collateral leg whose value contribution is provably de-minimis (e.g., seize nothing from an unpriceable leg and treat it as zero-value collateral), or allow a `SeizeMode` that skips legs whose price read fails.
- Let `clean_bad_debt` bypass strict per-leg pricing for supply legs it cannot value, writing them down via the supply index instead of reverting.
- Set `min_pool_value_wad` floors conservatively relative to observed pool depth so that crossing the floor requires withdrawing a large fraction of pool TVL, and prefer collateral listings backed by deep pools.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed `LP(A/B)` as collateral on spoke `S` with an `AquariusLp` source and `min_pool_value_wad = F`; the pool currently sits modestly above `F`.
2. Attacker calls `controller.supply(caller, account_id, S, [(hub, LP(A/B), dust)])` — succeeds without any price read.
3. Attacker calls `controller.borrow` drawing maximum debt against their other collateral on the same account.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw` on the Aquarius pool for their own LP position, pushing pool value below `F`. Subsequent `aquarius::read` returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` [8](#0-7) .
5. Any `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, ...)` reverts: `calculate_ltv_collateral_wad` / seizure planning hits `cached_price` on the LP leg and panics [9](#0-8) .
6. `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` reverts for the same reason, so even forced socialization cannot proceed while the pool stays under the floor [10](#0-9) .
