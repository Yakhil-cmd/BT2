### Title
Scaled-position unscale during accrual overflows `i128` before the index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs, contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2015-9100 (a crafted input driving the program into a fatal crash/DoS) is the RAY-value ceiling in share unscale math: `scaled_to_original` multiplies `scaled_amount * borrow_index` and panics with `MathOverflow` once the product exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every pool verb accrues interest first, a whale can push a market to sustained ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve, let the borrow index compound for a few years, and permanently brick the market — the protocol's own `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never gets a chance to clamp the index, so no repay, withdraw, or liquidation ever succeeds again.

### Finding Description
Accrual and every operation compute totals via `Cache::calculate_utilization` in `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26`, which calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` — a `mul_div` over `i128`/`I256` that panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled * index` exceeds the representable domain (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The bounds `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (1e36) are applied to the *index*, but the value ceiling `scaled_shares × index` is roughly `i128::MAX / RAY` ≈ 170× the supplied amount; at high utilization the index compounds past ~170x while still far below 1e36, so the panic fires before any cap. The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`) confirms: after a few years at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the comment states the index cap never engages — "the market freezes: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation."

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and market-level insolvency. Every supplier's deposit, every borrower's collateral route, and every liquidation path through this market reverts on the same panic because accrual runs first in every verb. Funds in the pool are unrecoverable; `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` also accrue or read utilization, so no recovery path exists. This matches the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion — it is not a fail-closed guard but an arithmetic trap that silently bricked state.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. A single unprivileged address reaches it with two ordinary calls: `supply` a large amount of an 18-decimal asset and `borrow` ~98% of it (the test uses 1e9 × 10^18 base units — large but within lifted caps, and a whale or a market with legitimately large TVL hits the same shape). The trigger then requires only time: years of untouched high utilization on a steep curve. No governance action, leaked key, or oracle manipulation is needed; the attacker can even be a passive borrower while normal time accrual does the rest.

### Recommendation
Apply the index caps (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`) *before* any unscale that can overflow, and/or make `calculate_utilization` use a saturating mul-div (`mul_div_floor_saturating` already exists in `fp_core.rs`) so accrual clamps instead of panicking. Additionally, add an early-exit in accrual that clamps the index to the cap when the compounded value would exceed the RAY-value ceiling, keeping exits (repay/withdraw/liquidate) always reachable.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`: supply `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal asset as BOB, borrow 98% of it as ALICE against COL collateral, advance time in yearly steps calling `update_indexes`; after a few years all calls (`try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, `try_repay`) revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, proving the cap never engages before the unscale panic.
