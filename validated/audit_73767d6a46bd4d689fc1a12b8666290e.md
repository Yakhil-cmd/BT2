### Title
Stale or skewed anchor leg blends into the midpoint price, letting a borrower over-borrow against inflated collateral - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
The external report describes value extraction enabled by a slow-reacting single oracle: the protocol's accepted price lags the market, and a user monetizes the gap. XOXNO Lending's price aggregator has a structural analog: a configured two-source oracle accepts the integer midpoint of the two legs as long as they sit inside the tolerance band and each leg is within its own `max_stale_seconds`. A leg that is stale-but-in-budget (up to 86,400 s in tested configurations) or that moves within the tolerance band drags the accepted midpoint away from the true market price by up to roughly half the tolerance width. An unprivileged borrower calls `Controller::borrow` and the risk gate values collateral and debt at this midpoint, so the skew is directly convertible into over-borrowing and bad debt.

### Finding Description
`engine.rs` resolves each source leg into a `Reading { price_wad, timestamp, stale, nature }` and, when both legs are usable, blends them at their midpoint (documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-ORACLE-02: "The accepted price is their integer midpoint, rounded down"). Staleness is only rejected when a leg exceeds its configured `max_stale_seconds` or `max_price_stale_seconds`, or when two Market legs differ in age by more than `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS`. A leg that is up to ~23 hours old on a Fundamental-nature feed is still accepted.

Crucially, the tolerance check only compares the two legs to each other; it does not bound the midpoint against reality. If one leg is frozen at a stale high price while the other tracks the market lower (up to the tolerance boundary), the accepted midpoint lands halfway between them — up to ~half the band width above the fresh leg's price.

`Context`-cached prices from `prices()` feed `calculate_account_risk_totals` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:175-197`, which computes `ltv_collateral` and `health_factor`, and `require_post_pool_risk_gates` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:29-53` admits the borrow whenever `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` and `health_factor >= 1e18` at the skewed price.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker supplies a volatile collateral asset whose oracle has one lagging leg, waits for (or benefits from) a real price decline that stays within tolerance of the stale leg, then borrows up to the inflated LTV-weighted collateral value. The borrowed asset can be withdrawn/sold; once the stale leg refreshes to the true price, the account's real health factor is below 1 and the excess debt becomes bad debt that suppliers absorb via the supply-index write-down in `clean_bad_debt`. This is a leak of protocol value mirroring the UDT arbitrage: the protocol's internal price lags external reality and the lag is monetizable through a permissionless `borrow` call.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged access is needed: the attacker only supplies collateral and calls `borrow`. The precondition is a leg that is old within its `max_stale_seconds` budget while the partner leg moves — realistic for push-based feeds (e.g., the documented 24h-heartbeat RedStone leg with an ~26h staleness budget) during volatile markets, or a leg the attacker can influence (own Aquarius trades) within the band. The skew is bounded to roughly half the tolerance (up to ~12.5% upper ratio, so up to ~10%+ midpoint skew per `BadLastTolerance` bounds of 10,150–12,500 bps), which caps but does not eliminate per-transaction extraction; repeated cycles across markets compound it. Medium severity: assets are not at direct instant risk, but a hypothetical attack path leaks value under stated external assumptions.

### Recommendation
- Prefer the safer of the two legs for collateral valuation rather than the midpoint (e.g., use the lower leg for collateral, higher for debt), or weight toward the fresher leg.
- Tighten the effective lag: cap `max_stale_seconds` for legs paired with Market-nature feeds regardless of declared nature, or reject readings whose timestamp spread vs the partner leg hides a directional move.
- Optionally enforce a deviation bound between the accepted midpoint and the last accepted price to limit per-update drift exploitable within tolerance.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains a harness demonstrating exactly this path in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`:

1. XLM oracle is configured with two sources: a Reflector primary (fresh) and a RedStone anchor with `max_stale_seconds = 86_400` and 1000 bps tolerance.
2. The anchor's `set_price_data` is stamped `ANCHOR_LAG_SECONDS = 82_800` s old at the frozen price `usd(1)` while the fresh leg reports `usd_cents(91)` — a 9.9% divergence inside the band.
3. ALICE supplies 100,000 XLM and calls `borrow` for 70,000 USDC.
4. Result: collateral is valued >4% higher than the honest fresh-anchor control (`inflation > 1.04`), the skewed `borrow` succeeds, and the identical borrow with a fresh anchor reverts with `InsufficientCollateral` — proving the stale-leg midpoint alone creates the excess borrowing power that converts to bad debt when prices converge. ### INV-ORACLE-02 — A dual source requires both legs

A configured two-source price requires both usable legs. The accepted price
is their integer midpoint, rounded down, within both the input range and the
final sanity band. One surviving leg cannot serve as a fallback.

A partial reading is unusable; a stale surviving leg reports staleness before
disagreement. Separately admitted single-source configurations retain freshness,
positivity and sanity checks.
