### Title
Unscaled debt value overflows i128 before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2017-14865 is a heap buffer overflow reachable by a crafted input that crashes the process (DoS). The XOXNO Lending analog is an unchecked `i128` overflow inside the accrual path: `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies scaled debt (`borrowed`, a `Ray` at 27 decimals) by the new borrow index with `Ray::mul`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every state-changing pool verb accrues the market first, once this product overflows, the market is permanently frozen — no withdraw, repay, or liquidation can execute.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` calls `calculate_supplier_rewards`, which computes `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`). `Ray::mul` is the panicking `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`), not a saturating variant. The index cap `update_borrow_index` clamps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), but the value product `scaled_debt * index` can overflow `i128` well before the index reaches that cap whenever `borrowed` is large.

The repository's own harness proves the end state: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/liquidation_accrual_timing.rs` is unaffected, but `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356` demonstrates that at ~98% utilization on the XLM curve with a large book, `try_update_indexes_for` starts failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequently `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` also revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — every verb accrues first via `interest::global_sync`/`simulate_update_indexes` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:51-94`), so the panic propagates to every entrypoint (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, flash paths).

An unprivileged attacker seeds the condition with only `supply` and `borrow`: push a market to extreme scaled-debt size (large `scaled_amount` on an 18-decimal asset) at high utilization, then simply let the permissionless `update_indexes` compound. No privilege, price manipulation, or route abuse is needed — only ledger time and ordinary borrower/lender calls.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every participant in that market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay (locking their collateral), and liquidations cannot execute, so collateral backing the debt becomes unreachable. The failure mode matches the report's class — a value that overflows the destination representation and aborts processing — with impact escalated from a per-call DoS to a permanent market freeze.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a very large scaled debt book at sustained high utilization; on a steep rate curve the product crosses `i128::MAX` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is reached, which the in-repo test confirms is reachable. This needs whale-scale capital and accrual time the attacker cannot accelerate, keeping it at Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
In `calculate_supplier_rewards` (and `supply_index_reward_shortfall`/`update_supply_index`, which also multiply `supplied`/`borrowed` by indexes), use `try_mul`/`mul_div_floor_saturating` or an explicit `I256` path instead of the panicking `Ray::mul`, and treat index-cap engagement as the bound on growth so accrual never panics. Alternatively, cap `scaled_amount` at listing/`require_cap_within_asset_domain` time to a value where `scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cannot overflow `i128`.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`: supply `10^9` of an 18-decimal asset, borrow ~98% of it, advance ledger time until `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, then observe `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` fail identically — the market is frozen while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
