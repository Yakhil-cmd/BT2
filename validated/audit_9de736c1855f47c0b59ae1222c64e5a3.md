Meanwhile, `headers_commission.js:calcHeadersCommissions` (`headers_commission.js:12-247`) distributes header commissions automatically at stabilization time to whichever address authored the "winning" child unit of a parent — determined purely by a deterministic hash-based algorithm over `unit_authors`, independent of any trigger or AA logic. ### Title
AA-authored units can passively and automatically accrue headers-commission bytes that are permanently invisible and unspendable by the AA - (File: `aa_composer.js`)

### Summary
Analogous to the wibBTC/Curve issue — where a token's balance grows automatically outside of the pool's own explicit balance-tracking mechanism, causing the pool's internal accounting to permanently diverge from the token's real balance and become unspendable/lost from the pool's perspective — an Autonomous Agent (AA) address in ocore can passively earn headers-commission bytes through the network's own automatic commission-distribution algorithm, completely outside of any AA trigger, oscript execution, or the AA's own balance bookkeeping (`aa_balances` table / `balance[]` builtin). Because the AA composer never queries or spends from `headers_commission_outputs`, and an AA cannot construct a `type: "headers_commission"` input via oscript, these earned bytes become permanently stranded, unspendable, and untracked by the AA logic.

### Finding Description
`headers_commission.calcHeadersCommissions` (`headers_commission.js:12-247`) runs automatically for every stabilized MC unit, independent of AA triggers, AA execution, or any oscript logic. It looks at `unit_authors` of "child" units on the DAG and deterministically picks a "winning" child unit per parent (`headers_commission.js:144-152`, `getWinnerInfo` at `headers_commission.js:249-259`), crediting the headers commission of the parent to the address(es) that authored the winning child (`headers_commission.js:222-227`, writing into `headers_commission_outputs`).

AA response units are posted with the AA's own address as the sole author (`aa_composer.js:952`, `authors: [{address: address}]`). Consequently, whenever an AA-authored response unit happens to be selected as the hash-winning "best child" of some parent unit in the DAG (a purely probabilistic, unit-topology-driven event that the AA has no control over and that requires no trigger to the AA), the AA address is credited bytes in `headers_commission_outputs` automatically, exactly as wibBTC's `pricePerShare` grows automatically without any action by the Curve pool.

However:
- The AA's balance bookkeeping (`aa_balances` table, used by `balance[]`/`trigger.output[]` in oscript, see `evaluation.js:1510-1528` and `updateInitialAABalances` in `aa_composer.js:475-541`) is populated only from the `outputs` table, never from `headers_commission_outputs`.
- `completePaymentPayload`'s input-selection logic (`aa_composer.js:1140-1173`, `readStableOutputs`/`readUnstableOutputsSentByAAs`) only ever selects from the `outputs` table.
- Claiming a `headers_commission`-type input requires constructing a unit with an explicit input of `type: "headers_commission"` (see `inputs.js:173-176`, `validation.js:2541-2600`), which is only ever done by wallet logic in response to a user request — AAs have no oscript primitive to request this, and no external party can post a unit on behalf of the AA (AAs are single-authored by their own address per protocol design, and cannot delegate signing to attach such an input into an AA-composed unit).

Thus the bytes are earned by the AA address at the protocol level but are structurally unreachable by both the AA's own logic and by any outside actor, since only the AA itself (which cannot generate such an input) is the "author" who could claim it.

### Impact Explanation
This causes a silent, permanent loss/freezing of AA funds: real bytes credited to the AA address (visible on-chain in `headers_commission_outputs`) are never reflected in `balance[base]` seen by oscript, are never included in the AA's spendable input pool, and can never be moved out by the AA or reclaimed by users. Over the life of a busy/long-running AA (especially high-throughput AAs that post many response units, increasing the statistical chance of "winning" headers commissions), this represents an unbounded, uncapped, and unrecoverable drain of real byte value that should have belonged to the AA (and, transitively, to its state/users) — a fund-loss impact directly analogous to the wibBTC/Curve pool losing track of the yield-bearing token's growing balance.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no attacker action, malicious peer, or protocol violation — it occurs naturally as a side effect of an AA authoring more than one response unit that becomes eligible in the deterministic headers-commission "best child" selection performed at every MC stabilization. Given ocore's headers-commission algorithm runs unconditionally on every stabilized unit's DAG structure, and given that popular/heavily used AAs generate large numbers of response (child) units, this is a naturally-occurring and repeatable class of silent, protocol-level fund loss rather than a contrived edge case.

### Recommendation
Either (a) exclude AA addresses from headers-commission distribution eligibility entirely (since AAs cannot claim such earnings), redirecting the commission to another eligible recipient, or (b) extend `aa_composer.js`'s balance tracking (`updateInitialAABalances`/`aa_balances`) and its payment-input selection (`completePaymentPayload`) to recognize and be able to consume unclaimed `headers_commission_outputs` credited to AA addresses, effectively synthesizing the missing "sync" mechanism (analogous to Uniswap v2's `sync()`) between the AA's oscript-visible balance and its true on-chain balance.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy an AA that posts frequent secondary/response units (e.g. a bouncer/relay AA pattern as seen in `test/aa_composer.test.js:459-471`), so its address regularly appears as `unit_authors` of many child units in the DAG.
2. Over enough MC stabilizations, the network's `calcHeadersCommissions` (`headers_commission.js:12-247`) will, by its hash-based winner selection, occasionally select one of the AA's response units as the "best child" of some parent unit, crediting headers-commission bytes to the AA's address into `headers_commission_outputs`.
3. Query `balance[base]` from within the AA (or inspect `aa_balances` via `storage.readAABalances`, `storage.js:984-994`) — it will not reflect these credited bytes.
4. Attempt to have the AA send out its full byte balance via a `send-all` payment message — `completePaymentPayload` (`aa_composer.js:1140-1173`) will only draw from the `outputs` table, never touching `headers_commission_outputs`, leaving the earned commission bytes permanently stuck at the AA's address, unspendable by the AA and unclaimable by anyone else (since only the AA itself is the address's controller, and it lacks the ability to construct a `headers_commission`-type input).
