### Title
Share/payout calculations in AA `balance[]` reads use live raw balance instead of a locked accounting state, enabling a WithdrawProxy-style front-run/donation dilution attack on ocore AAs - (File: `formula/evaluation.js`, `aa_composer.js`, and the reference AA templates `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, `test/samples/51_attack_game.oscript`, `test/samples/fundraising_proxy.oscript`)

### Summary
The `balance[asset]` primitive available to every Autonomous Agent (AA) always returns the AA's *live, currently-known* balance (its already-credited balance plus whatever the current trigger is delivering), not a value that is locked or "finalized" at the moment a payout/redemption formula is evaluated for a given depositor. Any AA that (like the reference templates shipped with ocore itself) computes a user's payout as `share = trigger.output[[asset=X]] / balance[...]` is exposed to exactly the class of bug described in the WithdrawProxy report: a party can post a payment to the AA immediately before another party's redemption/closing trigger is ordered, changing `balance[]` at redemption time and skewing the payout the victim actually receives, at the benefit (or detriment) of other holders — without any additional "locked/open" state flag guarding the moment redemption is safe.

### Finding Description
`balance[asset]` (and `var[addr][asset]`) is evaluated in `formula/evaluation.js` via `readBalance()`, which returns `objValidationState.assocBalances[param_address][bal_asset]` — the AA's currently accounted balance in `aa_balances`, updated for the *current* trigger by `updateInitialAABalances()` in `aa_composer.js` (aa_composer.js:475-541). This value is a running total, exactly analogous to WithdrawProxy's `totalAssets()` reading `ERC20(asset()).balanceOf(address(this))` directly rather than consulting a separately tracked, gated variable. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Because any address can trigger the AA at any time by sending a payment (or minimal dust) to it, an attacker can insert a payment between two triggers issued by legitimate participants, changing the `balance[]` value read by the second trigger's payout formula. The official reference templates that ship with ocore demonstrate exactly this vulnerable pattern:

- `51_attack_game.oscript`: on `finish`, `var['total'] = balance[base]`, and later payouts are computed as `$share = $asset_amount / var['team_...amount']; $amount = round(...$share... * var['total'])` — the total distributed pot is fixed to whatever `balance[base]` happens to be exactly when the `finish` trigger executes, so anyone able to get a payment processed just before/after `finish` skews the pot every subsequent winner receives from. [3](#0-2) 

- `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`: share issuance on "invest" and payout on "divest" are both computed directly from `balance[$asset]`/`balance[base]` at the moment of the triggering unit, with `mm_asset_outstanding` tracked as an internal counter but the actual token amounts distributed still depend on the *live* balance rather than a value fixed/locked for that operation. [4](#0-3) 

- `fundraising_proxy.oscript`: the "done" threshold and forwarded amount are computed from `balance[$destination_aa][base] + $total_raised`, a live read of another AA's balance that can move between the time a contributor's trigger is composed/broadcast and the time it is actually processed. [5](#0-4) 

This mirrors the root cause in the external report precisely: relying on an instantaneously-read balance instead of an explicitly gated/locked "safe to distribute" state variable, so participants can be paid out of a pot that does not yet (or no longer) reflects the funds/shares they are actually entitled to.

### Impact Explanation
Any oscript AA following the pattern demonstrated by ocore's own bundled sample templates for shares/pooled-fund distribution is exposed to fund-loss/griefing: a malicious participant can post a small payment to the AA timed to land between a victim's contribution and a payout-determining trigger (e.g., `finish`, `divest`, "contribute-to-goal"), causing the payout formula to compute a smaller or larger share than intended. This results in concrete AA fund loss for legitimate participants and an outsized, unearned benefit to other AA-fund holders — matching the "AA fund loss or freezing" impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering an AA is permissionless — anyone can send any amount of the base asset or a custom asset to any AA address at any time, and there is no cooldown, ordering guarantee, or "locked" phase preventing a third party's payment from landing between two other participants' triggers. Because the sample templates that document the recommended usage of `balance[]` for shares/payouts do not include any additional accounting variable to insulate a specific operation from concurrent external payments, any AA author copying this common, officially-demonstrated pattern is likely to inherit the vulnerability, making exploitation straightforward for an unprivileged unit poster who merely needs to observe pending triggers and time a payment.

### Recommendation
Encourage (and document) an accounting pattern where AAs track committed/allocated amounts in explicit state variables (e.g., `var['total_raised']`, `var['locked_pot']`) that are updated deterministically by each trigger's own logic, rather than basing payout math directly on `balance[asset]`, which reflects funds outside the AA's control at the time of read. Where a "final pot"/threshold concept is needed (as in `51_attack_game.oscript`'s `finish` case or `fundraising_proxy.oscript`'s goal-reached case), the amount should be locked into a state variable at the moment the condition is met and never re-derived from a live `balance[]` read afterward, and additional post-goal contributions/payments should be explicitly rejected or routed separately rather than being silently absorbed into the distributable pot.

### Proof of Concept
1. Users A and B contribute to a team in `51_attack_game.oscript`; team’s cumulative `var['team_X_amount']` crosses 51% of `balance[base]`, setting `var['winner']` and starting the challenge period. [6](#0-5) 
2. A malicious actor (not part of the winning team, e.g. sending a payment that doesn't match any `if` filter cleanly, or a losing-team contributor before `finish`) posts a unit sending extra bytes to the AA, timed to land right before someone calls `finish`.
3. `finish` executes: `var['total'] = balance[base]`, now including the attacker’s dust/payment, which the attacker did not intend to donate to the winning pot but which is now irreversibly distributed among winning-team shareholders. [7](#0-6) 
4. Conversely, if a contributor’s output is instead processed as part of a losing team's activity (or their payout unit lands before another expected top-up), the winner payout math (`$amount = round(($share*(1-$founder_tax)+...)*var['total'])`) can pay out disproportionate shares relative to what participants actually intended to have contributed to the pot, since `var['total']` was fixed to whatever `balance[base]` happened to be at `finish` time rather than to a value participants explicitly agreed on.
