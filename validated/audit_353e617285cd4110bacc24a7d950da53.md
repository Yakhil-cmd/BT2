### Title
Unpriced dust collateral can indefinitely block liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs)

### Summary
An indebted account can add a very small collateral position whose oracle is stale or whose Aquarius LP source is below `min_pool_value_wad`. Supply does not require the asset to be priceable, but liquidation, withdrawal while debt remains, and bad-debt cleanup price every supply and borrow position in the account. One unavailable price therefore reverts the entire risk calculation and shields the debt from liquidation and socialization.

### Finding Description
`supply` credits a listed asset after reading its spoke configuration and transferring tokens; it never calls `Context::load_markets` or otherwise proves that the newly supplied asset has a usable price. [1](#0-0) 

By contrast, `calculate_account_risk_totals` loads every supply and borrow key before computing collateral and debt. [2](#0-1)  `Context::load_markets` fetches prices for all unique token addresses, and `cached_price` panics when the strict aggregator result is absent. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`liquidate` calls this all-position valuation before even checking `health_factor < 1`. [5](#0-4)  `clean_bad_debt` reaches the same valuation before applying the insolvency and dust gates. [6](#0-5) 

For an Aquarius LP collateral, the LP provider deterministically rejects the feed when total pool value falls below `lp.min_pool_value_wad`. [7](#0-6)  The strict resolver converts a stale, unavailable, unsafe, or sanity-failing outcome into a contract error. [8](#0-7) 

The repository already contains a regression-style PoC: the borrower supplies `0.001` WBTC after making WBTC stale; subsequent `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and even a partial WBTC `withdraw` all revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE` until the feed becomes fresh. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
A borrower can place otherwise liquidatable debt behind an unpriceable dust leg. Permissionless `liquidate` cannot execute, `clean_bad_debt` cannot socialize residual debt, and the borrower cannot remove the poisoned collateral while debt remains because withdrawal still runs the post-pool solvency valuation. If the borrower keeps the selected oracle unavailable—for example, by keeping an Aquarius pool they control below the configured minimum value—the account remains outside normal liquidation and cleanup while interest continues accruing. This can convert a recoverable unhealthy position into protocol bad debt, satisfying the Medium/High impact category of temporary freezing and potential insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires the borrower to control the account and supply a listed collateral whose price path can fail. Supply is permissionless for the account owner and does not price the new leg. For Aquarius LP collateral, an LP holder controlling enough shares can reduce the pool's measured total value below `min_pool_value_wad`; alternatively, an already stale single-source collateral can be selected without relying on oracle dishonesty. The exploit requires an unhealthy account and a listed fragile collateral, but no privileged role or victim interaction.

### Recommendation
Do not make global account risk depend on collateral that was admitted without a strict price read. At minimum, require a strict price check when opening a new supply position, or isolate unavailable collateral from risk and liquidation planning. A stronger fix is to let liquidation and cleanup skip zero-value/unpriceable dust collateral while explicitly treating it as unseizable and refusing account cleanup until the leg is removed or priced. Any skip rule must avoid letting a materially valuable unpriceable collateral mask insolvency.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create an account with `supply(caller = borrower, account_id = 0, spoke_id = S, assets = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: USDC }, 10_000_000_000)])`.
2. Borrow with `borrow(caller = borrower, account_id = id, borrows = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: ETH }, amount)], to = None)` such that the position becomes liquidatable after the expected price move.
3. Supply a dust amount of collateral `X` with `supply(caller = borrower, account_id = id, spoke_id = S, assets = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: X }, dust_amount)])`. Choose `X` whose strict feed is stale, or an Aquarius LP token whose referenced pool is below `min_pool_value_wad`.
4. Move USDC/ETH prices until the account's health factor would be below 1.
5. Call `liquidate(liquidator = anyone, account_id = id, debt_payments = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: ETH }, repay_amount)], seize_mode = SeizeMode::Transfer)`. The call reverts while fetching prices for all positions, before liquidation can proceed.
6. Call `clean_bad_debt(caller = anyone, account_id = id)`. It reverts in the same all-position valuation before the bad-debt gate.
7. Attempt `withdraw(caller = borrower, account_id = id, withdrawals = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id: H, asset: X }, 1)], to = None)`. With debt still present, the post-pool solvency check prices the same unavailable leg and reverts, preventing removal of the poisoned collateral.
