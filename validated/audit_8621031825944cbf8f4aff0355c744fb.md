### Title
Interest-index desynchronization lets a low-cost caller permanently freeze supplier yield while debt still accrues - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` and other `v0-vault-*.clar` files)

### Summary
`accrue()` derives `next-index` (debt index) and `next-liquidity-index` (supplier index) from the same `time-delta`, but rounds them in opposite directions and re-anchors `last-update` whenever *either* index changes. When utilization/reserve-factor combine such that the double-BPS-rounded `liquidity-rate` truncates to `0`, the supplier index (`lindex`) freezes for that period, yet the debt index (`idx`) still advances (its rounding is round-up), which resets `last-update`. The elapsed window's supplier yield is permanently lost while borrower debt keeps compounding correctly — a socialized loss to all zToken holders that any low-cost caller can trigger repeatedly by forcing `accrue()` every block.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` computes both indexes from a shared `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update`, but resets `last-update` based on an `or` of the two index deltas: [1](#0-0) 

`next-index` (debt) rounds its rate*time multiplier **up** via `calc-multiplier-delta rate time-delta true`, while `next-liquidity-index` (supplier) rounds **down** via `calc-multiplier-delta liquidity-rate time-delta false`: [2](#0-1) 

`liquidity-rate` itself is derived by two chained `mul-bps-down` truncations of the borrow rate: [3](#0-2) 

Because `mul-bps-down` (`(/ (* x y) BPS)`) truncates twice — once by utilization, once by `(1 - reserve-factor)` — a small but real borrow rate at low utilization (or high reserve factor) can truncate `liquidity-rate` to exactly `0`, while the underlying `borrow-rate` used for `next-index` is nonzero and rounds *up*, guaranteeing `idx` changes on essentially every call. Since `idx` changed, the `(or ...)` condition fires and `last-update` is reset to `stacks-block-time` even though `lindex` did not move — this is structurally identical to the reported bug where `lastBalance` (here, `last-update`, the anchor for the next accrual window) advances without the corresponding index advancing, permanently discarding the reward/interest owed for that window because the next computation starts its `time-delta` from zero.

The debt side is unaffected: the treasury's cut is minted directly from `debt-delta` (computed off `idx`), so the protocol/treasury share is captured in full regardless of whether `lindex` moves: [4](#0-3) 

Attacker: any unprivileged address (borrower or depositor) who repeatedly triggers `deposit`/`redeem`/`system-borrow`/`system-repay`/`accrue` in consecutive blocks at low utilization, at negligible cost, keeping `time-delta` small each call so that `idx` keeps ticking up (round-up favors nonzero) while `lindex` keeps truncating to zero (round-down favors zero) and `last-update` keeps resetting. Victim: all current zToken holders (suppliers), whose entitlement to interest is computed off `lindex` via `convert-to-assets`/`total-assets-preview`. Shared state harmed: the vault-wide `lindex` and `last-update` variables, which are primed/reset by one caller's transaction and consumed by every other supplier's redemption value.

### Impact Explanation
This is a socialized loss of unclaimed yield: suppliers permanently lose interest that borrowers effectively continue to pay into `idx`/debt growth (and that the treasury still receives its reserve-factor cut of), but which never reaches `lindex` because the window's `liquidity-rate` rounded to zero and its `time-delta` was discarded by the `last-update` reset. This matches the accepted impact class of "theft/freezing of unclaimed yield," and it disadvantages every existing supplier of the affected vault, not just the caller — the caller does not need to hold any position to trigger the loss.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is non-trivial but conditional: it requires low utilization combined with a non-negligible reserve factor (both are common, realistic parameter regions), and requires a caller willing to submit cheap transactions every block/short interval (deposits/redeems/borrows/repays of trivial size, or simply calling `accrue()` — if that function is public/no-op-safe, as suggested by the "PAUSED: Pass-through" branch, it can be called by anyone freely). No privileged access or governance compromise is required.

### Recommendation
Decouple the `last-update` anchor per index, or compute a single incremental "delta" for `time-delta` consumption and only advance `last-update` for the index-time-window that actually produced a nonzero increment; alternatively, only reset `last-update` when *both* `idx` and `lindex` have advanced, and separately track any residual un-accrued liquidity-rate-time product (analogous to the LoopFi fix pattern of tying `lastBalance`/anchor advancement strictly to the amount by which the index actually advanced) so that fractional/truncated windows are carried forward instead of discarded.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure/observe a vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) at low utilization and a reserve factor such that `mul-bps-down(mul-bps-down(borrow-rate, utilization), 10000 - reserve-factor)` truncates to `0` (e.g., `borrow-rate = 100`, `utilization = 100`, `reserve-factor = 5000` bps ⇒ `util-applied = floor(100*100/10000) = 1`, `liquidity-rate = floor(1*5000/10000) = 0`).
2. Attacker repeatedly calls any public entrypoint that triggers `accrue()` (e.g., tiny `deposit`/`redeem`) every block.
3. Each call: `next-index` rounds its multiplier up ⇒ `idx` changes ⇒ `last-update` is reset to `stacks-block-time`; `next-liquidity-index` computes multiplier `= INDEX-PRECISION` exactly (since `liquidity-rate = 0`) ⇒ `lindex` unchanged.
4. Over N such calls/blocks, `idx` (and hence the treasury's minted share and borrower debt) has advanced N times, but `lindex` — and thus supplier redemption value via `convert-to-assets` — has not moved at all, permanently losing the yield suppliers were owed for those windows.
