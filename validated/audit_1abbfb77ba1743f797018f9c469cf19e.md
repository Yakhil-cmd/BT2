### Title
Improper Authorization in `arbiter_contract_update` allows a cosigner or counterparty to modify sensitive contract fields (`cosigners`, `resolution_unit`, `peer_pairing_code`, contact info) without state/role validation - ([File: wallet.js])

### Summary
The device-message handler for `arbiter_contract_update` in [1](#0-0)  only checks whether the sender is *some* party to the contract (the peer, a cosigner, or, for status only, the arbstore), but does not validate the *value* or contract-state preconditions for most of the writable fields. Only three fields (`status`, `unit`, `shared_address`) get explicit state-machine and content validation; every other field accepted by `arbiter_contract.setField()` — `my_contact_info`, `peer_contact_info`, `peer_pairing_code`, `resolution_unit`, `cosigners` — is written verbatim from the incoming message body with no checks on its content or on whether the current contract state permits changing it [2](#0-1) . This mirrors the Superset bug class: an authorization check exists for "is this caller a party to the resource" but the specific nested attribute being mutated (dashboards in Superset; `cosigners`/`resolution_unit` here) is never validated for the caller's actual authority over that attribute.

### Finding Description
`handleMessageFromHub` validates only identity/role for `arbiter_contract_update`: [3](#0-2) 
Then it special-cases `status`, `unit`, `shared_address` [4](#0-3) , but for any other allowed field (`my_contact_info`, `peer_contact_info`, `peer_pairing_code`, `resolution_unit`, `cosigners`) it falls straight through to: [5](#0-4) 
`arbiter_contract.setField()` only whitelists field *names*, not field *values* or the sender's right to set that particular value at that particular contract state: [2](#0-1) 

In particular, `cosigners` is used later to compute the set of signing device addresses when the shared address is funded and the payment is composed: [6](#0-5) 
Because `cosigners` can be silently overwritten by either the counterparty (`peer_device_address`) or a cosigner (`from_cosigner`) at any point via `arbiter_contract_update`, with no check that the value is a subset of previously agreed cosigners, no check on array shape/content, and no restriction tied to contract status, a malicious peer or cosigner can rewrite the list of devices asked to co-sign the multisig payment for the contract's shared address, injecting or dropping cosigning device addresses that `sendMultiPayment` will subsequently prompt for signatures.

### Impact Explanation
This falls under "AA/wallet fund loss or freezing" and "unauthorized modification" analogs allowed by the rules: the `cosigners` list directly influences which devices are treated as authorized to co-sign an arbiter-contract shared-address payment. An attacker able to send this message (the contract's designated peer or a cosigner — i.e., a paired-device counterparty, in scope per the rules) can silently substitute the cosigner set used at signing time, potentially getting funds moved to a shared address it can unilaterally influence at signing coordination time, or disrupting the counterparty's ability to obtain the intended signatures (denial of the payment/freezing of funds committed to the shared address). Additionally, tampering with `resolution_unit` and `peer_pairing_code` can redirect dispute resolution or re-pair communication channels for the contract, undermining the contract's dispute/arbitration integrity.

### Likelihood Explanation
The message handler is reachable by any device already paired as the contract counterparty or listed as a cosigner — a normal, unprivileged step in the arbiter-contract flow (offer → shared → accepted). No special privilege beyond being one of the two parties or a cosigner is required, and no additional validation gates the abuse of the non-status fields, making exploitation straightforward once a contract exists.

### Recommendation
Add explicit per-field authorization and value validation in the `arbiter_contract_update` handler (or in `arbiter_contract.setField`) mirroring the `status`/`unit`/`shared_address` cases:
- Validate `cosigners` against the pre-agreed cosigner set (or restrict `cosigners` update to my own device/only additive/subtractive of one's own device, not arbitrary overwrite by the counterparty).
- Restrict `resolution_unit` writes to only the arbstore/authorized party and only during appropriate contract statuses (e.g., `dispute_resolved`/`in_appeal`).
- Validate `peer_pairing_code`/contact fields format and restrict to the originating party's own info field (`peer_*` fields should only be settable by the peer, not by a cosigner and vice versa).
- Reject any field update that isn't accompanied by an explicit content/state check rather than relying solely on the generic whitelist in `setField`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Establish an arbiter contract between attacker device (as `peer_device_address` or a `cosigner`) and victim, reaching `status = "accepted"`.
2. Attacker sends a device message: `{"hash": "<contract.hash>", "field": "cosigners", "value": ["<attacker_device_address>"]}` to the victim.
3. `handleMessageFromHub` verifies attacker is `peer_device_address`/`from_cosigner` (passes) [3](#0-2) , does not match `status`/`unit`/`shared_address` cases, and calls `arbiter_contract.setField(hash, "cosigners", ["<attacker_device_address>"], ...)` unchecked [5](#0-4) .
4. Victim's `wallet_arbiter_contracts.cosigners` is overwritten; when the contract advances to fund the shared address, `createSharedAddressAndPostUnit` uses the tampered `contract.cosigners` to decide which device addresses are asked to co-sign the multisig payment [6](#0-5) , letting the attacker manipulate the signing-request flow for funds sent to the contract's shared address.
