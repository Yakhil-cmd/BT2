### Title
Accrual `scaled_to_original` RAY-value overflow permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
`accrue_step` computes `borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — an `i128` half-up `mul_div` of `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` that panics with `MathOverflow` when the product exceeds `i128::MAX` — *before* `update_borrow_index` clamps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. On a large-decimal, high-utilization market, a single unprivileged whale can push `borrowed × borrow_index` past the `i128` ceiling while the index is still below its cap. Every state-mutating entrypoint calls `global_sync` first, so once this line panics the market is permanently frozen: no supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidation, or `update_indexes` can ever succeed again. The codebase's own test documents the cliff: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` asserts `try_update_indexes`, `try_withdraw`, and `try_repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

### Finding Description
The overflow site is `scaled_to_original`, a plain `scaled.mul(env, index)` with no saturation or ceiling guard, at `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`. It is invoked on the market's *aggregate* scaled totals — user-influenced values — at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`, inside `accrue_step`, which `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:39-48` (`accrue_chunk`, driven by `global_sync` at lines 20-33) runs on every pool verb before any user action is processed.

The asymmetry is the bug: `update_borrow_index` clamps the *index* at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36), but nothing clamps the *value* `borrowed × borrow_index` — which the test measures can exceed the index ceiling's safe domain by ~170× on an 18-decimal market at XLM-curve rates (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`). RAY-value headroom is therefore the binding constraint, and it is crossed by market state (`supplied`/`borrowed`/`borrow_index`) that ordinary users grow through `supply`, `borrow`, `flash_loan`/`flash_position`/`multiply` debt, and interest accrual. Like the bootloader finding, arithmetic on user/operator-influenced magnitudes lacks an upstream bound; here the consequence is worse than a zero fee — the trap is unrecoverable because the recovery path itself accrues first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all user funds in the affected (hub, token) market. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows `i128`, `global_sync` panics on every subsequent call, so suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `update_indexes` itself reverts — the market is bricked forever, matching the "permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate" acceptance criterion. No admin intervention is documented that bypasses accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address given sufficient capital or a thin high-decimal market: supply and borrow near-total utilization on a market whose caps were lifted (or whose caps are generous), then let time accrue — the borrower's own `repay`, a third party's `update_indexes`, or any `withdraw`/`liquidate` triggers the panic. The protocol's own regression test demonstrates it concretely, and nothing in `accrue_step` orders the index clamp before the value multiplication.

### Recommendation
Apply the index cap *before* unscaled-value reads, or make `scaled_to_original` saturate at a `MAX_RAY_VALUE`-style ceiling the way `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates via `mul_div_floor_saturating` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:26-33`). Concretely: clamp `borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `accrue_step` prior to `scaled_to_original`, and/or bound `borrowed`/`supplied` scaled totals at entry so `scaled * index` cannot exceed `i128::MAX` for any reachable index. Add a release-mode test mirroring the existing harness case.

### Proof of Concept
A permissionless sequence reproducing the documented test:

1. On an 18-decimal market with lifted caps, `supply(hub_asset=BIG18, amount=1e9 * 10^18)`, supply a collateral asset, and `borrow(BIG18, ~98% of supplied)`.
2. Advance ledger time (years at high utilization on the steep curve segment) — achievable permissionlessly via repeated `update_indexes` calls or simply waiting.
3. Any subsequent `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, `repay`, or `liquidate` on that market reverts with `GenericError::MathOverflow` from `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, freezing all supplier principal permanently.

Caveat: I verified the arithmetic and control flow in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`, `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`, and `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, and the project's own test asserts the freeze; I did not execute the scenario live.
