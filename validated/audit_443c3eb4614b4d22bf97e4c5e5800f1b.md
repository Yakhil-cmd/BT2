### Title
Position NFT seller can drain collateral by front-running the buyer's `transfer_from`, since `withdraw` settles immediately with no timelock — (File: contracts/controller/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending accounts are transferable position NFTs: `transfer`/`transfer_from` move the entire account — collateral and debt — to a new holder, and the controller resolves ownership live from `owner_of` at each call (`contracts/position-nft/README.md`, lines 39–41, 162–166; `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/position_nft.rs:67-78`). A seller can therefore list an NFT that appears to hold large collateral, approve a marketplace, watch for the buyer's purchase transaction, and front-run it with `controller::withdraw` — an owner-gated call that executes atomically with no announce/execute delay (`docs/reference/endpoints.md`, line 26). The buyer receives an NFT wrapper over a drained account while the seller keeps the sale price plus the withdrawn collateral.

### Finding Description
In FlatMoney the analog required a 10-second delayed `announceLeverageAdjust`; XOXNO Lending is worse in one dimension and identical in effect — there is no delay at all between the decision to withdraw and settlement:

1. `withdraw(caller, account_id, withdrawals, to)` is callable by the current NFT owner, remains open even during global pause, and pays out measured amounts in the same transaction (`docs/reference/endpoints.md:26`). The only constraint is that remaining LTV-weighted collateral covers remaining debt and HF ≥ 1 (`docs/reference/endpoints.md:55`), so any surplus collateral above debt — or all collateral in a debt-free account — is extractable instantly.
2. Ownership is resolved live: `storage/account.rs` maps `account_id → owner_of(token_id)`, and the harness test `old_owner_is_rejected_after_transfer` shows authority flips the moment the NFT moves (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/position_nft.rs:67-78`). Until that moment, the seller retains full withdrawal authority.
3. Non-atomic sale rails exist: `approve`/`approve_for_all`/`transfer_from` (`contracts/position-nft/README.md:63-65`) let a marketplace pull the NFT in a separate transaction, creating the mempool gap the attack needs. A debt-free seller can also pre-position a huge collateral balance purely to inflate the listing.

There is no minimum-executability-age, withdrawal delay, or lock on sale; the honeypot requires a single `withdraw` submitted ahead of the buyer's `transfer_from` in ledger order.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: the buyer pays the agreed price for an NFT whose backing was drained in the same ledger sequence, receiving an account worth a fraction of the purchase price. The seller captures sale price + withdrawn collateral, matching the report's "99 rETH + 80 rETH vs 1 rETH" outcome. Scale is bounded only by the collateral the seller can post and the sale price.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only an unprivileged address calling `withdraw` before the victim's `transfer_from` commits — no privileged role, no oracle manipulation, no protocol defect beyond the missing settlement delay. Exploitability depends on a secondary market settling NFT sales non-atomically (approve-then-pull), which is the standard marketplace pattern the NFT explicitly supports.

### Recommendation
- Introduce a withdrawal delay/lock on accounts whose NFT has an active approval, or clearable-on-transfer "pending withdrawal" escrows, so collateral cannot leave between listing and settlement.
- Alternatively/ additionally, expose an on-chain `get_account_value`/`health`-style view buyers must check atomically, and document that sales must settle via atomic `transfer`-in-same-tx trades rather than `approve` + `transfer_from`.
- At minimum, revoking `approve` should be required before `withdraw` pays out, or `withdraw` should cancel outstanding approvals.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `controller::supply(caller=alice, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, assets=[(key(USDC), 100_000e7)])` → mints NFT `id`, account holds 100k USDC, no debt.
2. Alice lists the NFT for 80k USDC on a marketplace and calls `position_nft::approve(approver=alice, approved=market, token_id=id, live_until_ledger=L)`.
3. Bob submits purchase; the market submits `transfer_from(spender=market, from=alice, to=bob, token_id=id)`.
4. Alice observes the pending sale transaction and submits `controller::withdraw(caller=alice, account_id=id, withdrawals=[(key(USDC), 0)], to=None)` — `0` means full withdrawal (`docs/reference/endpoints.md:26,51`) — ordered before the market's call in the same ledger.
5. Alice's `withdraw` authorizes against the live owner (still Alice) and pays 100k USDC to Alice; the market's `transfer_from` then moves the now-empty NFT to Bob.
6. Result: Alice holds 100k withdrawn + 80k sale proceeds; Bob holds an NFT over an account with ~0 collateral. The harness tests `transfer_hands_full_control_to_new_owner` and `old_owner_is_rejected_after_transfer` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/position_nft.rs:45-78`) confirm authority and collateral move together with no interim protection.
