### Title
Inflated Aquarius LP collateral price via direct reserve donation enables under-collateralized borrow and bad debt - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to the `portalEnergy` buy-before/`convert`/sell-after extraction pattern, an unprivileged attacker can push value into the reserve pool that backs the internal price of an LP share token, let the protocol reprice that token upward, and then withdraw value against the inflated mark. The price-aggregator derives an Aquarius LP share price directly from the pool's live `get_reserves` and `get_total_shares` (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs::fair_lp_price_wad`, `common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs::fair_stable_lp_price_wad`), and a direct token transfer to the Aquarius pool raises reserves without minting shares, raising the fair price by `sqrt(1 + donation/reserve)` (constant-product) or roughly proportionally through `D` (stable). The attacker then borrows against LP collateral priced at the inflated mark and abandons the position, leaving the protocol with bad debt.

### Finding Description
`aquarius::read` re-reads `reserve_a`, `reserve_b`, and `total_shares` from the live pool contract on every price resolution and feeds them into the fair-value formula: `2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b) / share_supply` for constant-product pools, and `D * min(price_a, price_b) / share_supply` for stable pools [1](#0-0) . Nothing in the read path distinguishes reserves that arrived via swaps/liquidity provision from reserves pushed by an unsolicited `token.transfer` into the pool address — the pool's own `get_reserves` reports raw balances. The only sanity checks are positivity, decimal matching, and a `min_pool_value_wad` floor (a lower bound, so inflation is not bounded by it) [2](#0-1) .

The exploit sequence, all reachable by a single unprivileged address:

1. Supply Aquarius LP shares as collateral via `controller.supply` into an account (the LP token must be a listed collateral whose oracle is `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp`).
2. Transfer a large amount of reserve token A directly to the Aquarius pool contract (no entrypoint call needed — SAC transfer). `get_reserves` now reports inflated `reserve_a`.
3. Call `controller.borrow` (or `multiply`/`flash_position`). The operation context resolves the LP price through `engine::resolve_nested`, reads the manipulated reserves, and values the attacker's collateral at the inflated price, permitting a borrow larger than the collateral's true value.
4. Withdraw the borrowed assets. Then optionally remove liquidity from the Aquarius pool to recover the attacker-owned share of the donated reserves — if the attacker holds most of the pool's LP supply (easy in a thin pool they partially seeded), nearly all of the donation is recoverable.
5. The account is left under-collateralized; after prices normalize, liquidation cannot recover the shortfall and `clean_bad_debt` socializes the loss via `supply_index` write-down.

The attack's cost is approximately `donation × (1 − attacker_pool_share)` plus the true collateral pledged; the gain is `borrowed − collateral_value`. For the constant-product formula, raising the price by factor `f` requires donating `(f² − 1) × reserve_a` of one leg, which is expensive only when the attacker does not already control most of the pool's shares; for a pool where the attacker is the dominant LP, a 1.5× collateral reprice costs ~1% of `1.25 × reserve` in unrecoverable donation.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: the attacker borrows more than the collateral is worth and exits; repaying is never economically rational. The deficit is borne by honest suppliers through the bad-debt supply-index write-down (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`). Unlike a spot-price oracle manipulation, no oracle signer or admin is involved — the manipulated input is on-chain DEX state the in-repo pricing code trusts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a listed LP collateral priced via `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp`, an Aquarius pool shallow enough (relative to the borrow ceiling and collateral LTV) that a donation can move the fair price past the needed margin while remaining inside the configured `min/max_sanity_price_wad` band, and the attacker owning a meaningful fraction of that pool's LP shares to recover most of the donation. The residual risk of the mirrored exploit (unrecoverable donation share, slippage recovering the skew) is real but bounded — this is a Medium-severity economic manipulation, not a guaranteed drain. The codebase's own test `swap_cannot_move_the_price` covers swap-induced skew but does not cover donation-induced reserve inflation, which is exactly the "inject value into the reserve pool, extract at the new mark" class from the report.

### Recommendation
Bound the LP share price against manipulation rather than trusting raw balances: (a) use Aquarius's invariant/`virtual_price` where available instead of raw `get_reserves`, or compute the constant-product price from the product `k` per share rather than per-leg balances; (b) cross-check the derived LP price against a TWAP or a secondary source and reject deviations beyond a tolerance, mirroring the dual-leg tolerance already used for feed sources; (c) add an upper sanity bound that a single-block reserve change cannot breach (the `min_pool_value_wad` check is one-sided — add an effective cap on per-read price growth); (d) for stable pools, prefer `min(price_a, price_b) × D` computed from normalized balances that discount unsolicited donations where the venue exposes them.

### Proof of Concept
Not executed against this codebase (read-only analysis). The traceable evidence is:

- The LP price is a pure function of live reserves and share supply: `fair_lp_price_wad` computes `2 * sqrt(value_a * value_b) / share_supply_wad` [3](#0-2)  and `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` computes `D * min_price / share_supply_wad` [4](#0-3) .
- Reserves come straight from `AquariusPoolClient.get_reserves` with no donation filtering [5](#0-4) , and `read` wires them into the fair-price call each resolution [1](#0-0) .
- The only value guard is a floor: `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad → InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` [2](#0-1) .
- The existing regression test proves swaps cannot move the stable fair price but donation/imbalance inflation is untested: `swap_cannot_move_the_price` only checks swap-induced skew bounded by the invariant [6](#0-5) .
- Borrow paths consume this price through the operation `Context` for collateral valuation and health-factor/min-borrow gates, so an inflated LP mark directly enlarges permitted borrow capacity.

pub fn aquarius_pool_reserves_call(env: &Env, pool: &Address) -> Option<(i128, i128)> {
    let reserves = match AquariusPoolClient::new(env, pool).try_get_reserves() {
        Ok(Ok(reserves)) => reserves,
        _ => return None,
    };
    if reserves.len() != 2 {
        return None;
    }
    Some((
        i128::try_from(reserves.get_unchecked(0)).ok()?,
        i128::try_from(reserves.get_unchecked(1)).ok()?,
    ))
}
