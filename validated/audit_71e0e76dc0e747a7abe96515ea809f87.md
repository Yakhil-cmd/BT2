### Title
Allowlist-gated RWA collateral changes hands via Credit-mode liquidation and position-NFT transfers with no issuer admission, letting non-allowlisted actors acquire and monetize gated shares - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
The Liqvid/Asterizm-style RWA gate enforces its issuer allowlist only at the token-transfer boundary: `ensure_can_hold` checks `from` and `to` on `transfer`/`transfer_from`/`burn`/`mint`. XOXNO Lending holds all gated collateral in a single allowlisted pool balance and moves ownership internally — scaled supply shares via `SeizeMode::Credit` liquidation and whole accounts via the position NFT — with no token transfer and therefore no allowlist check. This is the same bug class as the report: an admission check anchored to a token/contract boundary that a proxy layer (internal shares + transferable position NFT, or an allowlisted smart-wallet holder) silently bypasses.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs`, `SeizeMode::Credit` credits seized supply shares to a receiver `account_id` through pure bookkeeping; no `transfer`/`transfer_from` is invoked on the gated token, so `ensure_can_hold` never runs for the receiver. The test `lqv_credit_liquidation_moves_shares_without_a_token_transfer` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/liqvid_rwa_collateral.rs:530-555`) proves any liquidator can move gated Liqvid shares into a receiver account that is not on the issuer allowlist, and `lqv_issuer_pool_freeze_blocks_token_movement_until_unfrozen` (`:561-602`) and `lqv_a_frozen_liquidator_can_only_liquidate_in_credit_mode` (`:634-652`) show Credit settlement works even while the pool or the liquidator is frozen/de-listed.

Independently, `contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs` is a stock OpenZeppelin enumerable NFT (`NonFungibleToken`/`NonFungibleEnumerable`, `:131-173`); token id = account id and `owner_of` is the account owner (`:1-5`). Nothing in the NFT or the controller consults a collateral token's gate on `transfer`/`transfer_from` — the controller itself is deliberately kept off the allowlist (`:434-437`), so no protocol-level check can exist. An allowlisted holder can `supply` gated shares into a fresh account and simply `transfer` the NFT to an arbitrary non-allowlisted address, delivering full control of the position — including the ability to call `borrow` (collateral valuation is WAD-cached in `Context`, no token movement) and to receive further Credit-seized shares — without the issuer ever admitting that address.

### Impact Explanation
A non-allowlisted (including issuer-frozen or sanctioned) entity acquires economically live exposure to gated collateral and can borrow permissionlessly-lent assets (e.g. USDC) against it — the exact "verified account as swap proxy" outcome, mapped onto the pool as the liquidity venue instead of a swap pool. There is also a freezing/insolvency tail: once exposure is distributed to non-admitted accounts, an issuer de-listing or freeze of the pool blocks every `withdraw`, `SeizeMode::Transfer` liquidation, and `claim_revenue` touching the gated market (`:561-629`, `:768-818`), while the internally credited shares remain claimable liabilities — collateral the protocol may be unable to move against debt it has already paid out.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires only one allowlisted participant (or liquidator) willing to act as proxy — `liquidate` with `SeizeMode::Credit` and NFT `transfer` are both permissionless entrypoints reachable by a single unprivileged address in one transaction each. It becomes fully permissionless for the receiving party since no auth or admission is ever requested from the receiver.

### Recommendation
For assets registered as gated/RWA (or universally, as cheap defense-in-depth), either (a) settle gated-asset liquidation legs only via `SeizeMode::Transfer` so every change of economic ownership crosses the token's `ensure_can_hold` check, or (b) before crediting Credit-mode shares / honoring an NFT transfer on accounts holding gated collateral, probe the token's gate (e.g. a read-only `is_allowlisted`/`can_hold` view) and revert for non-admitted receivers. Document the residual proxy risk for allowlisted smart-wallet holders in the market listing ADR.

### Proof of Concept
1. Issuer activates the gate; only `pool` is allowlisted (`setup_listing` + `set_gate_active(true)` + `add_to_allowlist(pool)`, `:153-155`).
2. Allowlisted `alice` supplies gated units (`supply` pulls `alice → pool`, both admitted) and borrows USDC — `open()` at `:423-429`.
3. Path A (Credit): NAV drops; any `liquidator` calls `liquidate(account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Credit(0))`. The receiver account is credited whole-unit shares (`units(receiver) > 0`, `:536-543`) while `liquidator` was never on the issuer allowlist — the gate never saw a transfer.
4. Path B (NFT): `alice` calls `PositionNft::transfer(alice, mallory, token_id)` — stock OZ path, no gate — handing `mallory` (non-allowlisted) full control of the account holding gated collateral; `mallory` then calls `borrow` to extract USDC against it.
5. Issuer removes `pool` from the allowlist: all withdrawals, Transfer liquidations and `claim_revenue` revert (`:607-629`, `:800-808`), but the credited/transferred positions remain live claims the protocol must honor.

Note: I verified the Credit-mode and gating behavior through the harness tests and the NFT/contract sources; I did not get to read the controller's `SeizeMode::Credit` implementation or the `owner_of` auth lookup directly (iteration limit), so the exact line-level mechanics of internal crediting are inferred from the passing tests cited above rather than confirmed in the source.
