### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps the supply index to a nonzero floor, leaving wiped-out suppliers a phantom claim that drains future depositors' cash - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The TBIS report is a scam class: tokens sold as "backed" by assets and partnerships that did not exist — holders' claims exceeded real backing, and early redeemers were paid out of later victims' money. The on-chain analog in XOXNO Lending is `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`. When bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, the supply index is clamped up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of reaching zero. Wiped-out suppliers keep floor-valued "phantom" claims backed by nothing. Any unprivileged liquidator can drive the index to the floor, and any legacy share-holder can then withdraw fresh depositors' cash — a textbook unbacked-claim drain, proven by the pool's own test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369`).

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes unpaid debt by scaling the supply index:

- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:80-88`: `capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value)`, `remaining = total_supplied_value − capped`, `new_supply_index = supply_index * (remaining/total)`, then `set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)))`.

On a full wipeout (`bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`), `remaining == 0`, so the mathematical index is 0 — but the `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` clamp resurrects it to a small positive constant. Every pre-existing scaled supply share retains `unscale_supply_floor(scaled) > 0` of redeemable value, even though the corresponding debt and collateral are gone and the book holds zero cash. Those shares are claims with no backing — exactly the "services/assets that did not exist" shape of the source incident, expressed in solvency terms.

Reaching this state needs no privilege: any liquidator performing a partial/full liquidation that leaves unpaid debt flows through the controller's bad-debt path (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs`, `ops/seize.rs` in the pool) into this function. `clean_bad_debt` is likewise callable by unprivileged keepers. After the clamp, the stranded holder calls `withdraw` with `i128::MAX` (0/full withdrawal semantics per `docs/reference/endpoints.md:51`) and receives `resolve_withdrawal` gross paid from whatever cash the pool then holds — i.e., from the *next* depositor's `supply`. The pool debits real cash to settle a claim whose backing was already destroyed.

The test at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369` demonstrates the exact sequence in-cache: wipeout clamps the index to the floor, userA retains a `stranded > 0` phantom claim with `cash == 0`, userB supplies `c`, then userA withdraws `gross == c` and the pool is drained to `cash == 0` while `b_claim > cash` — the honest supplier loses funds.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Legacy (pre-wipeout) share-holders hold positive withdrawable claims backed by zero assets. The first post-wipeout supplier — or anyone supplying during the window — funds the stranded holders' withdrawals dollar-for-dollar. Since the floor-valued claim of *every* pre-wipeout supplier persists, total phantom claims equal `supplied_total × floor`, which can exceed a single fresh deposit; repeated withdrawal races drain successive depositors until phantom claims are exhausted or the book is abandoned. The wound is silent: events and indexes look consistent, and `require_reserves`/`credit_cash` bookkeeping all succeeds because cash genuinely moves — it just belongs to the new depositor.

### Likelihood Explanation
Bad debt is an expected event in this design (dust-threshold cleanup, `clean_bad_debt`, and seizure write-downs exist precisely for it), and full-wipeout conditions arise whenever unpaid debt ≥ remaining supplied value — realistic in thin/deprecated spoke markets or after oracle-driven cascades. Triggering requires only ordinary liquidation/`clean_bad_debt` calls. The attacker needs to hold any scaled supply position before the wipeout (attainable with a trivial supply, since the phantom claim scales with `scaled_amount × floor` regardless of index), then wait for — or induce via normal usage — a subsequent deposit. No flash loan, no privileged role, no oracle manipulation.

### Recommendation
When `remaining == 0` (or the computed index falls below the floor), zero out `supplied`/`revenue` scaled-share books alongside the index — i.e., burn the stranded shares or record them as irrecoverable — so no `unscale_supply_floor` claim survives a wipeout. Alternatively, gate withdrawals on index > floor (`resolve_withdrawal` returns 0 when `supply_index <= SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`) so floor-clamped shares can never be redeemed against future cash. Any fix must preserve the nonzero floor for accrual continuity while severing the redeemable claim.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains the executable proof; sketch at controller level:

1. Supplier A `supply`s into a market; Borrower takes debt that becomes unrecoverable (liquidation leaves residual debt with no collateral to seize).
2. Any unprivileged caller runs liquidation/`clean_bad_debt` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:88`). A's scaled shares now represent a claim `> 0` while the market's cash is 0.
3. Supplier B `supply`s `c` of the token — cash enters the pool's real balance.
4. A calls `withdraw(caller, account_id, [(hub_asset, i128::MAX)], to)` → `resolve_withdrawal` unscale-floors the stranded shares, `require_reserves`/`debit_cash` succeeds against B's deposit, and the pool transfers `c` to A (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:351-368` shows `gross == c`, `cash == 0`, `cash < b_claim`).

B's claim is permanently under-collateralized; A stole the fresh deposit through a claim the protocol itself resurrected via the floor clamp.
