### Title
Sole-source Aquarius LP oracle lacks an agreement fallback — in-transaction reserve donation inflates LP collateral price within the sanity band to over-borrow - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The WooFi bug class is a price source that can be pushed outside its expected range within one transaction while the configured cross-check does not cover that asset. XOXNO Lending's `PriceAggregator` has the same shape for `AquariusLpSource` oracles: they are sole-source by construction (`has_lp && sources.len() != 1` panics), so `validate_asset_oracle` waives the dual-source tolerance check and the smoothing check, leaving the sanity band as the only backstop — the exact analog of WooFi's missing Chainlink fallback on WOO. The LP price is derived from the pool's *live* `get_reserves`/`get_total_shares` readings, which an unprivileged attacker can inflate inside a single transaction by donating tokens to the pool (a plain token transfer, explicitly an allowed attacker action). `fair_lp_price_wad` computes `2·sqrt(value_a·value_b)/share_supply`, so donating asset A raises the reported LP price by `sqrt(1 + donation/reserve_a)`; `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` similarly grows with `D`. The attacker funds the donation with the controller's own `flash_loan`, deposits the LP token via `supply`, and calls `borrow` — the `Context`-cached inflated price feeds the health-factor and LTV gates — then reclaims most of the donation via `remove_liquidity` and repays the flash loan.

### Finding Description
- `read` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:69-131` resolves leg prices, then reads `reserve_a, reserve_b` and `total_shares` directly from the pool contract in the same ledger, with no cross-check against any second source and no TWAP.
- `validate_asset_oracle` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/admin.rs:146-201` enforces `sources.len() == 1` for LP oracles and skips `validate_oracle_tolerance` and `validation::smoothing` for them (lines 191-197); the only runtime bound is `[min_sanity_price_wad, max_sanity_price_wad]` capped by `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` (`SanityBandTooWideForSingleSource`, error 226).
- A constant-product swap preserves `reserve_a·reserve_b` (fees only raise it), so swaps cannot move `fair_lp_price_wad` — but a *donation* raises `value_a` without changing `value_b` or the share supply, inflating the fair price monotonically in `sqrt(reserve_a)`. For stable pools, `solve_stable_d` grows with the donated reserve so `D·min_price/supply` rises as well.
- The donation is partially recoverable: after borrowing, the attacker calls the pool's `remove_liquidity`, receiving their pro-rata share of the now-larger reserves, and repays the flash loan. Net manipulation cost is `donation·(1 − share_fraction)` plus LP acquisition.
- The controller consumes this price through `prices`/`Context` caching for `supply`/`borrow` solvency gates (INV-ORACLE-03 retains it for the whole transaction), so one manipulated reading drives the entire borrow.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency, conditional on market parameters. The threat model (`docs/explanation/threat-model.md:194-210`) proves lenders are safe only while `LT < 1/u`, where `u = max_sanity/min_sanity` is the band ratio; the documented Liqvid margin assumes `u ≤ 11/9` from the 10% single-source cap, but LP oracles use the separate `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` cap, and the proof explicitly fails when the debt-side feed can also move within its own band (effective `u` becomes the product). Whenever `u·LTV > 1` for an LP-collateral market — or when the borrowed asset's price can be co-moved — an attacker borrows `LTV·n·p1` against collateral truly worth `n·p2` with `p1/p2 ≤ u`, creating instant bad debt borne by suppliers. Independently of that bound, an attacker can inflate the LP price of a *victim's* collateral to push their HF above 1 and block `liquidate` while the donation persists (temporary freezing of liquidation), or inflate then unwind to trigger self-liquidations at bonus-favorable prices. Since the band admits the deviation, no gate reverts the transaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address in one transaction: `flash_loan` (funds the donation) → token `transfer` to the Aquarius pool → `supply` LP shares → `borrow` → pool `remove_liquidity` → flash repayment. Requires (a) a listed LP-share collateral market, (b) pool liquidity shallow enough that the donation cost is below extractable borrow value, and (c) `u·LTV > 1` or a co-manipulable debt feed — the same preconditions as WooFi (newly listed low-liquidity collateral). The `min_pool_value_wad` floor limits this to pools above a governance-set value but does not prevent it.

### Recommendation
Do not rely on a sole live-reserve reading: pair the LP source with an independent second source so `within_tolerance_band` applies, or price LP collateral off a manipulation-resistant reserve snapshot (e.g., TWAP/virtual-price from the pool contract rather than raw `get_reserves`). Tighten `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` so that `u < 1/LT` holds with margin for every listed LP collateral, and enforce that per-market at listing time rather than globally.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires LP shares of Aquarius pool P (share token listed as collateral with liquidation threshold `LT`, band ratio `u > 1/LT`).
2. In one transaction: `controller.flash_loan(X)`, transfer `0.25·reserve_a` of token A to P (`reserve_a → 1.25·reserve_a`, `sqrt` factor `≈1.118` ≤ band), `supply` the LP shares, `borrow` `LTV·n·p_inflated` of asset D, call P's `remove_liquidity` (recovering ≈ share·donation), repay flash loan.
3. Price reverts to `p2 = p1/1.118`; the account is immediately undercollateralized by `u·LTV − 1`, and liquidation cannot recover the deficit (seizure pays `≤ n·p2·(1+b)` against debt `LTV·n·p1 > n·p2`), leaving bad debt to suppliers via `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize`.

Confidence caveat: profitability of the borrow-and-default variant depends on `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` versus each market's `LT`/`LTV` (the exact constant and the shipped Liqvid LP bands could not be verified within the search budget); the donation-inflation mechanism and missing fallback are confirmed by the cited code.
