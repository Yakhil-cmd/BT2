### Title
Residual bad-debt write-down lets stranded supply shares drain later deposits - (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
Bad-debt cleanup caps the supply-index write-down at a nonzero floor, leaving previously supplied shares with a positive claim after catastrophic debt socialization. A later depositor can fund that residual claim, after which the pool lacks enough cash to honor the new deposit.

### Finding Description
`execute_bad_debt_cleanup` forwards each remaining debt position to `pool_seize_positions_call`, which handles borrow entries by calculating the bad debt, applying it to the supply index, and burning the debt shares. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The write-down deliberately clamps the resulting supply index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zero, so surviving supply shares retain a positive scaled claim even when the debt write-down consumed the entire supplied value. [3](#0-2) 

A withdrawal subsequently resolves and burns the stranded shares, requires only that cash covers the payout, and transfers the amount out of the pool. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The repository’s own regression scenario demonstrates that a stranded claim is masked while the market has zero cash, but can extract a newly deposited amount and leave the new depositor under-collateralized. [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
A fresh depositor’s funds can be paid immediately to the holder of residual pre-wipe supply shares, leaving the fresh depositor with a claim exceeding pool cash. Because the debt has already been burned, ordinary supply-for-debt checks cannot detect that this residual claim is economically unbacked. This is theft of user funds and creates protocol insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires a market to reach catastrophic bad debt where the write-down equals or exceeds total supplied value, causing the index to hit its floor. An unprivileged caller can then invoke `clean_bad_debt(caller, victim_account_id)` once the victim’s collateral is at or below the dust threshold and its debt exceeds collateral. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

No privileged action is required for the stranded supplier to withdraw through the ordinary withdrawal path once another user supplies enough cash to cover the residual claim. The condition is market-dependent rather than freely manufacturable, but a severe collateral-price move can create it.

### Recommendation
Do not leave transferable residual supply claims after a catastrophic wipe. Track a `supply_wiped`/deficit state at the floor, block new supply and residual withdrawals until recapitalization restores backing, or atomically burn/rebase all surviving supply and revenue shares when bad debt reaches total supplied value. Any chosen remediation should preserve the controller’s account-position records and spoke usage consistently with the pool’s scaled-share totals.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `S` units of an asset and keeps `S_scaled` supply shares.
2. A separate victim borrows at least `S` units of that asset against collateral in another market.
3. The collateral falls enough that the victim is insolvent and its remaining collateral is at or below the fixed dust cap.
4. An unprivileged caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(caller, victim_account_id)`.
5. Controller cleanup emits a borrow seize entry and calls the pool. [1](#0-0) 
6. `seize::apply` computes `bad_debt`, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the victim’s debt shares. [2](#0-1) 
7. Because `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero and the supply index is set to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; Alice’s `S_scaled` remains stored and unscales to a positive residual amount `R`. [9](#0-8) 
8. Bob supplies `R` into the empty-cash market.
9. Alice submits a maximum/full withdrawal for her old account and asset; the withdrawal resolves `R`, passes `require_reserves(R)`, burns her shares, debits cash, and transfers Bob’s deposit to Alice. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 
10. Bob retains a positive supply claim while pool cash is zero or below that claim, demonstrating that Alice extracted Bob’s funds. [10](#0-9)

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

```rust
#[test]
fn test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let alice_scaled_raw = 1_000 * RAY;
        let borrowed_scaled_raw = 1_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: alice_scaled_raw,
            borrowed: borrowed_scaled_raw,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let alice_scaled = Ray::from(alice_scaled_raw);
        let borrow_scaled = Ray::from(borrowed_scaled_raw);

        let bad_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(borrow_scaled);
        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt);
        cache.burn_debt(borrow_scaled);

        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "seize wipeout clamps supply_index UP to RAY/1000, leaving unburned shares a residual"
        );

        let alice_stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled);
        assert!(alice_stranded > 0, "wiped survivor keeps a stranded claim");
        assert_eq!(
            cache.cash(),
            0,
            "empty market: claim masked by require_reserves"
        );

        let deposit = alice_stranded;
        let bob_scaled = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(deposit);
        cache.mint_supply(bob_scaled);
        cache.credit_cash(deposit);

        let total_owed = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
        assert!(
            total_owed > cache.cash(),
            "post-deposit books insolvent: owed {} > cash {}",
            total_owed,
            cache.cash()
        );

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "wiped position pays out real cash");
        assert_eq!(gross, deposit, "Alice extracts exactly Bob's fresh deposit");

        let bob_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(bob_scaled);
        assert!(
            cache.cash() < bob_claim,
            "cash {} cannot cover Bob's honest claim {}: fresh depositor lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            bob_claim
        );
    });
}
```
