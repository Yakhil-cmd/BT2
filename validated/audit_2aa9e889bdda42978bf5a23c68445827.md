### Title
Suppliers can front-run bad-debt socialization (`clean_bad_debt` / liquidation seizure) to withdraw at the pre-write-down index, dumping the entire haircut on remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs)

### Summary
When an account's debt is written off, the pool socializes the loss by writing the supply index down via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-27`). Because a withdrawal pays out at whatever supply index is live when the withdrawal executes — gated only by cash reserves and utilization (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-118`) — any supplier who observes a pending `clean_bad_debt` or loss-socializing liquidation can submit `withdraw` first and exit at the old, higher index, taking full cash while the remaining suppliers absorb the whole write-down. This is the same class as the reference finding: a state-changing "blocking" transaction (the write-down that effectively seizes a fraction of every supplier's claim) can be front-run by an immediate withdrawal.

### Finding Description
- `seize::apply` handles the `AccountPositionType::Borrow` side by unscaling the position, calling `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt)`, and burning the debt (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`). The write-down lowers `supply_index`, so every scaled supply share is worth less underlying — a pro-rata haircut on all suppliers.
- `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` builds `PoolSeizeEntry` items for both the deposit and borrow legs and calls `pool_seize_positions_call` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:21-49`); it is permissionless — any unprivileged address can call `clean_bad_debt` once the account meets the dust/eligibility condition, and liquidations that leave residual debt take the same `Borrow`-side path.
- `withdraw::accounting` resolves the gross payout from the cached live `supply_index` (`resolve_withdrawal` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:65,94`) and only enforces `require_reserves`, `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-118`). There is no two-step withdrawal, no withdrawal queue, and no per-transaction index epoch check.
- Therefore, in the same ledger (or by sequencing ahead of the cleanup tx), a supplier calls `controller.withdraw(...)` with `amount = i128::MAX`, burns shares at the pre-write-down index, and receives the full underlying amount while cash exists. The cleanup then applies the entire `bad_debt` write-down against the shrunken supplier base, so suppliers who did not front-run bear more than their pro-rata share — the front-runner's avoided haircut is transferred to them. In the worst case the front-runner drains cash such that `require_reserves` blocks later withdrawals, leaving honest suppliers temporarily frozen until `recapitalize`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / unfair loss allocation: the front-running supplier extracts underlying that, by design, should have been haircut by the bad-debt write-down; the avoided loss is borne by all remaining suppliers (their shares are written down by the full `bad_debt` over a smaller base). It can also cause temporary freezing of funds if the front-runner's exit removes enough cash that subsequent withdrawals hit `require_reserves` before `recapitalize` replenishes the shortfall (the floor/clamp behavior is exercised in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3170-3251`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The preconditions are (1) a bad-debt event large enough to create a meaningful index write-down (liquidations leaving residual debt, or a dust account eligible for `clean_bad_debt`), and (2) the supplier observing the pending cleanup/liquidation transaction before it lands. Both `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` are permissionless, so on a Soroban network the transactions are publicly visible in flight, and the value at stake scales with the write-down size. The dust-cleanup path caps any single write-down, but a partial-liquidation socialization can be large; against that, the front-runner's edge is bounded by available cash and the max-utilization gate.

### Recommendation
- Short term (mirrors the report's recommendation): introduce a withdrawal-delay or epoch mechanism — e.g., a withdraw request that locks shares at the current index and only settles after a waiting period or after pending accrual/settlement — so a supplier cannot atomically exit ahead of a known write-down.
- Alternatively, process `clean_bad_debt` and the write-down atomically with a supply-side settlement barrier: snapshot/settle accrued bad debt before honoring withdrawals in the same ledger (e.g., force `update_indexes`-style settlement or a per-market "last write-down ledger" check in `withdraw::gate_and_debit`).
- Long term: audit all instant-exit paths (`withdraw`, `flash_loan`, `close_position` withdraw leg, `SeizeMode::Credit` exits) for ordering sensitivity relative to loss-socializing operations.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 1000 units of token T via `controller.supply`; Bob borrows most of the cash, becomes deeply insolvent, and liquidation leaves residual debt D that will be socialized (or Bob's account becomes eligible for `clean_bad_debt`).
2. A liquidator/keeper submits `controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id)` (or a `liquidate` call whose plan includes the `Borrow` seize leg) — visible publicly.
3. Alice front-runs it with `controller.withdraw(receiver, false, entry)` requesting `i128::MAX`. `resolve_withdrawal` prices her shares at the pre-write-down `supply_index` (`withdraw.rs:94`), `require_reserves` passes because the cash is still there (`withdraw.rs:112`), and she receives the full underlying.
4. The cleanup executes: `seize::apply` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`seize.rs:26`), writing the supply index down by the full D — now distributed only over the suppliers who did not exit. Their effective loss is greater than the intended pro-rata share; if Alice's exit drained cash, their subsequent withdrawals revert on `require_reserves` until `recapitalize`.

The mechanism — withdrawal at stale index skipping a pending write-down — is directly demonstrated in the codebase's own accounting test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495`), which shows a withdrawal resolving at a favorable index against cash that a write-down was meant to redistribute.
