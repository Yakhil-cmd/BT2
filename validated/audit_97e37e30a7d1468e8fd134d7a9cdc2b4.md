### Title
Permissionless LTV restamp bypasses the HF gate and freezes a victim's withdrawals — (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
The Grafana bug is a scoping restriction (a locked time range) that is enforced on the main path but bypassed on a secondary read path. The XOXNO Lending analog is the stamped-risk gate: tightening a position's liquidation tuple is HF-gated (`HF >= 1.05`), but the LTV half of the same stamp is refreshed unconditionally, on paths reachable by any unprivileged address. Because the post-pool solvency gate requires `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` for withdrawals, a forced downgrade of a victim's stored LTV can push a healthy account's LTV-weighted collateral below its debt and revert every `withdraw`, even though its health factor is unaffected.

### Finding Description
`refresh_supply_risk_params` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs` sets `position.loan_to_value = effective_config.loan_to_value` unconditionally (line 35); only the `FullTuple` branch wraps `apply_gated_liquidation_params`, and that gate protects only `liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus` and `liquidation_fees` (lines 36-38, 76-93). The same unconditional LTV copy exists in `restamp_listed_supply_ltv` (lines 56-60).

Two unprivileged paths apply it:

1. `update_account_threshold(caller, has_risks = false, account_ids)` — requires only `require_authorized_caller`, so any caller restamps any account's LTV (params.rs:124-144). `has_risks = false` loads no debt positions and performs no HF check at all.
2. Third-party supply to an existing leg — `merge_supply_leg` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params`, so a 1-stroop top-up of a victim's existing supply position restamps LTV (documented in `docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md`: "every borrow and every withdrawal refreshes the stored LTV of each listed supply position without a condition", and `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/third_party_supply_and_risk_restamp.rs` confirms a stranger's dust supply re-runs the restamp).

Once the stored LTV is lowered, `require_post_pool_risk_gates` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs` asserts `totals.ltv_collateral >= totals.total_debt` before any withdrawal (lines 41-45). `ltv_collateral` is computed from `min(stored LTV, stored LT)` per INV-RISK-02. An account stamped at e.g. LTV 75% that is restamped to 10% loses ~87% of its LTV-weighted collateral; if that drops below `total_debt`, every `withdraw` reverts with `InsufficientCollateral` while `HF` may still be comfortably above 1 — the HF leg of the gate is irrelevant because the LTV leg fails first.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds. A single unprivileged address can, at negligible cost, force a healthy borrower into a state where `withdraw` always reverts, provided governance has lowered the asset's `loan_to_value` at any point after the victim's position was stamped. The victim cannot restore the old LTV (stamps only move toward the live listing), cannot `borrow`, and cannot `swap_collateral`/`multiply` (all debt-bearing strategies run the same gate). Escape requires repaying the entire debt (repay is open to anyone) or waiting for governance to raise the LTV listing again. The HF gate that exists precisely to prevent adverse stamped-parameter changes on indebted accounts is simply bypassed for the LTV component.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a precondition: an `editAssetInSpoke` that lowers `loan_to_value` on an asset with open positions stamped at the old value. LTV reductions are a routine risk-off action during volatility, so the window opens naturally. Once open, exploitation is one cheap permissionless call (`update_account_threshold(false, [victim])` or a dust supply) per victim, repeatable across every account holding the asset. Impact is temporary freeze rather than theft, consistent with Medium.

### Recommendation
Apply the same protection used for the liquidation tuple to LTV decreases on accounts with debt: gate `position.loan_to_value` reduction on the hypothetical/post-state check (e.g. require the account to remain withdrawable, or reuse the `THRESHOLD_UPDATE_MIN_HF_RAW` floor with an LTV-based check), or exempt the LTV leg of `require_post_pool_risk_gates` from using a restamp the owner never triggered — e.g. use `max(stored LTV at borrow time, listing LTV)` when evaluating withdrawals, or only restamp LTV upward on third-party paths (`merge_supply_leg`, `update_account_threshold` on foreign accounts) and reserve downward restamps for owner-initiated actions.

### Proof of Concept
1. Admin lists USDC in the victim's spoke with `loan_to_value = 7500`. Victim supplies $10,000 USDC and borrows $7,000 of debt (`ltv_collateral` $7,500 ≥ $7,000; HF ≈ 1.14).
2. Governance tightens risk: `editAssetInSpoke` sets `loan_to_value = 5000` (LT and bonus unchanged).
3. Attacker calls `update_account_threshold(attacker, false, [victim_id])` — or supplies 1 stroop of USDC to the victim's existing position. `refresh_supply_risk_params` writes `loan_to_value = 5000` unconditionally; no HF gate runs for LTV.
4. Victim's `ltv_collateral` is now $5,000 < `total_debt` $7,000. Every `withdraw` reverts in `require_post_pool_risk_gates` with `InsufficientCollateral` (`contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:41-45`), even though the victim's HF (computed on the still-stamped liquidation threshold) is ≈ 1.14 and unchanged.
5. The victim's collateral is frozen until it repays the full debt or governance raises the LTV listing; the attacker paid only transaction fees and optionally dust.

Note: the unconditional LTV restamp is partially documented as intended in `docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md`, so this finding hinges on the freeze-through-LTV consequence being unintended; if the behavior is a deliberate ADR choice, reject this analog.
