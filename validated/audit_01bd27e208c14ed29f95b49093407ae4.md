### Title
Interest accrual overflow permanently freezes a whale-sized market — repay, withdraw and liquidation all revert on `MathOverflow` - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The buffer-overflow / denial-of-service class of CVE-2023-30086 maps onto XOXNO Lending as unchecked arithmetic overflow in the interest accrual path. Every market mutation on the pool (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`-driven legs, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize`) first calls `interest::global_sync`, which rescales share totals through `scaled_to_original` in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`. That helper is `scaled.mul(env, index)`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once `scaled * index` exceeds `i128` — even though the borrow index itself is still far below its `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling. Because every verb accrues first, the market becomes permanently frozen: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize.

### Finding Description
- `scaled_to_original(env, scaled, index)` at `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16` performs `scaled.mul(env, index)`; `Ray::mul` in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs` panics with `MathOverflow` when the exact `x*y/RAY` result does not fit `i128` (widened `I256` path still traps via `to_i128`).
- The index ceiling `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^36, i.e. 10^9 × the initial index per `docs/reference/formulas.md`) is enforced on the index, but the *value* `supplied_scaled × index` overflows `i128` long before that ceiling is reached for whale-scale positions (up to ~170 billion whole tokens per the token-to-RAY input bound).
- The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` demonstrates exactly this: after ~years of accrual at 98% utilization on the XLM curve, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engaged), and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert with the same error. The test comment notes the bound documented in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong — the documented formula implies the index cap protects the market, but it does not.
- The accrual ordering is normative: `contracts/pool/README.md` shows every mutation runs `Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate → guards`, so there is no entry path that skips the panicking computation.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds. Once the panic threshold is crossed, no `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, or `recapitalize` affecting that market can succeed — the shared accrual step traps first. All supplied and collateral value locked against that market is unrecoverable. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion and is reachable entirely through unprivileged entrypoints: `supply`, `borrow`, and `update_indexes`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low but real: an attacker (or simply organic market growth) must push a market's scaled supply/debt large enough that `supplied × index` approaches `i128::MAX` while utilization stays high so the index keeps compounding. This requires whale-scale deposits (billions of whole tokens on an 18-decimal asset, per the test's `BILLION * 10^18` fixture) and sustained high utilization so the index compounds toward the cliff. No privileged action is required; any unprivileged address can supply and borrow to inflate the book, and anyone can call `update_indexes` to advance accrual. Caps can be the only mitigating control, and the test disables max-utilization and lifts caps — an admin-set cap limits but does not eliminate the exposure since admitted caps are themselves enormous.

### Recommendation
- Make accrual saturate rather than panic: clamp the index at a value-ceiling-aware bound (e.g. stop index growth when `scaled * next_index` would leave `i128`) instead of trapping in `scaled_to_original`, so the index cap engages *before* the value overflow.
- Alternatively, enforce a market-level ceiling on `supplied_scaled`/`borrowed_scaled` (or on `scaled × index`) at entry time so admitted positions can never reach the overflow corner — a tighter analogue of `max_cap_for_decimals`.
- Fix the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md`, which the test proves wrong: the index ceiling does not bound total accrued value.

### Proof of Concept
The repository's own harness test is a working PoC (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`):

1. Unprivileged `supply` of `BILLION * 10^18` units of an 18-decimal asset on the XLM rate curve (`lift_caps` removes cap interference).
2. Unprivileged `borrow` of 98% of it against collateral in a second market.
3. Advance ledger time year by year calling `update_indexes` (permissionless).
4. Within 40 simulated years, `update_indexes` reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Thereafter `withdraw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `repay(ALICE, "BIG18", ...)` both revert with `MathOverflow` — funds permanently frozen; the same trap blocks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup on that market.
