## Title
Attacker-Controlled "Always-Bounce" AA as Refund Recipient Causes Full Reversion and Permanent Freezing of a Primary AA's Response (Secondary-Trigger Cascade Revert) - (File: aa_composer.js)

### Summary
`aa_composer.js`'s trigger-handling engine treats any payment output sent by a primary AA to another AA address as a **secondary trigger**, and if that secondary AA bounces, the *entire* primary AA response — all state changes, balance updates, and payments to unrelated parties — is unconditionally rolled back and the primary trigger itself is forced to bounce. An attacker who controls the destination address that a lending/vault/liquidation-style AA is programmed to pay out to (e.g. a stored "borrower" or "recipient" address) can pre-register that address as an always-bouncing AA. Every attempt by the protocol AA to settle/liquidate/refund funds to that address will then cascade-revert the whole operation, indefinitely blocking liquidation or fund release — the same "reject the transfer to dodge finalization" bug class described in the Gearbox report, but reachable purely through normal AA/oscript usage rather than native ETH `receive()` reverts.

### Finding Description
When a primary AA response includes a payment message whose output address happens to be another AA, `handleSecondaryTriggers()` invokes `handleTrigger()` recursively for that address as a **secondary trigger**: [1](#0-0) 

If any secondary AA in the chain bounces, its failure is propagated up and the *primary* trigger's `revert()` is invoked rather than simply failing the secondary leg in isolation: [2](#0-1) 

`revert()` discards all logs/state and issues a full `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT`, undoing every effect the primary AA produced in this trigger (state var updates, all payment outputs to any other party), then forces the primary trigger itself into `bounce()`: [3](#0-2) 

An AA can be made to bounce unconditionally and trivially — e.g. simply omitting `messages` from its template causes `handleTrigger` to bounce with `"no messages"` on every trigger it ever receives: [4](#0-3) 

Deploying such an "always-bounce" AA is fully permissionless: any actor can post a unit containing an `app: 'definition'` message defining an arbitrary `['autonomous agent', {...}]` definition, since AA definitions carry no signing keys and are activated purely by their deterministic `chash160` address: [5](#0-4) 

Combining these facts: if a lending/vault/liquidation AA design stores a user-controlled "borrower" or "beneficiary" address (set at position-open time, or taken from `trigger.data`) and later sends collateral, remaining balance, or liquidation proceeds to that address via a `payment` message, an attacker who ensures that address is the deterministic address of a pre-deployed always-bounce AA can force **every** attempt to pay them (including third-party-triggered liquidation) to cascade-revert the whole response. This is structurally identical to the Gearbox `WETHGateway.unwrapWETH()`-reverts-on-`receive()` bug: a hostile "recipient" that intentionally rejects an incoming transfer to indefinitely block a settlement/liquidation flow that must pay it.

### Impact Explanation
This falls under "AA fund loss or freezing" from the accepted-impact list: a position/vault controlled by an attacker-designated recipient AA becomes permanently non-liquidatable/non-closable, because any attempt by the protocol (whether by the borrower, a liquidator, or an automated process) to route funds to that recipient triggers a full revert of the settling AA's response, undoing collateral seizure, debt write-off, and any other state changes intended by that operation. Since the failure is deterministic and reproducible on every invocation, this is not a transient DoS but an indefinite freeze of correct protocol operation for that position, and by extension a freeze/lock of the funds the protocol is meant to redistribute (e.g., undercollateralized debt can never be recovered).

### Likelihood Explanation
The steps require no special privilege: (1) precompute the `chash160` of a trivial always-bouncing AA definition, (2) deploy it via a normal `app: 'definition'` message, (3) ensure that address becomes the registered "borrower"/"recipient" identity used by the target lending/vault AA before opening a position. This pattern (paying back to a caller-supplied or previously-recorded address) is common practice in Oscript AA design for lending, escrow, and liquidation protocols, since AAs frequently return remaining/refund amounts to `trigger.address` or a stored beneficiary address, exactly mirroring the WETHGateway refund-to-borrower pattern in the Gearbox report.

### Recommendation
- Document and strongly discourage AA developers from allowing liquidation/closing code paths to depend on unconditional success of a payment to an externally-controlled address; provide/standardize a "safe transfer" pattern in Oscript guides that separates the state-finalizing step (marking a position closed/liquidated) from the best-effort transfer step, so a bounce in the latter cannot roll back the former.
- Consider giving `handleSecondaryTriggers` an option (or protocol convention) to isolate/quarantine failures of specific secondary AA branches (e.g., treat as "fire and forget" beyond a fee-bearing amount) rather than always fully reverting the entire primary response, at least for outputs explicitly marked as best-effort refunds.
- At minimum, update AA-development documentation and any linter/validator warnings to flag "payment to an address later usable as an arbitrary/attacker-influenced beneficiary in a liquidation/close code path" as a known revert-cascade risk analogous to the Gearbox `unwrapWETH` issue.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker computes `chash160(['autonomous agent', {}])` (no `messages` field) → `ADDR_H`. Any unit received by `ADDR_H` will hit the `template.messages` falsy branch and unconditionally `bounce('no messages')`. [6](#0-5) 
2. Attacker deploys `ADDR_H` as an AA by broadcasting/having any AA post an `app:'definition'` message for it (permissionless, no signature needed for AA definitions). [5](#0-4) 
3. Attacker opens a position with a target lending AA, supplying `ADDR_H` as the stored "borrower"/"beneficiary" address (via `trigger.data` or similar recorded state var), as demonstrated by the codebase's own pattern of forwarding funds to stored addresses in secondary triggers: [7](#0-6) 
4. When the lending AA later attempts liquidation/closing and sends the refund/collateral-remainder payment to `ADDR_H`, `handleSecondaryTriggers` invokes `ADDR_H` as a secondary trigger, which bounces; the outer `revert()` rolls back all of the primary AA's liquidation effects and forces it into `bounce()`, exactly as coded in: [2](#0-1) 
5. Every subsequent liquidation attempt against this position deterministically fails the same way, permanently freezing the position/funds.
