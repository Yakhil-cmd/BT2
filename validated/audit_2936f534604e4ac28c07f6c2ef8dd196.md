### Title
Seller front-runs a pending position-NFT transfer by max-borrowing and withdrawing collateral, leaving the buyer a debt-laden account - (File: contracts/position-nft/src/contract.rs)

### Summary
A lending account's entire economic state — collateral plus outstanding debt — travels with the position NFT. When an owner approves a buyer or marketplace contract to pull the token via `transfer_from`, there is a window in which the current owner can still call `borrow` and `withdraw`, converting the position into a maximally levered, near-empty account just before the token moves. The buyer receives a fraction of the agreed collateral encumbered by debt it did not take on. This is the same bug class as the Opyn `transferRepoOwnership` front-run: ownership handoff is not atomic with a solvency/collateralization guarantee, and the outgoing owner retains full economic control until the transfer executes.

### Finding Description
The controller never stores an owner; `owner_of(token_id)` on the position NFT is resolved live on every call, so whoever holds the token at the moment of a `borrow`/`withdraw` is fully authorized [1](#0-0) . Ownership moves with a plain OpenZeppelin `transfer`/`transfer_from` — the inherited `NonFungibleToken` interface is exported unchanged with no transfer hook, no two-step acceptance, and no collateralization check at handoff [2](#0-1) . `approve` and `approve_for_all` give a third party (buyer escrow, marketplace, OTC settlement contract) the right to pull the token later [3](#0-2) .

Until that pull lands, the seller remains the live owner and can invoke `borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` and `withdraw(caller, account_id, withdrawals, to)` — both gated only by "NFT owner/delegate" and a post-check that LTV-weighted collateral covers debt with HF ≥ 1 [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . The seller can therefore extract borrowed assets to `to: Some(seller)` plus withdraw all collateral consistent with HF ≈ 1, then let the pre-approved `transfer_from` deliver the depleted, max-debt NFT to the buyer. The docs confirm the token carries the debt: "the account carries both collateral and debt ... an underwater position stays liquidatable after transfer" [6](#0-5) . There is no lock, snapshot, or acceptance step binding the account's state to what the buyer agreed to purchase.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. The buyer pays (off-chain or through a settlement contract pulling `transfer_from`) for a position worth its collateral, but receives collateral minus whatever the seller borrowed out and withdrew — up to nearly the entire collateral value, leaving a position at HF ≈ 1 that may already be liquidatable by the time the buyer can act. The loss is bounded by the position's borrow capacity but can be the majority of its value.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only two unprivileged actions the current owner is always permitted to take (`borrow`/`withdraw`) plus the standard `approve` → `transfer_from` flow that any NFT marketplace or OTC escrow necessarily uses. Stellar transaction ordering lets the seller place the draining transactions in a ledger before the settlement transaction executes; the only precondition is a negotiated sale of a position NFT, which is the natural way such positions change hands. Documented behavior ("approval hands over the whole account", "delegates lapse on transfer") addresses the buyer's incoming authority, not the seller's residual authority to drain between approval and transfer [7](#0-6) .

### Recommendation
Offer a settlement-safe path: either (a) a controller entrypoint that transfers the NFT only if a caller-supplied health-factor/collateral floor still holds (atomic condition check), or (b) a two-step handoff where the proposed owner accepts and the outgoing owner's mutating verbs are frozen once a transfer is pending. Absent a code change, document that buyers must measure the account's collateral and debt in the same transaction as the `transfer_from` pull (e.g., an escrow contract that asserts `get_health_factor`/`get_collateral_amount` against agreed minimums inside one call), since approval grants a time window, not a state snapshot.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice owns account `id` with $10,000 USDC collateral and no debt. She lists it via an escrow contract: calls `position_nft.approve(escrow, id, ledger+N)`.
2. Escrow is scheduled to pay Bob's $9,000 and call `transfer_from(escrow, alice, bob, id)`.
3. Before the settlement transaction lands, Alice submits `borrow(alice, id, [(ETH, 3.0)], Some(alice))` followed by `withdraw(alice, id, [(USDC, 8_000e7)], Some(alice))` — both authorized because `owner_of(id)` is still Alice and the post-check only requires HF ≥ 1 [5](#0-4) .
4. The escrow's `transfer_from` succeeds (approval is still live; `transfer_from` checks spender auth and token existence only [8](#0-7) ). Bob now holds an account with ~$2,000 collateral against ~$9,000+ equivalent debt; Alice keeps the borrowed ETH and withdrawn USDC plus the sale proceeds.
