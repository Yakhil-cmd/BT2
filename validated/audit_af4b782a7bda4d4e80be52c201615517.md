### Title
Liquidators can selectively withhold or supply the in-band Pyth `price-feeds` update to pick whichever valid price maximizes their liquidation profit at the borrower's expense - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate` accepts an optional `price-feeds` parameter that lets the caller push a fresh Pyth update in the same transaction, but this is fully optional and the caller decides whether to include it or not [1](#0-0) . Because the oracle only enforces a per-asset staleness window (up to `MAX-STALENESS` = 120s in production) rather than requiring the freshest available price, both the stale on-chain-stored price and any newer off-chain price the liquidator chooses to submit are equally "valid" [2](#0-1) . A permissionless liquidator therefore controls which of two legitimately-fresh prices is used to value the borrower's collateral/debt, and can pick whichever one maximizes the liquidation penalty/seized collateral extracted from the borrower — the exact bug class described in the Flatmoney report (keeper chooses whether to update the oracle before executing, biasing the outcome against the victim).

### Finding Description
`liquidate` is permissionless — `contract-caller` becomes the `liquidator`, distinct from `borrower` (the victim) [3](#0-2) . It processes the optional `price-feeds` argument through `write-feeds`, which is a no-op when `none` is passed: [4](#0-3) 

The actual price used for the LTV / penalty computation comes from `price-resolve`, which only asserts that the timestamp is within `max-staleness` seconds of block time and monotonic — it does not require the price to be the newest one available off-chain: [5](#0-4) 

`max-staleness` is configured per asset (production value 120 seconds, per `MAX-STALENESS`) [6](#0-5) . This means at any point within that 120-second window there can be two "fresh" prices for the same asset: the one already stored on-chain (posted by anyone earlier) and a newer one available from Pyth's off-chain Hermes feed that nobody has pushed on-chain yet. Both would pass `price-resolve`'s check.

The liquidation math directly consumes this price to compute `current-ltv`, whether the position is even eligible for liquidation, and — for eligible positions — the graduated penalty tier via `calc-liquidation-params`, and the final swapped amounts via `calc-final-liquidation-amounts`: [7](#0-6) 

Since the liquidator alone decides whether to submit `price-feeds` (fresh update) or pass `none` (use the last stored on-chain price), they can:
- Withhold an update when the stale stored price is more unfavorable to the borrower (e.g., lower collateral price → higher apparent LTV → bigger liquidation percentage / higher penalty tier), even though the true current price would show the position healthier or in a lower-penalty tier; or
- Submit a fresh update carrying whichever off-chain price is worse for the borrower, if that price is more unfavorable than what's already stored.

Either way, the liquidator picks, among the set of prices that are all "valid" per the staleness/monotonicity check, the one that maximizes their own profit (bigger `debt-final`/`coll-actual` seized, or an eligibility flip from healthy to liquidatable) — this is a decision made unilaterally by an unprivileged third party and directly determines how much of the borrower's collateral is taken. This is structurally identical to the Flatmoney H-6 pattern: keepers "choose whether to update the Pyth price to the latest price or not, depending on whether it is in their favor," at the cost of the counter-party.

### Impact Explanation
The liquidator's choice can (a) push a position that is not truly liquidatable into the liquidatable state, or (b) push an already-liquidatable position into a higher liquidation-percentage/penalty tier than the "true" current price would justify. In both cases the borrower loses collateral beyond what an accurate, unbiased price would produce — a direct, permanent loss of the borrower's funds (collateral seized with an inflated penalty) triggered by another unprivileged party's price choice. This falls under theft/permanent loss of user funds (collateral) due to price manipulation enabled by the protocol's own optional price-feed design, not by any third-party oracle defect.

### Likelihood Explanation
Liquidation is fully permissionless and the `price-feeds` argument is optional by design ("allows for backward compatibility") [4](#0-3) . Any liquidator bot can trivially compare the currently on-chain-stored price against the latest Hermes price before crafting the transaction and simply omit `price-feeds` (or include a specific one) whenever that is more profitable. No special access or race condition is required beyond normal price volatility within the 120-second staleness window, making this readily exploitable by ordinary keeper/liquidator infrastructure.

### Recommendation
Do not allow liquidation (and other victim-affecting flows) to rely on an optionally-stale, already-stored price when a plausibly different valid price exists within the staleness window. Options: require `liquidate` to always be paired with a fresh price update (make `price-feeds` mandatory, not optional, for calls that affect a borrower other than the caller), and/or drastically shrink the acceptable staleness window for liquidation specifically so on-chain-stored vs. off-chain-latest price divergence is economically negligible, and/or add a price-deviation guard between the price actually used and an independent reference (e.g., DIA) before finalizing the liquidation penalty tier.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice borrows USDC against sBTC collateral; her position sits close to (but under) the partial-liquidation LTV threshold at the currently stored on-chain BTC price P0 (posted, say, 100 seconds ago — still "fresh" per `MAX-STALENESS` u120).
2. In the intervening 100 seconds BTC's true off-chain price has dropped to P1 < P0, which would push Alice's LTV over the partial-liquidation threshold, or into a higher-penalty tier than P0 would.
3. Charlie (a permissionless liquidator) observes both P0 (already stored via `last-update`/`pyth-storage-v4`) and P1 (available from Hermes but not yet posted). Charlie calls `liquidate(alice, sbtc, usdc, amount, min-expected, none, (some [P1-update]))`, deliberately supplying the update carrying P1 because it yields a larger `liq-penalty`/`debt-final-usd` than P0 would have — see `liquidate`/`calc-liquidation-params`/`calc-final-liquidation-amounts` at [8](#0-7) . Both P0 and P1 pass `price-resolve`'s freshness/monotonicity check [5](#0-4) , so the contract accepts whichever Charlie chose to push.
4. Alice loses more sBTC collateral (higher penalty/percentage) than an unbiased, instantaneous price would have produced, purely because Charlie selected the more damaging of two "valid" prices when deciding whether to include `price-feeds`.

```text
(define-private (price-resolve
  (data { type: (buff 1), ident: (buff 32), callcode: (optional (buff 1)), max-staleness: uint }))
  (let ((type (get type data))
        (ident (get ident data))
        (key { type: type, ident: ident })
        (resolution (try! (resolve-price-feed type ident)))
        (price (get value resolution))
        (callcode (get callcode data))
        (final-price (try! (resolve-callcode price callcode)))
        (last-update-time (oracle-last-update key))
        (timestamp (get timestamp resolution))
        (max-staleness (get max-staleness data)))

    ;; validate price and timestamp using max-staleness from oracle data
    (asserts! (and (oracle-price-legal final-price) (oracle-timestamp-fresh timestamp last-update-time max-staleness))
              ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT)

    ;; update timestamp if newer
    (if (> timestamp last-update-time)
        (map-set last-update key timestamp)
        false)

    (ok final-price)))
```

```text
(define-public (liquidate
                (borrower principal)
                (collateral-ft <ft-trait>)
                (debt-ft <ft-trait>)
                (debt-amount uint)
                (min-collateral-expected uint)
                (collateral-receiver (optional principal))
                (price-feeds (optional (list 3 (buff 8192)))))
  (let (
    (feeds-check (try! (write-feeds price-feeds)))
    (liquidator contract-caller)
    (position (try! (get-liquidation-position borrower)))
    (pos-full (try! (get-full-position borrower)))
    (mask (get mask position))
    (group (try! (get-egroup mask)))

    (coll-address (contract-of collateral-ft))
    (debt-address (contract-of debt-ft))
    (coll-asset (try! (get-asset coll-address)))
    (debt-asset (try! (get-asset debt-address)))
    (coll-aid (get id coll-asset))
    (debt-aid (get id debt-asset))

    ;; accrue FIRST - populates cache for zToken price resolution
    (u-debt (accrue-user-debts (get debt pos-full)))
    (u-coll (accrue-user-collateral (get collateral pos-full)))

    ;; NOW safe to resolve prices (cache is populated)
    (assets (get-assets mask))
    (notional-valued-assets (get-notional-evaluation { position: position, assets: assets }))
    (total-collateral-usd (get collateral notional-valued-assets))
    (total-debt-usd (get debt notional-valued-assets))

    ;; LTC thresholds, liq params, health
    (ltv-liq-partial (buff-to-uint-be (get LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL group)))
    (ltv-liq-full (buff-to-uint-be (get LTV-LIQ-FULL group)))
    (liq-penalty-min (buff-to-uint-be (get LIQ-PENALTY-MIN group)))
    (liq-penalty-max (buff-to-uint-be (get LIQ-PENALTY-MAX group)))
    (curve-exponent (buff-to-uint-be (get LIQ-CURVE-EXP group)))

    ;; LTV = (debt x 10,000) / collateral
    ;; handle edge case: If collateral = 0, return max LTV (BPS) or 0 if debt also 0
    (current-ltv   (if (is-eq total-collateral-usd u0)
                       (if (is-eq total-debt-usd u0) u0 BPS)
                       (mul-div-down total-debt-usd BPS total-collateral-usd)))
    
    ;; Oracle frontrunning protection: prevent same-block liquidation
    ;; This blocks flash-loan based attacks where user borrows + gets liquidated in same block
    (last-borrow-block (get last-borrow-block position))
    (same-block-check (asserts! (not (is-eq last-borrow-block stacks-block-height)) ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK))

    ;; health check (FAIL-FAST) 
    ;; Check position is liquidatable BEFORE calling calc-liq-factor
    (health-check  (asserts! (>= current-ltv ltv-liq-partial) ERR-HEALTHY))

    ;; liquidation parameters (graduated liquidation calculation)
    (liq-params (calc-liquidation-params 
                  current-ltv ltv-liq-partial ltv-liq-full
                  liq-penalty-min liq-penalty-max 
                  curve-exponent total-debt-usd))
    (liq-pct-scaled (get liq-pct-scaled liq-params))
    (liq-penalty (get liq-penalty liq-params))
    (max-debt-usd (get max-debt-usd liq-params))

    ;; debt processing
    (debt-info (process-debt-asset debt-amount debt-aid max-debt-usd assets))
    (debt-actual-usd (get debt-actual-usd debt-info))
    (debt-actual (get debt-actual debt-info))
    (debt-price (get debt-price debt-info))
    (debt-decimals (get debt-decimals debt-info))

    ;; collateral processing
    (user-coll-balance (find-collateral-amount (get collateral pos-full) coll-aid))
    (coll-info (process-collateral-asset coll-aid debt-actual-usd liq-penalty 
                                         user-coll-balance assets coll-asset))
    (coll-actual (get coll-actual coll-info))
    (coll-expected (get coll-expected coll-info))
    (coll-price (get coll-price coll-info))
    (coll-decimals (get coll-decimals coll-info))

    ;; final liquidation amounts (with proportional adjustment if needed)
    (final-amounts (calc-final-liquidation-amounts
                     debt-actual-usd coll-actual coll-expected
                     coll-price coll-decimals
                     debt-price debt-decimals liq-penalty))
    (debt-final-usd (get debt-final-usd final-amounts))
    (debt-final (get debt-final final-amounts))
```
