### Title
Transient collateral price can permanently stamp worse liquidation parameters onto borrower accounts - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs))

### Summary
An unprivileged caller can invoke `update_account_threshold(caller, true, account_ids)` for another account and permanently write that account’s liquidation threshold, bonus, and fees from the current spoke configuration. When the new tuple favors liquidators, the gate checks only the current spot-price health factor under the proposed threshold, so a temporary collateral-price increase can make the account appear sufficiently healthy even though it would fail the same gate at its normal valuation. The adverse tuple remains stored after the price falls, potentially moving the account immediately below the liquidation boundary. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`update_account_threshold` is permissionless for any authenticated caller and accepts arbitrary account IDs rather than requiring authorization from each account owner. [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1) 

For every listed supply asset, `sync_account_thresholds` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params`, which always copies the live LTV and, under `FullTuple`, attempts to copy the live liquidation threshold, bonus, and fees. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

A tuple is treated as favoring the liquidator when it lowers the liquidation threshold, raises the bonus, or lowers the fees. [6](#0-5) 

For an account with debt, the only protection against such a change is `clears_min_hf`, which substitutes the proposed threshold and calculates health factor using the prices cached at that instant. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

Once `clears_min_hf` returns true, the full adverse tuple is assigned and subsequently persisted to the account’s supply positions; no later price decline causes the protocol to restore the prior borrower-favorable tuple. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

Consequently, the gate is not a sustained-solvency check: a short-lived price increase that raises the hypothetical health factor above `1.05` is enough to latch parameters that would not have passed the gate immediately before or after that price excursion. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
A borrower can permanently lose the protection of a higher liquidation threshold because a third party opportunistically refreshes the account during a temporary collateral-price spike. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) 

After the price returns to its prior level, the stored lower threshold remains in the position and is used to calculate weighted collateral and health factor. [15](#0-14) 

The borrower can then become liquidatable even though the same account would have remained above `HF = 1` if the older liquidation threshold had been retained. [16](#0-15) 

The result is a preventable loss of collateral to a liquidator and can include a liquidation bonus, making this a theft-of-user-funds scenario rather than merely an accounting inconsistency. [17](#0-16) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires a listed supply position whose live liquidation tuple is more favorable to liquidators than the tuple currently stored on the position and whose hypothetical health factor is close to the `1.05` cutoff. [18](#0-17) 

This state can arise after a parameter change because existing positions retain their stored liquidation tuple until an eligible path refreshes it. [19](#0-18) 

The attacker does not need account-owner authorization: any caller can authenticate as `caller`, pass the victim’s account ID, and use `has_risks = true`. [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1) 

The attacker can monitor oracle updates or volatile market conditions and submit the refresh only during the block or ledger in which the victim’s spot health factor exceeds the gate. [8](#0-7) [20](#0-19) 

The likelihood is therefore bounded by the availability of a pending adverse parameter tuple and sufficient oracle volatility, but no special privilege, leaked key, malformed parameter, or victim cooperation is needed. [2](#0-1) 

### Recommendation
Do not let a single spot-price observation permanently approve a liquidation-parameter change that favors liquidators.

Use a time-weighted or otherwise conservative valuation for `clears_min_hf`, or maintain a pending tuple and apply it only after the hypothetical health factor has remained above `1.05` for a defined observation period. [7](#0-6) 

Alternatively, require authorization from the account owner or delegate before applying a tuple classified by `favors_liquidator`, while retaining the permissionless refresh path for tuples that do not worsen the borrower’s liquidation terms. [6](#0-5) 

The implementation should also emit enough event data to distinguish a merely evaluated refresh from a persisted liquidation-tuple update, since the current security boundary is the one-time write at lines 90–92 and 217–218. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) 

### Proof of Concept
Assume an account supplies `$8,000` of volatile collateral, borrows `$5,000` of debt, and has a stored liquidation threshold of `8000` bps.

The account’s normal health factor is `0.80 × 8000 / 5000 = 1.28`, so it is not liquidatable under its stored tuple. [15](#0-14) [21](#0-20) 

A live configuration now specifies liquidation threshold `6100` bps for that collateral, while the victim has not yet been restamped. [22](#0-21) 

At the normal `$8,000` collateral valuation, the proposed threshold would produce `HF = 0.61 × 8000 / 5000 = 0.976`, so applying it would make the account liquidatable rather than merely tighten its parameters. [11](#0-10) [21](#0-20) 

A temporary collateral-price increase to `$8,640` raises the hypothetical health factor to `0.61 × 8640 / 5000 = 1.05408`, which passes the `1.05` gate. [7](#0-6) 

An attacker submits:

```text
update_account_threshold(
    caller = attacker,
    has_risks = true,
    account_ids = [victim_account_id]
)
```

Because the gate sees the temporarily elevated price, `apply_gated_liquidation_params` writes `liquidation_threshold = 6100` into the victim’s supply position and `set_supply_positions` persists it. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) 

When the collateral price returns to `$8,000`, the stored `6100` bps threshold produces `HF = 0.976`, and `build_liquidation_plan` accepts the account for liquidation because its health factor is below `1`. [15](#0-14) [16](#0-15) 

The prior `8000` bps threshold is never restored, so the temporary price movement permanently converts a healthy account into a liquidatable one. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13)

/// Refreshes the liquidation threshold, bonus, and fees together. With debt,
/// changes favoring liquidators require hypothetical health factor >= 1.05.
pub(crate) fn apply_gated_liquidation_params(
    env: &Env,
    cache: &mut Context,
    account: &Account,
    hub_asset: &HubAssetKey,
    position: &mut AccountPosition,
    effective_config: &AssetConfig,
) {
    if favors_liquidator(position, effective_config)
        && !account.debt_free()
        && !clears_min_hf(
            env,
            cache,
            account,
            hub_asset,

            effective_config.liquidation_threshold,

        return;

    position.liquidation_threshold = effective_config.liquidation_threshold;
    position.liquidation_bonus = effective_config.liquidation_bonus;
