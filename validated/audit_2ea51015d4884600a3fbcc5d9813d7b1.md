### Title
Permanent market freeze via i128 value overflow during interest accrual on a large high-utilization market - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The MySQL CVE class is a remotely triggerable crash causing a complete denial of service. The analog in XOXNO Lending is a permanent, state-level crash: once a market's accrued RAY-domain value exceeds `i128::MAX`, `global_sync` panics inside `scaled_to_original` with `MathOverflow` before any operation body runs. Because every pool verb (withdraw, repay, borrow, seize, net_settle, claim_revenue, recapitalize) accrues the market first, the entire market freezes forever — suppliers can never withdraw, borrowers can never repay, and liquidators can never seize. There is no recovery path: accrual runs inside the same call, so the panic cannot be bypassed, and `recapitalize`/`clean_bad_debt` accrue through the same code path. The borrow-index ceiling (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36`) never engages because the value overflow fires first.

### Finding Description
Accrual is driven by `global_sync` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33), which loops `accrue_chunk` and calls `common::rates::accrue_step` on the cached `borrowed`/`supplied` share counts and indexes. Utilization and all position conversions go through `scaled_to_original` (contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26, via `common::rates`), which multiplies `scaled_shares (RAY) * index (RAY)` — an `i128` checked multiply. When `supplied * supply_index` or `borrowed * borrow_index` no longer fits `i128`, the multiply panics with `MathOverflow` (error 33).

Two properties make this reachable by unprivileged callers rather than hypothetical:

1. The admitted token→RAY domain (`i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)`) lets a whale supply roughly 170 billion whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset (docs/reference/formulas.md:429). Supply and borrow caps are governance-settable up to that same admitted maximum, so no privileged cooperation is required beyond an ordinary listing.
2. At high sustained borrow utilization (the test uses 98%), the borrow index compounds past ~170x the initial RAY well before the `1e36` index cap, and the very next `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original` overflows. `update_indexes` is permissionless, so anyone can deliver the fatal accrual transaction — but the freeze is also triggered by the next user verb.

The pinned reproduction is `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360): after the cliff, `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, confirming "the market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic."

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and a market unable to operate — matching the accepted "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate" impact classes. Every supplier's deposit, every borrower's collateral backing this debt, and all unclaimed revenue in that market become unreachable, including to liquidators, so underwater positions cannot be closed and the bad debt is permanently borne by the book. The freeze is not temporary: no entrypoint skips `global_sync`, and the panic reverts the whole call, so no partial state change (repay, seize, recapitalize, index clamp) can ever be committed.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium, consistent with the 4.9 source CVE. The attack requires: a listed market whose governance-set caps admit deposits near the token→RAY domain maximum (the caps *allow* this — `calculate_scaled_cap` saturates rather than rejects at that boundary, docs/reference/formulas.md:411-417), a whale willing to park ~170B-unit supply, a borrower pushing utilization high enough for the steep segment of the rate curve, and enough elapsed time for compounding (bounded by `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` per chunk; the test reaches the cliff in under 40 simulated years at 98% utilization on a steep curve). Costly and slow, but fully within unprivileged entrypoints (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`), needs no oracle manipulation, no privileged action, and no leaked key — and once reached it is irreversible.

### Recommendation
Enforce the RAY-domain value ceiling as an invariant rather than a documented numeric limit:
- Cap admitted supply/borrow caps (or runtime total `supplied`/`borrowed` scaled totals) so that `scaled_shares * index` can never approach `i128::MAX` before the `1e36` index ceiling engages — i.e., clamp admitted caps to `i128::MAX / MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`-style headroom per decimals.
- In `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, saturate total-value computation at `i128::MAX` (or clamp the borrow index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* the value multiply) so accrual degrades (interest stops accruing at the cap) instead of panicking. The borrow index already has a ceiling; extending the same clamp-before-multiply discipline to the value conversions preserves withdraw/repay/liquidation liveness.
- Emit an event or expose a view when utilization/index approach the domain bound so caps can be lowered before the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists `BIG18` (18 decimals) with caps at the admitted maximum; also lists a collateral market `COL`.
2. Attacker calls `supply` with `~1e29` base units (~170B whole tokens) — inside `max_cap_for_decimals(18)`.
3. Attacker supplies collateral and calls `borrow` for 98% of `BIG18` liquidity, passing `require_liquidation_buffer`/`require_reserves`.
4. Time advances; anyone calls `update_indexes([BIG18])` repeatedly. When `borrowed * borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX`, `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` panics `MathOverflow`.
5. From that ledger onward every call touching the market — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue` — reverts with error 33 at the accrual preamble, and the market is permanently bricked (verified by tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:343-357).

Note: this condition is acknowledged as a numeric limit in docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437 ("value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal"), but it is documented as an unmanaged risk to integrators rather than an ADR-accepted design, and its impact (irreversible freeze of all user funds in the market) is not itself waived anywhere.
