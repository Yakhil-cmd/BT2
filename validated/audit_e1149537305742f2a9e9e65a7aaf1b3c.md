### Title
Permanent market freeze: i128 overflow in `scaled_to_original` during accrual bricks every pool verb — ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
Analog of CVE-2018-2600 (reachable hang/crash → complete DoS). In XOXNO Lending, every state-changing pool entrypoint runs `interest::global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` before doing any work. Once `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, that multiplication panics with `MathOverflow` forever — no repay, withdraw, supply, liquidation, or bad-debt cleanup can ever execute again on that market. All supplier cash in the market is permanently frozen.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60` computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)`, a RAY multiplication that panics on `i128` overflow via `Ray::mul`/`checked_add_raw` (`common/src/math/fp.rs:13`). The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (1e36 raw = 1e9 RAY), but the value product overflows long before the cap binds: a book of ~1e36 raw borrowed shares (1 billion whole tokens at 18 decimals, ~1e9 RAY) only needs the index to grow past ~170× for `borrowed × borrow_index` to exceed `i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38`. The production test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321`) demonstrates exactly this: at 98% utilization on the XLM curve the index crosses the cliff within a few years, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` attempts fail identically — confirmed at lines 354–356. Because `last_timestamp` is only advanced after the loop completes (`global_sync` at `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`), the panic is not transient; every later call re-runs the same overflowing step. Smaller books reach the same cliff at correspondingly larger index multiples, and liquidation cannot rescue the market because `seize_positions` also accrues first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: all supplied cash in the affected market becomes unreachable — suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot seize, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` accrue before mutating. The protocol has no escape hatch that skips accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger is permissionless: any address can supply and borrow to push a market toward the cliff (the attacker supplies the liquidity and another account they control holds the debt at high utilization). It requires whale-scale nominal balances or many years of compounding at high utilization on a steep rate curve, so likelihood is low — hence Medium, matching the source's medium-severity crash/DoS class.

### Recommendation
Cap the value product rather than letting it panic: in `accrue_step`, saturate `scaled_to_original`/`update_borrow_index` so that the borrow index is clamped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* the `borrowed × index` product overflows (e.g., compute the cap as `min(index, i128::MAX / borrowed)`), or widen the debt valuation to I256 inside accrual so the index cap engages instead of the arithmetic ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-360`: supply `1e9` whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98% of it, advance time year-by-year; `try_update_indexes_for(["BIG18"]` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and `try_withdraw_raw`/`try_repay` revert with the same error — market permanently frozen.
