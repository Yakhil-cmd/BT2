### Title
Fee-on-transfer / short-transfer underlying token causes `assets` accounting to overstate real vault balance, freezing other depositors' funds - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`)

### Summary
All six ZEST vault contracts credit the shared `assets` accounting variable with the caller-supplied `amount` parameter of `deposit`, without ever verifying that the vault actually received that amount of underlying tokens. This is the same root cause as the Velodrome `Bribe.sol` finding: a value is recorded into shared state based on the transfer *request* rather than the transfer *result*, and that shared state is later relied upon by other, unrelated parties.

### Finding Description
In `deposit`, the vault calls `receive-underlying` and then unconditionally does `(var-set assets (+ current-assets amount))` using the nominal `amount`, never comparing it to the vault's actual token balance: [1](#0-0) 

`receive-underlying` simply forwards to the underlying SIP-010 token's `transfer` and trusts its return value, but a SIP-010 `transfer` returning `(ok true)` says nothing about whether the full `amount` reached the vault's balance (e.g. a token with an internal fee, blacklist-partial-transfer, or rebasing/deflationary behavior would still return `ok` while delivering less than `amount`): [2](#0-1) 

The `assets` variable is not a per-user value — it is the shared denominator/numerator used by every other participant's share pricing (`total-assets-preview`, `convert-to-shares-preview`, `convert-to-assets-preview`) and by the redeem-time liquidity check (`get-available-assets`): [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Once `assets` is inflated beyond the vault's real token balance, `redeem` calculates `inkind` (assets to pay out) from the inflated `assets`/`total-supply-preview` ratio and only checks `available-assets` (also derived from the inflated `assets`) before calling `send-underlying`: [5](#0-4) 

The contract also separately exposes a raw balance getter `ubalance`, proving the code base is aware that "recorded assets" and "actual token balance" can diverge, yet `ubalance` is never used to reconcile or cap `assets` after a deposit: [6](#0-5) 

**Attacker / Victim / shared state:**
- Attacker: any depositor who deposits (or is forced to deposit, e.g. via a token that later becomes deflationary/fee-charging, or any underlying token behavior that delivers less than `amount`) using the vault's underlying token.
- Victim: any other liquidity provider or redeemer of the same vault.
- Shared state: `assets` var (and everything derived from it — `total-assets-preview`, conversion rates, `get-available-assets`).

**Outcome with vs without the attacker's transaction:**
- Without the deposit-amount/actual-received mismatch: `assets` accurately tracks the vault's real underlying balance; all depositors receive shares priced correctly, and all redeemers can be paid in full up to `available-assets`.
- With the mismatch: `assets` is permanently inflated above the real token balance held by the vault. Later redeemers computing `inkind` from the inflated ratio will attempt to withdraw more underlying than physically exists in the vault; at least one redeemer's `send-underlying` call will fail (transaction revert) or, if partial redemptions have already drained the real balance, some depositors will be permanently unable to redeem their full pro-rata share — a socialized shortfall imposed on strangers by one caller's deposit.

### Impact Explanation
This lands on **temporary/permanent freezing of funds** for other, unrelated depositors: the shared `assets`/share-price state becomes desynchronized from the real underlying balance, so honest users who deposited before or after the discrepancy-causing deposit can be blocked from redeeming their full entitled amount, and the vault's insolvency (assets recorded > assets held) is borne collectively by all shareholders, not just the depositor who caused it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends entirely on whether any of the underlying SIP-010 tokens used by these six vaults (`usdcx`, `usdh-token-v1`, and the sBTC/stSTX/stSTXbtc/STX equivalents) ever exhibits fee-on-transfer, deflationary, or partial-delivery-on-transfer behavior. I was not able to verify from the indexed contracts here whether any of these specific tokens currently implement such behavior — SIP-010 as a standard does not forbid it, and Clarity's `ft-transfer?` primitive only guarantees an atomic amount move when both parties use the *same* token's native balance, but these vaults call an external contract's `transfer`, whose internal accounting is outside the vault's control and could change with a token upgrade/migration. Given the current design blindly trusts `amount` rather than measuring actual balance delta, any future or unnoticed transfer-fee behavior in the underlying token immediately becomes exploitable/triggerable by any single caller, without requiring privileged access.

### Recommendation
In every vault's `deposit` (and `receive-underlying`), measure the vault's underlying balance before and after the transfer (or use `ubalance` before/after) and credit `assets` with the actual delta received rather than the nominal `amount` argument. Symmetrically, `redeem`/`send-underlying` should verify the outgoing transfer actually reduced the vault balance by the expected amount, and any shortfall should be reflected in `assets` immediately (not discovered only when a later redeemer's transfer fails) so the loss is not silently socialized across unrelated depositors.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume (hypothetically) that the underlying token used by one of the vaults (e.g. `usdcx` in `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) is or becomes a token whose `transfer` delivers less than the requested `amount` to the recipient (fee-on-transfer, deflationary, etc.).
2. User A calls `deposit(amount=1000, ...)`. `receive-underlying` invokes the token's `transfer`; only 980 units actually land in the vault contract's balance, but `(ok true)` is returned. [7](#0-6) 
3. `assets` is incremented by the full `1000`, not the `980` actually received: [8](#0-7) 
4. `total-assets-preview`/`convert-to-shares-preview` for all subsequent depositors and `convert-to-assets-preview`/`get-available-assets` for all redeemers now operate on a value 20 units higher than the vault's real token balance.
5. When later redeemers collectively attempt to withdraw the vault's full recorded `assets`, the last redeemer(s)' `send-underlying` call reverts (insufficient real balance) or, in partial-liquidity vaults, the shortfall is absorbed by whichever redeemer is last in line — a loss caused entirely by User A's deposit, borne by unrelated third parties.
