## Finding

### Title
Borrowers are incentivized to self-liquidate instead of repaying when a position is underwater, socializing the discount as bad debt onto vault lenders - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The `liquidate` function in `market.clar` places no restriction on the caller relative to the `borrower` being liquidated [1](#0-0) , and `collateral-receiver` defaults to the liquidator itself when unspecified [2](#0-1) . When a borrower's position is already underwater (debt USD value ≥ collateral USD value, i.e. LTV ≥ 100%), the liquidation math lets the caller repay less debt than is actually owed while still seizing 100% of the remaining collateral, and the residual debt is then written off ("socialized") across all lenders of that debt vault. This makes it more profitable for an underwater borrower to call `liquidate` on their own position than to close it through the ordinary `repay`/`collateral-remove` path, transferring the shortfall to other vault depositors instead of the borrower absorbing it.

### Finding Description
`calc-liquidation-params` caps `liq-pct-scaled` at `BPS` (100%) and derives `max-debt-usd` and `liq-penalty` from it [3](#0-2) . When `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-full` (deeply underwater), `liq-pct-scaled` saturates at `BPS`, so `max-debt-usd` = full outstanding debt and `liq-penalty` = `liq-penalty-max`.

`process-collateral-asset` then computes the collateral the caller should receive as `debt-repay × (BPS + liq-penalty) / BPS` [4](#0-3) , capped at the user's actual collateral balance when the position doesn't have enough collateral to cover the bonus [5](#0-4) .

Crucially, `calc-final-liquidation-amounts` then re-derives the debt actually repaid from the capped collateral using `calc-liq-debt-repay-real`, i.e. `collateral-usd / (BPS + liq-penalty)` [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) . This means when collateral is insufficient to cover debt-repaid-plus-bonus (LTV ≥ 100%), the caller pays a **discounted** debt amount (discounted by the penalty factor) while taking **all** the borrower's remaining collateral.

Because `borrower` and `liquidator` (`contract-caller`) are never checked to differ, a borrower can call `liquidate` against their own account, set `collateral-receiver` to themselves (the default), and thereby withdraw all their remaining collateral while only repaying `collateral-usd / (1 + liq-penalty-max)` worth of debt — strictly less than what ordinary `repay` would require to unlock the same collateral. The residual, un-repaid debt is then classified as `no-collateral-left` and socialized via `socialize-debt-asset` → `vault-socialize-debt`, which writes down the vault's `borrow-index`/reserves for that asset, spreading the loss across every lender/depositor of that debt vault [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) .

Compared to ordinary `repay`, which requires repaying the exact scaled debt (no discount) before any collateral can be freed [10](#0-9) , self-liquidation is strictly more favorable for an underwater borrower, and the difference is paid for by other principals — the vault's depositors — not by the borrower.

### Impact Explanation
This causes a shortfall in the shared debt vault that must be absorbed by all depositors of that vault (temporary/permanent freezing or dilution of their claimable yield/principal, since `socialize-debt-asset` reduces the value backing every outstanding zToken/deposit share in that vault) rather than by the borrower who created the shortfall. This is a theft-by-shortfall from one set of unprivileged principals (other vault lenders) caused by another unprivileged principal's (the borrower's) self-dealing call, landing in the "temporary/permanent freezing of funds" impact class since the socialized amount is deducted from the shared vault backing available to all suppliers.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any borrower whose LTV crosses `ltv-liq-partial` (and especially those who cross into `ltv-liq-full`/beyond, i.e. LTV ≥ 100%) can trivially front-run any genuine third-party liquidator by calling `liquidate` on their own `borrower` address in the same manner a real liquidator would, since there is no access-control differentiating "liquidator" from "borrower" and no check preventing `collateral-receiver` = borrower. This requires no privileged access, oracle manipulation, or flashloan — only a naturally occurring or self-induced (e.g. borrowing to the edge and waiting for a price move) underwater position.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit check in `liquidate` (and `liquidate-redeem`/`liquidate-multi`) that `contract-caller` (and/or `tx-sender`) is not equal to `borrower`, or otherwise disallow `collateral-receiver` being set to the borrower's own address when the position is being liquidated by itself. Additionally, consider disallowing the debt-discounting path (`calc-liq-debt-repay-real`) from applying when `contract-caller == borrower`, ensuring self-repayment always requires full debt value rather than the discounted liquidation-bonus math.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice borrows against collateral up to her egroup's max LTV.
2. Collateral price drops (or interest accrues) such that `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-full`, making her position eligible for the maximum `liq-penalty` and `liq-pct-scaled = BPS`.
3. Before any third-party liquidator acts, Alice (as `tx-sender`/`contract-caller`) calls `liquidate` with `borrower = <herself>`, `collateral-receiver = none` (defaults to herself as `liquidator`) [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) .
4. Because her collateral is insufficient to cover `debt-repay*(1+liq-penalty-max)`, `coll-actual` is capped to her entire remaining collateral, and `calc-final-liquidation-amounts` computes `debt-final-usd = coll-actual-usd / (1+liq-penalty-max)` — a discount versus her true debt [7](#0-6) .
5. Alice repays only this discounted amount via `vault-system-repay`, and receives all her remaining collateral back to herself.
6. Since `coll-removed` now leaves `no-collateral-left = true`, the remaining scaled debt is passed to `socialize-debt-asset`/`vault-socialize-debt`, which is charged against the debt vault's reserves/index, socializing the loss across all depositors of that vault [8](#0-7) .
7. Alice ends up having extracted her full collateral for less than its owed debt value, with the difference paid by other vault depositors — an outcome unreachable via the ordinary `repay` path, which would have required her to fully clear her debt before unlocking any collateral.

;; Returns: { debt-final-usd: uint, debt-final: uint }
(define-private (calc-final-liquidation-amounts
  (debt-actual-usd uint)

  (coll-decimals uint)
  (debt-price uint)
  (debt-decimals uint)

  (let ((coll-actual-usd (normalize (* coll-actual coll-price) coll-decimals false))
