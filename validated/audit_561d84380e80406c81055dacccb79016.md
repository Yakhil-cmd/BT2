### Title
Account delegate grants have no expiry and remain executable indefinitely under changed market conditions - (File: contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs)

### Summary
The original Teller issue concerned a lender commitment that could be accepted arbitrarily late, locking the lender into stale terms. The XOXNO Lending analog is the per-account delegate registry: `DelegateGrant` is written once by the account owner and stays live forever until the owner notices and calls `remove_delegate`. There is no `live_until_ledger`/deadline field on the grant, in contrast to position-NFT approvals (`approve`, `approve_for_all`) and ownership transfers, which all mandate an expiry ledger. A delegate can keep invoking controller actions (`borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`) on the account long after the conditions under which the grant was made have changed.

### Finding Description
`add_delegate` persists `DelegateGrant { granted_by, delegates }` under `ControllerKey::Delegates(account_id)` in persistent storage, and `renew_user_account` keeps renewing its TTL, so the grant never ages out:

- `contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs` (`get_delegates`, `add_delegate`, `remove_delegate`, `DelegateGrant` write) — no expiry/timestamp field exists on the grant; revocation is the only teardown.
- Contrast with `contracts/position-nft` where `Approval(token_id)` and `ApprovalForAll(owner, operator)` are temporary entries that expire at `live_until_ledger` (see `contracts/position-nft/README.md`, storage table), and governance ops that expire via `require_operation_not_expired` in `contracts/governance/src/timelock/mod.rs`.
- The only lifecycle safety is `granted_by` stamping: a grant is silently invalidated when the NFT changes hands, but for a *stable* owner nothing bounds the grant's lifetime.

So a delegate authorized once (e.g., a strategy bot or helper contract) retains full standing authority to open borrows or restructure collateral on the account at arbitrary later times, at whatever the current prices/indexes/rates happen to be — exactly the "commitment executed under stale assumptions" shape of the source report.

### Impact Explanation
A stale delegate can drive the account into an unfavorable state after market conditions move: borrow at a much worse collateral ratio, execute `multiply`/`swap_*` strategies that push health factor toward the liquidation curve, or restructure collateral so the position becomes liquidatable. The owner suffers realized loss via accrued debt interest, liquidation bonus leakage, or forced position unwinding — permanent loss of funds rather than a mere inconvenience. Because the grant never expires, the owner bears indefinite monitoring burden; a forgotten delegate (or one whose off-chain mandate lapsed) is a live key to the account's risk surface.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate. Granting a delegate is an explicit owner action and revocation is a single call, which bounds the exposure. However, the realistic failure mode from the source report — owner forgets about a standing authorization, or an automated agent acts on parameters that were sensible at grant time — is unmitigated by any on-chain expiry, and the delegation mechanism explicitly renews its own TTL so storage archival never disables it either. The asymmetry with NFT approvals (which the protocol forces to be time-bounded) suggests the missing expiry is an oversight rather than an ADR choice.

### Recommendation
Add an expiry ledger to `DelegateGrant` (e.g., `expires_at_ledger: u32`) and enforce it in `get_delegates`/the authorization check: grants with `expires_at_ledger < env.ledger().sequence()` are treated as empty and lazily deleted. Allow `0` (or `u32::MAX`) to mean "until revoked" if indefinite grants are desired, mirroring the report's recommendation that the grantor chooses their risk horizon. Alternatively, reuse the NFT `live_until_ledger` convention and require `add_delegate` to take an expiry argument.

### Proof of Concept
1. Owner of `account_id` calls `add_delegate(account_id, delegate)` — `DelegateGrant { granted_by: owner, delegates: [delegate] }` is written to persistent storage with no expiry field.
2. Ledgers advance arbitrarily far; prices and borrow indexes drift. `renew_user_account` (invoked by unrelated owner actions) keeps the grant's TTL alive indefinitely.
3. Long after the owner has forgotten the grant, `delegate` calls `borrow(account_id, hub_asset, amount)` or `multiply(account_id, ...)`. The delegate check reads `get_delegates` → `granted_by == owner` still holds → authorization succeeds.
4. The account now carries debt minted at post-drift terms; the owner's HF is lower and, if the delegate pushed past the liquidation threshold, liquidators can immediately seize collateral with bonus — a loss the owner never re-consented to after conditions changed.

Note: the severity depends on whether delegated `borrow`/`withdraw` proceeds can be routed to the delegate rather than the account owner; even when proceeds go to the owner, the forced debt/strategy execution still produces loss via interest and liquidation exposure, consistent with a Medium rating.
