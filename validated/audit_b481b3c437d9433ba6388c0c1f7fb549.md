## Title
Asset-level `transfer_condition`/`issue_condition` and AA-defined dynamic `is_transferrable` allow an asset issuer to indefinitely pause transfers, freezing funds/collateral in dependent AAs - ([File: validation.js], [File: aa_validation.js])

### Summary
Obyte's `ocore` asset model lets an asset definer attach a `transfer_condition`/`issue_condition` (an address-definition-style boolean expression) that is re-evaluated on every payment involving the asset, and — when the asset is defined by an AA — lets `is_transferrable` itself be a live formula rather than a fixed boolean. This is the direct structural analog of a "pausable" NFT contract (`whenNotPaused` in CryptoKitty/CryptoFighter): the party who controls the condition/formula can make transfers of that asset fail at will, for as long as they like. Any oscript AA that accepts an arbitrary externally-issued asset as collateral (e.g., a lending/marketplace/escrow AA modeling the same "loan + repay/liquidate" pattern described in the report) is exposed to this: the AA's own `payment` message trying to return/liquidate the collateral asset will fail unit validation, bouncing the AA and locking the position.

### Finding Description
When a unit issues or transfers a non-base asset, `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` checks `objAsset.is_transferrable` and then evaluates the asset's `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition` through `Definition.evaluateAssetCondition`: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

These conditions are ordinary address-definition-like expression trees (`arrCondition`), validated once at asset-definition time via `Definition.validateDefinition(..., bAssetCondition=true, ...)`: [3](#0-2) 

Crucially, when the asset is defined by an Autonomous Agent, several boolean fields — including `is_transferrable`, `is_private`, `auto_destroy`, `issued_by_definer_only`, etc. — are permitted to be *formulas* rather than fixed literals, meaning their value is computed dynamically at every evaluation from AA state vars, data feeds, timestamp, mci, etc.: [4](#0-3) 

This is functionally equivalent to a Solidity `Pausable` modifier: the entity that controls the referenced state (an AA's own `var[]`, or the outcome of `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition` evaluated against on-chain facts such as attestations, "has" filters, or data feeds) can flip transferability off indefinitely, and any unit (including one composed by a *different* AA that merely holds/uses this asset) that tries to move the asset will have that message rejected during validation.

### Impact Explanation
Consider an oscript AA implementing the same "loan" pattern as the Kairos contracts: it accepts a user-supplied asset as collateral (representing an NFT-like unique token, `fixed cap`, `is_transferrable` normally true) and, on repay or liquidation, emits a `payment` message transferring that asset out of its own balance to the borrower or liquidator. If the asset's `transfer_condition` (set once, immutably, by the asset's original definer — who is not the lending AA and not the borrower) can be made to evaluate to `false` (e.g., it requires a data-feed value, an attestation, or a "has output" condition that the definer refuses to provide), the AA's repay/liquidate payment message fails validation, the whole response bounces, and:
- The borrower cannot get the collateral back even after fully repaying the borrowed bytes (fund freezing analogous to being unable to clear debt in the report).
- A liquidator cannot seize the collateral after the loan's due date, freezing the AA's balance and blocking protocol state transitions.
- Because the condition is controlled by a third party (the asset issuer) outside the AA's control, this is worse than a temporary pause — it can be permanent, with no code path in `ocore` to bypass, force-expire, or override a failing asset condition for an AA that merely holds the asset.

This matches the required impact bar: AA fund loss/freezing, reachable by an ordinary asset issuer (unprivileged) simply defining an asset with a self-controlled `transfer_condition`/`issue_condition` and later refusing to satisfy it, or in the AA-asset case, an AA definer flipping a formula-based `is_transferrable`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: this requires an AA design that accepts arbitrary third-party assets as collateral (a common oscript/DeFi pattern, directly analogous to accepting arbitrary ERC-721 collateral as in the Kairos report). Any address can define such a "poison" asset trivially — `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition` are ordinary user-supplied fields validated only for correct definition-expression syntax and complexity limits, not for "liveness" or fairness. No special privilege is needed; only asset creation (`app: 'asset'`) is required, something available to any address or AA.

### Recommendation
- Lending/collateral AAs should not accept arbitrary externally-defined assets as collateral without whitelisting or validating that the asset has no `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition` and (if AA-defined) that `is_transferrable` is a fixed `true` literal, not a formula.
- Alternatively, protocol AAs should implement a timeout/escape hatch that does not depend on being able to transfer the asset itself (e.g., pay out an equivalent value in bytes if the collateral transfer condition has been failing for a defined period), mirroring the report's suggestion to stop charging interest or extend the loan while the underlying asset is "paused."
- Document in `ocore`/oscript guidance that asset `transfer_condition`, `issue_condition`, and AA-formula `is_transferrable` give unilateral, persistent pause power to the asset definer, and that any AA design relying on being able to move a foreign asset (for repayment, escrow release, or liquidation) is exposed to this identical risk.

### Proof of Concept
1. Address `Issuer` defines an asset `A` with `is_transferrable: true` but `transfer_condition: ["in data feed", {oracles: ["Issuer"], feed_name: "unlocked", feed_value: 1}]` (a standard address-definition-style condition, validated by `Definition.validateDefinition` at asset creation) — see [3](#0-2) .
2. `Borrower` deposits `A` as collateral into a lending AA and borrows bytes.
3. `Borrower` repays the loan in full; the lending AA composes a `payment` message returning `A` to `Borrower`.
4. `Issuer` never posts the `unlocked=1` data feed. During validation of the AA's response unit, `Definition.evaluateAssetCondition` returns `bSatisfiesCondition=false`, and `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` returns `"transfer or issue condition not satisfied"` — see [5](#0-4) .
5. The AA's response bounces; the collateral remains stuck in the AA's balance indefinitely, and `Borrower` can neither retrieve the collateral nor have it liquidated, mirroring the CryptoKitty/CryptoFighter "paused NFT blocks repay/liquidation" scenario from the external report.

**Caveat:** I could not fully trace every opcode allowed inside asset conditions (e.g., whether `in data feed`, `seen address`, or similar oracle-driven ops are permitted under `bAssetCondition`) within the available tool budget — I confirmed `Definition.validateDefinition` is invoked with `bAssetCondition=true` for `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition`, but did not exhaustively verify the full whitelist of ops permitted in that mode. If oracle-driven ops turn out to be disallowed in asset conditions specifically, the "third-party-controlled, indefinitely-refusable" variant would be weaker, but the AA-formula `is_transferrable` path (aa_validation.js:297-313) — which is not restricted to any op subset and can reference any AA state var or data feed via a general formula — remains a fully confirmed, unconditional way to pause transfers of an AA-issued asset indefinitely.
