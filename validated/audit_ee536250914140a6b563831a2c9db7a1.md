### Title
Unprivileged callers can restamp a victim’s LTV and freeze withdrawals - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs))

### Summary
`update_account_threshold` allows any authenticated address to rewrite the stored `loan_to_value` of another account’s supply positions. If governance lowers a market’s LTV while its liquidation threshold remains higher, an attacker can immediately apply the lower LTV to a victim. Subsequent withdrawals then fail the `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` gate even when the position remains healthy under the unchanged liquidation threshold. A third-party dust `supply` can trigger the same refresh.

### Finding Description
`controller::update_account_threshold(caller, has_risks, account_ids)` requires only `caller.require_auth()` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:124-130`. It then iterates arbitrary `account_ids` and calls `sync_account_thresholds` without checking that the caller owns or delegates those accounts at `params.rs:140-143`.

With `has_risks = false`, the selected `RiskRefreshScope::LtvOnly` writes only `position.loan_to_value` at `params.rs:34-38`. The changed position is persisted at `params.rs:217-219`; the liquidation threshold remains untouched.

Withdrawals use both stored risk parameters in `calculate_account_risk_totals`: `ltv_collateral` is weighted by `min(loan_to_value, liquidation_threshold)` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs:95-96`, while the health factor uses `liquidation_threshold`. `require_post_pool_risk_gates` requires both LTV-weighted collateral to cover debt and HF ≥ 1 in `contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:34-53`.

A stranger can also trigger the same risk refresh through `controller::supply`. Third-party supply is restricted to an already-held supply leg at `contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:86-93`, but merging that leg refreshes its risk snapshot through the supply merge path beginning at `supply.rs:273-280` and `refresh_supply_risk_params` in `params.rs:25-39`.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can permanently or temporarily freeze a victim’s collateral behind the withdrawal risk gate after a legitimate LTV reduction.

For example:

1. Alice supplies `$10,000` of collateral with stored LTV/LT `80%`.
2. Alice borrows `$7,000`.
3. Governance lowers the collateral market’s LTV to `50%`, while Alice’s stored liquidation threshold remains `80%`.
4. Eve calls:
   ```text
   update_account_threshold(
       caller = eve,
       has_risks = false,
       account_ids = [alice_account_id]
   )
   ```
   Alternatively, Eve supplies one unit of the same collateral leg to Alice’s account.
5. Alice’s stored LTV becomes `50%`, reducing `ltv_collateral` to `$5,000`, while her stored liquidation threshold remains `80%`.
6. Alice remains above HF 1 because threshold-weighted collateral is `$8,000`, so she is not liquidatable.
7. Every withdrawal still reverts because `ltv_collateral < total_debt`.

The victim can recover access only by repaying debt, receiving enough third-party collateral/repayment, or waiting for configuration to become permissive again. This matches temporary freezing of user funds and can also trap collateral during adverse market movement.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is medium:

- The attacker needs no ownership, delegation, role, or victim authorization.
- Either `update_account_threshold` or a dust third-party `supply` reaches the write.
- The attack requires a market LTV reduction whose new LTV is below the victim’s debt utilization while the unchanged liquidation threshold keeps HF above 1.
- Such conservative risk reductions are normal governance operations, and the permissionless keeper function applies their account-level effect immediately.
- The impact is limited to withdrawal denial rather than direct theft, supporting Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Do not allow an unrelated caller to apply an adverse LTV snapshot to an account. Suitable mitigations include:

- Restrict `update_account_threshold` to the account owner, an active delegate, or a privileged keeper.
- Make `LtvOnly` refresh only upward, and require owner action or a solvency-safe migration path for downward changes.
- Gate withdrawals on liquidation threshold/HF rather than stored LTV, reserving LTV for new borrowing and risk-increasing actions.
- For third-party `supply`, avoid refreshing stored risk parameters on an existing foreign position.

### Proof of Concept
Assume:

- USDC collateral price: `$1`
- ETH debt value: `$7,000`
- Alice’s initial stored USDC LTV and liquidation threshold: `80%`
- Alice supplies `10,000 USDC`
- Alice borrows `$7,000` of ETH

Before the LTV restamp:

```text
LTV-weighted collateral = 10,000 * 0.80 = $8,000
Debt                    = $7,000
HF collateral           = 10,000 * 0.80 = $8,000
HF                      ≈ 1.142
```

Governance lowers the market’s `loan_to_value` to `50%` but does not lower Alice’s stored liquidation threshold. Eve then invokes:

```rust
controller.update_account_threshold(
    eve,
    false,
    vec![alice_account_id],
);
```

The call passes because `update_account_threshold` only authenticates `caller`; it does not require Eve to own Alice’s account. The account’s USDC position is rewritten to LTV `50%` while liquidation threshold remains `80%`.

Alice attempts:

```rust
controller.withdraw(
    alice,
    alice_account_id,
    vec![(usdc_hub_asset, 1)],
    None,
);
```

The post-operation check evaluates:

```text
LTV-weighted collateral ≈ $5,000
Debt                    =  $7,000
HF                      ≈  1.142
```

The withdrawal reverts at the `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` assertion in `require_post_pool_risk_gates`, even though Alice remains above the liquidation threshold and cannot be liquidated. Any withdrawal amount fails until debt is repaid or the stored LTV is raised.
