## Title
Asset definer can retroactively change the trusted-attestor list to freeze already-issued/held assets — reputation/rug-pull risk analogous to owner-upgradable contracts (File: validation.js, definition.js)

### Summary
The PoolTogether finding warns that an upgradable contract lets its owner change code after users have deposited funds, enabling a rug pull / freeze of user assets even though it requires trusting the owner not to abuse the privilege. The closest reachable analog in `ocore` is the `spender_attested` asset mechanism: an asset's issuer (definer) can publish an `asset_attestors` message at any time after the asset has been issued and distributed, changing which addresses are considered "attested." Because the attestation list is evaluated dynamically at spend time (not fixed at issuance), the definer effectively has late-bound, unilateral control over who is allowed to move a token they no longer custody — the on-chain equivalent of an owner "upgrading" a contract's rules to disadvantage existing holders.

### Finding Description
When an asset is defined with `spender_attested: true`, only addresses attested by the trusted attestor list may hold/spend outputs of that asset. The attestor list is not baked into the asset definition immutably — it can be updated later via a separate `asset_attestors` message, validated in `validation.js` (`validateAttestorListUpdate`, referenced at [1](#0-0) ) and written by the asset definer, then persisted into `asset_attestors` table (see writer.js [2](#0-1) ).

Critically, whether an address is "attested" is checked **at payment-validation time**, using the current (most recent, up-to-`last_ball_mci`) attestor list, not a snapshot from when the recipient acquired the asset: [3](#0-2) 
and the underlying check in the definition/asset-condition evaluator: [4](#0-3) 

This means a holder who received the asset while being attested can later be excluded simply because the definer posts a new `asset_attestors` update dropping that attestor (or narrowing the set), which causes `filterAttestedAddresses` to return fewer addresses and the transfer/issue condition (`objAsset.spender_attested`) to fail for previously-valid holders: [5](#0-4) 

There is no restriction preventing the definer from doing this after significant value has accumulated in the asset (i.e., no "one-time-only" or "immutable-after-first-issuance" rule on the attestor list), and ordinary unprivileged holders of the asset have no way to prevent or override this — the update message is authored solely by the asset definer.

### Impact Explanation
This is functionally identical in spirit to the PoolTogether report: the asset issuer holds unilateral, post-hoc, "upgradable" control over the rules governing already-distributed funds. A malicious or compromised definer can:
- Freeze specific holders' funds by de-attesting their addresses, preventing them from spending/transferring the asset they legitimately hold.
- Selectively re-attest only addresses under their control while excluding others, effectively confiscating usable liquidity from existing token holders (a form of fund freezing/discriminatory lock-out).

This falls under "AA fund loss or freezing" / unauthorized control over already-issued value, satisfying the validation bar for a concrete impact (freezing), not merely a theoretical "trust the owner" comment.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is limited by opt-in trust: `spender_attested` assets are explicitly designed around a trusted attestor model, and users acquiring such assets are expected to understand that attestation is a live condition. However, unlike immutable AA bytecode (AA definitions cannot change post-creation because the AA address equals the chash of its own definition — see comment in `validation.js`), the asset attestor list is explicitly designed to be mutable after the fact, so the risk is inherent to normal, unprivileged operation of any `spender_attested` asset, requiring no bug exploitation — only a change of intent by the definer. This mirrors the "reputation risk" framing of the original report: technically permitted by protocol design, but a rug-pull/freeze vector that should be clearly disclosed, since ordinary token holders bear the risk without recourse.

### Recommendation
- Document explicitly (in asset metadata / wallet UI) that `spender_attested` assets carry a standing freeze risk because the definer can update the attestor list at any time.
- Consider adding a protocol option to "lock" the attestor list after a grace period or after total issued supply exceeds a threshold, so late holders cannot be arbitrarily excluded.
- Alternatively, allow wallets to warn users when interacting with `spender_attested` assets whose attestor list has changed since they received the asset.

### Proof of Concept
1. Definer issues asset X with `spender_attested: true` and an initial attestor list `A = [attestor1]`, where `attestor1` currently attests `alice`.
2. Alice receives a transfer of asset X; her address currently satisfies `filterAttestedAddresses`, so the transfer validates per [3](#0-2) .
3. Later, the definer posts an `asset_attestors` message dropping/replacing `attestor1` (or the definer directly controls the attestor and stops attesting alice) — this is accepted per [1](#0-0)  and stored in `asset_attestors`.
4. Alice now attempts to transfer her existing (already-held) X tokens; `filterAttestedAddresses` no longer includes her address, so the payment fails validation at [5](#0-4) , freezing her funds despite her holding them legitimately and having done nothing wrong.
