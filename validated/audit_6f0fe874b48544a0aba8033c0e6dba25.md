### Title
Dust collateral in a secondary asset lets a borrower's bad debt evade socialization indefinitely, silently inflating vault share price for unrelated suppliers - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate` only triggers bad-debt socialization for a borrower's remaining, uncollateralized debt when it determines `no-collateral-left`. That determination is gated by an estimate (`other-debt-repayable`) of whether the borrower's *other* collateral assets could theoretically still cover the remaining debt. As long as any non-zero (even dust) balance remains in another collateral asset, `other-debt-repayable` computes non-zero and socialization is skipped — even though that dust position will never realistically be liquidated (no minimum-size incentive exists). The unrecovered "bad debt" then keeps accruing interest in the vault's accounting via `accrue-and-cache`/`vault-accrue`, inflating `lindex` (the supplier share index) with value that is not actually backed by seizable collateral. When the debt is eventually written off (if ever), the resulting downward correction of `lindex` is borne by whichever suppliers hold zTokens for that vault at that moment — an unprivileged party unrelated to the original under-collateralized borrower.

### Finding Description
In `liquidate` (`mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`), after seizing the target collateral, the contract computes whether the borrower has any other usable collateral: [1](#0-0) 

```
(target-coll-full-usd ...)
(other-coll-usd (if (> total-collateral-usd target-coll-full-usd) (- total-collateral-usd target-coll-full-usd) u0))
(other-debt-repayable (if (> other-coll-usd u0) ... u0))
(no-collateral-left (and (is-eq coll-removed u0)
                          (or (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) u1)
                              (and (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) (len (get collateral position)))
                                   (is-eq other-debt-repayable u0)))))
```

Only when `no-collateral-left` is true does the code fold over the remaining debt and call `socialize-debt-asset`, which calls `vault-socialize-debt` (writing down `lindex`) and removes the debt obligation: [2](#0-1) 

The problem is that `other-debt-repayable` only checks whether some *theoretical* USD value of other collateral exists — it does not check whether that collateral is large enough to ever be economically liquidated. Since `liquidate` only requires `coll-final > 0` (`ERR-ZERO-LIQUIDATION-AMOUNTS` just checks non-zero, not a minimum), a borrower can deliberately retain a tiny (dust) balance in a second collateral asset. That dust keeps `other-coll-usd > 0` and therefore `other-debt-repayable > 0`, permanently blocking `no-collateral-left`, even though no rational liquidator will spend gas to seize dust collateral for a negligible bonus. As a result, the borrower's real bad debt is never socialized: it stays on the books as "outstanding debt" and continues to accrue interest each block via `accrue-and-cache`, inflating `total-borrowed`/`lindex` in the debt vault without any real backing asset (`vault-stx.clar`, `vault-usdc.clar`, etc. `socialize-debt`): [3](#0-2) 

Because `lindex` growth from phantom interest is shared pro-rata across all zToken holders of that vault, every supplier who deposits/holds zTokens while the bad debt is unsocialized is exposed to a share price that overstates real backing. When the debt is ultimately written down (via a fortuitous dust liquidation), the correction lands on whichever suppliers hold the zToken at that time — not on the borrower who caused the shortfall.

### Impact Explanation
This is a supplier-vs-borrower harm through shared vault state (`lindex`), not ordinary shared-pool economics: the borrower unilaterally controls (via dust retention) *when* — or whether — a loss they caused gets recognized against the pool, letting the vault temporarily (or indefinitely) misprice zToken NAV. Suppliers who withdraw while the bad debt is unrecognized get an inflated redemption value at the expense of the pool's real assets; suppliers who remain (or who deposit late) absorb an oversized write-down when it eventually occurs. This lands in the "temporary freezing of funds" impact class (delayed/blocked loss-recognition distorts redemption value for other suppliers), and in the worst case (dust never gets liquidated) becomes a permanent misstatement of vault solvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires no special privilege — any borrower being liquidated to zero on their primary collateral can simply hold ≥1 unit of a second, low-value collateral asset (e.g., 1 satoshi of a low-price asset) to keep `other-debt-repayable > 0`. No liquidator has economic incentive to clean up the dust position (bonus proportional to seized dust value), so the condition can persist indefinitely absent an altruistic/protocol-run sweep.

### Recommendation
Gate `no-collateral-left`/socialization on whether the *actual remaining* debt exceeds what could reasonably be recovered, rather than any non-zero `other-debt-repayable`; alternatively, add a minimum economically-liquidatable threshold so dust collateral cannot indefinitely block bad-debt recognition, or allow the DAO/protocol to force-socialize debt when the only remaining collateral is below a dust threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower deposits sBTC (large) and 1 unit of USDC as collateral, borrows against sBTC to the max.
2. Price of sBTC crashes; a liquidator liquidates the entire sBTC position, driving `coll-removed` (remaining sBTC) to `0`.
3. Because the borrower still holds 1 unit of USDC collateral, `other-coll-usd > 0` ⇒ `other-debt-repayable > 0` ⇒ `no-collateral-left = false`; the remaining large USDC debt is **not** socialized.
4. That unpaid debt continues accruing via `accrue-and-cache`/`vault-accrue` in `vault-usdc.clar`, inflating `lindex` with uncollectible interest.
5. No liquidator ever bothers to seize the 1-unit USDC collateral (bonus is negligible), so the bad debt (and the inflated `lindex`) can persist for an extended period, misleading zUSDC holders about real redemption value until/unless someone eventually forces the tiny liquidation and triggers the deferred write-down.
