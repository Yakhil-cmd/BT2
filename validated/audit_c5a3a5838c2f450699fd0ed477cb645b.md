### Title
Interest accrual overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap engages, permanently freezing a market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The bug class in CVE-2017-14862 is an unchecked dereference that crashes the process — a denial of service with no state corruption. The analog in XOXNO Lending is an unguarded `i128` overflow in the interest-accrual math that every state-changing pool verb must pass through. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds the `Ray` ceiling inside `scaled_to_original`/`Ray::mul`, the accrual panics, and because `global_sync` runs before every mutation, the market freezes permanently: no withdraw, repay, liquidation, or even `update_indexes` can succeed again. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never engages because the *value product* overflows while the index itself is still below the cap.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` chunks elapsed time and calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step`, which computes supplier rewards via `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:73-89`). That function unconditionally evaluates `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` and `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)` (lines 80-81). `Ray::mul` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` on `i128` overflow (`common/src/math/fp.rs:13-25`). The index cap in `update_borrow_index` (lines 13-19) only bounds the *index*, not the `borrowed * index` product, so a whale-scale market at sustained high utilization reaches the RAY value ceiling while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.

The in-repo harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`) proves the freeze end-to-end: after the accrual fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` fail with the same error, and the assertion `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` confirms the cap did not protect the product.

Unprivileged reachability: `update_indexes` on the controller is permissionless, so anyone can trigger the panic path once the market state crosses the threshold; thereafter every controller verb (`supply`/`borrow`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`liquidate`) that routes through `Cache::load` → `global_sync` reverts.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: all supplied liquidity in the affected `(hub, token)` market becomes unwithdrawable and all debt unrepayable/unliquidatable forever, since accrual precedes every mutation and there is no recovery path short of an upgrade (privileged, out of scope). This is the accepted-impact class "permanent freezing of funds."

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the cliff requires an extremely large supplied/borrowed base (test uses ~10^9 × 10^18 units) and sustained ~98% utilization over many years on a steep rate curve — far beyond realistic capital for a single attacker, though a whale can seed the condition and organic growth can cross it. The preconditions make this Medium at most; it is not a cheap attacker trigger.

Caveat: this behavior is already encoded as a test in the repo and the test itself notes the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong, so it may be a known limitation rather than a novel finding.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic in the accrual path: wrap `borrowed.mul(env, index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` and `scaled_to_original`-equivalent accrual arithmetic with saturating multiplication (`mul_div_floor_saturating` is already used in `update_supply_index` and `protocol_fee_shares` — apply the same treatment to the borrow-side product), or pre-clamp `borrowed`/`index` products to `i128::MAX` before differencing so accrual can proceed and the index cap remains the operative bound.

### Proof of Concept
Existing executable proof: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`. It builds a market with `principal = BILLION * 10^18` supplied and 98% borrowed, advances time year-by-year calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` until it fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` (inside `scaled_to_original` during accrual), then demonstrates `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` revert with the same error while `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — a permanent market freeze reachable via the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint.
