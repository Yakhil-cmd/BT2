### Title
Bad-debt socialization is deferred to liquidation time, letting zToken holders exit before a known write-down hits the vault - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar`, `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
Zest's vaults price zToken shares off `assets`/`lindex`, and the only mechanism that discounts the vault for uncollectible debt is `socialize-debt`, called exclusively at the moment `liquidate()` determines a borrower has no collateral left [1](#0-0) . Until that call executes, `redeem()` continues to pay out at the pre-write-down share price, computed from `convert-to-assets-preview`/`total-assets`/`lindex` that has not yet been decremented [2](#0-1) . Because collateral prices, debt balances and position health are all public on-chain state, any zToken holder can observe that a borrower's position is (or is about to become) unrecoverable before anyone actually calls `liquidate()`/`socialize-debt`, and redeem out at the stale, inflated NAV. This is the same "public, predictable, discrete negative event + freely-timed exit" pattern as the Buffer option-expiry bug, just with "bad-debt socialization" standing in for "option expiry."

### Finding Description
`socialize-debt` writes down `lindex` (and therefore share price) proportionally to the loss only when invoked, and it is only invoked from inside `liquidate()`'s `no-collateral-left` branch [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . `liquidate()` is a permissionless, opportunistic action - nothing forces it to run the instant a position becomes insolvent; a rational liquidator has no incentive to liquidate a position whose collateral no longer covers the debt (that's precisely the "no collateral left" bad-debt case), so it can sit unliquidated for a variable, market-dependent window (i.e., low liquidator incentive when the seized collateral is worth less than the socialization gas cost or when there's simply no bot watching that specific position).

During that window, `redeem()` uses `convert-to-assets-preview` derived from `assets`/`lindex`, which still reflects the vault as if the bad debt were fully collectible [2](#0-1) . Any zToken holder who is aware of the undercollateralized position - which is trivial, since collateral/debt/oracle prices are all public on-chain reads, exactly as option price/expiry data was public in the Buffer report - can call `redeem()` immediately and cash out at the stale NAV, extracting a share of vault assets that ignores the pending loss.

Once someone eventually calls `liquidate()` (possibly the delinquent-monitoring team, possibly days later), `socialize-debt` writes down `lindex` for whoever is still holding zTokens at that time, spreading the entire loss over a smaller remaining pool of shares than existed when the bad debt occurred [5](#0-4) . The shared state being gamed is the vault's `assets`/`lindex` (and hence zToken NAV), primed by a discrete on-chain public event (an undercollateralized position) and consumed asymmetrically by whichever LPs redeem before vs. after the socialization call.

### Impact Explanation
This is loss shifting between unprivileged LPs sharing the same vault, not merely economics of a shared pool reacting to organic utilization/interest. Early-exiting LPs (who monitor positions/oracle feeds) redeem at full NAV and escape their pro-rata share of a loss that is already baked into the vault's economic reality (the debt is uncollectible the moment collateral is exhausted, price crashes past the liquidation threshold, or, more precisely, the moment coll-realized < debt-owed for a position that will inevitably be liquidated at that shortfall). Remaining LPs then bear a disproportionately larger write-down via `new-lindex` at actual `socialize-debt` time, computed over the smaller `old-total-assets` base that remains after the early exits [6](#0-5) . This matches "temporary freezing/loss of funds" for the remaining, honest LPs - value they were owed under fair pro-rata treatment is instead captured by the LPs who front-run the socialization.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (1) a position to become genuinely bad-debt (collateral fully exhausted relative to debt, which does happen given the protocol's own test coverage of this exact scenario: `ATK-LG-05: Bad debt cannot be artificially created` [7](#0-6) ), and (2) a nonzero delay between the position becoming bad debt and someone calling `liquidate()`. Since `liquidate()` gives the liquidator only the (now-insufficient) collateral and there's no guaranteed immediate incentive to liquidate a hopeless position, that delay window is realistically nonzero, especially for smaller positions where gas/opportunity cost of liquidating exceeds the seized collateral value. No lockup/epoch mechanism exists on `redeem()` to prevent LPs from exploiting that window [2](#0-1) .

### Recommendation
Decouple loss recognition from the discretionary `liquidate()` call: either (a) allow/require permissionless "mark bad debt" write-downs the moment a position's collateral value falls below its debt (independent of who calls liquidate or when), so `lindex` degrades immediately and uniformly for all current holders, or (b) introduce a withdrawal buffer/cooldown (as recommended for the analogous Buffer finding) so that redemptions cannot outrun a pending, publicly-visible insolvency event, or (c) maintain a reserve/insurance buffer that absorbs socialized losses instead of applying them solely to whoever remains at the moment `liquidate()` happens to be called.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice borrows USDC against a small sBTC collateral position; sBTC price crashes so that Alice's collateral no longer covers her debt (mirrors the setup in `ATK-LG-05` [8](#0-7) ).
2. No liquidator immediately calls `market.liquidate(...)` because the seized collateral is worth less than the outstanding debt, so `socialize-debt` has not yet been triggered and `assets`/`lindex` in the USDC vault still reflect the (uncollectible) debt as good.
3. Any USDC zToken holder who observes on-chain price/position data calls `vault-usdc.redeem(...)` (or `market.collateralRemove`/withdraw path), receiving the stale, inflated NAV per share, unaffected by the impending loss.
4. Later, someone finally calls `market.liquidate(...)`, triggering `no-collateral-left` and `socialize-debt-asset` → `vault-usdc.socialize-debt`, which writes down `lindex` based on `old-total-assets` at that later point in time - now smaller because early redeemers already exited - so the per-share write-down applied to remaining holders is larger than if the loss had been recognized/spread at step 2 [5](#0-4) .
5. Net effect: early-exiting LPs receive full value while remaining LPs absorb an inflated proportional loss, exactly the LP-vs-LP gaming described in the Buffer H-2 report, mapped onto Zest's bad-debt-socialization design.

        (current-assets (var-get assets))

        (debt-reduction (mul-div-down scaled-amount idx INDEX-PRECISION))
