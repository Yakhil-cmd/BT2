Confirmed: `aa_composer.js` contains zero references to `is_transferrable`, `cosigned_by_definer`, or `spender_attested` in its unit-composition logic, while `divisible_asset.js` (the wallet's own payment composer) explicitly checks all three before building a payload.

### Title
AA payment composer omits `is_transferrable`/`cosigned_by_definer`/`spender_attested` pre-checks that the wallet composer enforces, causing AA responses to unnecessarily bounce for asset edge cases - (File: aa_composer.js)

### Summary
The external report's root cause is that a component builds and finalizes payment transfers for arbitrary tokens without validating the token's special transfer semantics (no-bool-return, no-revert, fee-on-transfer), leading to reverts or silent fund loss. In ocore, the analogous "special asset semantics" are `is_transferrable`, `cosigned_by_definer`, `spender_attested`, `auto_destroy`, and `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition`, which are stored on custom assets and enforced by `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` [1](#0-0) . The wallet's own composer (`divisible_asset.js`) proactively checks `is_transferrable`, `cosigned_by_definer`, and `spender_attested` before spending, failing fast with a clear error [2](#0-1) . The AA payment-composition path in `aa_composer.js` (`sendUnit` → `completePaymentPayload`) has no equivalent checks: it only special-cases `fixed_denominations` and `is_private` [3](#0-2)  before assembling inputs/outputs and only discovers a problem when the final `validateAndSaveUnit` call fails [4](#0-3) .

### Finding Description
When an AA's oscript emits a `payment` message for a custom asset, `sendUnit()` calls `storage.loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors` and then immediately proceeds to `completePaymentPayload`, skipping only `fixed_denominations` and `is_private` assets [3](#0-2) . It does not check:
- `objAsset.is_transferrable` — whether the AA (as sender) and the output address(es) satisfy the non-transferrable exception rules enforced later in `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` [5](#0-4) .
- `objAsset.cosigned_by_definer` — whether the definer must co-sign (an AA can never satisfy this, since AAs cannot cosign, per `aa_validation.js` enforcing `cosigned_by_definer !== false` is only checked at asset-definition time [6](#0-5) , not at spend time).
- `objAsset.spender_attested` and whether the destination addresses are attested, enforced later at `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` via `storage.filterAttestedAddresses` [7](#0-6) .
- `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition` formulas evaluated via `Definition.evaluateAssetCondition` [8](#0-7) .

Because none of these are pre-checked, the AA fully computes its state-update formula, builds inputs/outputs, computes fees/change, and only discovers the incompatibility when `validateAndSaveUnit` rejects the assembled unit [4](#0-3) . At that point the only recourse is `bounce(err)` [9](#0-8) , which discards all AA state changes for the primary trigger and returns the *base-asset* trigger outputs (minus bounce fees) to the sender — but it does **not** and cannot refund the *asset* amount that triggered the call, since `bounce()` iterates `trigger.outputs` (the amounts sent in the trigger) and this asset was never the trigger asset in this scenario, only an asset the AA itself was trying to originate as a response. This means any AA logic built around dynamically discovering the transferability/attestation/condition rules of an asset it does not control (e.g., a marketplace, custodian, or bridge AA paying out third-party assets) will unconditionally fail late, after having spent gas/complexity, rather than being rejected deterministically the way the wallet's own composer does it. This mirrors the underlying ERC20 bug class: code that assumes a "plain" token/asset transfer semantics and defers all special-case handling to a downstream check instead of validating upfront, risking either avoidable failures or — in more complex chained-AA scenarios where a secondary AA relies on a payment from the primary AA actually arriving — a downstream contract logically expecting funds that never arrive because the whole unit bounced.

### Impact Explanation
Medium: any AA (autonomous agent) that issues or forwards custom assets with `is_transferrable=false`, `cosigned_by_definer=true`, `spender_attested=true`, or a nontrivial `transfer_condition`/`issue_condition` to third-party addresses under attacker or user influence can be forced into an unconditional bounce for otherwise legitimate operations, because the AA composer has no way to detect and gracefully branch around these restrictions before committing to the state-update formula. This limits the practical set of assets AAs can safely route (analogous to "limitation of possible pools/tokens" in the original report) and can cause secondary triggers/response chains that depend on the payment succeeding to silently receive nothing while assuming otherwise, unless the oscript author manually re-implements all of these checks via the `asset[...]` getter in the formula language.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium: it requires an AA developer to define or interact with an asset that has one of `is_transferrable=false`, `cosigned_by_definer=true`, `spender_attested=true`, or `transfer_condition` set, and to route it through the AA's payment mechanism to an address that doesn't satisfy the condition. Since `cosigned_by_definer` can never be satisfied by an AA-authored unit (AAs cannot cosign), any AA sending a `cosigned_by_definer=true` asset will unconditionally bounce every time, which is a deterministic, easily triggered condition, not merely a theoretical edge case.

### Recommendation
Add the same pre-flight checks used in `divisible_asset.js` (`is_transferrable`, `cosigned_by_definer`, `spender_attested` via `arrAttestedAddresses`) into `aa_composer.js`'s `sendUnit`/`completePaymentPayload` path immediately after `storage.loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors` is called [3](#0-2) , mirroring [10](#0-9) , so that an AA response either bounces early with a clear, deterministic error, or better, exposes these asset attributes (already available via the formula `asset[...]` getters) so oscript authors can branch and avoid the payment entirely instead of relying on a late, expensive bounce.

### Proof of Concept
1. AA `X` defines/uses asset `A` with `cosigned_by_definer: true` (or `is_transferrable: false` with an incompatible output set, or `spender_attested: true`).
2. A trigger causes AA `X`'s oscript to emit `{app: 'payment', payload: {asset: 'A', outputs: [{address: trigger.address, amount: ...}]}}`.
3. `sendUnit()` proceeds through `completePaymentPayload` without rejecting the message early [3](#0-2) , selects inputs, computes change, and calls `validateAndSaveUnit`.
4. `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` rejects the unit for "must be cosigned by definer" / "the asset is not transferrable" / "some output addresses are not attested" [11](#0-10) , causing `bounce(err)` to fire and discard the entire state update [9](#0-8) , even though the condition was knowable in advance from `objAsset` fields already fetched at line 1323.
