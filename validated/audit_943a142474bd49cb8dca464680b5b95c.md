### Title
Direct pool deposits are permanently unaccounted and unreachable - ([File: contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs](contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs))

### Summary
Tokens transferred directly to the liquidity pool are excluded from the market's `cash` accounting and cannot be recovered or credited through any unprivileged controller path. Normal withdrawals are bounded by `cash`, while `repay` and `recapitalize` refund only the amount the controller measured and prefunded for that invocation, leaving any pre-existing token surplus stranded.

### Finding Description
The pool treats `cash` as the sole accounting reserve and explicitly keeps token custody separate from that book [1](#0-0) . Withdrawals and other reserve-backed outflows are limited by `Cache::require_reserves`, while `Cache::transfer_out` only performs a token transfer and does not reconcile excess token balance against `cash` [2](#0-1) .

The permissionless `Controller::recapitalize` path first transfers `amount` from the payer to the pool and passes only the measured receipt to the pool [3](#0-2) . `ops::recapitalize` credits at most the accounting shortfall and refunds `received - applied`; it does not account for pre-existing token balance [4](#0-3) . Likewise, `ops::repay` books only `amount - overpayment` as cash and sends the declared overpayment back without treating prior surplus as part of the repayment [5](#0-4) .

There is no in-scope unprivileged sweep, donation accounting, or mechanism that recognizes `token.balance(pool) - cash` as supplier assets. Consequently, any accidental direct token transfer to the pool remains outside all position books and reserve accounting.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged user who transfers tokens directly to the pool permanently loses those tokens: they are not credited as supply, do not increase `cash`, cannot be withdrawn by the sender, and are not absorbed by recapitalization. This is permanent freezing of funds within an allowed user action.

The issue can also leave economically useful liquidity unusable. For example, after bad-debt socialization creates a backing shortfall, a direct transfer does not reduce `backing_shortfall`, because that calculation uses only recorded `cash` plus outstanding debt [6](#0-5) . A subsequent recapitalizer must still supply new funds, while the donated balance remains idle.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires an accidental or third-party direct token transfer rather than a complex protocol sequence. Stellar token contracts permit arbitrary token holders to transfer to the pool address, and the pool has no way to reject or classify such inbound transfers. Although the loss depends on mistaken deposits, once present the stranded state is deterministic and persists across `supply`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize`, and flash-loan flows.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit policy for unsolicited pool balances.

One option is to introduce a permissionless reconciliation operation that converts `token.balance(pool) - cash` into credited cash only when doing so cannot create unbacked supplier claims, such as routing the surplus to protocol revenue or applying it to a backing shortfall. If surplus is credited as cash, ensure supplier shares or revenue shares are created consistently rather than silently increasing withdrawal capacity.

Alternatively, add an explicit recovery mechanism for provably unattributed donations. That mechanism must distinguish pre-existing surplus from transfers currently being measured by controller operations and should not allow callers to drain user prefunds, refunds in progress, or flash-loan balances.

### Proof of Concept
1. A supplier deposits `S` tokens through `Controller::supply`; the pool records `cash = S` and holds token balance `S`.
2. An unrelated caller invokes the token contract directly: `token.transfer(donor, pool, D)`. The pool token balance becomes `S + D`, but `cash` remains `S`.
3. A borrower withdraws all available cash through ordinary `borrow`/`withdraw`-backed paths. Reserve checks and accounting can release at most `S`; `D` remains in token custody.
4. If the market later has a shortfall, `Controller::recapitalize(donor, hub_asset, X)` transfers `X` from `donor`, computes `applied = min(X, backing_shortfall)`, and refunds `X - applied`. The pre-existing `D` does not reduce `backing_shortfall` and is never selected as the applied contribution.
5. With no shortfall, `applied = 0` and the recapitalizer receives the newly supplied `X` back, while the earlier `D` remains stranded. The same behavior is exercised by the pool tests documenting that refund logic uses the declared/measured inbound amount rather than reconciling total custody [7](#0-6) .

/// Asset units by which supplier claims exceed cash + debt (0 if solvent).
pub(crate) fn backing_shortfall(cache: &Cache) -> i128 {
    let supplied_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let outstanding_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil(cache.borrowed());
    let backing = cache.cash().saturating_add(outstanding_debt);
    supplied_claim.saturating_sub(backing).max(0)
}
