### Title
Missing party-restriction check in the `fraud_proof` case of the payment-channel AA sample allows any trigger sender to redirect the full channel balance to themselves - (File: test/samples/payment_channels.oscript)

### Summary
The `payment_channels.oscript` autonomous-agent template implements a two-party payment channel between `$addressA` and `$addressB`. Every state-changing case in the AA ("refill", "start closing", "confirm closure") explicitly restricts execution to `$bFromParties` (i.e. `trigger.address == $addressA OR trigger.address == $addressB`), except the `fraud_proof` case, which omits this restriction entirely. Combined with the fact that the fraud-proof payout message sends the entire channel balance to `trigger.address` (the caller), rather than to the legitimate counterparty address, this allows any third party who obtains (or observes) a validly signed `sentByPeer` package to trigger the case themselves and steal the funds meant for the honest party.

### Finding Description
In the AA definition [1](#0-0) , the "start closing" case checks `$bFromParties AND trigger.data.close AND !var['close_initiated_by']` before allowing state changes, and the "confirm closure" case similarly requires `$bFromParties AND var['close_initiated_by'] != $party OR timestamp > ...` [2](#0-1) .

However the "fraud proof" case's trigger condition is only:
```
if: `{ trigger.data.fraud_proof AND var['close_initiated_by'] AND trigger.data.sentByPeer }`
``` [3](#0-2) 
There is no `$bFromParties` (or any `trigger.address` equality) check restricting who may submit this trigger — unlike its sibling cases. The case's payment message then sends the entire remaining balance to `trigger.address`:
```
messages: [
    { app: 'payment', payload: { asset: 'base', outputs: [
        // send all
        { address: '{trigger.address}' }
    ]}},
    ...
]
``` [4](#0-3) 

The only real gate is `is_valid_signed_package(trigger.data.sentByPeer, $bInitiatedByA ? $addressA : $addressB)`, which merely proves that the embedded `sentByPeer` data was signed by the correct counterparty at some point — it says nothing about who is allowed to submit the unit or who should receive the payout. Since signed packages of this kind are ordinary data (not secret, and potentially exchanged over channels that can be intercepted, replayed, or even publicly disclosed by design in some payment-channel protocols), any unprivileged unit/trigger sender who acquires a copy of a validly signed `sentByPeer` package can submit their own trigger unit with `trigger.data.fraud_proof = true`, `trigger.address` set to themselves, and the captured `sentByPeer`, causing the AA to pay the entire channel balance to the attacker instead of the honest channel party.

This is the direct analog of the reported issue: a family of sibling operations shares an authorization check (`onlyPartyAOfQuote` / `$bFromParties`), but one operation in the family omits it, making it callable by any unprivileged trigger sender, with financial consequences.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who is not a party to the channel can, upon obtaining a signed `sentByPeer` fraud-proof package, redirect the entire channel balance to their own address instead of the legitimate channel party, causing direct unauthorized loss of AA funds for the honest party (or the AA itself sending funds to the wrong recipient). This matches the accepted impact class of "concrete unauthorized spending / AA fund loss."

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires possession of a validly signed `sentByPeer` package — signed data that is exchanged between channel counterparties as part of normal channel operation and is not inherently secret (it's just a signed message, and knowledge of its content does not require possession of a private key). Any party who can observe or otherwise obtain this data (e.g., a leaked message, a shared device, network capture during an unencrypted or intercepted relay, or one of the two channel members maliciously acting as a "trigger sender" pretending to be a third party) can front-run the legitimate resolution and redirect funds to themselves before the rightful party submits their own fraud-proof unit.

### Recommendation
Add the same `$bFromParties` restriction used in the other cases to the "fraud proof" case's `if` condition, and change the payout address from `trigger.address` to the appropriate legitimate party address (e.g., `$bInitiatedByA ? $addressB : $addressA`, the non-initiating party who is entitled to claim the fraud proof), rather than an address supplied by the caller.

### Proof of Concept
1. Channel is opened between `$addressA` and `$addressB`; `$addressA` initiates closing (`var['close_initiated_by'] = 'A'`).
2. At some point, `$addressB` signs a `sentByPeer` package (e.g., during the closing negotiation) proving a certain `amount_spent` for the current `period`.
3. An unrelated attacker address `$addressC` obtains a copy of this signed package (through any leak/observation channel).
4. `$addressC` submits a trigger unit to the AA with:
   - `trigger.address = $addressC`
   - `trigger.data.fraud_proof = true`
   - `trigger.data.sentByPeer = <the captured signed package>`
5. Since the "fraud proof" case has no `$bFromParties` check, the AA evaluates `is_valid_signed_package(...)` (which passes, since the package is a validly signed one from `$addressB`) and executes the payout `{ address: '{trigger.address}' }`, sending the entire channel balance to `$addressC` instead of `$addressA`.
