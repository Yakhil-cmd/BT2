The supply-index floor clamp is the strongest candidate. Let me verify whether the withdrawal path actually lets a stranded (wiped) claim extract cash after bad-debt socialization.### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp leaves phantom supply claims that drain recapitalized or repaid cash - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When bad-debt socialization exceeds the total supplied value of a market, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing it. Every unburned supply share in that market retains a residual claim equal to `scaled_shares * RAY/1000`. Because `withdraw` is gated only by `require_reserves` (cash) plus `require_supply_for_debt`/`require_utilization_below_max` (both vacuous once `borrowed == 0`), a wiped-out supplier can withdraw real tokens the moment any cash enters the market — via a `repay` on a surviving debt leg, a `flash_loan` fee, or `recapitalize`, which computes its shortfall from the inflated `supplied_claim` itself.

### Finding Description
`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at `total_supplied_value` and then floors the resulting index:

```rust
let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining = 0` and `new_supply_index` would be 0 — but the clamp forces it to `RAY/1000`. All supply shares are left intact (`supplied` is never reduced by socialization), so each holder keeps a claim of `0.1%` of their original scaled shares denominated at floor index — value the market no longer backs. The pool's own tests prove the residual drains fresh cash: `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs` (lines 372–495) show a wiped position paying out a fresh deposit in full, leaving the honest supplier's claim uncovered.

The exit path confirms reachability: `ops::withdraw::accounting` → `gate_and_debit` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`) runs `require_reserves`, then `require_utilization_below_max` (early-returns when `borrowed == 0`, `guards.rs:24-27`), then `require_supply_for_debt` (passes when `borrowed == 0`, `guards.rs:69-72`). No backing check on exit — `require_backed_market` gates only `supply` (pool README "Guards" table). Meanwhile `recapitalize` credits cash up to `backing_shortfall`, which is computed as `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) - (cash + debt)` — the phantom claims inflate the shortfall, so recapitalized funds are sized to cover claims that should not exist.

The whole chain is reachable by unprivileged addresses: `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless for any insolvent account with ≤ $5 collateral, `repay` can be made on any account, `recapitalize` accepts any `payer`, and the attacker withdraws through their own account via controller `withdraw`.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. A wiped-out supplier (or an attacker who deliberately becomes the residual claimant) holds an unbacked claim that converts 1:1 into real tokens the moment the market holds any cash. The most direct theft: a keeper or protocol multisig calls `recapitalize` to revive a wiped market — the shortfall includes the phantom claims, the deposited cash satisfies `require_reserves`, and the residual claimant immediately withdraws it. Alternatively, other borrowers' `repay` inflows in the same market are drained the same way. Honest suppliers' realized loss is strictly larger than the intended pro-rata socialization: they are written down to the floor *and* the residual claims compete for whatever cash re-enters.

### Likelihood Explanation
High reachability, moderate setup. Creating bad debt requires an account to go insolvent with dust collateral — a routine occurrence in volatile markets, and an attacker can manufacture it cheaply by supplying small collateral, borrowing, and self-liquidating or waiting for a price move (own trades on Aquarius/Soroswap are in scope for price pressure within oracle tolerance). The attacker must hold supply shares in the debt market beforehand to own a residual claim — trivially done since the attacker can be the supplier of the very market they bankrupt. Once `clean_bad_debt` executes, the claim persists indefinitely until cash appears.

### Recommendation
When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, burn or zero the `supplied` share counter (and per-position claims) rather than clamping the index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; or alternatively set the index to zero and special-case zero-index conversions to zero claims. If the floor must be retained, `withdraw`, `claim_revenue`, and `net_settle` should apply `backing_shortfall` on exit for a market at the floor, and `backing_shortfall`/`recapitalize` should exclude residual claims created by a floor-clamped wipeout (e.g., by recording a per-market "wiped" flag that forces all claims to zero).

### Proof of Concept
```
1. Eve supplies S shares of token T into hub market M (e.g., 1,000,000 units).
2. Eve (second account) supplies tiny collateral C (≤ $5) and borrows
   as much T as LTV allows; Carol also holds T debt.
3. Price of C collapses (or Eve pushes it on a listed DEX within bands);
   account becomes socializable (debt > collateral, collateral ≤ $5).
4. Any keeper calls controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id):
   seize → apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index writes down more than the total
   supplied value → supply_index clamps UP to RAY/1000.
   Eve's S shares now claim floor(S * RAY/1000 / RAY) = S/1000 tokens.
5. Carol repays part of her T debt (controller.repay credits pool cash),
   or a payer calls recapitalize — whose accepted amount is inflated by
   Eve's phantom claim via backing_shortfall.
6. Eve calls controller.withdraw(her_account, [(M, i128::MAX)]):
   withdraw passes require_reserves (cash now > 0), utilization and
   supply_for_debt checks are vacuous (relevant debt written off),
   and transfer_out sends real T to Eve — funds belonging to the
   recapitalizer / repaying borrowers.
```
Pool-level reproductions already exist as tests: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`) and `:430-495` (`test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`), both asserting a wiped position withdraws exactly a fresh deposit, leaving the honest claim unbacked.
