### Title
Front-running of `is_valid_signed_package`-authorized AA triggers causes griefing of counterparties' orders/payments - (File: `formula/evaluation.js`, `test/samples/order_book_exchange.oscript`)

### Summary
The `is_valid_signed_package(signed_package, address)` oscript function, implemented in [1](#0-0) , only checks that the signature inside `signed_package` was produced by the private key of `address`. It never checks that the unit's trigger sender (`trigger.address`) is the same party who owns or submitted that signed package. This is architecturally identical to the reported Aave/ZeroLend `repayWithPermit`/`supplyWithPermit` bug: a signed authorization (an ERC-20 `permit` signature there, a `signed_message`/`signed_package` here) can be extracted from a not-yet-final, publicly observable transaction and resubmitted by an unrelated third party before the original submitter's transaction is processed.

### Finding Description
An AA that accepts off-chain signed orders (e.g. limit orders, payment-channel closing proofs) via `is_valid_signed_package`, such as the reference implementation in [2](#0-1) , derives a unique id for the order (`$id1`, `$id2`) purely from the *signed content* (`sha256($order1.address || ... || trigger.data.order1.last_ball_unit)`), not from anything tying it to the specific unit or author who posts the trigger. The AA guards against replay only with `var['executed_' || $id1]` after the fact, at [3](#0-2)  and [4](#0-3) .

Because units in ocore's DAG are broadcast to peers before they become stable, a pending unit containing `trigger.data.order1`/`order2` (the `signed_message` + signature) is visible to any observer well before it is confirmed. Since `is_valid_signed_package` validates only the embedded signature against the referenced address — not the actual author/sender of the enclosing unit — anyone who observes this pending, still-unconfirmed unit can copy the exact `signed_package` payloads out of it and submit their own competing trigger unit to the same AA. If that copycat unit ends up ordered first on the main chain, the AA marks `executed_<id>` for the order(s), and the legitimate submitter's original trigger evaluates the `if` branch to `false` (because `var['executed_'||$id]` is now truthy), causing the AA to silently swallow it as a no-match/empty response, exactly analogous to the reverted `repayWithPermit`/`supplyWithPermit` calls in the reported bug.

This pattern is systemic in the `is_valid_signed_package` design: the primitive that verifies "this message was signed by X" says nothing about who is allowed to relay it, mirroring the underlying `permit()` root cause where "this signature was produced by X" is treated as sufficient authorization regardless of who calls the function.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker (with no signing keys, no funds at risk, and no profit motive) can observe pending AA-trigger units carrying `signed_package` data in the mempool/DAG and front-run them by submitting a copy to the target AA first. This:
- Griefs the legitimate submitter, whose trigger is processed as a no-op/failed-match instead of executing the intended trade/settlement, wasting the bytes/fees spent constructing and broadcasting the original unit (AA fund loss for the submitter through wasted commissions, and potential missed trade execution/loss of price).
- Can be repeated indefinitely against any AA relying on this pattern (order books, payment-channel dispute resolution such as `test/samples/payment_channels.oscript`'s fraud-proof handling at [5](#0-4) ), denying legitimate use of the contract's signed-message based authorization path.

This matches the "Griefing" impact class of the original report: no profit motive is required for the attacker, but real damage (wasted fees, denied service, possibly stale/failed trades) is inflicted on users of any AA built on this common, documented `is_valid_signed_package` pattern.

### Likelihood Explanation
High for any AA that follows the documented `is_valid_signed_package` pattern (as shown in the two official sample scripts). No special privileges are needed by the attacker: only the ability to observe not-yet-stable units on the network (a normal, permissionless capability in ocore) and the ability to post an ordinary AA trigger unit with copied `trigger.data`.

### Recommendation
- In application-level AA templates using `is_valid_signed_package`, bind the resulting order/proof id and effect to `trigger.address` (or otherwise verify that only the intended relayer/party benefits, or make the operation idempotent/replay-safe so that a copycat submission simply confirms rather than "steals" the update), rather than relying purely on content-derived ids marked `executed_*`.
- Consider extending `is_valid_signed_package` documentation/guidance (and possibly adding an optional binding parameter) to make clear that verifying a signature does not authenticate the trigger sender, so AA authors do not assume the two are equivalent — the same class of misassumption that produced the original `permit()` front-running bug.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice constructs a trigger unit to `order_book_exchange` AA containing `trigger.data.order1` = her own valid `is_valid_signed_package` order and `trigger.data.order2` = a matching counter-order she obtained off-chain, and broadcasts it (unstable, visible on the network).
2. Mallory, monitoring the network, copies `trigger.data.order1`/`order2` verbatim (she does not need Alice's or the counterparty's private keys — only the already-produced signatures) into her own trigger unit to the same AA, and gets it included with better parents/witness ordering so it lands earlier on the main chain.
3. Mallory's copycat unit executes first: `var['executed_' || $id1]` and/or `var['executed_' || $id2]` are set per [4](#0-3) .
4. When Alice's original trigger is later processed, the check `if (var['executed_' || $id1] OR var['executed_' || $id2]) return false;` at [3](#0-2)  makes the `if` condition false, so the AA falls through/matches no case for that trade, and Alice's intended trade is denied — the analog of the pool's `repayWithPermit`/`supplyWithPermit` revert caused by a front-run `permit()`.
