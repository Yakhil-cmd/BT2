Based on extensive investigation into claim/reward mechanisms across `polkadot-sdk`, I could not find a genuine analog to this Sherlock finding.

The reported bug is Solidity/EVM-specific: `FootiumPrizeDistributor.sol` allows a party to claim an Eth/ERC20 prize using a stale merkle proof tied to a snapshot of team/player ownership, without re-checking that the claimant is still the *current* owner of the underlying NFT/team at claim time.

In `polkadot-sdk`, every claim-style extrinsic I examined derives eligibility directly from live storage keyed by the actual `AccountId`/relayer/staker/pool-member identity at call time, rather than from a stale snapshot or proof that can be replayed after an ownership transfer:

- `pallet-bridge-relayers::claim_rewards`/`claim_rewards_to` look up `RelayerRewards` keyed by the signed relayer account and consume the entry, so there's no separate "team owner" whose stale claim right could be exploited after a transfer. [1](#0-0) 
- `pallet-nomination-pools::claim_payout`/`unbond` and `do_reward_payout` compute rewards from the live `PoolMember` and `RewardPool` records for the signer, checking `active_points()` is non-zero, so a member who has already exited/unbonded cannot claim on stale state. [2](#0-1) 
- `pallet-nomination-pools::do_claim_commission` re-derives the current payee from the live `bonded_pool.commission.current` rather than a cached/stale value, and checks `can_claim_commission`.
