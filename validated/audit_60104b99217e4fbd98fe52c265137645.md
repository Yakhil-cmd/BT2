### Title
Accrual-time `MathOverflow` permanently freezes a whale-scale market, trapping all suppliers, borrowers and liquidators — (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Every state-mutating pool entrypoint (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `net_settle`, `seize_positions`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue`, `update_indexes`) runs `interest::global_sync` first via `ops::synced_market`/`renewed_market` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs:30-40`). When the RAY-domain debt or supply value overflows `i128` during a chunked accrual step, `accrue_step`'s checked arithmetic panics with `MathOverflow` before the market's own `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` caps engage. Once crossed, the overflow is permanent: the computed value only grows, so every subsequent call to the market reverts at the same point. An unprivileged whale can deliberately create the precondition — a supply book near the token-to-RAY input maximum (`i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)`, ~170B tokens) borrowed to near-max utilization — after which ordinary time accrual bricks the market for every user.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` accrues from `last_timestamp` to now in chunks of at most `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`). Each chunk calls `accrue_step`, which multiplies `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` in RAY-fixed-point with checked arithmetic. Docs confirm "Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first" (`docs/reference/formulas.md:433-437`) and the harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` proves it: after enough years at 98% utilization on a steep curve, `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and then `try_withdraw` and `try_repay` fail identically — "The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic" (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-357`).

The index ceiling never engages because the panic happens inside value computation (`borrowed_value`/`supplied_value` in `scaled_to_original`), before `set_borrow_index` can clamp at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. There is no early-exit or saturation in `accrue_step`, and no entrypoint exists that can mutate market state without first calling `global_sync` — not even a risk-reducing `repay`, bad-debt `seize_positions`, or `recapitalize`. This mirrors the dovecot class: one malformed/extreme input (an oversized book pushed to sustained max utilization) permanently breaks the shared session (the market's global accrual) for all concurrent users.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every participant in that market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay (so their collateral in other markets stays locked against a debt that can never be settled), liquidations via `liquidate` revert on the same `global_sync`, permissionless `clean_bad_debt` and governance `force_socialize_bad_debt` paths that touch the market also revert, and `claim_revenue`/`recapitalize` cannot run. The pool's token custody for that market is stranded.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market sized near the RAY input ceiling (~170B whole tokens at 18 decimals, less for low-decimal assets since the cap scales as `i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)`), sustained high utilization on a steep rate curve, and years of unaccrual-free growth — the harness reproduces it within ~40 years at 98% utilization, sooner at higher effective compounding. The attacker needs whale-scale capital but no privilege: `supply`, `borrow`, and `update_indexes` are all unprivileged entrypoints, and governance-set caps can admit the size (caps convert at the live index and can even fail open via saturation, `docs/reference/formulas.md:411-416`). Medium likelihood, critical-blast-radius → Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Clamp accrual inputs before the value multiplication can overflow: in `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, saturate the computed debt/supply value at `i128::MAX` and engage the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap instead of trapping, or add a pre-check in `global_sync` that caps `borrow_index` once the projected value nears the RAY ceiling so `mark_accrued` still advances `last_timestamp` and the market remains callable for repay/withdraw.

### Proof of Concept
`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`: supply `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98%, advance `YEAR_SECS` repeatedly until `try_update_indexes_for` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, then observe `try_withdraw` and `try_repay` failing with the same error — the market can never be touched again.
