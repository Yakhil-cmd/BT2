### Title
Unsafe `uint64`→`int64` Cast in `consolidatedNode.Split` Corrupts CN/CL Staking Reward Distribution — (File: `kaiax/staking/staking_info.go`)

---

### Summary

`consolidatedNode.Split` converts both `c.StakingAmount` and `c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount` (type `uint64`, in KAIA units) to `int64` via `big.NewInt(int64(...))`. Any value exceeding `math.MaxInt64` (~9.22 billion KAIA) silently wraps to a large negative integer. `big.NewInt` faithfully preserves the sign, so the proportional split of block rewards between the CN staking address and the CL pool address is computed with a corrupted denominator, producing a `clAmount` that can exceed the total reward or become negative, and a `cnAmount` that is correspondingly wrong. The corrupted amounts are then credited directly to on-chain addresses during `FinalizeState`.

---

### Finding Description

`consolidatedNode.Split` at `kaiax/staking/staking_info.go:169-187` computes the proportional share of a reward `amount` between a CN's staking contract and its consensus-liquidity pool:

```go
// kaiax/staking/staking_info.go:174-186
cnAmountBig = big.NewInt(int64(c.StakingAmount))                    // ← unsafe cast
clAmountBig = big.NewInt(int64(c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount))    // ← unsafe cast
totalAmount = new(big.Int).Add(cnAmountBig, clAmountBig)

clAmount := new(big.Int).Mul(clAmountBig, amount)
clAmount = clAmount.Div(clAmount, totalAmount)

cnAmount := big.NewInt(0).Sub(amount, clAmount)
return cnAmount, clAmount
```

`c.StakingAmount` and `c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount` are both `uint64` (KAIA units, confirmed by `params.KAIA = 1e18` in `params/denomination.go:32`). The cast `int64(x)` is a silent two's-complement truncation: for any `x > math.MaxInt64` (9,223,372,036 KAIA), the result is a large negative number. `big.NewInt` preserves the sign, so `cnAmountBig` or `clAmountBig` becomes negative.

Consequences when `cnAmountBig < 0` and `clAmountBig > 0`:
- `totalAmount` shrinks toward zero or goes negative.
- `clAmount = clAmountBig × amount / totalAmount` can vastly exceed `amount` (tiny positive denominator) or become negative (negative denominator).
- `cnAmount = amount − clAmount` then becomes negative or far exceeds `amount`.

`Split` is called in three reward-distribution paths:

1. `assignStakingRewards` — Prague staker reward allocation
2. `assignStakingRewardsFlex` — Flex (Osaka) staker reward allocation
3. `specWithProposerAndFunds` — proposer reward allocation

In all three paths the corrupted `cnAmount` and `clAmount` are stored in `alloc` and later credited to `cn.RewardAddr` and `cn.CLStakingInfo.CLPoolAddr` via `spec.IncRecipient`, which executes the actual KAIA balance changes in `FinalizeState`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

---

### Impact Explanation

This is a direct unauthorized redistribution of KAIA block rewards:

- The CL pool (`CLPoolAddr`) receives far more KAIA than its proportional share (or a negative credit, reducing its balance).
- The CN reward address receives a negative credit (balance reduced) or far more than its share.
- The total credited to the two addresses no longer equals `reward`, breaking the conservation invariant verified by `sanityCheckRewardSpec`.
- The error occurs every block for any validator whose `StakingAmount` or `CLStakingAmount` exceeds `math.MaxInt64` KAIA, compounding over time.

This matches the allowed impact gate: unauthorized reward distribution affecting KAIA and system-managed funds.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

The trigger is `c.StakingAmount > 9,223,372,036` KAIA or `c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount > 9,223,372,036` KAIA. Staking amounts are stored as `uint64` in KAIA (divided by `params.KAIA = 1e18` kei per `kaiax/staking/impl/getter.go:287-299`). No on-chain guard in the repository caps staking amounts below this threshold. The condition requires no special privilege — any validator or CL pool operator who accumulates sufficient stake triggers it automatically. While the threshold is large relative to current supply, the bug is latent and will activate as total supply grows or if governance raises the minting amount. [6](#0-5) 

---

### Recommendation

Replace the unsafe casts with `new(big.Int).SetUint64(...)`, which correctly handles the full `uint64` range without sign truncation:

```go
cnAmountBig = new(big.Int).SetUint64(c.StakingAmount)
clAmountBig = new(big.Int).SetUint64(c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount)
```

### Proof of Concept

```
Given:
  c.StakingAmount              = 10_000_000_000  // 10 billion KAIA > math.MaxInt64
  c.CLStakingInfo.CLStakingAmount = 1_000_000_000  // 1 billion KAIA
  reward                       = 1_000_000_000_000_000_000 kei (1 KAIA)

Step 1: int64(10_000_000_000) = -8_446_744_073_709_551_616  (wraps)
        cnAmountBig = big.NewInt(-8_446_744_073_709_551_616)
        clAmountBig = big.NewInt(1_000_000_000)
        totalAmount = -8_446_744_073_709_551_616 + 1_000_000_000
                    ≈ -8_446_744_072_709_551_616

Step 2: clAmount = 1_000_000_000 × 1e18 / (-8_446_744_072_709_551_616)
                 ≈ -118 kei  (negative)

Step 3: cnAmount = 1e18 − (−118) = 1_000_000_000_000_000_118 kei

Result:
  CN reward address receives 1e18 + 118 kei  (118 kei more than total reward)
  CL pool address receives   −118 kei        (balance reduced by 118 kei)
  Conservation invariant violated: cnAmount + clAmount ≠ reward
```

The staking amounts are read from the AddressBook and CLRegistry contracts via `parseCallResult` / `parsePermissionlessCallResult`, both of which perform `big.Int.Div(amount, big.NewInt(params.KAIA)).Uint64()` — a `uint64` that can hold up to ~18.4 billion KAIA — before storing in `StakingInfo`. No saturation or bounds check is applied before the value reaches `Split`. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)
