### Title
Attacker can force `protocol_fee_shares` to mint zero revenue shares by inflating the supply index, permanently stranding protocol fees - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`protocol_fee_shares` converts an accrued protocol fee into revenue shares as `floor(fee * RAY / supply_index)` and silently returns `Ray::ZERO` for a positive fee when `supply_index` is large enough. Because `supply_index` grows monotonically with supplier rewards and never decreases through accrual, an unprivileged attacker acting as sole supplier and sole borrower can pump the index with self-paid interest, exit to dust shares, and make every subsequent sub-threshold fee mint zero revenue shares. The unminted fee remains as pool `cash` that no share can ever claim, permanently freezing protocol revenue — the direct analog of driving `calcrMintQty` to zero in the Kyber report.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes `protocol_reward = protocol_fee + supplier_shortfall` and mints `protocol_fee_shares(env, protocol_reward, new_supply_index, supplied)` (common/src/rates/simulate.rs:83-87). The conversion is `mul_div_floor_saturating(fee, RAY, supply_index)` capped by `i128::MAX - supplied` headroom (common/src/rates/index.rs:94-98). Two manipulable inputs drive the mint to zero for a positive fee:

1. **Inflated `supply_index`**: `update_supply_index` is monotone non-decreasing under accrual (common/src/rates/index.rs:43-44). A user who is the only supplier borrows against their own supply (`controller::borrow`), lets interest accrue (`update_indexes`), and pays real borrow interest that lands in `supply_index` (the `(1 - reserve_factor)` portion is fully recaptured by them as sole supplier). When shares mint as `fee * RAY / supply_index`, any fee below `supply_index / RAY` raw units floors to zero.
2. **Headroom exhaustion**: `supplied` capped near `i128::MAX` makes `headroom = 0`, capping the mint to zero regardless of fee size.

Crucially, neither caller reverts on a zero mint: `add_protocol_revenue` early-returns only on `fee == Ray::ZERO`, not on `fee_scaled == Ray::ZERO` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:59-66), and `accrue_chunk` books `step.revenue_shares` unconditionally (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:50-52). The protocol fee is therefore "booked" into zero shares while the underlying tokens — borrower interest, flash fees, liquidation fees, withdraw fees — stay in pool `cash`.

### Impact Explanation
Revenue is only claimable via `burn_claimable_revenue`, which pays `min(cash, floor(revenue * supply_index))` and burns `revenue` shares (contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs:54-74). A fee that minted zero shares contributes nothing to `revenue`, so `claim_revenue` can never touch the stranded cash; suppliers' claims are `shares * supply_index` and also exclude it. The fee value is permanently frozen in the pool — theft/freezing of unclaimed protocol yield, inflicted by an unprivileged address through `supply`/`borrow`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`update_indexes`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the attacker to be the marginal index-payer on a market (cheap on a fresh or drained market — the documented first-depositor test shows markets can be fully drained, tests/test-harness/tests/controller/dust_threshold_and_decimal_floor.rs:122-133). Cost is the `reserve_factor` fraction of self-paid interest plus capital lockup; the freeze threshold scales linearly with the index multiple achieved, so pushing index to e.g. 100× freezes all fees below ~100 token units. Persistent and repeatable once established since the index cannot fall except via bad debt. Rated Medium: real, permissionless, permanent loss of protocol revenue, but bounded to fees under the manipulated threshold and requiring upfront cost rather than direct theft to the attacker.

### Recommendation
Revert or clamp when a positive `protocol_reward` converts to zero shares — e.g., treat a zero result from `protocol_fee_shares` for nonzero fee as `InternalError`, or mint a minimum of 1 share, or fold the unminted remainder into `supplier_rewards`/index so the value stays claimable instead of stranded.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker `supply(X)` into a fresh market; `borrow` against it up to max LTV.
2. Advance time / call `update_indexes` repeatedly; each chunk runs `accrue_step`, raising `supply_index` via `supplier_rewards` while the attacker recaptures ~`(1 - reserve_factor)` of the interest as sole supplier. Repeat until `supply_index / RAY` exceeds typical per-accrued fee sizes.
3. `repay` debt, `withdraw` supply down to dust shares (keeps `supplied > 0`).
4. Any subsequent borrow interest split, `flash_loan` fee, or liquidation fee computes `revenue_shares = floor(fee * RAY / supply_index) = 0`; `accrue_revenue(0)` mints nothing and `revenue` stays flat while `cash` grows.
5. `claim_revenue` returns `min(cash, floor(revenue * index))` covering only previously-minted shares; the stranded fee cash is unclaimable by treasury and unreachable by suppliers — frozen permanently.
