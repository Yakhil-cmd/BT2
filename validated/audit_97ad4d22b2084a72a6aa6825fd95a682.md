### Title
Predictable pseudo-randomness via `number_from_seed()` allows attackers to precompute/grind outcomes and drain AA funds - (File: `formula/evaluation.js`)

### Summary
The `number_from_seed` opcode in the OScript formula evaluator computes a value deterministically as `sha256(seed)`, with no protocol-enforced guarantee that `seed` is unpredictable or unbiasable by the party supplying it. Just like the Meebits `randomIndex()` bug — where anyone could recompute `keccak256(nonce, msg.sender, block.difficulty, block.timestamp)` offline until they got a favorable index — an unprivileged AA trigger sender can compute `sha256(seed)` locally for any candidate `seed` value they intend to place in their trigger unit (via `trigger.data`, `trigger.unit`, or any other value they fully control) before ever broadcasting anything, and only submit the one unit whose resulting "random" number produces the outcome most favorable to them.

### Finding Description
`number_from_seed` is implemented in `formula/evaluation.js` as: [1](#0-0) 

The output is `sha256(seed)` mapped deterministically into `[0,1)` (or into `[min,max]` when bounds are given). This is a pure hash function with no entropy source outside of `seed` itself. If an AA developer builds a lottery/dice/raffle-type contract that seeds this function with data the trigger sender controls — e.g. `trigger.unit`, `trigger.data.*`, or any other user-supplied field in the triggering `payment`/`data` message — the sender can:

1. Compute the AA's win condition and `sha256`-based random draw off-chain for arbitrary candidate seed values (a purely local, free computation — no on-chain cost, no waiting for confirmation, unlike PoW-based randomness that at least costs hash power per attempt).
2. Choose the specific seed/message content that yields the desired outcome.
3. Submit only that one unit as the actual trigger.

This mirrors the report's root cause precisely: "randomIndex" (and here, `number_from_seed`) is not truly random because all its inputs are known/controllable in advance by the actor who benefits from the outcome, letting them "test the... condition until they get the [outcome] they are looking to access." The formula/AA layer performs no validation that a `number_from_seed` seed is derived from data outside the trigger sender's control (there is no built-in commit-reveal, no requirement to mix in the responding AA unit's own hash or a future/independent value); this is left entirely to AA authors' discipline. This class of bug is reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who can trigger an AA with a `data` message.

### Impact Explanation
Any AA that uses `number_from_seed` (or `sha256`) seeded, directly or indirectly, with sender-controlled trigger data to decide payouts, winners, asset issuance, or fund distribution can be gamed deterministically by the trigger sender to always win or to bias outcomes in their favor. Because AAs autonomously move funds based on such formulas, this leads to concrete AA fund loss/drainage for lottery-, dice-, raffle-, or randomized-distribution-style AAs, and can also be used to bias randomized selection used for fee/allocation decisions in more complex contracts. This satisfies the "AA fund loss" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any deployed AA that relies on `number_from_seed`/`sha256` combined with attacker-supplied trigger fields for randomness, since exploiting it requires no special privilege — only crafting message content before broadcasting, which any unprivileged unit poster can do — and the "grinding" step is entirely off-chain and free, unlike PoW-based analog systems where grinding at least costs computational work per attempt.

### Recommendation
- Document/enforce that `number_from_seed` (and `sha256`) must never be seeded solely with values the trigger sender/AA caller controls (e.g., raw `trigger.data`, `trigger.unit`) when the result is used to gate payouts.
- Where randomness is required, use commit-reveal schemes or mix in data unknown to the sender at trigger-submission time (e.g., a future stable unit hash unknown at broadcast time, or a secret pre-committed by the AA in an earlier response) so outcomes cannot be precomputed by the party who benefits.
- Consider adding built-in guidance/lint warnings in `aa_validation.js` when `number_from_seed`'s seed expression is derived directly from `trigger.data`/`trigger.unit` without additional un-attacker-controlled entropy.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy an AA whose oscript computes `number_from_seed(trigger.data.seed, 1, 100)` and pays out a prize if the result equals a fixed "winning" number, per `formula/evaluation.js` lines 1872–1919.
2. Off-chain, the attacker iterates over candidate strings and locally evaluates `sha256(candidate)` (same algorithm as `formula/evaluation.js:1893-1896`) to find a `candidate` value that yields the winning number.
3. The attacker submits a single trigger unit with `trigger.data.seed = candidate`.
4. The AA computes the identical `number_from_seed` result deterministically and pays out the prize as if it were "random," even though the attacker chose it deterministically before ever broadcasting the unit.
