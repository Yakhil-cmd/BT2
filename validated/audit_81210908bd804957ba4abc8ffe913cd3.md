### Title
Non-atomic "already claimed" check for textcoin/mnemonic-based one-time payment addresses allows double-claim race - ([File: wallet.js])

### Summary
A textcoin is a one-time bearer payment: funds are sent to an address derived from a mnemonic (`expandMnemonic`), and the mnemonic (shared out-of-band as a link/QR/email) is the single-use "credential" that lets anyone claim the funds by spending from that address. `receiveTextCoin` treats "already claimed" purely as a database-state check performed before composing/broadcasting the claiming unit, with no lock or atomic reservation between the check and the DAG write.

### Finding Description
`receiveTextCoin` in [1](#0-0)  derives the spending key/address from the mnemonic and immediately proceeds to check spendable outputs before composing a payment. The "claimed" state is inferred only from `outputs`/`is_spent` at the time of the query in [2](#0-1) , and the decision to compose and broadcast the claim transaction happens after that read, in [3](#0-2) . There is no mutex/lock scoping the read-check-and-spend sequence for a given textcoin address, unlike the atomic-per-unit protections used elsewhere in the codebase (e.g., `mutex.lock(["private_write"], ...)` in the double-spend acceptance path in `validation.js`, [4](#0-3) , or the unique DB constraints backing spend-proof enforcement, [5](#0-4) ).

Because the same mnemonic can be redeemed by multiple parties who received/derived it (email forwarding, screenshot, log leakage, or by the original sender attempting `claimBackOldTextcoins`), and because `checkStability`/composition is not serialized per textcoin address, two concurrent `receiveTextCoin` calls for the same mnemonic can both pass the "has unspent output" check before either one's claiming unit is written, leading both to attempt to compose payments from the same output. Ordinary unit-level double-spend protection in `validateInputsAndOutputs` (`validation.js`, [6](#0-5) ) will ultimately reject one of the two competing spends once units are exchanged/validated, but the *application-level* single-use guarantee ("claim once, from one path, cleanly") that this analog targets is enforced only by a non-atomic read in `wallet.js`, not by a reservation/lock — mirroring the Apache Answer flaw where the validity of a single-use resource was checked without invalidating it atomically at the point of use.

### Impact Explanation
If the race is won by an unintended party (e.g., a sender who calls `claimBackOldTextcoins` concurrently with the legitimate recipient's claim, or two colluding light clients racing a claim before broadcast), funds intended for one recipient can be diverted or contested, and the resulting conflicting spends must be resolved by the normal DAG double-spend/serial-ordering mechanism, causing wasted fees, failed claims, and recipient confusion about fund custody. This does not break DAG consensus (double-spend is still correctly resolved at the protocol level), but it undermines the intended single-use security property of the textcoin/mnemonic scheme at the wallet layer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability requires two callers (e.g., original sender via `claimBackOldTextcoins` and the actual recipient, or two devices holding the same mnemonic) racing `receiveTextCoin`/`checkStability` for the same address within the same window before either unit is broadcast/validated — a real but narrow race window, and it depends on the mnemonic being available to more than one party, which is an explicit design feature of textcoins (shareable link) rather than an external secret leak.

### Recommendation
Serialize claim attempts per textcoin address with the mutex module (similar to `["private_write"]` locking) around the check-then-spend sequence in `receiveTextCoin`, or persist an explicit "claim in progress" reservation keyed by the derived address before composing the payment, releasing it only after the unit is confirmed accepted or the attempt fails.

### Proof of Concept
1. Generate a textcoin (mnemonic M) and send funds to `addrInfo.address` derived from M.
2. From two different processes/wallets holding M, call `receiveTextCoin(M, addressA, ...)` and `receiveTextCoin(M, addressB, ...)` concurrently.
3. Both calls pass the `checkStability` unspent-output check in [2](#0-1)  before either composed unit is committed, both attempt to compose a spend from the same output; one will eventually fail validation as a double-spend once units propagate, but funds allocation/timing is not atomically single-use at the point of claim-check, unlike a proper reservation.
