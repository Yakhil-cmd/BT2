### Title
Reusable, non-expiring permanent pairing secret is disclosed in plaintext to arbiter-contract counterparties and arbstore servers, allowing unauthorized re-pairing as a trusted device correspondent - (File: `arbiter_contract.js`)

### Summary
`arbiter_contract.js` repeatedly embeds the caller's *permanent* device-pairing secret (`my_pairing_code`) in plaintext messages sent to the contract peer and to the external arbstore HTTP API. Because the underlying pairing secret is permanent, reusable, and never invalidated after use, anyone who ever legitimately received it (a contract counterparty, or an arbstore operator/staff with access to dispute submissions) can replay it at any later time to silently register themselves as a new, fully-confirmed correspondent device of the victim's wallet — without the victim's explicit re-approval. This mirrors the GLPI bug class: a party with legitimate access to sent message/notification contents can extract a credential/secret intended to establish trust with a higher-privileged principal, and later use that secret to take over/impersonate that principal's session.

### Finding Description
`device.getOrGeneratePermanentPairingInfo` creates (once) or reuses a single permanent pairing secret stored in `pairing_secrets` with `is_permanent=1` and an expiry of `'2038-01-01'`: [1](#0-0) 

This same permanent secret is embedded, in cleartext, as `my_pairing_code` and handed to the contract peer when an arbiter contract is created: [2](#0-1) 

It is embedded again (still in cleartext, outside of the `encrypted_contract` field) inside the JSON body POSTed to the arbstore's HTTP API when opening a dispute: [3](#0-2) 

...and again when filing an appeal: [4](#0-3) 

On the receiving side, `handlePairingMessage` in `device.js` accepts any valid, non-expired `pairing_secret` and automatically inserts/confirms a new `correspondent_devices` row with `is_confirmed=1` — with no additional user consent step. Critically, for secrets flagged `is_permanent`, the code explicitly does **not** delete the secret after use, allowing it to be replayed indefinitely by multiple different parties: [5](#0-4) 

Because the same permanent secret is reused for every arbiter contract a user ever creates (`ORDER BY expiry_date DESC LIMIT 1` always returns the same row), every past counterparty and every arbstore this user has ever disputed/appealed through retains a durable, never-expiring credential capable of re-establishing trusted-correspondent status with that user's device at will — at any future point, even outside the context of the original contract.

### Impact Explanation
Becoming a confirmed `correspondent_devices` entry is a meaningful trust escalation in ocore's wallet-messaging model: a confirmed correspondent is treated by device.js/wallet.js as a legitimate paired device for that device_address, satisfying checks such as `SELECT hub, pubkey, is_blackhole FROM correspondent_devices WHERE device_address=?` in `sendMessageToDevice`, and forms the basis on which subsequent protocol messages (`arbiter_contract_offer`, `arbiter_contract_response`, `arbiter_contract_shared`, `pairing`-based chat, wallet/xpubkey exchanges) are accepted from that address. An attacker who silently re-pairs as a "new device" of a known counterparty can impersonate a trusted party in subsequent multisig/arbiter/shared-address workflows, send spoofed contract or wallet messages, and set up social-engineering attacks (e.g., forged arbiter contract offers, forged responses) against the victim's wallet — a foothold that can be leveraged toward tricking the user into co-signing malicious shared-address definitions or accepting fraudulent private-payment/contract state, ultimately risking fund loss. This satisfies the "node disagreement / unauthorized privileged access" bar even though the direct spend path requires an additional social-engineering step; the trust bypass itself (bypassing what should be a one-time, revocable pairing ceremony) is the root-cause defect.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by an ordinary, unprivileged actor: any private-payment/contract counterparty who ever received an `arbiter_contract_offer` (a normal, unprivileged flow available to any wallet user creating a contract) — or an arbstore operator processing a dispute/appeal submission — automatically and passively obtains the victim's permanent pairing secret. No special privileges beyond "having done business with the victim once" are required, and the secret can be replayed at any time in the future since it never expires and is never revoked after use. This is a realistic, low-effort reuse of already-legitimately-received data, directly analogous to the GLPI scenario where a party with access to *sent* notification contents could extract and reuse a security-critical token intended for a one-time, higher-privilege operation.

### Recommendation
Do not reuse a single long-lived, non-expiring `is_permanent` pairing secret across arbiter contracts. Generate a fresh, single-use pairing secret (or a per-contract expiring token) for each contract/dispute/appeal exchange, delete/invalidate it immediately after the intended one-time pairing use (as is already done for `is_permanent=0` secrets), and never transmit any pairing/authentication secret to the arbstore in cleartext outside the encrypted contract payload. Consider also requiring explicit user confirmation before automatically upgrading an incoming `pairing_secret` match into a fully-`is_confirmed` correspondent, especially for secrets that were generated far in the past or reused across multiple unrelated contracts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice creates an arbiter contract with Bob via `createAndSend`; her `my_pairing_code` (built from her permanent pairing secret) is sent to Bob in the `arbiter_contract_offer` message [6](#0-5) .
2. Later, Alice opens a dispute on an unrelated contract; her same permanent `my_pairing_code` is POSTed in cleartext JSON to the arbstore's `/api/dispute/new` endpoint [7](#0-6) .
3. Months later, Bob (or a compromised/curious arbstore employee who saw the dispute submission) extracts the `pairing_secret` portion of `my_pairing_code` and sends a `pairing` message to Alice's hub using that secret and a device pubkey of their choosing.
4. `handlePairingMessage` finds the secret still valid (never expired, never deleted because `is_permanent=1`), and automatically inserts a new `correspondent_devices` row with `is_confirmed=1` for the attacker's device [8](#0-7) , without any additional confirmation from Alice.
5. The attacker's device is now a confirmed correspondent of Alice's wallet and can send/receive further protocol messages (contract offers, wallet/chat messages) that Alice's client will treat as coming from a trusted paired device.
