### Title
RAY-scaled market value overflows i128 during accrual, permanently freezing a market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2026-53432 (integer overflow → unrecoverable panic), `accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` before any index cap engages. When `scaled_shares * index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, `mul_div` in `fp_core` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because every state-changing verb (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_*`, `update_indexes`) accrues first via `Cache`/`global_sync`, the market is permanently frozen: no repayment, withdrawal, or liquidation ever succeeds again.

### Finding Description
`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` runs `scaled_to_original` (a `Ray * index` multiply) at the top of every accrual chunk. The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` (about 10^9 × initial), but the *product* `borrowed_shares × borrow_index` overflows `i128` (~1.7e38) when the market's ray value exceeds ~170 whole-token-equivalents in ray terms — i.e., the ray value cliff arrives long before the index ceiling. The overflow path in `try_mul_div_half_up`/`to_i128` panics with `MathOverflow` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:300-303`). The test at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` demonstrates this exactly: after sustained high utilization, `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the borrow index is still below its cap. `docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437` acknowledges the limit but provides no mitigation or alarm.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds: once `supplied * supply_index` or `borrowed * borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX` in ray terms, every interaction with that market reverts inside `accrue_step`, which runs before any verb's business logic (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`, `39-53`). All supplier principal and unclaimed yield in that market become unrecoverable; borrowers' collateral cannot be released via repay or liquidation; `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` cannot help because they too accrue first. This matches the accepted impact "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate".

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged whale can set it up: supply near the market cap and borrow near max utilization (`supply`/`borrow` are permissionless). It then requires only time — accrual is driven by any caller via `update_indexes` or any verb — and interest itself inflates `borrowed * borrow_index` without further attacker action. The required scale is large (caps admit up to ~170 billion whole tokens depending on decimals) and needs a steep-rate market left at high utilization for years, and the bound is documented as an arithmetic limit, so Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Clamp or saturate the ray-valued totals inside `accrue_step` (e.g., value them with `mul_div_floor_saturating` for utilization purposes), or cap `supplied`/`borrowed` scaled totals at listing/`update_indexes` so `scaled * index` always fits `i128` before the index ceiling. Alternatively enforce a per-market ray-value cap in `supply`/`borrow` that guarantees `max_cap_ray * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY < i128::MAX`, and emit an alarm as a market approaches the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
Existing harness proof: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (lines 321-361):

```rust
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);      // 1e9 whole tokens @ 18 decimals
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);         // ~98% utilization
// advance years; eventually:
assert_contract_error(t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

All verbs panic in `scaled_to_original` (via `accrue_step`) before the borrow-index cap engages, confirming the freeze is permanent and reachable through permissionless `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes`.
