### Title
Pool repay/recapitalize refund legs pay a caller-declared "excess" out of pool custody without verifying any tokens were received - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The bug class in the external report is *accepting a caller-supplied parameter and acting on it without validating the underlying reality it claims to represent* — `fundIntent` trusted a `routeHash`/`route.source` it never checked, so funds moved on the wrong premise. The same shape exists in the pool's measured-receipt paths: `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` refund a caller-declared `amount` minus what was booked, paying the difference to `payer` from live pool custody, under the unstated assumption (noted only in comments: "The controller transfers `amount` into the pool before this call") that the tokens were actually transferred in. An unprivileged caller can invoke the pool directly, declare any amount, and receive a refund of tokens that were never deposited.

### Finding Description
`repay::accounting` resolves `cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` into `(burned, overpayment)` purely from the declared `action.amount` — the actual balance delta of the pool is never measured. The `net_repay == 0 || burned.raw() > 0` guard at `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:48` explicitly passes on the `net_repay == 0` disjunct, so a repay against a market with zero debt makes the entire declared amount `overpayment`. `apply` then calls `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` at `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:32`, transferring real pool tokens to `payer`. Identically, `recapitalize::accounting` credits `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` and computes `refund = amount - applied` at `contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52`, then `apply` transfers the refund to `payer` at line 34 — with no check that `payer` (or the controller) funded anything. Both refunds bypass `cash` bookkeeping and `require_reserves`, so the books keep claiming the tokens exist while custody is drained. The repository's own tests demonstrate this: `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` and `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3408` and `:3590` show full custody drained to a `payer` that transferred nothing, leaving suppliers unable to withdraw (withdrawals pass the book-based liquidity guard and then revert inside the SAC transfer).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and protocol insolvency: a single unprivileged address can repeatedly call `repay`/`recapitalize` with a declared amount bounded only by the pool's token balance, receiving refunds paid entirely from other suppliers' deposits while the cash book still reports the pool as solvent. Legitimate withdrawals then fail inside the token transfer despite the market appearing funded.

### Likelihood Explanation
High: the entrypoints are permissionless, the payload is a single declared integer, no position, collateral, or prior deposit is required, and the exploit is repeatable until custody is empty.

### Recommendation
Measure the inbound transfer instead of trusting the declared amount: snapshot the pool's token balance before/after and cap `amount` (or the refund) at the observed balance delta, or restrict `repay`/`recapitalize` to the controller and have the controller perform the transfer within the same call so the receipt is enforced. Alternatively, refuse zero-net repays (`net_repay > 0` required) and require `recapitalize` callers to prove funding, eliminating the unfunded-refund surface.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker E calls `pool.repay(payer=E, action)` on any market whose position debt resolves to zero (e.g., a fresh market with `borrowed == 0`), with `action.amount` set to the pool's full token balance.
2. `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment == amount`, `burned == 0`; the `net_repay == 0` guard passes; `transfer_out(E, amount)` pays the entire pool balance to E. Book `cash` is unchanged.
3. Alternatively/again, E calls `pool.recapitalize(hub_asset, payer=E, amount=custody)` on a market with `backing_shortfall == 0`; `applied == 0`, `refund == amount`, and custody transfers to E.
4. A supplier's subsequent `withdraw` clears `require_reserves` (book still shows the cash) and reverts inside the SAC transfer for lack of balance — permanent loss for suppliers whose tokens funded E's "refund".
