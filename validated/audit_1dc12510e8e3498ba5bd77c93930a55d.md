### Title
Rounding of `other-debt-repayable` to zero triggers premature bad-debt socialization onto all suppliers - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
In `liquidate`, whether a borrower's remaining position is treated as "no collateral left" (and therefore has its unpaid debt socialized onto all vault suppliers) depends on `other-debt-repayable`, a value computed through three successive floor-rounding divisions (`div-bps-down`, `mul-div-down`, then `mul-div-up` on an already-rounded-down intermediate). A liquidator who structures a partial liquidation to leave the borrower with a small but non-zero amount of "other" collateral can make `other-debt-repayable` round down to `u0` even though that collateral still has real, non-zero USD value. This falsely satisfies the `no-collateral-left` condition and forces bad-debt socialization even though the position is not actually collateral-less, shifting the borrower's remaining (unliquidated) debt loss onto all vault suppliers instead of leaving it to be resolved by a subsequent, correctly-sized liquidation.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` computes, after removing the targeted collateral, the USD value of the borrower's *other* remaining collateral (`other-coll-usd`) and estimates how much debt that remaining collateral could still repay: [1](#0-0) 

```
(other-adj (div-bps-down other-coll-usd (+ BPS liq-penalty-max)))
(other-tokens (mul-div-down other-adj (pow u10 debt-decimals) debt-price))
(other-borrow-idx (get index (unwrap-panic (get-cached-indexes debt-aid))))
(other-scaled (mul-div-down other-tokens INDEX-PRECISION other-borrow-idx))
(mul-div-up other-scaled other-borrow-idx INDEX-PRECISION))
```

Every intermediate step (`div-bps-down`, `mul-div-down`, `mul-div-down`) rounds *down*, and only the very last step rounds up on an operand that has already been floored to zero by the prior three divisions. Consequently, if `other-coll-usd` is small — which a liquidator can engineer by choosing `debt-amount`/`min-collateral-expected` so the seized collateral leaves the borrower with a tiny dust amount of a second collateral asset — `other-debt-repayable` becomes `u0` even though `other-coll-usd > u0` (i.e., the remaining collateral genuinely still has value and could, in principle, cover some debt).

This value directly gates `no-collateral-left`: [2](#0-1) 

```
(no-collateral-left (and
                      (is-eq coll-removed u0)
                      (or
                        (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) u1)
                        (and
                          (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) (len (get collateral position)))
                          (is-eq other-debt-repayable u0)))))
```

When `no-collateral-left` is (mis)computed as `true`, the borrower's remaining debt for the asset is force-socialized across all suppliers of that vault via `socialize-debt-asset`: [3](#0-2) 

The socialization writes directly into the vault's shared `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed`/`assets`/`lindex` state, which is consumed by every depositor's redemption value: [4](#0-3) 

The attacker here is the liquidator (an unprivileged caller who fully controls `debt-amount`, `min-collateral-expected`, and which asset/borrower to target); the victims are all other depositors/suppliers of the affected vault, whose share value (`lindex`) absorbs debt that was, at the moment of liquidation, still nominally backed by dust collateral that the rounding chain discarded.

### Impact Explanation
Without the attacker's crafted liquidation, the position would either remain only partially liquidated (retaining its small residual collateral, to be liquidated/repaid normally later) or would legitimately require socialization only when truly collateral-less. With the attacker's transaction, the rounding-to-zero of `other-debt-repayable` reclassifies a position with non-zero residual collateral value as fully collateral-less, causing `socialize-debt-asset` to write off debt onto the shared vault state one step earlier / one dust-amount larger than the true economics justify. This is a temporary freezing/loss of supplier funds (their share of vault liquidity is reduced by the socialized bad debt) attributable to a rounding bug rather than genuine insolvency of the borrower's position, landing in the "temporary freezing of funds" impact category for suppliers.

### Likelihood Explanation
The exploit requires the liquidator to control the sizing of a (partial) liquidation so that the borrower's remaining collateral in a second asset is reduced to a small USD value — well within the liquidator's control since `debt-amount` and asset/borrower selection are liquidator-supplied parameters, and multi-collateral positions with small remaining balances are a normal occurrence near full liquidation. No special privileges are needed; only a position with more than one collateral asset and an achievable partial-liquidation state is required.

### Recommendation
Do not let `other-debt-repayable` rounding to zero be conflated with "the borrower has no collateral value left." Either: (1) use `other-coll-usd > u0` (a value already computed without lossy chained rounding) as the actual gating condition instead of `other-debt-repayable == u0`, or (2) require `other-coll-usd` to itself be below a defined dust threshold before treating the position as collateral-less, and/or round the debt-repayable estimate up at each step (not just the final step) so it never reports "zero repayable" for a position that provably still holds positive collateral value.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower holds two collateral assets, A (large) and B (small, e.g. worth a few cents in USD), against debt in asset D.
2. Liquidator calls `liquidate` targeting collateral A with a `debt-amount` sized so that all of collateral A is seized (`coll-removed` for A becomes the entirety of that asset), while collateral B remains untouched and has `other-coll-usd` equal to B's small USD value (non-zero).
3. Because `other-adj = div-bps-down(other-coll-usd, BPS + liq-penalty-max)` and the two subsequent `mul-div-down` steps all floor toward zero, `other-debt-repayable` evaluates to `u0` despite `other-coll-usd > u0`.
4. `no-collateral-left` evaluates `true` (since collateral A is fully removed and `other-debt-repayable == u0`), triggering `socialize-debt-asset` over the borrower's remaining debt in asset D — even though collateral B still exists and has not been liquidated or returned to the borrower.
5. All suppliers of the debt-D vault absorb the socialized shortfall via the vault's shared `lindex`/`total-borrowed` state, while collateral B remains stranded in the borrower's position, unaccounted for in the socialization the liquidator triggered.

*Note: I could not execute this scenario against a live test harness within the scope of this review; the mechanics above are derived directly from reading `liquidate` and the vault `socialize-debt` function, and the exact dust thresholds needed to trigger `other-debt-repayable == 0` would depend on `liq-penalty-max`, `debt-price`, and `debt-decimals` for the specific asset pair, which should be confirmed with a Devin session that has full repository/test-harness access.*
