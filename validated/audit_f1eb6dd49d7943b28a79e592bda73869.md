### Title
RAY-value overflow in index accrual permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`update_indexes` (and every verb that accrues first — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `flash_*`) computes total debt as `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81) and total supplied value as `supplied.mul(old_index)` in `update_supply_index` (common/src/rates/index.rs:34). `Ray::mul` uses `mul_div_half_up`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled * index` leaves `i128` (common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118, via `scaled_to_original` in common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16). The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` / `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` caps are applied only after this multiplication, so the cap never engages before the value-overflow panic. Once `scaled_amount × index` exceeds ~`i128::MAX`, the panic is deterministic on every subsequent accrual, permanently freezing the market. This is the integer-overflow class of CVE-2022-2743 mapped onto Soroban arithmetic: an out-of-range product escapes into a trap instead of a bounded result.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: every user-facing entrypoint accrues indexes before touching position state, so once the product overflows, no supplier can withdraw, no borrower can repay, and no liquidator can act in that (hub, token) book. All supplied principal and unclaimed yield in the market are locked forever. The repository's own test harness demonstrates this end state: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361) asserts `MathOverflow` from `update_indexes`, then asserts `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` revert with the same error while `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — proving the index cap does not protect the value ceiling.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged calls: `supply` builds the scaled balance, `borrow`/`update_indexes` grow the borrow index. Overflow requires `scaled × index > i128::MAX`, i.e. roughly `C × index > 1.7e18` for a 7-decimal asset (or `> 1.7e7` whole-token units for an 18-decimal asset). That demands a large cap (listing caps are bounded only by `require_cap_within_asset_domain`/`max_cap_for_decimals`, ~i128::MAX scale) plus sustained high utilization so interest compounds the index several-fold — on a steep curve the harness reaches it within a few simulated years on a billion-unit 18-decimal market. Not reachable on small, conservatively capped markets, and requires no privileged action, leaked key, or off-chain component. Medium likelihood, Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Cap the effective exposure before the multiply, not the index after it. Concretely: (a) enforce a per-market bound on `scaled × index` (the RAY-denominated total value) at `supply`/`borrow` mint time so it can never approach `i128::MAX`; and/or (b) make `update_borrow_index` clamp `interest_factor` so that `borrowed.mul(new_index)` is proven to fit — e.g. compute `new_index` with `mul_div_floor_saturating`-style semantics or clamp against `i128::MAX / borrowed` before calling `calculate_supplier_rewards`; and/or (c) in `update_supply_index`/`calculate_supplier_rewards`, compare index deltas and multiply by `(new_index - old_index)` after dividing `borrowed` down, keeping intermediate products inside `i128` so accrual degrades to capped growth instead of a permanent trap.

### Proof of Concept
1. As an unprivileged address, call controller `supply` into a market whose supply cap admits it, until `scaled_supply` (RAY) is near `i128::MAX / current_index` — feasible for high-decimal assets or any asset whose configured cap is within `max_cap_for_decimals`.
2. Call `borrow` at high utilization on a steep rate curve (or simply let utilization stay high), then call the permissionless `update_indexes` repeatedly as time accrues interest.
3. When `borrowed × new_borrow_index > i128::MAX`, `calculate_supplier_rewards` at `common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81` panics with `MathOverflow` inside `Ray::mul` → `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:131-142`).
4. Thereafter every call that accrues — `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `supply`, `borrow`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize` — replays the same overflowing product and reverts. The market and all user balances in it are permanently frozen, as demonstrated by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:336-361`.
