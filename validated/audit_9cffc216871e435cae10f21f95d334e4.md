### Title
Seller can front-run a position-NFT sale to drain collateral via `borrow`/`withdraw`, leaving the buyer with a hollowed-out account - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
Every lending account in XOXNO Lending is a transferable position NFT (`account_id == token_id`), and transferring the token transfers the whole position — collateral and debt — atomically. Nothing binds the account's state at the moment of sale to what the buyer receives: the current holder may call `controller::borrow` (with `to: Some(seller)`) or `controller::withdraw` right up to the ledger where the NFT transfer executes, extracting all realizable value while the debt stays on the account. This is the direct analog of the Footium escrow-drain front-run: a trader buying a position NFT priced against its collateral can be left holding an account whose value has already been extracted.

### Finding Description
Ownership resolution is live: the controller stores no owner and calls `owner_of(account_id)` on the position NFT every time it needs authorization, so whoever holds the token at execution time controls the account. Both drain entrypoints are unprivileged and reachable by the seller until the exact moment the transfer lands:

- `process_withdraw` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:140-169) requires only `require_owner_or_delegate` (line 150), resolves `recipient = to.unwrap_or(caller)` (line 152), treats a zero amount as `WITHDRAW_ALL_SENTINEL` (lines 190-194), and pays the full position to any non-protocol `recipient`. A debt-free account can be emptied in one call.
- `process_borrow` (contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:33-66) likewise requires only owner/delegate (line 43), then `settle_borrow` sends the borrowed tokens to `recipient` (line 109) while the debt shares are merged onto `account_id` (lines 104-122). The seller can borrow the account to the solvency boundary — `enforce_post_pool_solvency` only requires HF ≥ 1 — pocketing the proceeds while the obligation stays with the NFT.

Neither function records any "pending sale" state, and `withdraw` is even callable during global pause. After the NFT moves, the buyer's calls succeed (they are now the owner) but act on a drained/maxed-out account; per the documented design, "transferring the token transfers the whole position" and "the new holder can withdraw only what leaves the debt covered" — i.e. the debt the seller created travels with the token.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. A buyer who prices a position NFT off `get_collateral_amount` / `get_total_collateral_usd` quoted before settlement receives an account worth far less: in the `borrow` variant the collateral is encumbered by freshly minted debt up to the solvency limit; in the `withdraw` variant the collateral is gone entirely (and a fully emptied account plus its NFT can even be deleted/burned by `cleanup_account_if_empty`, leaving the purchased token id non-existent). The purchase price paid for the position is unrecoverable.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an OTC or marketplace trade where the NFT transfer and payment are not bundled atomically with a collateral snapshot — the same precondition as the Footium finding. Position NFTs are deliberately standard SEP-50-style transferable tokens so "wallets, indexers, and marketplaces can read and move it with no protocol-specific tooling", i.e. secondary-market trading is an intended use. Front-running costs the seller one ordinary `borrow` or `withdraw` transaction; on Stellar the seller can observe the buyer's signed/submitted transfer in flight (or simply drain right before executing their half of the swap). Severity Medium-High: a real, single-transaction theft path, but gated on off-protocol trade settlement design.

### Recommendation
Marketplaces settling position-NFT trades should atomically bundle the NFT transfer with the payment and assert post-trade invariants in the same transaction (e.g. a receiver contract that checks `get_health_factor`, `get_collateral_amount`, and `get_borrow_amount` for the token id and reverts if they deviate from agreed values), rather than relying on a pre-trade snapshot. At the protocol level, the only strict mitigation would be an owner-initiated "transfer lock" that freezes `borrow`/`withdraw`/strategy calls until the next transfer, which conflicts with the design goal of unrestricted exits; documenting that a quoted position is a snapshot and must be re-verified at settlement is the practical fix.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice owns account `id` (position NFT token `id`) holding 10,000 USDC collateral, no debt. She agrees to sell the NFT to Bob for 9,500 USDC via an escrow/marketplace transaction that will execute `position_nft.transfer(Alice, Bob, id)` on payment.
2. Bob's transaction is assembled. Before it lands (or front-running it in the same ledger), Alice submits `controller.withdraw(caller=Alice, account_id=id, withdrawals=[(hubAsset, 0)], to=Some(Alice))`. `process_withdraw` passes `require_owner_or_delegate` because `owner_of(id)` still returns Alice; the zero sentinel empties the whole position and `apply_withdraw_batch` pays Alice. Alternatively she calls `borrow(caller=Alice, account_id=id, borrows=[(hubAsset, maxAmount)], to=Some(Alice))`, which mints debt onto `id` while paying the tokens to Alice.
3. Bob's trade settles: `transfer` succeeds and `owner_of(id)` now returns Bob — but the account is empty (or burned by `cleanup_account_if_empty`) / carries debt up to the solvency floor. Bob's own `withdraw` now returns ~0 or cannot pass `enforce_post_pool_solvency` without repaying Alice's borrow.

Existing tests confirm every step: `transfer_hands_full_control_to_new_owner` and `debt_travels_with_the_token` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/position_nft.rs:46-130) show collateral and debt move with the token and the new owner acts immediately; `test_withdraw_to_pays_third_party_recipient` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/withdraw.rs:247-269) and `borrow_to` routing show proceeds can be directed to the seller; and there is no check anywhere that a pending transfer should block draining.
