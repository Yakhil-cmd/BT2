### Title
Borrower shields an underwater account from liquidation by adding a dust collateral leg whose price read can be forced to fail - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
All state-changing controller paths that value an account — `liquidate`, `withdraw`, `clean_bad_debt`, `borrow`, `update_account_threshold` — read strict, fail-closed prices for **every** supply position the account holds. `supply`, however, performs no price read at all. A borrower can therefore supply a dust amount of a listed Aquarius LP collateral whose pool value they can unilaterally push below `min_pool_value_wad` (by removing their own liquidity on Aquarius), making that leg's price read return `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`. From that point, the account cannot be liquidated, cleaned, or force-socialized until pool value recovers — while its debt keeps accruing into bad debt borne by suppliers.

### Finding Description
- `controller::supply` is permissionless for top-ups and imposes no solvency/price gate; a harness test confirms a supply leg is accepted even while that asset's feed is stale (`audit_supply_stale_shield`, `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs:26-35`).
- `build_liquidation_plan` computes `risk::calculate_account_risk_totals` over all `supply_positions` before any payment is processed, and context-cached prices are strict: one unreadable leg aborts the whole call (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs:34-44`). The same risk-total pass gates `withdraw` and `clean_bad_debt`.
- The Aquarius provider returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < lp.min_pool_value_wad` (`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:115-122`). Pool value is computed from live on-chain reserves (`aquarius_pool_reserves_call`, lines 90-93), so any LP holder — including the attacker — who removes enough liquidity pushes the read below the floor. `NoLastPrice`/`InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` propagate through `engine::resolve_nested` and fail the strict read.
- Mainnet lists Aquarius LP collateral with a real floor, e.g. `USTRYUSDC_LP` (hub 3) with `min_pool_value_wad = 1e24` ($1M) (`configs/mainnet/markets.json:1386-1408`). A thin pool above the floor is one withdrawal away from failing.
- The threat model acknowledges this exact surface (DoS.1: "an indebted borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral and choose which feed outage shields the account... For an Aquarius LP leg, liquidity providers can cause that outage"), but acknowledgement does not mitigate it — the protocol has no minimum collateral value per leg and no way to drop or exclude an unpriceable leg.
- The harness test `audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg` proves the mechanism end-to-end with a stale feed; substituting the Aquarius liquidity floor produces the identical revert path under an attacker's own control rather than waiting for oracle staleness (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_stale_leg.rs:36-47`).

### Impact Explanation
While the shield is active: `liquidate` reverts, so a position that slides below HF = 1 cannot be closed; `clean_bad_debt` reverts, so the growing insolvency cannot be socialized and flushed; `withdraw` by the borrower is also blocked (acceptable to the attacker — the leg is dust). Suppliers' backing erodes as interest accrues on debt that cannot be liquidated, and if the outage persists the bad debt is eventually written into the supply index — theft from suppliers via forced insolvency, and a temporary freeze of liquidation/cleanup paths for that account. If the LP asset is thin, the attacker can hold the shield for the cost of the dust leg plus keeping pool liquidity below the floor.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address can execute it: open account → `supply` collateral → `borrow` → `supply(account_id, spoke_id, [(LP_hub_asset, dust)])` → remove own liquidity from the Aquarius pool until `pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad`. No privileged role, no oracle compromise, no leaked key. The constraint is that the listed LP asset must be collateral-eligible on the borrower's spoke and the attacker must control (or wait for) enough of the pool's liquidity to cross the floor — plausible on thin mainnet Aquarius pools.

### Recommendation
- Enforce a minimum USD floor per supply leg (a dust collateral position must exceed a configured value at supply time, priced strict), so a new leg cannot be cheap enough to be a pure shield.
- Alternatively, skip below-floor/zero-value collateral legs in risk aggregation for liquidation and cleanup, or allow liquidation to seize only priced legs with the unpriceable leg left behind.
- Add a governance escape to delist/ignore a poisoned dust leg, and consider a `min_pool_value_wad` headroom check relative to the pool's owned-liquidity concentration at listing time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker creates account A (`supply` with `account_id = 0`), supplies USDC collateral, borrows ETH to the LTV limit.
2. Attacker calls `controller.supply(attacker, A, spoke_id, [(HubAssetKey{hub_id:3, asset:USTRYUSDC_LP}, dust)])` — succeeds; `supply` performs no price read.
3. Attacker withdraws their liquidity from Aquarius pool `CCX2TYR4AQTPPMTZOIMP3YIBHAYLLSTXVP47PAGTL753PCQFSKV32MIA` until `price_wad * total_shares / 1e7 < 1e24` (they need only enough LP share to cross the floor).
4. Market moves against A (or interest accrues) → HF < 1. Any `liquidate(liq, A, payments, mode)` reverts inside `calculate_account_risk_totals` with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` (#235); `clean_bad_debt(caller, A)` reverts the same way; `update_account_threshold` with `has_risks` reverts.
5. Debt accrues `max_borrow_rate`-capped interest while unliquidatable. When convenient, attacker re-adds liquidity to the pool (or waits), restoring the price — the shield was entirely self-inflicted and reversible. The pattern is verified by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_liquidate_and_clean_bricked_by_unpriceable_dust_leg.rs` (stale-feed variant) and `contracts/price-aggregator/tests/oracle/registry.rs:1153-1167` (one-wei-under-floor refusal).
