### Title
Stale-but-within-window oracle price is accepted for collateral valuation, enabling undercollateralized borrows - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator marks a feed stale only when its age strictly exceeds a configured time budget (`feed.max_stale_seconds` and `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds`), both of which may be configured as high as `MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS = 93_600` (26 hours). Any observation younger than that boundary is treated as fully fresh and usable for health-factor and borrowing-power computation, even if the feed has stopped updating and the real market price has moved significantly. This is the same bug class as the external report: a time-window-only freshness check with no round-completeness/`answeredInRound`-style proof that the feed actually updated.

### Finding Description
Staleness is decided exclusively by elapsed time in `is_stale` (`common/src/oracle/observation.rs:55-57`): `now_secs > feed_ts && (now_secs - feed_ts) > max_stale`. `read_feed` computes staleness per feed against `feed.max_stale_seconds` (engine.rs:613-617), and `read_source` ORs in staleness against `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds` (engine.rs:555-560). An observation is rejected only when the `stale` flag is set (`Outcome::failure`, engine.rs:133-135); a price 25h59m old under a 26h window passes with `stale == false`. Both bounds accept up to 26h (`validation.rs:44-54`, `validation.rs:160-162`), and the deployed runbook explicitly recommends 93,600 s for daily-posted feeds (docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md §6). There is no equivalent of Chainlink's `answeredInRound >= roundID` check: `OracleObservation::from_reflector`/`from_multi_feed` (observation.rs:25-57) validate only the future-skew bound and price normalization, never that a newer round exists. The controller then consumes `prices()`/`force()` results for collateral/debt valuation in the borrow/withdraw/liquidate paths, so a stale-but-in-window price is used as strict truth.

### Impact Explanation
For any asset whose feed heartbeat is slower than the market (daily-posted NAV feeds such as the documented Liqvid case, or any Reflector/RedStone feed that simply stops publishing mid-window), the protocol values collateral and debt at a price up to 26h old. If the real price fell, an attacker calls `borrow` against overvalued collateral and walks away with undercollateralized debt (protocol insolvency). If the real price rose, an attacker borrows the now-undervalued debt asset cheaply, or repays/withdraws at favorable stale pricing — direct theft of pool funds. Impact scales with the market move during the staleness window; sanity bands cap only absolute min/max price, not deviation from the real market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only that (a) an asset is configured with a wide staleness budget — explicitly the documented recommendation for daily feeds — and (b) the real price moves meaningfully while the feed is silent, a routine occurrence for volatile tokens and guaranteed for NAV feeds between daily postings. Fully reachable by any unprivileged address via `borrow`/`withdraw`/`liquidate`; no privileged role, leaked key, or off-chain component is needed.

### Recommendation
Add feed-level freshness proof beyond elapsed time: for providers that expose a round/sequence number (Reflector `lastprice` history, Xoxno adapter aggregates), require the observation to be the latest published round and, where available, that `updated_at`/`answered_in_round` monotonicity holds. More practically: enforce per-asset staleness budgets that match the feed's actual heartbeat (reject budgets much larger than the published update cadence rather than allowing the 26h ceiling indiscriminately), and consider rejecting single-source spot prices whose age exceeds a fraction of the sanity-band-implied volatility horizon.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance/admin configures token T with a Reflector or Xoxno feed, `feed.max_stale_seconds = oracle.max_price_stale_seconds = 93_600` (allowed by `validation.rs` and recommended by the Liqvid runbook).
2. Feed posts price P for T at timestamp t0, then stops updating (daily cadence or outage).
3. At t0 + 20h the real market price of T has dropped 30%. `is_stale(now, t0, 93_600)` is false, so `read_source`/`blend` produce `stale = false` and `Outcome::failure` returns `None`; `prices()` returns the stale P.
4. Attacker supplies T as collateral and calls controller `borrow`, drawing debt valued against P instead of 0.7·P, then defaults. The position is undercollateralized at market prices; liquidation cannot recover the shortfall, creating bad debt absorbed by suppliers via supply-index write-down.
