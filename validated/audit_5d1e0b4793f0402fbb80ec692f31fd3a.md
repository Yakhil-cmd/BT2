### Title
Stale `account.owner` grants withdrawn authorization after position-NFT transfer — ([File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs])

### Summary
Account authorization for the fund-moving entrypoints (`withdraw`, `borrow`, `multiply`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, `migrate_from_blend`, `flash_position`) is checked against `account.owner`, a field snapshotted in the `Account` storage record at creation time — not against the live position-NFT owner. When the NFT is transferred (the documented way to sell/move a whole lending account), `account.owner` is never updated, so the *previous* owner retains full owner-level authorization over the account they no longer hold. This is the missing-authorization analog of CVE-2021-35936: a reachable surface that serves an actor who should no longer be authorized.

### Finding Description
`load_or_create_account` and the strategy paths authenticate the caller via `require_owner_or_delegate(env, account_id, caller, &account.owner)`, where `owner` is the stored `Account.owner` field [1](#0-0) . `is_owner_or_delegate` returns `true` immediately when `caller == owner`, before any delegate lookup [2](#0-1) . `Account.owner` is set once in `create_account_with` from the NFT mint recipient and stored in the account struct [3](#0-2) .

The codebase itself treats the NFT owner as the source of truth elsewhere: `require_account_owner` deliberately resolves `storage::account_owner(env, account_id)` (the live NFT owner) rather than trusting the struct field [4](#0-3) , and the permissionless declaration states "The NFT owner is the account owner" and that delegate grants from a previous owner go inactive after transfer [5](#0-4) . No transfer hook or re-sync path updates `account.owner` when `position-nft::transfer`/`transfer_from` runs (the NFT contract is stock OpenZeppelin enumerable; the only owner references there are stock balance/ownership bookkeeping, and the controller exposes no owner-refresh entrypoint).

Net effect: after `transfer`, the *new* NFT owner fails `require_owner_or_delegate` (their address ≠ stale `account.owner` and they have no delegate grant), while the *old* owner still passes it. The old owner can call `controller::withdraw(account_id)` or `borrow(account_id)` and drain the buyer's collateral or mint debt against it — the declared justification for `withdraw` ("`require_owner_or_delegate` pins account_id to its owner") is violated because the pin is to a stale owner [6](#0-5) .

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and insolvency-adjacent griefing, reachable by any address that ever owned an account NFT:

- After selling/transferring a position NFT, the seller calls `controller::withdraw` with `account_id` of the sold account; `caller == account.owner` (stale) passes, collateral is pulled and sent to the seller-controlled flow.
- Or `controller::borrow` against the buyer's collateral, leaving the buyer an undercollateralized account.
- Corollary: the legitimate new NFT owner cannot call any owner/delegate-gated entrypoint (withdraw, borrow, repay through strategies, add/remove delegate) — they hold the NFT but are not `account.owner` — so their funds are also effectively frozen for position management until liquidation or a repay path.

This qualifies as theft of user funds plus temporary freezing of funds for the rightful owner.

### Likelihood Explanation
High under any scenario where position NFTs move: OTC position sales, wallet migration, custodial transfer, or marketplace trade — all supported since `transfer`/`transfer_from`/`approve`/`approve_for_all` are live stock entrypoints. Exploitation requires only that the former owner retained knowledge of `account_id` (public on-chain). The only uncertainty is whether an undocumented off-index sync exists in `storage::account` or a transfer hook I could not fully enumerate within tool limits; the greps found no `on_transfer`/owner-refresh code in the controller or position-nft contracts.

### Recommendation
Make NFT ownership the single source of truth for authorization:

- In `is_owner_or_delegate`, resolve the owner via `storage::account_owner(env, account_id)` (as `require_account_owner` already does) instead of the cached `account.owner` field, or
- Remove `Account.owner` entirely and always read the NFT owner, updating `load_or_create_account` and all guard callers accordingly.
- Add a regression test: create account → supply → `nft.transfer(owner, buyer)` → assert seller's `withdraw(account_id)` reverts with `NotAuthorized` and buyer's succeeds.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice calls `controller::supply(account_id=0, ...)`; `load_or_create_account` mints NFT #1 to Alice and stores `Account{ owner: alice, ...}` [7](#0-6) .
2. Alice calls `position-nft::transfer(alice, bob, token_id=1)`; NFT owner becomes Bob. `Account.owner` remains `alice` — no controller hook runs.
3. Alice calls `controller::withdraw(account_id=1, ...)`; the controller loads the account and calls `require_owner_or_delegate(env, 1, alice, account.owner=alice)` → `caller == owner` → authorized [8](#0-7) . Collateral exits to Alice.
4. Bob, the actual NFT owner, cannot withdraw: `is_owner_or_delegate(bob)` fails — `bob != account.owner` and Bob has no delegate entry keyed under `alice`'s owner slot [9](#0-8) .

```rust
pub(crate) fn is_owner_or_delegate(
    env: &Env,
    account_id: u64,
    caller: &Address,
    owner: &Address,
) -> bool {
    if caller == owner {
        return true;
    }
    let active_manager =
        storage::get_position_manager(env, caller).is_some_and(|config| config.is_active);
    active_manager && storage::get_delegates(env, account_id, owner).contains(caller)
}
```
