### No vulnerability found for this question.

The premise conflates two unrelated subsystems. `resolve-price-feed` [1](#0-0)  is a pure oracle-type dispatcher that reads a caller-supplied `(buff 1)` type tag and routes to `resolve-pyth`/`resolve-dia`; it has no relationship to efficiency-group (egroup) selection and no mutable state that `liquidate-redeem`'s seized/redeemed zToken amount could influence. `liquidate-redeem` only performs `liquidate` (with the market as zToken receiver) followed by `vault-redeem` of the seized amount for underlying [2](#0-1) ; the amount seized and redeemed is a liquidation-penalty-derived quantity computed by `liquidate` itself, not an attacker-chosen free parameter, and it does not write to any oracle `type`/`ident` dispatch table or egroup-selection map that a victim's separate transaction later reads.

Efficiency-group assignment/enumeration is governed by the DAO-controlled registry (`v0-egroup.clar`) [3](#0-2) , and per the rules, egroup invariants requiring global position knowledge are verified off-chain before DAO approval, and any issue requiring a DAO registry update or arising from that trust model is explicitly out of scope. No code path was found linking `resolve-price-feed`'s 1-byte dispatch or `liquidate-redeem`'s redeemed amount to a victim's egroup outcome, so the two-principal (attacker A / victim B) shared-state requirement cannot be established.
