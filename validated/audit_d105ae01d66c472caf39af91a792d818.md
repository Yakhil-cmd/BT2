### Title
Suppliers can front-run `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` with `withdraw` to exit at the pre-write-down index and dump the entire socialized loss onto remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The pending-loss pattern from the Y2K `triggerDepeg`/`triggerEndEpoch` issue maps directly onto XOXNO Lending's bad-debt socialization. `clean_bad_debt` resolves an insolvent account by lowering the debt market's `supply_index` (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`), which socializes the loss pro-rata across that market's suppliers. Nothing prevents a supplier who observes the pending cleanup transaction from calling `withdraw` first and being paid out at the old, higher index while the pool's `cash` is still intact. This is the same escape-the-loss ordering bug: the withdrawal path has no check that a loss-socializing resolution is imminent.

### Finding Description [1](#0-0)  shows `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`: it computes `reduction_factor = (total_supplied_value - capped_bad_debt) / total_supplied_value` and multiplies the market's `supply_index` down. The loss is allocated across whatever `supplied` shares exist at the moment `seize::apply` runs ( [2](#0-1) ), invoked from the controller's permissionless `clean_bad_debt` flow (eligibility: ceil risk debt > half-up unweighted collateral, collateral ≤ $5 dust threshold, INV-LIQ-04 [3](#0-2) ).

Meanwhile `withdraw` pays out `scaled × supply_index` in token units against the pool's `cash`. A debt-free supplier skips the post-action solvency gates entirely (INV-RISK-01: "Debt-free accounts skip these numerical gates", [4](#0-3) ). Because the write-down is index-based and only manifests when `clean_bad_debt` executes, a withdrawal confirmed in an earlier ledger entry redeems at the full pre-loss index and removes both shares and cash before the loss is priced in. The subsequent `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` then divides the same fixed `bad_debt` over a smaller `total_supplied_value`, producing a strictly larger `reduction_factor` haircut for everyone who did not front-run.

### Impact Explanation
A supplier (or a delegated/associated account) that monitors the mempool for an incoming `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` on a market it supplies can exit whole at par, converting a bounded pro-rata index write-down into zero loss for itself while amplifying the write-down borne by passive suppliers. In the extreme case, sophisticated early exiters drain `cash` until the pool can no longer pay claims at even the post-write-down index — residual suppliers are left with unbacked claims until `recapitalize` fills the shortfall (the docs already concede "a displayed claim does not guarantee that the supplier can withdraw that amount", ADR-0012). This is theft-by-ordering of the socialized loss, identical in structure to Y2K sellers escaping collateral transfer by resolving the epoch before the depeg path.

### Likelihood Explanation
`clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and its eligibility predicates (insolvent account, collateral ≤ $5) are publicly observable on-chain well before execution — a rational large supplier can compute that a write-down is imminent simply by watching the insolvent account and oracle prices. The only requirement is sufficient pool `cash` at withdrawal time, which holds precisely because the write-down has not yet been applied. No privileged access, oracle manipulation, or external dependency is needed; a single unprivileged address submits `withdraw` and (optionally) `clean_bad_debt` in adjacent calls. Medium likelihood: it requires monitoring and a front-running position on Stellar, but the window between "cleanup becomes eligible" and "cleanup executes" can be arbitrarily long for permissionless dust accounts nobody services.

### Recommendation
Check for pending adverse resolution before honoring full-value exits, mirroring the report's "check strike price at `triggerEndEpoch`". Concretely: before a `withdraw` (or same-market `swap_collateral` withdrawal leg) on a market, evaluate whether any known bad-debt candidate would force a write-down and, if so, apply the projected `supply_index` reduction to the withdrawal; alternatively, gate `withdraw` behind a per-market epoch/flag set when an account first becomes cleanup-eligible, or burn shares at the lower of the current index and the index implied by `cash + outstanding_debt` backing (i.e., cap the payout by `guards::backing_shortfall`). At minimum, allow `clean_bad_debt` to atomically combine with a withdrawal-block marker so exiters cannot reorder ahead of it.

### Proof of Concept
1. BOB supplies 100,000 USDC to hub market `H_USDC`; ALICE supplies 8 USDC and borrows ETH; ETH price spikes so ALICE's account is deeply insolvent with ≤$5 residual collateral.
2. `clean_bad_debt(alice_account)` becomes permissionlessly callable (INV-LIQ-04 conditions met). The pending call is publicly observable, or its eligibility is trivially derivable from `get_health_factor`/`get_total_collateral_usd` views.
3. BOB front-runs it with `withdraw(caller=bob, account_id, H_USDC, full_amount)`. BOB is debt-free, so no solvency gate runs; the pool pays BOB `scaled × supply_index_pre` in USDC from intact `cash`.
4. `clean_bad_debt` executes: `seize::apply` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` over the now-smaller `supplied` base; `reduction_factor = (supplied_value − bad_debt) / supplied_value` is smaller than it would have been had BOB stayed in, so every remaining supplier's `supply_index` falls further.
5. Result: BOB received par value for a claim that was economically impaired; the entire loss lands on non-front-running suppliers, exactly as insurance sellers escaping `triggerDepeg` via `triggerEndEpoch` in the source report.

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}
```
