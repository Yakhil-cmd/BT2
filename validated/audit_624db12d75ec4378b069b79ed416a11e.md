### Title
RAY value-ceiling overflow in interest accrual permanently freezes a market — all verbs that accrue panic in `scaled_to_original`/`Ray::mul` - ([File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs](contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs))

### Summary
Analog to CVE-2016-7054 (a large payload crashing TLS connections): oversized *values* rather than oversized *bytes* crash the protocol. When a market's scaled debt book times its borrow index exceeds the finite RAY (`i128`-backed) value capacity, the fixed-point multiplication inside accrual panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. Because every state-changing pool/controller verb accrues the market first, the market becomes permanently unrecoverable: no repay, withdraw, borrow, or liquidation ever succeeds again.

### Finding Description
The pool converts scaled shares to original value via `Cache::calculate_utilization`, which calls `scaled_to_original(&self.env, self.borrowed, self.borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(&self.env, self.supplied, self.supply_index)` [1](#0-0) . Interest accrual likewise computes `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` and `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` in `update_supply_index` [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

The borrow index itself is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` [4](#0-3) , but the cap guards the *index*, not the *product* `scaled_amount * index`. The product's representable range (~`i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38`, i.e. RAY values up to ~170 units of 1e27) can be exhausted by a sufficiently large scaled book well before the index cap engages.

A dedicated regression test demonstrates exactly this: with a whale-scale book at 98% utilization on the steep XLM rate curve, an accrual eventually panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and afterwards `withdraw` and `repay` both panic identically [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once `borrowed * borrow_index` (or `supplied * supply_index`) overflows the RAY value capacity, every subsequent accrual panics, and every user-facing verb — `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize`, `update_indexes` — performs accrual/utilization on that market before doing anything else. All supplied tokens in that market are permanently locked in the pool contract; all debt can never be repaid or liquidated, so the bad debt can never even be socialized. This is a contract unable to operate, not a transient fail-closed rejection of a single malformed input.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger is reachable entirely through unprivileged entrypoints: `supply`/`borrow` build the oversized book, and `update_indexes` (permissionless) performs the accrual that panics. The barrier is economic, not authorization-based: an attacker must push a market's scaled book to near the RAY value ceiling, which requires either a genuinely huge principal (billions of units of a high-decimals asset, i.e. lifted or absent supply caps) or a long horizon at high utilization on a steep rate curve (the test reaches the cliff only after years at 98% utilization). Where governance lists high-decimal assets with high or disabled supply caps, a whale-funded attacker can materially accelerate the approach; on capped markets the cliff is mostly a function of time and utilization. Given the capital/time cost, this rates Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Bound the *product*, not just the index. Options:
- Enforce a supply-side cap such that `scaled_supply * MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (and `scaled_borrow * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) provably fits `i128`, checked at admission in `validate_risk_bounds`/listing config and on every `supply`/`borrow` against the configured supply caps.
- Make `scaled_to_original` and `Ray::mul` saturate at `i128::MAX` instead of panicking in the accrual path (consistent with `mul_div_floor_saturating` already used in `update_supply_index`/`protocol_fee_shares` [6](#0-5) ), so a saturated market degrades to a capped index rather than a permanent panic.
- At minimum, clamp `total_supplied_value`/`new_total_debt` computations and skip interest distribution when the product would overflow, preserving liveness at the cost of under-distributing yield.

### Proof of Concept
Already encoded as a regression test: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320`. It supplies `BILLION * 10^18` units of an 18-decimal asset, borrows 98% of it, and advances time yearly until `try_update_indexes_for(["BIG18"])` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. It then asserts the freeze: `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, since every verb accrues first [7](#0-6) .

```rust
/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```
