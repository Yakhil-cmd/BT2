### Title
Liquidation Dust-Rounding Allows Excess Collateral Seizure With Bad-Debt Socialized to Suppliers - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
`liquidate()` computes a capped, proportional collateral seizure (`coll-final-raw`) and then checks whether the *leftover* collateral (`coll-remaining`) still corresponds to non-trivial debt (`remaining-debt-to-repay`). When that leftover-debt calculation rounds down to `0` due to `mul-div-down`/`div-bps-down` truncation, the contract silently switches to seizing the borrower's **entire** collateral balance (`coll-final = user-coll-balance`) while the liquidator only actually repays the smaller, previously-computed `debt-to-repay`. Any debt corresponding to the extra collateral handed to the liquidator is written off through `socialize-debt-asset`, spreading the loss across all suppliers of that debt vault.

### Finding Description
In `liquidate()`: [1](#0-0) 

- `debt-to-repay` and `coll-final-raw` are computed from the (possibly capped) liquidatable debt.
- `coll-remaining = user-coll-balance - coll-final-raw` is the amount of collateral not being seized in this call.
- `remaining-debt-to-repay` re-derives, in USD then back to debt tokens then to scaled units, how much debt the *leftover* collateral is worth. This chain uses `div-bps-down` and `mul-div-down`, both of which truncate toward zero.
- If `coll-remaining` is small enough (a dust amount), this chain can legitimately evaluate to `0`, even though `coll-remaining > 0`.
- When that happens, `coll-final` is overridden to `user-coll-balance` — the liquidator seizes the *entire* remaining collateral balance instead of the originally, correctly-capped `coll-final-raw`. Crucially, `debt-to-repay` (what the liquidator actually pays into `vault-system-repay`) is **not** recalculated to match this larger seizure — it stays at the smaller amount tied to `coll-final-raw`. [2](#0-1) 

- Afterwards, if this collateral asset was the borrower's only/last remaining collateral, `no-collateral-left` becomes true and any un-repaid debt tied to the position is force-written-off via `socialize-debt-asset`, which folds over the debt list and calls each vault's `socialize-debt`: [3](#0-2) 

- Vault-level `socialize-debt` reduces `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed`/`assets` and writes down the liquidity index (`lindex`) proportionally to the loss — this is the shared state that spreads the loss across **all suppliers** of the vault, not just the liquidated borrower: [4](#0-3) 

This mirrors the report's bug class: a value calculation (fee, here "remaining debt owed") can be legitimately non-zero in principle but rounds to zero under `mul-div-down`, and the code's fallback for the zero case ("give away the whole remainder") disproportionately benefits one unprivileged party (the liquidator) at another's expense — here, both the borrower (excess collateral seized beyond what was paid for) and, via socialization, all suppliers of the debt vault (who absorb the unrepaid debt as a permanent loss).

The liquidator additionally has some control over the inputs that determine whether this rounding-to-zero condition triggers: they choose `debt-amount`, and can supply `price-feeds` that get written via `write-feeds` before the position/collateral value is evaluated, giving them influence over `coll-price`/`debt-price` and therefore over `coll-remaining` and the truncation outcome: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
- The borrower loses more collateral than the liquidator actually paid for (seizure exceeding its calculated/entitled bound).
- The un-repaid portion of debt tied to that excess seizure is socialized onto all suppliers of the debt vault via `socialize-debt`, permanently diluting their claim on vault assets (`lindex` write-down) — this is a temporary/permanent freezing-of-funds-style loss borne by unrelated, unprivileged depositors who did nothing to cause it.
- This lands in the in-scope impact class of **temporary/permanent freezing of funds** for the socialized suppliers (and uncompensated collateral loss for the borrower), triggered entirely by an unprivileged liquidator's transaction exploiting rounding in this contract's own math — not an oracle-publisher error, not a deliberate safety design decision, and not DAO-registry related.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires a specific dust-sized `coll-remaining` to trigger the zero-rounding branch, which depends on token decimals, prices, and the borrower's exact collateral balance relative to `coll-expected`. However, the liquidator: (a) can choose `debt-amount` freely (subject only to `max-debt-usd` capping), (b) can supply fresh oracle price data via the `price-feeds` parameter before valuation, and (c) can scan for/target borrowers whose collateral balance sits just above the computed `coll-expected` threshold for a given debt amount. This combination gives a motivated liquidator meaningful control over whether the rounding-to-zero condition is hit, making deliberate exploitation plausible, though not trivial in every position.

### Recommendation
Do not let a rounding-to-zero result for `remaining-debt-to-repay` silently authorize seizing the full remaining collateral balance while leaving `debt-to-repay` unchanged. Instead:
- Require `debt-to-repay` to scale together with `coll-final` whenever the "seize all" branch is taken (i.e., recompute/require full debt corresponding to `user-coll-balance` be repaid, or explicitly cap `coll-final` at `coll-final-raw` when `coll-remaining` is non-zero).
- Alternatively, only allow the "seize-all" dust-cleanup path when `coll-remaining` is below an explicit, small protocol-defined dust threshold (in token or USD terms) rather than relying on `remaining-debt-to-repay == 0` from a truncating division chain, and require it to always correspondingly write off (not silently ignore) exactly the delta amount rather than the discretionary "socialize whatever debt is left" fallback.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (liquidator) identifies/engineers a liquidatable borrower position where, for a chosen `debt-amount`, `coll-final-raw` computed from `scale-debt-for-liquidation` leaves a small `coll-remaining = user-coll-balance - coll-final-raw` for the sole/last collateral asset of that borrower.
2. Attacker supplies `price-feeds` in the `liquidate()` call to nudge `coll-price`/`debt-price` such that when `coll-remaining` is converted through `normalize` → `div-bps-down` → `mul-div-down` → `mul-div-up`, the result `remaining-debt-to-repay` truncates to `u0` (lines 1477-1485).
3. Because `remaining-debt-to-repay` is `0`, the contract sets `coll-final = user-coll-balance` (line 1486) instead of the correctly capped `coll-final-raw` — the liquidator receives the borrower's entire collateral balance for that asset.
4. `debt-to-repay` was computed earlier from `coll-final-raw` and is unchanged, so the liquidator only repays that smaller amount via `vault-system-repay` (line 1496) — they receive collateral for free beyond what they paid for.
5. Because this was the borrower's only/last collateral asset, `no-collateral-left` is true and any remaining debt on the position is force-written-off through `socialize-debt-asset` → vault `socialize-debt`, which writes down `lindex` and reduces `assets`/`total-borrowed` for the vault — socializing the loss across all suppliers of that vault (lines 1526-1560; vault `socialize-debt` at `v0-vault-stx.clar:944-966`).

Note: I was unable to run this scenario against the test suite to empirically confirm the exact numeric conditions (decimals/price combinations) under which the rounding-to-zero branch is reachable in practice; this assessment is based on static analysis of the arithmetic and control flow in `v0-4-market.clar`. A background engineering session with test execution access would be needed to construct a concrete numeric PoC and confirm exploitability bounds.
