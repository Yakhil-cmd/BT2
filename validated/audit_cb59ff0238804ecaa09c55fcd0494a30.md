### Title
Stale pool `Cache` overwrites reentrant governance market mutation during flash loan - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs)

### Summary
`flash_loan` loads a mutable market snapshot before invoking an attacker-controlled receiver and commits that stale snapshot afterward, allowing a ready governance operation executed during the callback to mutate the same market and then have its state changes silently overwritten. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`LiquidityPool::flash_loan` calls `ops::flash::apply`, which creates `Cache` through `prepare`, computes repayment terms, transfers principal, invokes `execute_flash_loan`, collects repayment, and finally calls `finalize` on the original `Cache`. [3](#0-2) 

`Cache::load` copies all market state fields into memory, while `Cache::commit` blindly writes those same fields back to storage without checking whether another nested call changed them. [4](#0-3) 

During the receiver callback, the receiver can execute a previously ready governance operation that invokes `Controller::upgrade_liquidity_pool_params`; that owner-gated entry point renews storage and forwards the update without consulting the controller flash guard. [5](#0-4) 

The pool's `update_params` path intentionally accrues the market under the old model before writing the new parameters, so the nested governance call commits fresh indexes and timestamps for the same market while the outer flash loan retains the pre-callback copies. [6](#0-5) 

After repayment, `finalize` books the flash fee and calls `commit`, restoring the stale `supplied`, `borrowed`, `revenue`, `borrow_index`, `supply_index`, `last_timestamp`, and `cash` values over the nested mutation. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
If the governance operation lowers the market's future borrow rate, the nested `update_params` accrual is rolled back and the new rate is subsequently applied from the stale `last_timestamp`, permanently erasing yield that suppliers and protocol revenue had already accrued before the parameter change. [6](#0-5) [8](#0-7) 

A ready operation that mutates pool state more directly, such as forced bad-debt socialization, can likewise have its pool-level index and share mutations overwritten while its controller-side account mutations remain committed, corrupting the reconciliation between account books and pool totals. [9](#0-8) [8](#0-7) 

This permits theft of unclaimed yield and can create protocol insolvency through resurrected debt or erased supplier claims, rather than merely reverting the transaction. [10](#0-9)

The attacker only needs an authorized address, a deployed WASM flash receiver, a flashloanable market, and a ready governance operation affecting that market, all of which are unprivileged inputs to `Controller::flash_loan`. <cite repo
