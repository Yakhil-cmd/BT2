### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps `supply_index` up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving wiped suppliers a phantom claim that drains subsequently injected cash - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by scaling the supply index down by `remaining/total_supplied_value`, then clamps the result with `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))`. When bad debt equals or exceeds total supplied value (`remaining == 0`), the mathematically correct new index is `0`, but the clamp resurrects it to `RAY/1000`. Every pre-wipeout supply share is thereby left with a floor-valued claim on cash the holder was fully written out of — a truncation-in-the-middle mishandling directly analogous to the libarchive long-linkname truncation bug: a truncated/clamped intermediate value is later interpreted as a legitimate bound. The pool's own unit tests prove a stranded holder can then drain fresh cash deposited after the wipeout.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`: [1](#0-0) 

`capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value)` makes `remaining` saturate at `0`, `reduction_factor` becomes `0`, and `new_supply_index` becomes `0` — the write-down correctly intends a full wipeout. The clamp `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` then sets the index to a nonzero floor, so `unscale_supply_floor(old_shares)` still yields a positive claim.

The exploit path is fully permissionless:
1. `borrow` pushes utilization near `max_utilization` (capped at ~95% by `require_utilization_below_max` in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`); normal millisecond-chunked accrual in `global_sync` then grows `borrowed` past `supplied`.
2. A price move (or simply accrued interest) makes the position insolvent; a permissionless `liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt` routes through `ops::seize::apply` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`), which computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. The index clamps to the floor instead of zero.
3. The wiped supplier's shares retain a positive floor-valued claim. When new cash enters the market — a later `supply` (the unit tests demonstrate the drain at the cache layer), a permissionless `recapitalize` (which credits cash without minting shares), or direct token transfers — the stranded holder calls `withdraw`, and `resolve_withdrawal` pays them real tokens up to that phantom claim.

Proof at unit level: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-370` (`test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`) and `:373-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`) show `stranded > 0` after the clamp and `gross == fresh_cash` — the old holder "drains exactly the fresh deposit," leaving the honest supplier undercollateralized.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol cash: a fully wiped-out supplier keeps a nonzero claim at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` and can withdraw cash that belongs to later suppliers or to recapitalization donors. The magnitude is bounded by `shares × RAY/1000` per wiped market, but in a wipeout the entire supply base retains 0.1% of face value, and every unit of it is extracted from cash that entered after the loss was socialized. Caveat: whether the raw-cache drain replays end-to-end depends on whether the controller supply path runs `require_backed_market` before crediting new cash; even if new `supply` is blocked, `recapitalize` and direct transfers still inject drainable cash.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address. Creating debt ≥ supply value requires utilization to exceed 100% via interest accrual after borrowing near the cap — feasible on any market where borrow demand approaches `max_utilization` and time passes (the accrual loop has no utilization ceiling). The trigger (`liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`) and the extraction (`withdraw`) are permissionless entrypoints. The wipeout edge requires extreme debt/supply ratios, so likelihood is medium.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), the index should go to zero — the floor clamp should only prevent dust-level nonzero indexes, e.g. `if remaining == Ray::ZERO { set_supply_index(Ray::ZERO) } else { max(new_index, floor) }`, or equivalently burn/reset `supplied` shares to zero on full wipeout so no phantom claim survives. Add a guard in `seize::apply` asserting the post-write-down claim `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) <= cash` when `borrowed` is fully burned.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier B deposits `S` of asset X; attacker supplies collateral Y and `borrow`s X up to just under `max_utilization`.
2. Wait for interest accrual (or a price move) until `unscale_borrow_ceil(borrowed) >= supplied × supply_index`, then call `clean_bad_debt` on the attacker's own account (permissionless, dust-gated — use `force_socialize`-equivalent path via liquidate legs if needed).
3. `seize::apply` with `side = Borrow` clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; B's shares are wiped in intent but retain `unscale_supply_floor > 0`.
4. Inject cash via `recapitalize` or a fresh `supply`; B (or the attacker, who can also be the wiped supplier by supplying X from a second account) calls `withdraw(0)` and is paid real tokens from the post-wipeout cash, exactly as `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:339-368` demonstrates at the cache layer.
