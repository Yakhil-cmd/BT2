### Title
Repay/recapitalize overpayment refunds pay out of pool custody belonging to suppliers without debiting the cash book - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs, contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs)

### Summary
Like the OpenQ bounty that paid claims out of its whole balance (including funds owed to expired depositors), the XOXNO pool pays `repay` overpayment and `recapitalize` excess refunds out of its physical token balance using only the *declared* action amount — the refund is never debited from the `cash` book and never passes `require_reserves`. Funds that the books still attribute to suppliers are sent to the caller, so a single unprivileged repay can drain custody while the market reports itself solvent.

### Finding Description
`ops::repay::apply` resolves `action.amount` into burned debt shares plus `overpayment`, credits only `net_repay` to cash, then calls `cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` unconditionally [1](#0-0) . The refund is computed from the declared `amount`, not from a measured inbound balance delta, and `credit_cash(net_repay)` excludes it — so `cash` still reports the refunded tokens as present [2](#0-1) .

`ops::recapitalize::apply` does the same: `refund = amount - min(amount, backing_shortfall)` is transferred out of custody while only `applied` is credited [3](#0-2) .

The codebase's own tests prove the reachable path: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` calls `repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))` on a market with zero debt, the full declared amount is treated as overpayment, `token.balance(pool)` drops to 0, and `cash` is unchanged — so `Cache::require_reserves` keeps admitting withdrawals that then fail inside the SAC transfer [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
Custody and the `cash` book diverge: the pool's physical balance is reduced by refunds that were never booked. Consequences:

- If the refund exceeds what the payer actually transferred in (unfunded or under-delivered repay — exactly what the test exercises), the payer directly steals supplier funds: theft of user funds.
- Even when fully funded, the refund leg bypasses `require_reserves`, so the book overstates custody. Subsequent withdrawals/liquidation payouts pass the reserve check and revert inside the token transfer — the market reports itself solvent and cannot pay, i.e. freezing of funds and a contract unable to operate from lack of token funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged address through the public `repay`/`recapitalize` entrypoints; no price moves, liquidation state, or privileged role required. The only precondition for the pure-theft variant is that the declared amount is not verified against an actual inbound transfer (per the test, a repay against a zero-debt market suffices). The book/custody divergence persists after the call, so the insolvency is permanent once triggered.

### Recommendation
Measure the refund against a balance delta (record pool token balance before/after the inbound transfer) rather than trusting `action.amount`, and route the refund leg through the same cash accounting as other outflows — debit `cash` for the refund or require `require_reserves` on `transfer_out`, so custody and the book cannot diverge. Alternatively, have the controller pull exactly the net repay so no refund path exists on the pool leg.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market with suppliers and `cash == token.balance(pool) == B`, zero outstanding debt (`borrowed == 0`).
2. Attacker calls `repay(payer = attacker, ract(0, B))`.
3. In `resolve_repay`, `current_debt_ceil` is 0 → full-close branch → `net_repay = 0`, `overpayment = B`.
4. `transfer_out(attacker, B)` sends the pool's entire custody to the attacker; `cash` remains `B`.
5. `token.balance(pool) == 0` while the market still reports `cash == B`; the next supplier `withdraw` passes `require_reserves` and reverts in the SAC transfer. This is exactly `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs`.

The same sequence works through `recapitalize` when `backing_shortfall` is less than the declared `amount`.

```rust
/// Accrues interest, resolves the repay amount into burned debt shares and
/// overpayment, burns the shares, and credits the net repay to cash without
/// transferring the overpayment refund. Panics if a positive net repay would
/// burn zero scaled shares.
pub(crate) fn accounting(env: &Env, action: &PoolAction) -> RepayOutcome {
    let (mut cache, position) = ops::load_leg(env, action);
    let amount = action.amount;

    let (burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position);
    let net_repay = amount
        .checked_sub(overpayment)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        net_repay == 0 || burned.raw() > 0,
        GenericError::RepayRoundsToZeroShares
    );

    let position = position.checked_sub(env, burned);
    cache.burn_debt(burned);

    cache.credit_cash(net_repay);
```

```rust
/// Sizes and books the cash injection without transferring tokens.
///
/// Credits `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to cash and commits. `refund` is
/// `amount - applied`.
pub(crate) fn accounting(
    env: &Env,
    hub_asset: HubAssetKey,
    amount: i128,
) -> RecapitalizationOutcome {
    require_nonneg_amount(env, amount);
    let mut cache = ops::renewed_market(env, &hub_asset);

    let applied = amount.min(guards::backing_shortfall(&cache));
    let refund = amount
        .checked_sub(applied)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));

    cache.credit_cash(applied);
    cache.commit();
```

```rust
/// The refund gap is not specific to `recapitalize`. Two pool legs refund an
/// excess derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book:
/// `ops::recapitalize::apply` (the excess over the shortfall) and
/// `ops::repay::apply` (the excess over the debt). Neither refund debits `cash`
/// or passes `require_reserves`.
///
/// A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an
/// overpayment: `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the
/// full-close branch and `net_repay` is zero. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`
/// assert in `ops::repay::accounting` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct,
/// and the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched.
#[test]
fn test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    let token = token::Client::new(&t.env, &t.asset);
    let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);

    let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);
    let before = t.state_snapshot();
    assert_eq!(
        before.cash, custody_before,
        "fixture guard: book and custody must start in sync"
    );
    assert_eq!(before.borrowed, 0, "fixture must carry no debt");

    // Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
    let credited = t
        .client()
        .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
        .get_unchecked(0)
        .actual_amount;
    assert_eq!(credited, 0, "no debt was retired, so nothing is credited");
    assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
```
