### Title
Bad-debt socialization can clamp `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, making every subsequent deposit panic and bricking the market - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` is invoked from the permissionless liquidation/bad-debt seize path (`ops::seize::apply`, `AccountPositionType::Borrow`). When socialized bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, the function does not zero out supply shares; it clamps the new supply index *up* to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. At that index, `calculate_scaled_supply` produces scaled share amounts that overflow `i128` for ordinary deposits (the test `supply_index_floor_makes_a_ceiling_deposit_overflow_its_scaled_form` shows the overflow panic at the cap), so every later `supply`/`multiply`-style deposit reverts, and pre-existing suppliers retain "stranded" claims that can drain fresh cash once any deposit succeeds, as demonstrated by the raw-cache tests `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` — the only thing masking this in production is `require_reserves` against current cash, which does not protect against the phantom-claim drain of *future* deposits.

### Finding Description
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps `bad_debt` at total supplied value, computes `remaining / total` as a floor-rounded reduction factor, and sets `supply_index = max(new_index, SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. On a full wipeout `remaining == 0`, so the index collapses to the floor instead of zeroing shares.
- `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-27`: the borrow-side seize — reachable from permissionless `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` controller entrypoints — calls this with `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` and burns the debt.
- `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:30-37` + `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`: `calculate_scaled_supply` divides by the (now-floored) supply index via `mul_div_half_up`; with index ≈ `RAY/1000`, scaling a normal deposit produces a scaled share value that does not fit `i128` (or a pathological share price), panicking with `MathOverflow`. The test at `common/tests/rates/scaling.rs:258-272` confirms the overflow panic occurs at the floor index.
- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:316-370` and `373-428`: the committed tests show that after the floor clamp, `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` leaves a phantom claim, which then drains exactly a fresh depositor's cash via `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, …)` — i.e., the residual state is not just a DoS but a theft-of-funds primitive once the market accepts cash again (e.g., via `recapitalize`, which credits cash without minting shares, or any path that credits cash).

### Impact Explanation
- **Permanent freezing of funds / contract unable to operate**: after a wipeout-level `clean_bad_debt`, the hub's pool market is bricked — deposits panic on `MathOverflow`, so no new supply can ever enter and the market cannot recover organically.
- **Theft of user funds**: wiped-out suppliers keep positive floored claims (`stranded > 0` in the tests). Any later cash entering the pool (a fresh deposit that happens to scale, a `recapitalize`, or donations) is immediately withdrawable by the stranded claimant, paid out of other users' backing — the test asserts `gross == deposit` and `cash < fresh_claim`.
- Both effects are triggered by a single unprivileged address calling `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` on an underwater position, matching the assertion-failure-DoS bug class of CVE-2016-9393.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Triggering requires an actual bad-debt position large enough to consume the full supplied value of a spoke market — achievable in thin/young markets, or by a borrower whose collateral collapses (the protocol's own design contemplates this via `clean_bad_debt` and the `force-socialize-bad-debt` runbook). Once the condition exists, any unprivileged caller can invoke the seize; no privileged key, leaked parameter, or oracle dishonesty is required. The arithmetic panic itself is deterministic once the floor clamp is reached.

### Recommendation
When `remaining == 0` (or the write-down exceeds a dust threshold), burn/zero the outstanding supply shares rather than clamping `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` — e.g., set `supplied = 0` alongside the index reset so no residual claim survives. Alternatively, reject the clamp: keep the true (possibly sub-floor) index only if `calculate_scaled_supply` is made total (saturating/`try_*` variant returning a protocol error like `MarketWiped` instead of `MathOverflow`), and gate withdrawals on stranded claims via `require_backed_market` rather than only `require_reserves`, so fresh cash cannot be drained by wiped positions.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies a small amount to hub market `(H, T)` and borrows against collateral whose value subsequently falls below the debt (or uses a thin market where bad debt naturally forms).
2. Attacker (or any keeper) calls the permissionless bad-debt path — `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` on the underwater account — which reaches `pool::ops::seize::apply` with `side = Borrow` and `scaled_amount` equal to the full debt.
3. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `capped == total_supplied_value`, `remaining == 0`, `reduction_factor == 0`, and commits `supply_index = SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while `supplied` shares are untouched (`interest.rs:80-88`).
4. Any subsequent `supply(hub, token, amount)` calls `Cache::calculate_scaled_supply` → `calculate_scaled_supply(env, amount, decimals, floor_index)` → `mul_div_half_up` producing a scaled share that overflows `i128` → `panic_with_error!(MathOverflow)` (`fp_core.rs:117`, mirroring `scaling.rs:258-272`). All deposits permanently revert.
5. Meanwhile `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` (`interest.rs` tests at lines 339-341, 395-401): the wiped supplier calls `withdraw(i128::MAX)`; `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` passes once any fresh cash exists, and the phantom claim pays out real tokens belonging to other suppliers (`pool/tests/interest.rs:351-368`).
