## Analog Found: System Fee Parameter Votes Take Effect Immediately With No Timelock, Allowing Front-Running of Pending Fee-Paying Units

### Title
Instant Application of `system_vote_count`-Driven Fee Parameters (`base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`, `threshold_size`) Enables Front-Running of Users' Pending Units - (File: `main_chain.js`)

### Summary
ocore lets any unit author cast a `system_vote` for network-wide fee parameters and any unit author trigger a `system_vote_count` to tally and apply the new value. The report's bug class (privileged setters that change fee-critical parameters with no timelock, letting the setter front-run users into paying more) maps onto `countVotes()` in `main_chain.js`, which writes the new `system_vars` value to take effect starting from the very next main-chain index, with no delay window for users who already composed/broadcast a unit under the old parameters.

### Finding Description
Any address can submit a `system_vote` message (subject `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`, or `threshold_size`), weighted only by its GBYTE balance, as validated in [1](#0-0) . Any address can also trigger the tally via a `system_vote_count` message, processed in `writer.js` and counted in `countVotes()` [2](#0-1) .

Once counted, the new value is inserted into `system_vars` and becomes authoritative starting immediately from `vote_count_mci + 1`: [3](#0-2) 

`getSystemVar()` simply returns the newest entry whose `vote_count_mci` is below the queried mci, with no minimum activation delay enforced: [4](#0-3) 

This value directly drives the mandatory `tps_fee` and `oversize_fee` that every ordinary unit (posted by any unprivileged user) must pay, computed in `getCurrentTpsFee`/`getCurrentTpsFeeToPay` and enforced during validation: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

A holder of sufficient balance (there is no minimum quorum beyond `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`, and votes only need to exist, not be broadcast far in advance) can pre-stage `system_vote` messages, wait for favorable timing, then post a `system_vote_count` unit. As soon as that unit's MCI stabilizes, `base_tps_fee`/`tps_interval`/`tps_fee_multiplier`/`threshold_size` change for the *next* MCI — with zero advance notice to the rest of the network. Ordinary users who already composed and broadcast a payment/AA-trigger unit priced under the old fee schedule (a completely normal, honest action) can suddenly find their `tps_fee`/`oversize_fee` insufficient once validated after the new MCI, per the check at `validation.js:1088-1094`, and receive a transient rejection, forcing them to recompose and pay more — precisely the "admins can front-run users to make them pay more fees than intended" impact described in the source report, except here it is any large-balance voter acting as the de-facto "fee setter."

### Impact Explanation
- Pending, honestly-composed units can be invalidated/soft-rejected purely due to an instantaneous parameter flip with no timelock, forcing resubmission at a higher fee — an unprivileged holder of sufficient balance can engineer this to force other users to overpay or to get their own competing transactions confirmed first while others stall.
- Because the change also affects `threshold_size` (which drives `oversize_fee`) and the TPS-based fee curve, an actor can time a spike right when they know congestion-sensitive transactions from others are in flight, degrading the network's ability to confirm those units until they are recomposed at the new, higher price — a transient denial of service on honest, already-broadcast transactions.
- This is a protocol-level (not a compromised admin key) instance of the same root cause flagged in the source report: a critical, fee-determining parameter changes without any grace period/timelock that would let in-flight transactions or unaware users react.

### Likelihood Explanation
Casting `system_vote` and `system_vote_count` messages requires no special privilege — only owning/controlling GBYTE balance and paying the normal per-unit fees, so any sufficiently funded party (or a coalition reaching `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`) can trigger this at will and choose the timing adversarially. The mechanism is a designed governance feature, but the complete absence of an activation delay after `vote_count_mci` (activation is literally the next MCI) is what creates the front-running window; this makes exploitation straightforward for anyone with adequate stake, not merely theoretical.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum activation delay (a "vote_count_mci + N" grace period, or a fixed time delay) before a newly counted `system_vote_count` value for fee-related subjects (`base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`, `threshold_size`) becomes authoritative in `getSystemVar`/`countVotes`, and emit the pending change (already partially done via `eventBus.emit('system_vars_updated', ...)`) far enough in advance that composing wallets and light clients can react before the change takes effect, similar to how `op_list` emergency changes are gated by `EMERGENCY_OP_LIST_CHANGE_TIMEOUT`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (or coalition) with balance ≥ `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE * TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` posts `system_vote` messages for `base_tps_fee` well in advance (validated per `validation.js:1893-1907`), unnoticed among normal traffic.
2. Victim composes and broadcasts a normal unit; its `tps_fee`/`oversize_fee` is computed against the currently active `system_vars` values via `composer.js` (`estimateTpsFee`/`parentComposer.getTpsFee`).
3. Attacker posts a `system_vote_count` unit for `base_tps_fee`. Once its MCI stabilizes, `countVotes()` immediately writes the new (higher) value effective at `vote_count_mci + 1` (`main_chain.js:1900-1912`).
4. If the victim's unit stabilizes/validates on or after that MCI, `validateTpsFee` in `validation.js:1050-1097` computes `min_tps_fee`/`min_acceptable_tps_fee` under the new, higher parameters and rejects the victim's previously-valid unit as underpriced, forcing them to recompose and pay more — with no warning window having been available when they originally composed.

function getSystemVar(subject, mci) {
	for (let { vote_count_mci, value } of systemVars[subject])
		if (mci > vote_count_mci)
