### Title
Accrual `i128` overflow panic below the borrow-index cap permanently bricks a whale-scale market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
The reported bug class is an overzealous abort: `osdmap_apply_incremental()` called `BUG_ON` on an epoch mismatch instead of declaring the input invalid, turning a rejectable condition into a full system crash. The XOXNO Lending analog lives in the shared accrual step `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-81`), which the pool's mutating `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`) runs at the top of every market operation. When a market's scaled debt times its borrow index exceeds `i128::MAX`, `scaled_to_original` / `calculate_supplier_rewards` panic with `MathOverflow` instead of degrading gracefully — and because `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) multiplies *before* clamping to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, the panic fires while the index is still below the intended safety cap. Since every verb (supply, withdraw, repay, borrow, liquidate, seize) accrues first, the market is permanently frozen: the abort is re-triggered on every subsequent call, exactly like a BUG_ON that can never be cleared.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` unconditionally calls `accrue_step` for any elapsed time. Inside `accrue_step`, `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60`) and `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`) compute `scaled * index` with a widened intermediate whose *result* must still fit `i128`. For an 18-decimal market supplied at the validated cap domain (`max_cap_for_decimals`, reachable via `lift_caps` in the harness at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:81-95`), sustained high utilization compounds the borrow index to the point where the debt *value* overflows `i128::MAX` (≈1.7e38 raw ≈ 170×RAY-scale) while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The cap never engages because the panic happens on the value multiplication, not the index multiplication. Unlike the fixed kernel code, nothing marks the accrued state "invalid" and skips it — the same panic recurs on every entry point, so the freeze is permanent rather than a single-transaction revert.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all funds in the affected (hub, token) market: once `scaled_debt × borrow_index` crosses the `i128` boundary, `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize` all revert inside `global_sync`, so supplier principal and revenue in that book are unrecoverable and borrower collateral backing that debt can never be released through that market. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) demonstrates `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW` once the cliff is crossed.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged address can drive this: supply at the market's configured supply cap, borrow to ~98% utilization with collateral in another market, then let `update_indexes` (permissionless) accrue on the steep end of the rate curve. Two conditions constrain it: (1) governance must have listed a high-decimals market with caps near the validated maximum (the caps themselves pass `require_cap_within_asset_domain`, so this is a legitimate configuration, not a bad-parameter case), and (2) the debt value must compound to the cliff, which takes sustained high utilization over an extended period — nobody can repay or liquidate once near it, but reaching it requires whale-scale capital and time. That keeps this out of Critical; it is a genuine permanent freeze reachable under realistic-once-configured parameters.

### Recommendation
Mirror the upstream fix's intent — replace the abort with a degrading check. Concretely: in `accrue_step`/`calculate_supplier_rewards`, detect when `borrowed × borrow_index` would exceed `i128::MAX` and pin the borrow index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (or a derived "no further accrual" sentinel) instead of panicking, so accrual becomes a sticky no-op at the ceiling rather than a trap. Alternatively, order the index clamp before the value multiplication and gate `global_sync` on `borrow_index >= MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, letting exits proceed with capped debt math. A saturating `scaled_to_original` for the accrual-internal read (distinct from position-valuation paths) would also work, provided downstream interest splits treat the saturated value as the ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
The in-repo harness test already proves the path end-to-end: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` supplies `BILLION * 10^18` units of an 18-decimal asset, borrows at 98% utilization against collateral, advances time year-by-year calling `update_indexes`, and observes `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. It then confirms the freeze is total: `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert with the same `MATH_OVERFLOW` because every verb accrues first. Conceptual attacker steps: (1) `supply(BIG18, cap-scale amount)`; (2) `supply(COL, collateral)` + `borrow(BIG18, ~98%)`; (3) wait / periodically call `update_indexes`; (4) the first accrual that pushes `borrowed × borrow_index` past `i128::MAX` reverts, and every subsequent operation on that market reverts identically — permanently locking all suppliers' principal and revenue in the book.
