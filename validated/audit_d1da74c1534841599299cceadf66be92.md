### Title
First-depositor share-price manipulation causes rounding-to-zero fund loss in the AMM autonomous agent template - (File: `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`)

### Summary
The bundled Uniswap-like market-maker Autonomous Agent (AA) reference implementation issues its share token (`mm_asset`) to the first investor as `$issue_amount = balance[base]` with no minimum-liquidity floor, and for all subsequent investors computes `$issue_amount = round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * var['mm_asset_outstanding'])`. Because swaps change pool reserves without touching `mm_asset_outstanding`, and divestment can shrink `mm_asset_outstanding` down to a tiny integer, any AA trigger sender can replicate the classic ElasticSwap "first-minter" attack: mint shares as sole depositor, redeem almost all of them, inflate the raw reserves relative to the tiny remaining share supply, and thereby cause honest depositors' proportional share calculation to round down to zero, donating their funds to the attacker's remaining position.

### Finding Description
In `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, the "invest in MM" case computes the number of `mm_asset` shares to mint: [1](#0-0) 

For the very first deposit, `$issue_amount` is simply set to the AA's entire current `balance[base]`, with no minimum lock: [2](#0-1) 

For subsequent deposits, the newly minted shares are proportional to `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` via integer `round()`: [3](#0-2) 

Critically, the "exchange bytes to asset" and "exchange asset to bytes" swap cases mutate the pool's `balance[base]`/`balance[$asset]` reserves using a constant-product formula but never touch `var['mm_asset_outstanding']`: [4](#0-3) 

And the "divest MM shares" case lets any shareholder redeem proportionally and reduce `mm_asset_outstanding` arbitrarily low (down to a residual integer such as 1 or 2), while the reserves (`balance[base]`, `balance[$asset]`) remain whatever is left over: [5](#0-4) 

This is the same root cause as the ElasticSwap finding: the share/LP-token price (`reserves / totalSupply`) can be driven arbitrarily high by a single actor who controls both the numerator (reserves, via legitimate swap triggers) and the denominator (`mm_asset_outstanding`, via divestment), and the naive `round()` used to compute a new depositor's shares then truncates to zero for any deposit whose proportional share is smaller than `1/mm_asset_outstanding` of the pool.

All of the steps — invest, divest, and swap triggers — are ordinary unit outputs sent by any unprivileged address to a publicly reachable AA; no privileged role, malicious node, or off-chain component is required, satisfying the "AA trigger sender" reachability requirement.

### Impact Explanation
A subsequent, honest liquidity provider who sends bytes and the paired asset to invest in the pool can receive `0` `mm_asset` shares (due to the `round()` in the issuance formula) while their payment is retained as part of the pool's reserves. That value effectively becomes redeemable by the attacker's small residual share balance, i.e., a direct loss of AA-held funds for legitimate investors and an unjust profit for the attacker — the same "AA fund loss" impact class recognized as Medium severity in the original ElasticSwap report.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires no privileged access: an attacker just needs to be the first (or effectively first, after driving reserves/outstanding-shares to a favorable ratio) investor in a deployed instance of this pattern and to issue a sequence of ordinary triggers (invest → divest down to a small residual → swap to inflate reserves). Because this is a documented, bundled reference AA template, developers copying or basing production AMM-style AAs on it inherit the flaw; the ratio math itself (not a one-off typo) is inherently exploitable whenever the total minted-share supply can be driven low relative to reserves.

### Recommendation
- Require a minimum locked amount of `mm_asset` on the first deposit (e.g., mint a fixed minimum number of shares to a burn address/definer, unspendable, similar to Uniswap V2's `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY`) so `mm_asset_outstanding` can never be driven back down to a value where later depositors round to zero shares.
- Reject invests where the computed `$issue_amount` rounds to `0` (bounce with an error) instead of silently minting nothing while still accepting the deposit.
- Consider using higher internal precision (scaling factor) for `mm_asset_outstanding` accounting to reduce rounding error, and document this risk prominently for any developer basing a production AA on this sample.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker sends `1e5+1` bytes with `trigger.output[[asset=$asset]] = 0` and no prior `mm_asset` outstanding → hits the "define" then "invest" case's initial-deposit branch: `$issue_amount = balance[base]` (≈100001). Attacker now owns 100% of `mm_asset_outstanding = 100001`. [2](#0-1) 
2. Attacker divests `99999` of their `100001` `mm_asset`, leaving `var['mm_asset_outstanding'] = 2`, and reserves reduced proportionally but nonzero. [5](#0-4) 
3. Attacker performs one or more large "exchange bytes to asset" / "exchange asset to bytes" swaps to shift/inflate the raw `balance[base]` reserve while `mm_asset_outstanding` stays at `2`. [6](#0-5) 
4. A new, honest investor sends a normal-sized deposit; `$investor_share_of_prev_balance = trigger.output[[asset=base]] / $bytes_balance` is small (since `$bytes_balance` is now huge relative to their deposit and to `mm_asset_outstanding=2`), so `$issue_amount = round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * 2)` rounds down to `0`. [3](#0-2) 
5. The honest investor's bytes+asset remain in the pool's reserves and are now redeemable, in proportion to `mm_asset_outstanding`, by the attacker's `2` remaining shares — a direct transfer of value from the new investor to the attacker.
