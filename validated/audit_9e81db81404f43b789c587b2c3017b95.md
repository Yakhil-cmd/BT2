### Title
AA exchange formulas lacking minimum-output protection allow sandwich/front-run losses for trigger senders - (File: `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`)

### Summary
The external report describes a DSC `liquidate()` function that computes a collateral payout from a live oracle price with no caller-supplied minimum-output guard, letting a front-runner move the price between submission and execution so the caller receives less than expected. The same bug class is directly reachable in ocore's Autonomous Agent (AA) framework: AA "cases" that compute a payout as a function of the AA's *current* balances/price ratio (rather than a value fixed by the trigger sender) can be front-run by any unprivileged unit poster who submits a balance-shifting trigger that gets included and stabilized before the victim's trigger, causing the victim to receive fewer output tokens than they expected when they posted their unit.

### Finding Description
The bundled sample AA `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` implements a constant-product market maker whose swap payout is computed entirely from the AA's balance at the moment its trigger is processed: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

There is no field in the trigger data (e.g. a `min_amount`) that the AA checks against `$amount` before sending the payment, so the sender has no way to bound the price they will actually receive — exactly the missing check the external report calls out for `DSCEngine.liquidate()`.

Critically, this is exploitable because AA trigger execution order is *not* controlled by the trigger sender but by a deterministic ordering rule over `(mci, level, unit, address)` that any unprivileged unit poster can influence by choosing their own unit's parents/timing: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Because triggers destined for the same AA are queued and executed strictly in `level, unit` order within an MCI (and across MCIs in MCI order), an attacker can post their own swap-trigger unit so that it is ordered (and hence executed) immediately before the victim's already-broadcast trigger, shifting `balance[$asset]`/`balance[base]` and thus `$p`, `$new_asset_balance`/`$new_bytes_balance`, and `$amount` before the victim's swap is evaluated — a classic sandwich/front-run, mirroring the MEV scenario in the report.

### Impact Explanation
A victim who posts a swap trigger expecting a certain amount of `asset`/`bytes` back can receive materially less than expected once an attacker's manipulating trigger is processed first, extracting value from the victim in favor of the attacker (and possibly reverting the price afterward). This is a concrete AA fund loss for the trigger sender, matching the "AA fund loss" impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged address can trigger this AA with a payment; front-running only requires posting a second unit ahead of the victim's unit in the deterministic `(mci, level, unit)` ordering, which is achievable by any DAG participant (e.g. by choosing better parents/being faster), with no special privilege, oracle access, or node/network trust required. This mirrors a widely known and easily automatable attack pattern (as in the reported DeFi bug).

### Recommendation
- AA authors (and ocore's shipped sample/reference AAs) should support caller-supplied slippage bounds, e.g. requiring `trigger.data.min_amount` and bouncing the trigger if the AA-computed payout is below it, analogous to the `minimumOutputTokens` recommendation in the report.
- Update `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` (and any documentation referencing it as a template) to include and enforce a minimum-output/minimum-ratio check before sending the swap payment, e.g.:
```
if ($amount < trigger.data.min_amount)
    bounce('price moved, received less than min_amount');
```
- Document in the oscript/AA authoring guidance that balance/price-ratio-based payouts are subject to ordering-dependent front-running and that AAs must expose slippage protection to callers.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes an unconfirmed victim trigger unit sent to the market-maker AA at `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` performing "exchange bytes to asset" (`trigger.output[[asset=base]] > 1e5`).
2. Attacker posts their own large "exchange asset to bytes" (or "exchange bytes to asset") trigger unit, choosing parents so that, once both units are included and the MCI stabilizes, their trigger is ordered before the victim's in `aa_composer.js`'s `ORDER BY aa_triggers.mci, level, aa_triggers.unit, address` queue (`aa_composer.js:59-69`, `main_chain.js:1691-1706`).
3. When the MCI stabilizes and `handleAATriggers` executes both triggers in that fixed order, the attacker's trigger executes first, moving `balance[$asset]`/`balance[base]` and therefore `$p`/`$new_asset_balance`/`$amount` for the victim's subsequent execution (`test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript:102-123`).
4. The victim's trigger executes against the now-shifted balances and receives less `asset`/`bytes` than it would have at the price visible when they posted their unit, with no on-chain mechanism to reject the unfavorable fill since no minimum-output parameter exists.
