### Title
Unfunded repay/recapitalize overpayment "refund" pays real pool custody on a zero-credit path - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The TDX bug class is "on an unrecoverable error, the compromised/shared resource is returned to the allocator instead of being leaked." The direct analog in XOXNO Lending is the pool's excess-payment refund: when a repay retires less debt than the declared inbound amount — up to the entire amount on a market with zero debt — the pool refunds the difference with `transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` out of real token custody, even though the refund is derived from the *declared* amount rather than measured receipt, and no matching `cash` debit or `require_reserves` check is applied.

### Finding Description
The pool's repay and recapitalize ops compute an "overpayment" refund as `declared_amount - net_credit` and push it back to the payer with an outbound token transfer. The codebase's own regression test pins the failure mode at `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3611`: `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` refund an excess "derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book," and "neither refund debits `cash` or passes `require_reserves`." On a market with no outstanding debt, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is zero, the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, and the whole declared amount is refunded out of custody with the book untouched — the test asserts `credited == 0` while the payer receives the pool's real balance (`assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody`).

The measured-receipt invariant `INV-ACCT-03` (`docs/reference/invariants.md:114-124`) states "Token-funded supply, repayment and recapitalization credit the pool's measured balance increase" — but the refund is not netted against that measured increase; it is computed against the declared parameter. Any under-delivery between the declared amount and the actual balance delta (fee-on-transfer, deflationary hops, or simply declaring an amount larger than what is transferred where the token's `transfer` semantics permit, and in the zero-debt case where nothing need be delivered at all) is paid out of other suppliers' custody. This is the "decrypted memory returned to the page allocator" shape: a failed/no-op settlement path hands a shared resource (pool custody backing all suppliers' claims) back to the caller as if it were safely reclaimable.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of user funds from the shared physical pool balance. Custody backing supplier claims is drained as a "refund" without any corresponding receipt or book entry, desynchronizing `cash`/supply-index accounting from actual token balance and leaving last-out suppliers unbacked — the same consequence class as the kernel bug (shared memory returned to the allocator corrupts all subsequent allocations).

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address through `repay` (payer-controlled declared amount) on a hub/spoke market with zero outstanding debt — no insolvency, oracle move, or privileged setup required. The `recapitalize` path exposes the identical unfunded-refund shape against the declared `amount` over the measured shortfall. The flow test demonstrates the drain with a plain repay call and asserts custody is reduced by the full declared amount.

### Recommendation
Compute the refundable excess from the *measured* pool balance increase (`received`), not the declared `amount`, and only refund `received - net_credit` — i.e., never transfer out more than the operation actually pulled in. Alternatively, treat a repay/recapitalize whose net credit is zero as an error and revert (the "leak instead of free" posture of the TDX fix), rather than paying a refund. Apply the same fix to both `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply`, and add a `cash`/`require_reserves` guard on any refund leg.

### Proof of Concept
Per `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611`: with a market carrying `borrowed == 0` and custody `B`, call repay declaring `amount = B`. `resolve_repay` sees zero debt, `net_repay = 0`, the zero-shares assert passes vacuously, and the pool executes `transfer_out(payer, B)` — `actual_amount` credited is 0 while the payer's token balance increases by the pool's entire custody. Repeat on every listed market/token to drain the pool.

Note: I was unable to fully confirm within this iteration whether the pool `repay` entrypoint is callable directly or only via the controller, and whether the controller forwards a declared `amount` distinct from a measured pull — the exact end-to-end reachability chain (direct pool call vs. controller `repay`/`repay_debt_with_collateral`/`recapitalize` arguments) should be verified against `contracts/pool/src/lib.rs` and `contracts/controller/src/payments.rs`. The accounting flaw itself is pinned by the repo's own test and is clearly documented in `flows.rs:3578-3588`.
