### Title
Permanent market freeze: accrual overflows i128 in `scaled_to_original` before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Interest accrual multiplies scaled debt (`borrowed`, a RAY-scaled value) by the growing `borrow_index`. On a very large book the product overflows `i128` and `accrue_step` panics with `MathOverflow` — while `borrow_index` itself is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so the index cap never clamps it. Because `global_sync` runs at the head of every market mutation, once the index crosses the overflow point every verb on that market (supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, seize, flash, recapitalize, net_settle) reverts forever. This mirrors the CVE class: an assertion/arithmetic failure reachable only in a specific configuration, permanently denying service.

### Finding Description
`interest::global_sync` is invoked on `Cache::load` for every state-changing pool entrypoint, so accrual is a mandatory prefix of all operations (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`; flow documented in `contracts/pool/src/README.md:159-168`). Each chunk calls `common::rates::accrue_step`, which computes utilization and new totals via `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26`). Both operands are RAY-scale; their product must fit in `i128`. A billion whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset is ~1e36 raw-ray scaled shares — the value ceiling `i128::MAX` is only ~170x larger, so once `borrow_index` grows past ~170x, the unscaling overflows and panics. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap is meant to bound index growth, but it is a bound on the index, not on `scaled * index`; a large enough book hits the value ceiling while the index is still well below its cap.

The repository's own test proves the freeze end-to-end: after the overflow, `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MathOverflow`, and liquidation reverts identically since it also accrues first (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`).

The triggering setup is reachable by a single unprivileged address: `supply` and `borrow` require no role, and the permissionless `update_indexes` entrypoint can later be called by anyone to land the fatal accrual.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the cliff is crossed, suppliers can never withdraw and borrowers can never repay or be liquidated in that market — all tokens held by the pool for that `(hub, asset)` book are frozen indefinitely, and bad debt in the market can never be cleaned or recapitalized. This is a protocol-level liveness failure identical in shape to the CVE's assertion-failure DoS, but permanent rather than a restartable crash.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-moderate. The attack path needs (a) a high-decimals listed asset, (b) a book on the order of billions of whole tokens, (c) sustained near-max utilization on a steep rate curve, and (d) years of compounding before the index crosses the value ceiling. A whale attacker can accelerate this by supplying and borrowing the dominant share themselves at ~98% utilization, but the capital requirement is extreme and the payoff is griefing rather than profit. Realistically this materializes as an organic freeze on a wildly successful market rather than a targeted attack. Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Bound the accrual math on the value side, not just the index side:
- Clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` at a cap derived from `i128::MAX / supplied` (per-market, recomputed on supply changes), or
- Saturate `scaled_to_original` during accrual instead of panicking, or
- Cap `supplied`/`borrowed` scaled totals at listing time via `max_cap`/`max_borrow_cap` sized so `scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` can never overflow `i128` for the asset's decimals.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`):

```rust
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);          // whale supply, unprivileged
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", principal / 100 * 98); // ~98% utilization

// advance years until the accrual overflows
loop { t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS); if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) { break e; } }
// -> MathOverflow; borrow_index still < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY

// Market is permanently bricked:
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

Every subsequent mutation of the market — including liquidation and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize`, which also run `global_sync` first — reverts with `MathOverflow`. Uncertainty note: `accrue_step` lives in `common/src/rates.rs`, which I did not read directly; the panic site inside `scaled_to_original` is asserted by the test's own comments and its `MathOverflow` assertion, which I consider sufficient proof.
