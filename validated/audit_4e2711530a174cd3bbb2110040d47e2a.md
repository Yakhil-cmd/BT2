### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor resurrects written-off supply claims, letting wiped-out suppliers drain fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes unpayable debt by scaling `supply_index` down, but clamps the result to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zero. When a write-off meets or exceeds the market's total supplied value, every remaining supply share keeps a positive residual claim (`scaled × RAY/1000`). The withdraw path only enforces `require_reserves` (cash on hand), so once any new depositor adds cash to the wiped market, a holder of these dead shares can withdraw real tokens against a claim that should be worthless — theft of the new supplier's funds.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped_loss`, derives `reduction_factor = remaining / total_supplied_value`, then sets `supply_index = supply_index * factor`, finally clamping with `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`:

```rust
// contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:80-88
let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

On a total wipeout (`bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`), `reduction_factor` is 0, but the floor pushes the index back to `RAY/1000`. No supply shares are burned — `supplied` still holds every pre-wipeout share, so `unscale_supply_floor` returns a positive "stranded" claim for each holder.

The pool withdraw flow (`Cache::load → global_sync → resolve_withdrawal → require_reserves → burn_supply → debit_cash → transfer_out`, per `contracts/pool/src/ops` and `contracts/pool/README.md`) contains no guard distinguishing backed claims from floor-residual claims. `resolve_withdrawal` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:97`) unscales against the floored index and `require_reserves` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs:15`) only checks `cash >= amount`. The protocol's own unit tests prove the exploit mechanics at the cache layer: `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` and `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369` and `:431-494`) show a stranded holder extracting exactly a fresh depositor's full deposit, leaving the honest claim unbacked. `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-IDX-02 even concedes the bound "can preserve an unbacked residual claim."

This mirrors CVE-2017-10788's use-after-free class: shares whose backing was destroyed by the write-off remain live objects that can still be spent — a stale-reference payout.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a wipeout, each unit of fresh cash deposited into the dead market is withdrawable by stranded-claim holders up to their residual (`scaled_shares × RAY/1000 / 10^(27-decimals)`). Since `supplied` was never reduced, the sum of residuals can be large (it is `total_supplied × floor`, i.e., up to ~0.1% of pre-wipeout supply × index growth). The residual holder — who can be the same unprivileged attacker that caused the bad debt and retained a small supply position — withdraws first and steals the recapitalizing/fresh-supplier funds. `recapitalize` (`contracts/pool`, owner-relayed but funded by any payer) also credits raw `cash` without minting shares, so recapitalized funds are equally drainable by dead claims before honest suppliers recover anything.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market whose bad debt meets or exceeds total supplied value at write-off time. That is reachable by a single unprivileged actor: supply a small amount into a thin/low-liquidity market, borrow against overvalued collateral in another market or after a sharp oracle price drop, let the position reach `clean_bad_debt` eligibility (collateral ≤ $5 dust, debt > collateral — permissionless per INV-LIQ-04) or trigger it via `liquidate`'s `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation`. Because the write-down values debt at `unscale_borrow_ceil` and supplied value at a floor, near-total wipeouts on small markets clamp to the floor whenever `loss ≥ supplied_value × (1 - RAY/1000/index)`. All entrypoints used — `supply`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `withdraw` — are unprivileged. The attacker can keep a deliberately tiny supply position so their own residual survives, then wait for (or bait via `recapitalize`) fresh cash. One caveat: the two confirming tests exercise the raw `Cache` directly rather than the full controller `withdraw` entrypoint, so exact end-to-end dollar magnitudes depend on production position-limit and decimals handling; nothing in the inspected withdraw path blocks the flow, but I did not trace every controller-side spoke-usage release step.

### Recommendation
When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` hits the floor, zero out or burn the corresponding stranded supply shares (or record a per-market "wiped" flag that forces `resolve_withdrawal`/`calculate_scaled_supply` to treat pre-wipeout shares as zero). Alternatively, snapshot the residual claim value at write-down time into a capped recovery pool paid only from future revenue — never from fresh cash. At minimum, gate withdrawals on `supplied_value_floor ≤ cash + debt` after the floor clamp, or exclude floor-residual value from `resolve_withdrawal` so fresh deposits and recapitalized funds cannot be claimed by written-off positions.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies `S` units to thin market M, plus collateral in market C; borrows nearly all of M.
2. Collateral price crashes (oracle move); attacker calls `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`. Debt written off ≥ supplied value → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:88`). Attacker's `scaled` supply shares are not burned → stranded claim `unscale_supply_floor(scaled) > 0`.
3. Victim (or `recapitalize` payer) deposits `C` units → `mint_supply` + `credit_cash(C)`; pool cash becomes `C`.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw(amount = i128::MAX)`: `resolve_withdrawal` pays `min(claim, stranded) = C` — passes `require_reserves` since `cash == C` — `burn_supply`, `debit_cash(C)`, `transfer_out(attacker, C)`.
5. Pool books `supplied_value > cash`: victim's claim is unbacked. Attacker stole exactly `C`, matching `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:351-368` ("userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool").
