### Title
Sustained high utilization on a large-decimal market overflows `i128` in the accrual path before the index cap engages, permanently freezing the market - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The RAY borrow index can grow large enough that `scaled * index` overflows `i128` inside `scaled_to_original` during accrual, while the index itself is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Since every pool verb accrues first, the whole market then permanently reverts: no withdraw, repay, or liquidation. This is the in-scope analog of CVE-2024-32672's "crafted input causes a crash / denial of service" class, mapped onto Soroban checked arithmetic.

### Finding Description
Accrual computes total debt as `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — i.e. `scaled * index` in RAY — via `Cache::calculate_utilization` in `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs` and `calculate_supplier_rewards` in `common/src/rates/index.rs`. `Ray::mul` widens to `I256` for the product but the final result must still fit `i128`, so it panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` on overflow. `update_borrow_index` caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/rates/index.rs`), but that cap only bounds the *index*; the *value* `borrowed_scaled * index` can exceed `i128::MAX` long before the cap is reached when `borrowed_scaled` is large (high-decimal tokens like an 18-decimal market with ~1e9 units supplied).

The repository's own harness test proves the end state: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-362` supplies ~1e9 units of an 18-decimal asset on the XLM interest curve, borrows ~98% utilization, and advances time. After enough years, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ("the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"), and subsequently `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with the same error — "no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages."

An unprivileged attacker can set up the preconditions without any privileged call: open a supply position and a borrow position at high utilization in a large-decimal market (subject only to the market's caps, which can be pushed to their configured maxima), then let time do the work. Once the value ceiling is crossed, `update_indexes` — callable by anyone — and every accrue-first verb (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`) permanently reverts for that market.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for the affected market. All suppliers' deposits and all collateral value tied to that market become unreachable: withdrawals revert, repayments revert (so borrowers cannot free collateral through this market's accounting), and liquidations revert because they accrue first. There is no admin escape hatch that skips accrual — interest accrual is the first step of every verb — and no index-shrink path exists. This meets the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate rather than trivial: it requires a high-decimal market, very large supplied value (billions of units), ~98% sustained utilization on a steep rate curve, and multi-year accrual. However, no privileged action is required — ordinary `supply`/`borrow` at allowed caps plus permissionless `update_indexes` suffice — and the panic is deterministic once utilization and time accumulate. Notably, the millisecond-chunked accrual means partial accrual only delays the cliff; it cannot avoid it, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` do not help because they cannot reduce `borrowed_scaled` below the overflow threshold without first accrue-unscaling it.

### Recommendation
Bound the *value* computation, not just the index: make `scaled_to_original`/accrual saturating (e.g. use `mul_div_floor_saturating` or clamp `new_total_debt` to `i128::MAX` in `calculate_supplier_rewards`), or lower `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` / cap `borrowed_scaled` so that `borrowed_scaled * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` can never exceed `i128::MAX` for the maximum supported decimals. Alternatively, enforce per-market supply caps sized so `supplied * index` stays representable at the steepest rate curve.

### Proof of Concept
See the existing harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` at `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-362`: it supplies ~1e9 units of an 18-decimal token, borrows ~98%, advances years until `update_indexes` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and then confirms `withdraw` and `repay` revert identically — a permanent market freeze produced entirely by unprivileged calls.

Caveat: I verified the accrual math in `common/src/rates/index.rs` and `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs` and the harness test that demonstrates the freeze; I did not fully trace whether every listed verb invokes accrual unconditionally in all branches, though the test's own assertions cover `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW`.
