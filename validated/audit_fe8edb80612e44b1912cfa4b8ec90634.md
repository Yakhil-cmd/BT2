### Title
Suppliers can front-run bad-debt socialization by withdrawing before `clean_bad_debt` and re-supplying after, dodging their share of the supply-index write-down - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs)

### Summary
Bad-debt socialization lowers a market's `supply_index` pro-rata over whoever holds supply shares at the instant `seize_positions` executes (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`). Because `clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless, non-atomic-with-respect-to-exit entrypoint and its eligibility is publicly computable, a large supplier can withdraw at the pre-write-down index, trigger the cleanup themselves, and re-supply at the depressed index — extracting face value while the entire debt loss lands on the suppliers who remained. This is the same bug class as TRST-H-1: the set of shares charged by a global accounting event is manipulable by the attacker changing their own share count around the event boundary.

### Finding Description
`process_clean_bad_debt` only requires `caller.require_auth()` and no flash-loan guard violation (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:195-200`). Eligibility (`is_socializable_bad_debt`: `total_debt > total_collateral && total_collateral <= $5`) is a pure function of oracle prices and stored positions (`curve.rs:25-27`), so an attacker can compute exactly when the gate opens. `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` then sends `PoolSeizeEntry` batch to the pool, which burns the debt shares and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, computing `reduction_factor = (supplied_value - bad_debt) / supplied_value` against `cache.supplied()` at execution time (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-88`, `docs/reference/formulas.md:390-395`).

Meanwhile `withdraw` remains callable even under global pause (`docs/reference/invariants.md:490-491`), and a pure supplier's withdrawal is gated only by pool cash reserves and the utilization cap, not by pending bad debt. The write-down is therefore applied only to shares present in `supplied` during the `seize_positions` call; shares withdrawn beforehand settle at the old index in full.

Sequence (each a separate transaction; ordering guaranteed because the attacker calls `clean_bad_debt` themselves):

1. Attacker holds a large fraction `f` of a market's `supplied` shares.
2. An insolvent dust account appears (price move or interest accrual).
3. `controller.withdraw(caller, attacker_account, withdrawals=[all supply of that market], to)` — attacker is paid `floor(shares × supply_index)` at the pre-write-down index from pool cash.
4. `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, victim_account_id)` — the pool applies `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; the full debt loss is now divided over the remaining `(1-f)` of suppliers only.
5. `controller.supply(...)` — attacker re-deposits at the depressed index, receiving ~`1/(1-f)` more shares per token than before.

The attacker's net position: they avoided `f × bad_debt` of loss and capture additional share appreciation from re-entering cheap. Note the withdrawal leg is limited by pool `cash` (`require_reserves`), but partial coverage still yields partial theft, and a supplier can also pre-position in a low-utilization market where cash is plentiful.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds, bounded by `f × bad_debt` where `f` is the attacker's fraction of market supply and `bad_debt` the socialized amount. Remaining suppliers absorb a write-down amplified by `1/(1-f)`. If the index clamps at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, residual shortfall blocks the attacker's re-supply (`PoolInsolvent` on `backing_shortfall > 0`), but the withdrawal leg already extracted value at face price — the attacker still profits by the dodged write-down even without re-entering.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-high. Requirements: the attacker is a supplier in a market that develops dust-threshold bad debt (≤$5 collateral, insolvent — a routine occurrence), and the market has enough cash to fund the withdrawal. Eligibility is deterministic and publicly readable; the attacker executes `clean_bad_debt` themselves in the transaction immediately after their withdrawal, so no mempool race against other callers is needed — only against other suppliers who might withdraw first. No capital beyond the existing supply position is required.

### Recommendation
Socialize bad debt against a time-weighted or epoch-pinned supply denominator rather than instantaneous `supplied`. Concretely: snapshot `supplied`/`supply_index` per market at the last `update_indexes` boundary (or record a per-supplier checkpoint) and apply `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` against shares outstanding at the moment the account became eligible, or impose a withdrawal lock/cooldown on markets with pending socializable bad debt. Alternatively, process cleanup eligibility inside `withdraw`/`supply` paths (like `global_sync`) so any pending write-down is forced before shares move — this mirrors fixing the snapshot race by making the snapshot implicit in every mutation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Setup: market M with `supplied` = 10,000 shares at `supply_index` = RAY, `cash` = 10,000 tokens; attacker holds 9,000 shares; insolvent account V has $4 collateral and $20 debt.
2. `withdraw(caller=attacker, account_id=A, withdrawals=[(M, 0)], to)` — 0 withdraws entire position; attacker receives 9,000 tokens at index 1.0.
3. `clean_bad_debt(caller=attacker, account_id=V)` — `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` seizes V's positions; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value = 1,000 × RAY`, `bad_debt = $20`, writes index down by 2% — applied only to the remaining 1,000 shares.
4. `supply(caller=attacker, ...)` re-deposits 9,000 tokens, minting `9,000 × RAY / new_index` ≈ 9,184 shares.
5. Result: remaining suppliers bear the full $20 write-down (an effective 2× amplified haircut on their residual shares), and the attacker exits the sequence with more shares than they started with, funded by cash that should have been haircut 1.8% like everyone else.
