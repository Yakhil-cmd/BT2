### Title
Residual supply-index floor after bad-debt write-down lets stale claims drain fresh deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of zeroing it. The scaled supply shares of surviving depositors are not burned or adjusted in proportion, so after a full wipe-out the market still owes `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) > 0` while `cash == 0`. The next deposit makes the books insolvent, and the stale residual claim is paid out of the fresh depositor's cash — the same lifecycle bug class as CVE-2024-41057: a resource is logically freed (supply value written down to a floor) while outstanding references (unburned scaled shares) are still dereferenced later.

### Finding Description
In `pool::ops::seize::apply`, a `Borrow`-side entry computes `bad_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)`, calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt)`, then `cache.burn_debt(position)` — with no per-supplier share mutation: `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`. The write-down is applied only through the index. When the write-down exceeds what the index can represent, the index is floored at `RAY/1000` rather than zeroed; surviving suppliers keep their full `scaled_amount`.

The pool's own test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` proves the arithmetic end-to-end: after the clamp, `alice_stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled) > 0` with `cash == 0` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:454-464`); a fresh deposit of the same size then leaves `total_owed > cash` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:471-477`), and Alice's `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` + `debit_cash` extracts exactly Bob's deposit (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:479-485`), leaving `cash < bob_claim` — an honest depositor's funds are gone (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:487-492`).

On the controller side the sequence is reachable via `clean_bad_debt` → `execute_bad_debt_cleanup`, which submits the account's debt legs as `PoolSeizeEntry { side: Borrow }` and its supply legs as `side: Deposit` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:21-49`). The Borrow leg triggers the index floor clamp, but the write-down of *other* accounts' supply is not accompanied by share burns — their scaled shares survive at the floored valuation, minting the residual claim.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. After a catastrophic bad-debt cleanup clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`, the market retains a stranded aggregate claim (`unscale_supply_floor(supplied)`) backed by zero cash. The first subsequent depositor's tokens are immediately claimable by holders of the stale shares: `withdraw`/`resolve_withdrawal` pays the residual out of the fresh cash. The test demonstrates the fresh depositor loses the entire deposit.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a bad-debt event large enough to drive the supply index to the floor — i.e., a market where socialized debt exceeds ~99.9% of the index's remaining representable supply value. That is exactly the regime `clean_bad_debt` is designed for (total-collateral ≈ 0, debt written off). Once clamped, any unprivileged user can `supply` a small amount and `withdraw`, or simply be the first depositor whose funds are drained by a stale shareholder. No privileged action is needed to trigger cleanup or the drain.

### Recommendation
When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` would clamp at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, also zero or proportionally slash the outstanding `supplied` scaled total (or tombstone the market so `supply` is rejected until `supplied == 0`). Alternatively, gate `supply`/`withdraw` on `supplied == 0` whenever the index sits at the floor, mirroring the kernel fix's ordering: finish invalidating all outstanding references (residual supply claims) before allowing new objects (fresh deposits) into the freed region.

### Proof of Concept
See `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-496`: with `supplied = 1000·RAY`, `borrowed = 1000·RAY`, indexes at `RAY`, `cash = 0`, applying `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(borrowed))` clamps the supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`; Alice's stranded claim equals a fresh deposit `deposit = alice_stranded`; after `mint_supply` + `credit_cash`, `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, alice_scaled)` yields `gross == deposit`, and `cash < unscale_supply_floor(bob_scaled)` — the residual claim drains the new deposit in full.
