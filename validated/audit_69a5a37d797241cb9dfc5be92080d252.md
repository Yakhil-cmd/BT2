### Title
Sustained high-utilization whale market hits the i128 RAY-value ceiling and permanently freezes all market funds - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Every state-mutating controller and pool entrypoint accrues the market first. Accrual (`accrue_step`) unconditionally un-scales `borrowed` and `supplied` shares through `scaled_to_original`, which panics with `MathOverflow` once `scaled * index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. On a large market kept at high utilization, the index grows past the point where `supplied × supply_index` (or `borrowed × borrow_index`) no longer fits in `i128` well before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` index cap can engage. From that ledger onward, every operation on the market reverts inside accrual, so supplier principal, borrower repayment, liquidation, and bad-debt cleanup are all permanently unreachable — an analog to the report's crash/hang DoS, but with permanent effect.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` begins each chunk by un-scaling shares:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
```
(`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`)

`scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` — a half-up `x*y/RAY` that panics `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the quotient exceeds `i128::MAX` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The pool's mutating path `interest::global_sync` runs this on every accrual (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`, `39-53`), and every verb — `supply`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `update_indexes`, `recapitalize` — loads the market through a `Cache` that accrues first.

The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but the *value* ceiling `i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38` is hit first on a whale market. The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` demonstrates exactly this: a ~1-billion-token (18 decimals) market at 98% utilization on the steep segment of the XLM rate curve panics inside `scaled_to_original` after a few years, with `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls both revert with `MathOverflow` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-360`). `docs/reference/formulas.md` acknowledges "value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal," and the test itself notes the documented bound understates the cliff.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the market crosses the value ceiling, no operation can complete because accrual always runs first and always panics:
- Suppliers cannot withdraw any principal or yield.
- Borrowers cannot repay; their collateral in *other* markets is trapped behind an unliquidatable debt.
- Liquidators cannot liquidate (`liquidate` accrues the market first).
- `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and `claim_revenue` on that market revert for the same reason, so there is no in-protocol recovery short of an upgrade.

All suppliers to the affected market — not just the actor who created the conditions — lose access to their funds permanently.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged entrypoints: `supply` (anyone may supply into any listed market up to caps) and `borrow`/`update_indexes` (permissionless keeper). Preconditions:

1. A listed market whose total supplied RAY value approaches `i128::MAX / index`. For an 18-decimal asset this is ~1 billion whole tokens — large, but caps are configurable per market and the test lifts them via ordinary configuration; there is no protocol-level ceiling on market size below this bound.
2. Sustained high utilization so the index compounds at the curve's steep segment. Any large borrower can hold utilization high; with `max_utilization` configured high, a single whale supplying and borrowing against their own collateral can maintain it.
3. Time: the test shows the cliff within a few years at max-rate utilization, earlier than the documented bound suggests.

No privileged action is required; the attacker supplies and borrows their own capital and simply lets accrual run (or accelerates it with periodic `update_indexes` calls). The trade-off is capital lock-up — consistent with Medium severity.

### Recommendation
- Make accrual panic-safe: saturate `scaled_to_original` inputs or catch overflow in `accrue_step`/`global_sync` and clamp at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`, so the index cap — not an arithmetic trap — bounds growth.
- Alternatively, cap market `supplied` scaled shares at `i128::MAX / MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` at entry time (analogous to `calculate_scaled_cap`'s saturating design), so `supplied × supply_index` can never overflow regardless of index growth.
- Add the same bound for `borrowed` scaled shares.

### Proof of Concept
Covered by the in-repo harness test, which encodes the exact exploit path:

```rust
// tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);          // ~1e27 units -> 1e36 RAY
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);              // unprivileged supply
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);                 // 98% utilization
loop {
    t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
    if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) { break e; }
}
// update_indexes reverts MathOverflow; the market is frozen:
// try_withdraw_raw -> MathOverflow, try_repay -> MathOverflow
```

A single unprivileged address can substitute its own account for BOB/ALICE (supply the whale amount, borrow ~98% against its own collateral in another market), advance time naturally, and the market freezes on the next accrual — no privileged calls involved.
