The reported bug is a pure front-running/griefing pattern against OpenZeppelin's `Governor.cancel()`, where a proposal's identity is derived solely from a hash of its content (targets/values/calldatas/descriptionHash) with no `proposer` binding, allowing anyone to front-run and self-cancel a colliding proposal. This class of finding is explicitly excluded by the analysis methodology ("Reject ... pure front-running/economic attacks").

Beyond the exclusion, no structural analog exists in the FRAME governance pallets. In `pallet-democracy`, proposal cancellation is gated behind privileged origins rather than being callable by an unprivileged front-runner: `cancel_proposal` requires `T::CancelProposalOrigin`, `emergency_cancel` requires `T::CancellationOrigin`, and `blacklist` requires `T::BlacklistOrigin` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) . Similarly, `pallet-referenda`'s `cancel` extrinsic requires a privileged `CancelOrigin` and cannot be invoked by the original proposer or an arbitrary front-runner [4](#0-3) . Since the task requires the attacker to hold no privileged role, and every cancellation path in these pallets is origin-gated (unlike OZ Governor's proposer-cancellable design), the described grief vector has no reachable, unprivileged analog in this codebase.
