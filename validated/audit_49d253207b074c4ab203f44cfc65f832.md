### Title
Unprivileged dust deposits force adverse risk-parameter restamping on foreign accounts - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary

Any authenticated user can deposit a dust amount into another account’s existing collateral position. That deposit invokes the full risk-parameter refresh, copying newly configured LTV, liquidation threshold, bonus, and fee values into the victim’s stored position. An attacker can therefore choose when a victim’s collateral adopts tighter parameters, potentially blocking withdrawals or making the victim liquidatable earlier than under their previously stamped terms.

### Finding Description

`process_supply` only requires the supplied `caller` to authorize the transaction. For an existing foreign account, `require_third_party_existing_supply` permits the call when every supplied market is already present in the account’s supply map. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

After the attacker’s measured transfer reaches the pool, `merge_supply_leg` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple`. [3](#0-2)  That function unconditionally writes the current listed `loan_to_value` and then attempts to write the current liquidation threshold, bonus, and fees. [4](#0-3) 

The liquidation-tuple update is gated only when the new values favor a liquidator and the account has debt; in that case, the victim’s hypothetical health factor with the new threshold merely has to remain at least 1.05. [5](#0-4)  No victim authorization is consulted anywhere in this path.

### Impact Explanation

An attacker can prematurely impose adverse collateral terms on an account they do not control.

A reduced stored LTV can prevent the victim from passing the post-operation collateral checks used by withdrawals and borrows, temporarily freezing otherwise economically controlled collateral until debt is repaid or parameters become favorable again. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

A reduced liquidation threshold can also be stamped while the resulting hypothetical health factor remains above 1.05, but still below the level implied by the victim’s old threshold. The attacker can later liquidate the victim at a collateral price where the old stamped threshold would still have produced a health factor above one. This converts a dust deposit into control over the timing of the victim’s liquidation and can transfer liquidation bonus value from the victim to a liquidator.

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires three conditions:

1. Governance changes an existing collateral’s listed risk parameters.
2. The victim already holds that collateral as a supply position.
3. For a tightening liquidation tuple, the victim’s hypothetical health factor with the new threshold is at least 1.05.

The attacker can then invoke the public `supply` endpoint with their own address as `caller`, the victim’s `account_id`, the victim’s `spoke_id`, and a positive dust amount for an existing collateral leg. The attacker pays only the deposit amount, which is credited to the victim, and no owner or delegate authorization is required. [1](#0-0) 

### Recommendation

Do not let a third-party supply trigger a `FullTuple` risk refresh on an existing foreign account. Restrict third-party top-ups so they preserve the victim’s stored risk parameters, or require the position NFT owner or an active delegate before refreshing risk parameters. At minimum, separate collateral crediting from risk restamping so a dust deposit cannot change liquidation terms, LTV, bonus, or fees.

### Proof of Concept

Assume:

- Victim has a USDC supply position stamped with `LTV = 8000` and `liquidation_threshold = 8000`.
- Governance updates USDC to `LTV = 5000` and `liquidation_threshold = 7000`.
- The victim has debt such that health factor under the new threshold remains above 1.05.

An attacker executes:

```text
controller.supply(
    caller = attacker,
    account_id = victim_account_id,
    spoke_id = victim_spoke_id,
    assets = [(HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset: USDC }, 1)]
)
```

Execution path:

1. `process_supply` authenticates only `attacker`.
2. `require_third_party_existing_supply` succeeds because USDC is already a victim supply position.
3. `process_deposit` pulls the attacker’s dust payment into the pool.
4. `merge_supply_leg` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params(..., FullTuple)`.
5. The victim’s stored USDC position is rewritten to `LTV = 5000`, `liquidation_threshold = 7000`, and the current bonus/fee tuple.
6. The attacker may subsequently call `liquidate` when collateral value falls into the range where the new threshold produces `HF < 1` but the old threshold would still produce `HF >= 1`.

The regression tests demonstrate both the permissionless top-up and the resulting forced LTV update: a third-party `USDC` deposit changes the victim’s stored LTV from `8000` to `5000`, after which a previously possible borrow is rejected as insufficient collateral. [8](#0-7)

### Risk checks and pause flags

After pool accounting, `borrow`, `withdraw` and the six account strategies require LTV-weighted collateral to cover debt and health factor (HF) to be at least 1. If debt remains, LTV-weighted collateral must also meet the minimum-borrow floor. Ordinary `supply` and `repay` skip these checks; repayment loads debt positions only. Liquidation uses separate admission and sizing rules. See [formulas](formulas.md) for the calculations.
