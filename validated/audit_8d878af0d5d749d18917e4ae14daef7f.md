### Title
RAY-domain value overflow permanently freezes a market — every entry verb accrues first and panics in `scaled_to_original` - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs via `common/src/rates/scaling.rs`)

### Summary
CVE-2018-12405 is a memory-corruption class bug; in a Soroban lending protocol the analogous corruption of safety-critical state is unbounded fixed-point arithmetic. XOXNO Lending caps the borrow *index* at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10^9 × RAY), but accrual computes the market's total debt value as `borrowed_shares × new_borrow_index` in RAY — a product that overflows `i128` long before the index ceiling is reached on a large market. Because every state-changing entrypoint accrues interest first, the overflow permanently bricks the market: no repay, withdraw, borrow, liquidation, or bad-debt cleanup can ever execute again, permanently freezing all supplied collateral and debt.

### Finding Description
Index accrual clamps the index itself (`update_borrow_index` multiplies then clamps to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`), but the market-debt valuation inside `calculate_supplier_rewards` / accrual computes `Ray::from(borrowed).mul(env, Ray::from(new_index))` (`scaled_to_original` in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`), which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once `borrowed × index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX` (~1.7e38 RAY). For an 18-decimals asset the token-to-RAY admission bound is ~1.7e29 base units ≈ 170 billion whole tokens; at 98% utilization the debt value hits the RAY ceiling while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so the index cap never engages. The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360) proves the end state: `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and "The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic." Liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` accrue through the same `ops::synced_market` path (`contracts/pool/src/ops/net_settle.rs:31`), so they cannot rescue the market either. There is no ceiling alarm and no recovery verb that skips accrual.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and protocol insolvency: every supplier's tokens are locked forever, borrowers can never repay, collateral can never be seized, and bad debt can never be socialized or recapitalized in that market. This matches the accepted impact classes "permanent freezing of funds" and "protocol insolvency."

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a very large market (on the order of 10^11 whole tokens at 18 decimals, scaling inversely with decimals) sustained at high utilization for years at steep-curve rates — the harness reaches it within 40 simulated years at 98% utilization on the XLM curve. It is an arithmetic cliff rather than a cheap attacker trigger; an unprivileged whale can drive a market into it with `supply` + `borrow` (the in-scope reachable verbs) and simply wait, but the capital requirement is extreme. Medium-Low likelihood, but a guaranteed terminal state once reached, and it can also be reached organically.

### Recommendation
Cap the *accrual debt value*, not just the index: clamp `new_index` such that `borrowed × new_index / RAY <= i128::MAX` (i.e., `new_index <= i128::MAX * RAY / borrowed`), or saturate accrued interest at the point where further growth is unrepresentable, so `update_indexes` keeps succeeding and exits/repayments/liquidations stay live. Alternatively, introduce a dedicated `rescue` path that writes the accrued state forward without revaluing the full debt, and emit an event as the market approaches the RAY-value ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
Already encoded as a harness test: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` supplies `BILLION * 10^18` BIG18, borrows 98% of it, advances year-sized timestamps, and observes `try_update_indexes_for` fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequent `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` fail identically, demonstrating the permanent freeze.
