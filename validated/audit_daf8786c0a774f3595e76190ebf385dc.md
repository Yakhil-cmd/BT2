### Title
Unbounded staleness spread lets a stale fundamental anchor inflate dual-source collateral prices - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs)

### Summary
The price aggregator marks a two-leg price stale for excessive timestamp skew only when **both** legs are `FeedNature::Market`; a `Fundamental` anchor can therefore be nearly a full configured heartbeat old and still be averaged with a fresh market leg. `Controller.borrow` accepts that midpoint as a strict price when checking LTV-weighted collateral and health factor, allowing an unprivileged borrower to extract debt that the fresh market value would not support. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`blend` computes `stale` from each source's per-feed freshness, `oracle.max_price_stale_seconds`, and an age-spread check. The age-spread check is deliberately gated by `primary.nature == FeedNature::Market && anchor.nature == FeedNature::Market`, so a market primary and `Fundamental` secondary may differ in observation age by any amount while remaining non-stale if each individual bound passes. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

After the tolerance check, the accepted value is unconditionally the arithmetic midpoint of the two legs. The freshness flags do not exclude the old leg from valuation. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

The controller obtains the resolved price through `PriceAggregatorClient::prices`, caches it, and uses `feed.price` directly in collateral value, LTV collateral, weighted collateral, debt, and health-factor calculations. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

The deployed configuration shape makes this reachable rather than hypothetical: mainnet EURC uses a Reflector market leg with a one-hour freshness bound and a RedStone `Fundamental` leg with a sixteen-hour freshness bound. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
A stale-but-permitted fundamental quote skews collateral valuation by half of the distance between the old quote and the fresh market quote. With an old anchor at `$1.00`, fresh market price at `$0.91`, and a 10% tolerance, the protocol accepts approximately `$0.955`, inflating collateral by about 4.95% relative to the fresh market value. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

An attacker can supply the affected collateral, call `borrow`, and withdraw pool assets that would be rejected under the fresh market valuation. The harness reproduces this exact sequence: BOB supplies USDC liquidity, ALICE supplies XLM, and ALICE's `$70,000` borrow succeeds only in the stale-anchor run while the fresh-anchor control fails with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

This can create under-collateralized debt and protocol insolvency: `borrow` pays assets to an external recipient before enforcing the manipulated post-pool solvency calculation. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The exploit requires only ordinary `supply` and `borrow` calls after an authorized slow feed has not updated for a period shorter than its own `max_stale_seconds`. No privileged contract call, leaked key, or oracle-configuration change is required. [16](#0-15) [17](#0-16) 

Slow fundamental feeds are explicitly supported: `MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS` is 93,600 seconds to accommodate a 24-hour heartbeat, while the leg-age-spread bound is only 3,600 seconds and applies solely to two market legs. [18](#0-17) [19](#0-18) 

Likelihood depends on a real market move occurring while the fundamental leg remains within its configured heartbeat. That is an external oracle timing condition rather than direct price forgery, so Medium severity is more appropriate than High even though the resulting loss can be significant. [20](#0-19) [21](#0-20) 

### Recommendation
Apply `MAX_LEG_AGE_SPREAD_SECONDS` to every blended pair, not just two `Market` legs, or define a separate bounded spread for market/fundamental pairs. At minimum, exclude or fail a leg whose timestamp is too far from the other leg instead of averaging it into `price_wad`. [1](#0-0) 

If slow anchors are intentionally permitted, use a fresher-side conservative price rather than an average for risk decisions—for example, use the lower leg for collateral valuation and the higher leg for debt valuation—so stale support cannot enlarge borrowing capacity. [22](#0-21) 

### Proof of Concept
The repository contains a reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blend.rs`:

1. Configure XLM with a fresh Reflector primary and a RedStone `Fundamental` anchor whose freshness bound is 86,400 seconds. [23](#0-22) 
2. Move the market price from `$1.00` to `$0.91`, then leave the RedStone observation 82,800 seconds old in the exploit run and fresh in the control run. [24](#0-23) 
3. Supply `$500,000` USDC liquidity as BOB and `100,000` XLM collateral as ALICE. [25](#0-24) 
4. Call ALICE's `borrow` for `$70,000` USDC. The stale-anchor midpoint inflates collateral by more than 4%, the exploit borrow succeeds, and the fresh-anchor control rejects with `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL`. [26](#0-25)

/// Returns the average of `anchor_price` and `primary_price`, truncated
/// toward zero. Returns `0` if the sum overflows `i128`.
pub(crate) fn midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor_price: i128, primary_price: i128) -> i128 {
    anchor_price
        .checked_add(primary_price)
        .map(|sum| sum / 2)
        .unwrap_or(0)
}

    let mut account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

    let recipient = to.unwrap_or_else(|| caller.clone());

    let restamped = enforce_post_pool_solvency(env, &mut cache, &mut account);
    let sides = if restamped {
        PositionSides::Both
    } else {
        PositionSides::Debt
    };
    finalize_position_flow(env, account_id, &account, &mut cache, sides, false);
