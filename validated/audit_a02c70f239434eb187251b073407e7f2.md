### Title
Permanent market freeze: `scaled_to_original` overflows `i128` during accrual before the borrow-index cap can engage - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2024-4559 is a heap buffer overflow: an arithmetic bound is crossed before a guard catches it. The analog here is value-domain overflow during interest accrual. `accrue_step` unscales `borrowed` debt via `scaled_to_original` (an `i128`-bounded `Ray::mul`) at the top of every compound step, before `update_borrow_index` gets a chance to clamp the index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. On a sufficiently large market at sustained high utilization, the `borrowed * borrow_index` product overflows `i128` and panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Because `interest::global_sync` runs accrual at the head of every pool verb, the panic bricks every entrypoint that touches the market — permanently.

### Finding Description
- `global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs before any supply/borrow/withdraw/repay/liquidate operation; each chunk calls `accrue_step`.
- `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)`, which is `borrowed.mul(env, borrow_index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`, `common/src/math/fp.rs:50-52` → `mul_div_half_up`).
- `mul_div_half_up` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`) panics with `MathOverflow` when the exact quotient exceeds `i128`. The overflow here is on the *value* (`scaled shares × index`), not the index itself, so the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) is never reached — the panic happens one step earlier.
- The supply side mirrors this: `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` inside `update_supply_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:34`) and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` at `simulate.rs:61` overflow identically before `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` clamps.
- This is not hypothetical: the test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`) drives exactly this path — a 1e18-decimals market with ~10^27-unit supply at 98% utilization on the XLM curve — and asserts `MathOverflow` on `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay`, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` at failure.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the scaled-value product crosses the `i128` ceiling, every controller and pool entrypoint that accrues (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `flash_position`, `multiply`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize`) reverts for that market. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate — all deposits and collateral are locked in the contract. There is no recovery path short of a contract upgrade, and the panic is state-persistent because the oversized `borrowed`/`supplied` totals and the index only grow.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses with no privileged action required: an attacker (or simply organic whales) creates the condition via ordinary `supply`/`borrow` calls on a high-decimals market, and any later caller's `update_indexes` or verb triggers the freeze once index growth carries the scaled value past `i128::MAX / RAY` (≈1.7e11 RAY of supplied/borrowed value at index 1.0). The harness test demonstrates the cliff at ~170× index on the XLM curve at 98% utilization. No oracle manipulation, leaked keys, or admin misconfiguration is needed — only a large market and time at high utilization, which an attacker with capital can force (borrow against the market to pin utilization high). Severity is bounded by the capital required and the time dimension rather than by reachability.

### Recommendation
Enforce the value ceiling before the multiply, or bound the market below it:

- In `accrue_step`, clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` to their `MAX_*` caps *before* calling `scaled_to_original`, or compute `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` through `mul_div_floor_saturating`/`div_floor_saturating` so accrual degrades gracefully at the cap instead of panicking.
- Alternatively, fail-closed *at entry time*: reject `supply`/`borrow` amounts whose scaled-form (`amount_ray / index`) would push `supplied`/`borrowed` past `i128::MAX / MAX_*_INDEX_RAY`, so the overflow state is unreachable.
- Extend the existing large-position tests to cover the supply-index leg (`update_supply_index`'s `supplied.mul(old_index)` and `checked_add`) which has the same unguarded overflow.

### Proof of Concept
Mirrored by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360` (unprivileged roles `BOB`/`ALICE` only):

1. `supply(BOB, "BIG18", 1e9 * 10^18)` — whale supply on an 18-decimals market; `borrow(ALICE, "BIG18", 0.98 * principal)` after supplying `COL` collateral.
2. Call `update_indexes` (or any verb) repeatedly as ledger time advances.
3. Accrual reaches a step where `borrowed * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; `scaled_to_original` at `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60` panics with `MathOverflow` (error #33).
4. Thereafter `withdraw`, `repay`, `update_indexes`, and every other accrue-first verb revert permanently for the market; all supplied funds and collateral are frozen.
