### Title
Premature bad-debt socialization can write off debt that is still backed by unseized collateral — ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
`liquidate()` decides whether to socialize a borrower's remaining debt as bad debt using a `no-collateral-left` proposition that is only an approximation of "the borrower truly has zero residual collateral value." The check treats the borrower's non-targeted collateral as worthless whenever `other-debt-repayable` rounds to `0`, rather than verifying that its USD value is actually zero. This mirrors the reported bug class: the code's boolean condition does not precisely match the natural-language intent ("if position has collateral left, don't socialize; else socialize"), so a non-empty/non-zero-value case can slip into the "socialize" branch, harming the pool of suppliers who fund the debt vault.

### Finding Description
In `liquidate` [1](#0-0) , after seizing collateral for the targeted asset, the contract computes:

```
(target-coll-full-usd (normalize (* user-coll-balance coll-price) coll-decimals false))
(other-coll-usd (if (> total-collateral-usd target-coll-full-usd) (- total-collateral-usd target-coll-full-usd) u0))
(other-debt-repayable (if (> other-coll-usd u0) ... integer division chain ... u0))
(no-collateral-left (and (is-eq coll-removed u0)
                          (or (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) u1)
                              (and (is-eq (len (get collateral pos-full)) (len (get collateral position)))
                                   (is-eq other-debt-repayable u0)))))
```

`other-debt-repayable` is derived via a chain of `mul-div-down` / `div-bps-down` conversions from `other-coll-usd` (the USD value of the borrower's *other* collateral) into debt-token units [2](#0-1) . Because this is integer arithmetic, a small but strictly positive `other-coll-usd` (e.g. sub-cent dust from a high-decimals/low-price debt asset, or simply a low-value residual collateral relative to a high-priced debt asset) can round down to `other-tokens = 0` and therefore `other-debt-repayable = 0`, even though `other-coll-usd > 0`.

`no-collateral-left` is then used as the trigger to run `socialize-debt-asset` over the borrower's entire remaining debt list, wiping/socializing it as bad debt [3](#0-2) . This is a mismatch between the intended proposition ("borrower has literally zero collateral backing left, so remaining debt is genuinely unrecoverable and must be written off against suppliers") and the actual check ("the *rounded* debt-token equivalent of other collateral happens to be zero"). The borrower's other collateral asset is never seized in this branch — its value is left intact in the borrower's position — while the debt attributable to it is nonetheless erased and its cost pushed onto the shared debt-vault suppliers via `socialize-debt-asset`.

### Impact Explanation
The victims are the vault suppliers whose funds back the debt vault(s): they absorb the socialized bad debt, i.e., a shortfall that shouldn't exist because the borrower's other collateral still has positive value and was never liquidated to cover it. The borrower benefits by keeping collateral that should have secured the debt, and the debt itself disappears from their obligation, at the expense of the pool. This is a protocol-insolvency-class impact: the debt vault's outstanding debt is reduced by write-off while collateral remains locked in the (no-longer-indebted) borrower's account, permanently shifting a real loss onto lenders — the "socialization charged to all suppliers" pattern the scope explicitly calls out as in-scope.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering the exact rounding conditions requires a borrower holding a residual collateral asset whose USD value, after passing through the debt-token conversion (`div-bps-down` by `(BPS + liq-penalty-max)`, `mul-div-down` by `10^debt-decimals / debt-price`, and index scaling), rounds to zero debt tokens. This is most likely with combinations of low-value residual collateral and high-priced/low-decimal debt assets (e.g., a few cents of a stablecoin collateral vs. a BTC-denominated debt asset), or can be deliberately engineered by a liquidator/borrower pair who structure the liquidation to leave a specific dust amount in the non-targeted asset. Because the position's egroup/mask composition and per-asset prices are attacker-observable, the precise dust amount needed can be computed off-chain, making this reproducible rather than purely accidental.

### Recommendation
Redefine `no-collateral-left` to test actual remaining collateral *value* directly (i.e., `is-eq other-coll-usd u0`), not the rounded-to-zero token-equivalent `other-debt-repayable`. If some minimum-dust tolerance is desired, it should be an explicit, audited threshold rather than an artifact of unrelated unit conversions (BPS/price/decimals/index rounding). Additionally, before socializing debt, the code should positively confirm the sum of all remaining collateral's USD value is zero (or below an explicit dust bound), rather than inferring it indirectly from whether it would repay debt after applying `liq-penalty-max`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower deposits two collateral assets: `coll-aid` (large value, e.g. sBTC) and asset `X` with a small but non-zero residual USD value (e.g. $0.30 of a stablecoin), and borrows a high-priced debt asset `D` (e.g. an asset priced in the thousands with low decimals) such that the debt-token equivalent of $0.30 rounds to `0` through `div-bps-down`/`mul-div-down`.
2. Debt asset `D`'s price/decimals is chosen so `other-tokens` in the `other-debt-repayable` computation [2](#0-1)  rounds to `0`, even though `other-coll-usd = 0.30 USD > 0`.
3. Price of `coll-aid` drops, pushing the position into the full-liquidation zone (LTV ≥ `LTV-LIQ-FULL`).
4. A liquidator calls `liquidate` on `coll-aid` for the full seizable amount, driving `coll-removed` (remaining balance) to `0`.
5. Since `len(pos-full.collateral) == len(position.collateral)` (asset `X` still enabled/present) and `other-debt-repayable == 0`, `no-collateral-left` evaluates `true` at line 1526-1532, even though asset `X`'s $0.30 of value was never seized.
6. The remaining debt list is passed to `socialize-debt-asset`, which writes off/socializes the borrower's remaining debt to the vault's suppliers [4](#0-3) , while the borrower retains asset `X`'s collateral, unseized, in their position.

Note: exact PoC magnitudes (asset prices/decimals needed to force the rounding-to-zero condition, and confirmation of `socialize-debt-asset`'s exact write-off mechanics) were not fully traced within the available exploration budget; a Devin session with full repo access should trace `socialize-debt-asset` and reproduce this scenario in the `local-testing` test harness to confirm exploitability and quantify the exact dust thresholds per supported asset pair.
