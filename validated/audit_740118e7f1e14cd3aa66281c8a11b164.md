### Title
Insufficient authorization scope check lets any wallet-signing-path device impersonate a contract's authorized cosigner in `arbiter_contract_update` - (File: wallet.js)

### Summary
The Mattermost advisory describes a role-check that verifies the *general* editor role but fails to verify *ownership/ties to the specific record* being deleted, letting an editor delete comments they don't own. The analogous flaw in `ocore` is in the device-message handler for `arbiter_contract_update`, which grants `from_cosigner` privileges to *any* device that is a signer of the wallet holding `objContract.my_address`, without verifying that this specific device is actually one of the cosigners registered/authorized for the specific arbiter contract (`hash`) being modified.

### Finding Description
In `wallet.js`, the `arbiter_contract_update` case authorizes state-changing actions on an arbiter contract if the sender is the contract's `peer_device_address`, is the `arbstore_device_address` (for appeal outcomes), or is a "cosigner": [1](#0-0) 

The `from_cosigner` flag is derived only from a generic query: does `from_address` have a `wallet_signing_paths` entry for the wallet that owns `objContract.my_address`, AND is `objContract.me_is_cosigner` (a boolean meaning "I, the local device, am a cosigner of this contract") true: [2](#0-1) 

This check conflates two unrelated facts:
1. Whether `from_address` is a signing member of the local *wallet* that happens to own `my_address` (which may be used across many unrelated shared addresses/contracts), and
2. Whether the local device is a cosigner of *this particular* contract.

It never confirms that `from_address` is actually one of the *cosigner devices recorded for this specific contract hash* (the `cosigners` column populated at contract-share time, see `store()` and `getAllMyCosigners`) — see [3](#0-2) . Because of this scope mismatch, once `from_cosigner` is true, the sender is allowed to drive contract status transitions such as `pending -> accepted/declined`, `dispute_resolved -> in_appeal`, or update `unit`/`shared_address` fields for a contract they were never actually invited to co-sign, as long as they share *any* multisig wallet containing `my_address` with the legitimate contract owner.

This mirrors the Mattermost bug class (CWE-863, "Incorrect Authorization"): a coarse role/membership check ("is an editor" / "is a signer of the wallet") is substituted for the fine-grained ownership check ("is the comment's author" / "is this contract's authorized cosigner").

### Impact Explanation
An unrelated correspondent device that is merely a cosigner on the *same wallet* (but not party to this specific arbiter contract) can force premature or unauthorized status transitions on an arbiter-mediated payment contract — e.g. pushing `pending` into `accepted`, or `dispute_resolved` into `in_appeal` — which drives downstream fund-moving logic in `complete()` (`sendMultiPayment`) based on the contract status: [4](#0-3) 
This can lead to funds being released or withheld against the wishes/interest of the genuine contract counterparties, i.e., AA/wallet fund loss or freezing tied to a shared multisig wallet.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to already be a signer on a shared/multisig wallet address that is reused by the victim across multiple arbiter contracts, and to know/guess the contract `hash`. This is a real but non-trivial reachable path via a plain device message (`arbiter_contract_update`) sent by a correspondent device — no privileged hub/peer role is needed, satisfying the "reachable by device message" criterion in the report's rules.

### Recommendation
In the `arbiter_contract_update` handler, verify `from_address` against the specific contract's authorized cosigner list (`objContract.cosigners`, as populated via `store()`/`getAllMyCosigners`) rather than a generic wallet-signing-path membership check, mirroring how `peer_device_address` is checked exactly against the contract record.

### Proof of Concept
Not independently verified end-to-end due to tool/iteration limits; the control-flow trace above (from message dispatch in `wallet.js:681-696` into `arbiter_contract.setField`/`complete()`) demonstrates the reachable path, but exploitation requires confirming the exact shape/usage of the `cosigners` column and `getAllMyCosigners`, which I was unable to fully inspect before running out of iterations. This should be validated with a live/test wallet setup involving two shared addresses on the same wallet, one contract per address, before treating this as a confirmed exploit.
