### Title
Supply-index floor clamp after bad-debt wipeout preserves a phantom share claim that drains later deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs / apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index, exercised via controller `clean_bad_debt` then `withdraw`)

### Summary
The bad-debt write-down path clamps the market's `supply_index` at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (RAY/1000) rather than zeroing or proportionally invalidating the wiped suppliers' scaled shares. This is the accounting analog of CVE-2020-26959's "reference decrement on a previously freed object": the write-down economically destroys the claim backing the existing scaled balances, but the shares themselves remain stored and are still fully withdrawable. When fresh cash later enters the market, a wiped supplier's `withdraw` unscales its shares at the clamped floor index and is paid real tokens, stealing the new supplier's deposit. The codebase's own test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` demonstrates exactly this drain end-to-end at the cache layer.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt` (permissionless on the controller) drives `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which scales `supply_index` down by `1 - bad_debt_value / supply_value` but never below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (10^24 raw, i.e. RAY/1000). When a wipeout exceeds 99.9% of supply value, the index is clamped UP to the floor instead of reaching the economically correct near-zero value, leaving every existing scaled supply position with a positive unscales: `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0` (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:388-397).

The position map and the account's shares are untouched by the write-down. Later, the normal controller `withdraw` path reaches `resolve_withdrawal` → `burn_position` → `gate_and_debit` (contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:93-118). The only cash guard is `require_reserves(net_transfer)`, which passes once any fresh deposit has credited cash — the test shows `gross == fresh_cash` and the pool drained to zero while the fresh supplier's claim remains unpaid (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:403-426).

`merge_withdraw_leg` / `update_or_remove_supply_position` on the controller remove the position only when `scaled_amount == Ray::ZERO` (contracts/controller/src/account.rs:189-213), so a wiped position with nonzero shares always reaches the pool for payout. INV-ACCT-10 confirms the pool blindly trusts the controller-sent scaled position, and INV-IDX-02 explicitly concedes the floor "can preserve an unbacked residual claim" (docs/reference/invariants.md:240-247).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: any wiped supplier (or the bad-debt victim's counterparty holder) can withdraw a phantom claim equal to `scaled_shares × RAY/1000 / RAY`, paid entirely out of deposits made after the wipeout. The pool's cash falls below outstanding claims, so honest fresh suppliers cannot be made whole.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering requires a market where bad debt reaches ≥99.9% of supply value so the write-down hits the floor — e.g., a near-total collateral crash with debt left uncleared (then permissionless `clean_bad_debt`), or a thinly supplied market. It does not require any privilege: `clean_bad_debt`, `supply`, and `withdraw` are all in the unprivileged entrypoint set, and the attacker can be the wiped supplier themself by holding shares pre-wipeout and racing fresh deposits afterward. The precondition is extreme but is a real market failure mode, and the payout mechanism is deterministic once cash exists.

### Recommendation
On a write-down that would push `supply_index` to the floor, zero out (or proportionally haircut) the stored scaled supply of wiped accounts instead of leaving withdrawable shares, or record wiped shares as non-withdrawable. At minimum, `resolve_withdrawal`/`gate_and_debit` should reject withdrawals whose gross exceeds the residual backed claim computed against the pre-clamp index, rather than relying on `require_reserves` alone, which cannot distinguish backed cash from a stranded claim.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the in-repo test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-429), and its full-pool variant ending at line 369:

1. Market has supplier A with `scaled_a = 1000 RAY` shares; cash is 0.
2. Bad debt of 2,000,000 units is written down: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of ~0.
3. A's shares still unscale to `stranded > 0` — a phantom claim.
4. Supplier B deposits `c = stranded` fresh tokens (`mint_supply` + `credit_cash`); B's claim equals the deposit.
5. A calls controller `withdraw(i128::MAX)`: `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = stranded`, `require_reserves` passes (cash == gross), `burn_supply` + `debit_cash` execute, and `transfer_out` pays A.
6. Result: pool cash = 0 < B's claim `c`. A withdrew tokens it no longer owned — value decremented a second time against an already-freed claim — and B's deposit is unrecoverable.
