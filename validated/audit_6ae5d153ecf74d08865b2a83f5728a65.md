### Title
Spender-Attested Assets Permanently Freeze Holder Funds When Address Attestation Becomes Stale After a Definition Change - ([File: storage.js], [File: validation.js])

### Summary
Ocore lets an asset definer mark an asset `spender_attested: true`, meaning every author (sender) and every output address of any payment in that asset must be currently attested by one of the asset's designated attestors. This is directly analogous to the reported `CToken.sol` issue, where a compliance-style gating check (AML/passport attestation) blocks *all* fund movement for a wallet, including legitimate transfers, once the attestation condition can no longer be satisfied — with no mechanism in the protocol to release or seize the frozen funds.

### Finding Description
When validating a payment of a `spender_attested` asset, ocore requires:
- at least one author (sender) to be attested, and if it's an issue, the issuer specifically must be attested [1](#0-0) 
- every output address to be attested [2](#0-1) 

The check for "is address X attested" is performed by `filterAttestedAddresses`, which requires the attestation to be dated *after* the address's most recent `address_definition_change`: [3](#0-2) 

This means any address that rotates its definition (e.g., adding a co-signer, upgrading keys, or any other legitimate `address_definition_change`) instantly loses its "attested" status for the purposes of this asset, until the designated attestor re-attests it. If the attestor is unavailable, has gone offline, refuses to re-attest, or simply never issues a fresh attestation after the change, the address permanently fails both the "author is attested" check and the "output is attested" check.

Because *all* authors and *all* outputs must satisfy this, the holder cannot:
- Send the asset out to any other address (fails the output-attested check, `validation.js:2637-2638`).
- Even send it back to themselves or to the asset definer/attestor for remediation, since their own address also fails the author-attested check (`validation.js:2118-2122`).

There is no bypass analogous to the `is_transferrable` definer exception (`validation.js:2616-2628`), and no seize/liquidation mechanism exists anywhere in the asset schema or validation code to recover or reissue these frozen coins [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
This is a direct funds-freezing bug reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who holds or receives a `spender_attested` asset and later performs a routine `address_definition_change` (a core, permissionless ocore feature). Once the associated attestor does not (or cannot) re-attest the new definition state, the holder's balance of that asset becomes permanently unspendable — mirroring the reported impact where a "burnt passport" locks `CToken`s and blocks both redemption and liquidation with no recovery path.

### Likelihood Explanation
Address definition changes are a normal, expected user action (key rotation, adding co-signers, security upgrades) and are fully permissionless from validation's perspective; they do not require attestor cooperation. Any asset issuer who sets `spender_attested: true` (a supported, first-class asset flag) creates this risk for every holder of the asset. The likelihood of hitting this state is therefore high in any real deployment of attested assets combined with normal wallet security practices (definition changes), and it requires no malicious actor — just routine, honest use of two independently valid ocore features.

### Recommendation
- Reconsider tying attestation validity to `address_definition_change` timestamps (`storage.js:1964-1967`), or provide an explicit re-attestation grace mechanism/warning before the address is allowed to change its definition while holding spender-attested assets.
- Provide a documented recovery path (e.g., allow the asset definer to reissue/seize/redirect stranded balances of addresses that fail the attestation check, similar to the report's recommendation of "a method for seizing funds of locked tokens").
- At minimum, surface this risk clearly wherever `spender_attested` assets are composed/transferred, and consider relaxing the "all authors/outputs must be attested" requirement to allow at least a compliant fallback path (e.g., transfer-to-definer-only) for addresses that fall out of attestation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Asset definer publishes an asset with `spender_attested: true` and attestor `A` (`validateAssetDefinition`, `validation.js:2725-2827`; stored per `writer.js:218-235`).
2. Attestor `A` attests address `H` (holder). `H` receives a payment of the asset; validation passes because `H` is attested (`validation.js:2115-2122`, `2630-2641`).
3. `H` performs a legitimate `address_definition_change` (e.g., rotates its signing key) — a normal, unprivileged operation.
4. `filterAttestedAddresses` now requires a new attestation dated after this change (`storage.js:1959-1974`); attestor `A` never re-attests `H` (offline, discontinued service, or simply hasn't gotten to it).
5. `H` attempts to send the asset anywhere, including back to the definer/attestor: validation fails both the "author is attested" check (`validation.js:2118`) and, for any third-party recipient, the "output is attested" check (`validation.js:2637-2638`).
6. `H`'s balance of the asset is now permanently frozen with no protocol-level path to unlock, seize, or reissue it.

// filter only those addresses that are attested (doesn't work for light clients)
function filterAttestedAddresses(conn, objAsset, last_ball_mci, arrAddresses, handleAttestedAddresses){
	conn.query(
		"SELECT DISTINCT address FROM attestations CROSS JOIN units USING(unit) \n\
		WHERE attestor_address IN(?) AND address IN(?) AND main_chain_index<=? AND is_stable=1 AND sequence='good' \n\
			AND main_chain_index>IFNULL( \n\
				(SELECT main_chain_index FROM address_definition_changes JOIN units USING(unit) \n\
				WHERE address_definition_changes.address=attestations.address AND main_chain_index<=? AND is_stable=1 AND sequence='good' ORDER BY main_chain_index DESC LIMIT 1), \n\
			0)",
		[objAsset.arrAttestorAddresses, arrAddresses, last_ball_mci, last_ball_mci],
		function(addr_rows){
			var arrAttestedAddresses = addr_rows.map(function(addr_row){ return addr_row.address; });
			handleAttestedAddresses(arrAttestedAddresses);
		}
	);
}
