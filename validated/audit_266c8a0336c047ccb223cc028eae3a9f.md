## Analog Found

### Title
Donation-based price manipulation via unguarded `balance[asset]` primitive in AA formulas - (File: `formula/evaluation.js`)

### Summary
Obyte's oscript language exposes a `balance[asset]` primitive that AAs use to price shares/exchanges against their own on-chain reserves. This value is the *raw* balance of the AA's address, which includes any plain payment sent to that address by an unprivileged unit poster — even a "donation" with no `data` message and no relation to any specific trigger case. Any AA that computes issuance/exchange ratios from `balance[...]` (as ocore's own shipped example AA templates do) can have that ratio manipulated by an attacker who sends a bare payment to the AA immediately before invoking a price-sensitive branch, exactly mirroring the donation-based oracle/vault manipulation that caused the Nitron/dGLP loss.

### Finding Description
`balance[asset]`/`balance[address][asset]` in the oscript formula evaluator resolves to the AA's current stored balance with no filtering for "reserves belonging to this operation" vs. "unsolicited donations": [1](#0-0) 

That balance is populated in `aa_composer.js`'s `updateInitialAABalances()`, which folds *every* output sent to the AA address in the triggering unit (or any prior unit) into `assocBalances`/`aa_balances`, regardless of whether the sender is a legitimate counterparty of the specific price computation being performed: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The repository's own canonical AA template for constant-product/AMM-style logic computes issuance and exchange amounts directly from this raw `balance[...]`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

and the shipped fundraising-proxy template similarly gates a "target reached" decision on `balance[$destination_aa][base]`: [6](#0-5) 

Because `balance[...]` mixes donated/unsolicited funds with the funds that should legitimately back a share price, any unprivileged unit poster can send a bare payment (an ocore payment message needs no `data` and can target any address, including an AA) directly to the AA's address to inflate `balance[asset]` before triggering the price-computing case. This is structurally identical to the Nitron root cause: a donation directly increasing the value used as the pricing input for a share/exchange formula, without any oracle- or protocol-level safeguard distinguishing "expected reserve" from "arbitrary donation."

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can:
1. Donate assets/bytes directly to a live pricing AA (no trigger required) to shift `$current_ratio`, `$p` (constant-product invariant), or `$missing_amount`/target computations.
2. Immediately follow with a legitimate trigger (invest/exchange/divest) computed off the now-inflated `balance[...]`, minting itself an outsized share of the pool asset or extracting more of the reserve asset than it deposited, at the expense of other depositors.
This results in concrete AA fund loss for other participants and mispriced share issuance — the same class of impact ($950k) as the Nitron incident, applicable to any Obyte AA (including officially-distributed sample/reference AAs) that prices operations from `balance[...]`.

### Likelihood Explanation
High for any deployed AA following the shipped reference pattern: sending a payment to an arbitrary address (including any AA) requires no special privilege, no data payload, and no interaction with the AA's trigger logic — it is a bare `payment` message, fully within reach of "an unprivileged unit poster." The attack requires only two ordinary units (a donation payment, then a normal trigger) and no validator collusion, malicious peer, or leaked key.

### Recommendation
- In the reference/sample AA templates (and in guidance for AA authors), avoid using raw `balance[asset]` for share-price or ratio computations; instead maintain an explicit state variable (e.g. `var['reserve_base']`, `var['reserve_asset']`) that is updated only by the AA's own accounted deposit/withdraw logic, decoupled from unsolicited transfers to the AA address.
- Consider documenting/flagging in oscript validation or AA best-practice tooling that formulas depending on `balance[...]` for pricing are subject to donation manipulation, and provide a safer accounting primitive that AAs can rely on instead of raw address balance.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy an AA based on `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` (or any AA using the same pattern) with initial reserves of `base` and `$asset`.
2. Attacker sends a plain `payment` message with a large amount of `base` directly to the AA address (no `data`, so it doesn't hit the "invest" case by itself if amounts don't line up, or crafted to land right before the attacker's real trigger unit in the same/adjacent MC).
3. Attacker then sends the real "invest" trigger (`trigger.output[[asset=base]] > 1e5 AND trigger.output[[asset=$asset]] > 0`). Because `$bytes_balance = balance[base] - trigger.output[[asset=base]]` now includes the donation, `$current_ratio` and `$issue_amount = round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * var['mm_asset_outstanding'])` are computed off an inflated denominator, letting the attacker mint a disproportionate `$mm_asset` share relative to the `$asset` amount actually contributed, or, in the reverse direction, extract disproportionate reserves on divest.
