### Title
Governance vote weight is based on current balance at count time, not held-through-voting-period balance, enabling flash-balance vote manipulation - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
The `countVotes` function in `main_chain.js` determines the weight of each `system_vote` (used to select `op_list`, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) using the voter address's *current* stable-good byte balance at the moment the vote count executes, rather than the balance the address held while its vote was cast or throughout the counting window. This is structurally the same flaw as the Audius "AUD lending market" report: a party can temporarily acquire a large balance just before the decisive moment, inject an outsized vote weight, and immediately dispose of the funds, with no analog to Audius's `decreaseStakeLockupDuration` vs `votingPeriod + executionDelay` protection.

### Finding Description
`countVotes(conn, mci, subject, ...)` first selects every address that has *ever* cast a `system_vote` for the subject: [1](#0-0) 
It then computes each of those addresses' balances from currently unspent stable-good outputs — a snapshot taken at the moment the counting MCI stabilizes, with no restriction tying the counted balance to when the vote was posted: [2](#0-1) 
These balances are loaded into `voter_balances` and used directly to weight the tally for `op_list` (majority of stake-weighted votes decides the operator/witness list) and for numeric parameters via a stake-weighted median: [3](#0-2) 

There is no requirement, comparable to Audius's `decreaseStakeLockupDuration` being forced larger than `votingPeriod + executionDelay`, that a voter must hold (or be prevented from moving) its balance for any minimum duration before/after voting. `validateInlinePayload`'s `system_vote` validation only checks payload shape (subject/value) and does not impose any stake-lock or balance-freeze condition: [4](#0-3) 

The only mitigating parameter, `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`, only expands the *time window of votes considered* if total participating balance is too small — it does not require that an individual voter's balance be stable/held for that period: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Consequently, an address that registered a `system_vote` at any point in the past (even a stale vote cast with a trivial balance) remains eligible to be counted, and will be counted with whatever balance it happens to hold at the exact moment the counting query runs. An attacker who can briefly acquire a large stable balance (e.g., via a short-term loan/DEX swap for bytes, analogous to borrowing AUD on a lending market) immediately before a `system_vote_count` unit stabilizes can inflate their vote weight, then transfer the funds away right after counting completes.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` votes directly determine the set of order/witness providers used for main-chain stability and unit sequencing (`storage.systemVars.op_list`, later consumed in `readWitnesses`/`getOpList` and used throughout witness-level and best-parent computations): [7](#0-6) 
An attacker who manipulates this vote with a transiently-inflated balance could install colluding/malicious operators into the OP list, which can affect stability determination and cause nodes to disagree on unit validity/stability, or manipulate numeric system parameters (fees, TPS interval/multiplier) that affect network-wide fee economics and throughput — both fall into the accepted impact categories (node disagreement on validity/stability, or a network unable to confirm new units in a fee-broken state).

### Likelihood Explanation
The vote-counting logic runs automatically whenever a `system_vote_count` message stabilizes (deterministically, on every full node), so no cooperation from a hub or witness is needed — this is reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who casts a `system_vote` at low cost and then arranges to hold a large stable balance at the address exactly when the count executes. The main practical constraint is acquiring (even briefly) enough real, stable, confirmed byte balance — analogous to the AUD lending-market prerequisite of sufficient collateral — which could become trivial as byte-denominated lending/DEX products mature, exactly as anticipated in the original report for AUD.

### Recommendation
Bind the counted voter weight to a balance that was actually held for the full voting/observation window, rather than a point-in-time snapshot: e.g., require the address's balance to have been unspent since (or continuously held through) `since_timestamp`, or use the minimum balance observed over the counting window, similar in spirit to Audius's enforced relationship between `decreaseStakeLockupDuration` and `votingPeriod + executionDelay`. Alternatively, weight votes by balance held at the time the `system_vote` unit itself was posted (recorded then) instead of balance queried at count time in `countVotes` (`main_chain.js`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker's address `A` posts one low-cost `system_vote` unit for `subject: "op_list"` (or a numeric subject) at any point — validated purely on message shape (`validation.js` system_vote case), no balance check is performed at vote time.
2. `A` is now permanently listed in `SELECT DISTINCT address FROM system_votes WHERE subject=?` (`main_chain.js:1741`), regardless of its balance when it voted.
3. Shortly before a `system_vote_count` unit is expected to stabilize, the attacker acquires a large stable byte balance at `A` (e.g., via a short-lived loan/DEX swap), so that when `main_chain.js`'s `bal_rows` query runs (lines 1757-1773) at the counting MCI, `A`'s balance dominates `voter_balances`.
4. `countVotes` tallies `op_list`/numeric votes using this inflated `voter_balances` entry (lines 1850-1903), letting the attacker dictate the resulting `op_list` or system parameter.
5. Immediately after the count, the attacker moves the borrowed balance away; the effect on `storage.systemVars` (and consequently on the OP list used for stability) persists.
