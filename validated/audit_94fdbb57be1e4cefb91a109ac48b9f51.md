The interesting analog candidate here is `supply`/`update_account_threshold` restamping a lowered LTV onto a foreign account unconditionally (no HF gate), potentially blocking the owner's withdrawal. Let me check whether withdrawal solvency reads the stored LTV.### Title
Permissionless dust top-up or `update_account_threshold` can silently downgrade a borrower's stored LTV and freeze their withdrawals - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
Each supply position stores its own `loan_to_value`, `liquidation_threshold`, bonus and fee, stamped at creation. When governance lowers an asset's LTV, existing positions keep the old stamp until refreshed. Two unprivileged paths force the refresh onto a foreign account: `update_account_threshold(caller, has_risks = false, [victim])` (zero cost) and a dust `supply` top-up on an asset the victim already holds (~1 stroop). Both reach `refresh_supply_risk_params`, which writes `position.loan_to_value = effective_config.loan_to_value` unconditionally — unlike the liquidation tuple, which is gated on post-update HF >= 1.05 for accounts with debt (`params.rs:35` vs `params.rs:76-88`). Because the post-withdraw/borrow solvency check reads `ltv_collateral`, computed as `min(stored LTV, stored LT) × collateral value` (`risk/totals.rs:95`, `:190`), the attacker can drop a healthy borrower's borrow/withdraw capacity below their debt and make every `withdraw` and `borrow` revert, without ever touching the HF-based liquidation safety check. This mirrors the Cooler `rollLoan` class: an unprivileged caller unilaterally worsens another borrower's loan terms.

### Finding Description
`update_account_threshold` is explicitly permissionless (`lib.rs:385-388`, `params.rs:124-144`): `sync_account_thresholds` loads any `account_id` and calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::LtvOnly`, which writes the new listing LTV with no health-factor gate (`params.rs:35`). The same refresh runs inside `merge_supply_leg` (`positions/supply.rs:288-296`) with `FullTuple`, and third-party `supply` is only restricted to assets the account already holds (`positions/supply.rs:78-97`) — so a stranger can supply a dust amount of the victim's own collateral asset and trigger the ungated LTV restamp as a side effect. In both paths the LT/bonus/fee restamp is correctly gated by `apply_gated_liquidation_params` + `clears_min_hf` (HF >= 1.05), but the LTV write is not gated at all. The solvency invariant enforced after `withdraw`/`borrow` (`enforce_post_pool_solvency`) uses `ltv_collateral = Σ min(LTV, LT) × value`, so lowering the stamped LTV below `debt / collateral` makes all further withdrawals and borrows revert, while `get_health_factor` (which uses LT only) still reports a healthy account and the docs' own justification ("restamps LTV only, which the health factor does not read") understates the effect.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds: the victim cannot withdraw any collateral or borrow until the LTV parameter is raised again, the victim repays enough debt to satisfy the new `ltv_collateral >= debt` bound, or positions are otherwise restructured. For accounts that borrowed close to the old LTV limit, a meaningful LTV reduction can freeze all withdrawals immediately. No collateral or debt is stolen and liquidation eligibility (HF) is unchanged, so this is a Medium-severity availability/griefing analog rather than the Cooler High.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance LTV reduction on a listed spoke asset (timelocked, routine risk maintenance — exactly what the stamped-params design anticipates). Once such an edit lands, anyone can apply it to any account at near-zero cost via `update_account_threshold(caller, false, [ids])` or a one-stroop top-up, before the affected borrowers react. Every impacted account stays frozen for the duration.

### Recommendation
Gate the LTV write the same way the liquidation tuple is gated: in `refresh_supply_risk_params`, when the account has debt and `effective_config.loan_to_value` is lower than `position.loan_to_value`, only apply it if the account's hypothetical solvency (`ltv_collateral` recomputed with the new LTV) still covers debt — or extend `update_account_threshold`'s `has_risks` path so that LTV tightening on indebted accounts requires owner/delegate auth or passes an explicit solvency floor. Alternatively, have third-party `supply` skip the risk restamp entirely for positions it only tops up.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 10,000 USDC (LTV 8000, LT 8000) and borrows 7,000 USD of ETH. `ltv_collateral = 8,000 ≥ debt`; HF = 8,000/7,000 ≈ 1.14. She cannot be liquidated.
2. Governance timelock executes `edit_asset_in_spoke` lowering USDC `ltv` to 5,000 (LT unchanged). Alice's stored stamps still read 8,000; her withdrawals and borrows still work.
3. Bob calls `update_account_threshold(bob, false, [alice])` — permissionless, no tokens needed — or `supply`s 1 stroop of USDC into Alice's account via `require_third_party_existing_supply`. `refresh_supply_risk_params` writes `loan_to_value = 5,000` unconditionally (`params.rs:35`); the LT gate is irrelevant because only LTV moved.
4. Alice's `ltv_collateral` is now `0.5 × 10,000 = 5,000 < 7,000` debt. Every `withdraw` and `borrow` reverts on `enforce_post_pool_solvency`, while `get_health_factor` still shows 1.14 and `is_liquidatable` returns false. Alice's collateral is frozen until governance restores LTV or she repays 2,000+ USD of debt — a freeze Bob triggered alone.
