### Title
Lack of a Vetoer for Balance-Weighted System-Vote Governance Enables Majority Takeover of OP List and Network Parameters - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
`ocore`'s `system_vote` mechanism lets any address change critical network-wide parameters — the Order Provider (OP/witness) list, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier` — via balance-weighted voting counted in `countVotes()`. Unlike Velodrome's governor, which the audit found lacked a vetoer able to cancel a malicious majority proposal, ocore's on-chain governance has no equivalent authority that can veto/cancel a winning vote once `countVotes()` applies it to `system_vars`, and the result is enacted automatically on the very next MCI with no additional confirmation delay.

### Finding Description
`system_vote` messages are validated in [1](#0-0)  and, once stable, their tallying is driven purely by GBYTE balance of voting addresses. `countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` computes `voter_balances`, expands the vote look-back window only until `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE * TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` of balance has participated, and then picks the winner by balance-weighted plurality/median: [2](#0-1) .

For `op_list`, the top-voted addresses (by summed balance) become the new OP list unconditionally, with no separate quorum, cooldown, or veto step: [3](#0-2) . For numerical subjects (`threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`), the balance-weighted median is applied the same way: [4](#0-3) . The result is written straight into `system_vars`, which takes effect on the very next MCI, with no delay for OPs, network participants, or any privileged party to react or override it: [5](#0-4) .

There is also an "emergency" fast-path (`applyEmergencyOpListChange`) that can force-count unstable votes and swap the OP list even before normal stabilization, triggered merely by a `system_vote_count` message once a timeout elapses, again with no veto/cancel mechanism: [6](#0-5) , [7](#0-6) .

This directly parallels the Velodrome finding: governance power is concentrated in whichever address(es) can accumulate/borrow enough GBYTE balance to dominate the voting window, and once the vote is tallied there is no vetoer, council, or time-lock capable of cancelling the outcome before it takes effect — unlike the recommended `veto()`/two-step vetoer pattern the auditors proposed for VeloGovernor.

### Impact Explanation
If an attacker (or colluding set of addresses) can command enough active-voting balance to win the balance-weighted vote — a bar that can be lowered by low participation, since the algorithm only requires reaching `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE` of total supply of *actively participating* balance, not `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE` of the whole supply as an absolute floor guarding against a low-turnout capture — they can:
- Replace the entire OP (witness) list with attacker-controlled addresses, which is used to determine stability of the DAG (governor/witness hijack analogous to "Set governor to an EOA / Hijack it").
- Raise or lower `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, or `tps_fee_multiplier` to disrupt network throughput/fee economics or make recovery harder (analogous to "Raise quorum to make it harder / impossible to catch-up").
- Because there is no vetoer/council able to cancel a bad vote outcome, and the change is enacted the moment `countVotes` runs (no separate execution delay), the network has no built-in circuit breaker once a malicious majority is achieved. This can lead to node disagreement on validity/stability of units (since witness composition drives DAG stability) or a network unable to correctly confirm new units if the attacker-controlled OP set misbehaves.

### Likelihood Explanation
Achieving control requires accumulating (or otherwise controlling) a majority of the *actively voting* GBYTE balance during the relevant look-back window, which is a real but non-trivial economic bar — similar to needing 51% voting power in Nouns-style governance. Given the sliding-window design (`since_timestamp` expands until minimum participation is met), an attacker could exploit periods of low overall voter turnout to reach the effective supermajority with a smaller absolute balance than the full circulating supply. This mirrors Spearbit's observation that transitional/low-activity periods make such governance especially cheap to attack.

### Recommendation
Introduce a vetoer/council role (or equivalent multi-party emergency-cancel mechanism) that can invalidate an in-flight or just-applied `system_vote` outcome (particularly for `op_list`) within a grace window before/after `countVotes()` commits it to `system_vars`, analogous to Velodrome's adopted fix of reusing `_cancel` plus a settable vetoer. Additionally, consider requiring `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE` to be measured against total circulating supply rather than only the currently-participating balance, and add a time-lock between vote tallying and activation of OP-list/parameter changes so that a malicious majority result can be detected and challenged before taking effect.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker accumulates or controls addresses holding a majority of the GBYTE balance that historically participates in `system_vote` for `op_list` within the current look-back window (per the loop in [8](#0-7) ).
2. Attacker's addresses each post a `system_vote` unit with `subject: "op_list"` and `value` containing 12 attacker-controlled addresses, validated per [9](#0-8) .
3. Once these units stabilize, `saveSystemVote()` records the votes and a `system_vote_count` message triggers `countVotes()` at end of MCI processing: [10](#0-9) .
4. `countVotes()` selects the top `COUNT_WITNESSES` OP addresses by summed voter balance — the attacker's set wins by weight — and immediately overwrites `system_vars.op_list`, which becomes the new witness set on the next MCI with no vetoer able to intervene: [11](#0-10) , [5](#0-4) .
5. With attacker-controlled OPs, subsequent stability determination and witnessing of units is now governed by the attacker, enabling disagreement/manipulation of network finality — the ocore analog of the "51% governor takeover" described in the report.
