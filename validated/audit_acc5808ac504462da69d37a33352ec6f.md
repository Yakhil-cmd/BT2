## Analysis

The reported bug class (issuer-controlled pause halting `transfer()`/`transferFrom()` and thereby permanently breaking `repay()`/`liquidate()`/`sweepTo()`) maps cleanly onto Obyte's **asset spending-condition** and **AA payment/bounce** machinery.

### Root cause

An asset in ocore can carry issuer-controlled restrictions that are re-evaluated on *every* transfer, not just at issuance:
- `spender_attested` + a dynamically-updatable `attestors` list (`asset_attestors` message, editable only by the definer) [1](#0-0) 
- `transfer_condition` / `issue_condition`, arbitrary address-definition-style predicates evaluated at spend time [2](#0-1) 
- `is_transferrable` restricting who transfers may involve [3](#0-2) 

These checks are enforced in `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs`, i.e. on every payment message that spends the asset, exactly like an ERC20 `whenNotPaused` modifier that can revert `transfer`/`transferFrom` at any time the issuer chooses: [4](#0-3) 

When an Autonomous Agent tries to pay out such an asset (e.g. returning collateral, repaying a loan, or sweeping funds to a liquidator/depositor), `aa_composer.js`'s `sendUnit` loads the asset info and attempts to complete the payment payload; any failure (spender not attested, condition unsatisfied, private/fixed-denomination asset, etc.) causes the whole trigger to `bounce()`: [5](#0-4) 

`bounce()` discards **all** state changes made by that trigger (`objStateUpdate = null`), which is functionally identical to a Solidity `revert()` — the entire repay/liquidate/withdraw logic executed in that trigger is undone: [6](#0-5) 

### Why this reproduces the ERC20-pause DoS

If an AA (e.g. a lending, AMM, or escrow contract) is designed to accept and later return an arbitrary/user-specified asset as collateral or debt token — a common oscript pattern, see the generic asset template `create_an_asset.oscript` — and that asset was defined (or later modified via `asset_attestors`) with `spender_attested: true`, a `transfer_condition`, or non-trivial `is_transferrable` semantics, then:

1. The asset issuer (an allowed, unprivileged-relative actor per the asset's own definer rights) can revoke an address from the attestor list at any time via `asset_attestors`, which only requires being the asset's `definer_address`: [7](#0-6) 
2. Any subsequent AA attempt to pay that asset out to the now-unattested address (the depositor, borrower, or liquidator) fails validation in `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` and the AA's `sendUnit` bounces the entire trigger: [8](#0-7) 
3. Because bouncing wipes out the whole trigger's effects rather than partially completing, and there is no built-in AA fallback for "pay to an alternate address" or "credit an internal balance for later withdrawal," every repay/liquidate/withdraw attempt for that asset keeps failing indefinitely — the funds are frozen in the AA exactly as USDC/USDT pause freezes funds in the vulnerable Solidity protocol.

This is a direct structural analog of the reported bug: the AA has no way to "try an alternative payout" or defer settlement the way the recommendation in the report suggests, because the AA template language processes payment messages atomically and any one failing payment reverts the entire response.

### Title
Asset issuer-controlled transfer restrictions (`spender_attested`/`attestors`/`transfer_condition`) can permanently DoS AA payouts and freeze collateral/debt funds - (File: aa_composer.js, validation.js)

### Summary
An AA that accepts an arbitrary or user-specified asset (collateral/debt token pattern) and later pays it back out (repay, liquidate, sweep, withdraw) can be permanently denial-of-serviced if the asset's issuer changes the `spender_attested` attestor list, or if the asset carries a `transfer_condition`/`is_transferrable` restriction that the intended recipient no longer satisfies. Because `aa_composer.js`'s `bounce()` discards the entire trigger's state effects on any payment failure, the AA can never complete the payout, and any funds tied to that address/asset combination remain frozen in the AA forever.

### Finding Description
`validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` re-validates `spender_attested`, `transfer_condition`/`issue_condition`, and `is_transferrable` on every spend of an asset, not just at issuance [9](#0-8) . The attestor list backing `spender_attested` can be updated at will by the asset's definer via an `asset_attestors` message [1](#0-0) , effectively acting like an ERC20 "pause" targeted at specific addresses/assets.

When an AA composes a payout message for such an asset, it loads the asset info and calls `completePaymentPayload`; any error (including validation-time failures surfaced later when the resulting unit is submitted, or preemptive checks such as `objAsset.is_private`) causes `cb(err)` to propagate to `bounce(err)` [5](#0-4) . `bounce()` sets `objStateUpdate = null`, discarding every state variable change the trigger made [6](#0-5) , i.e. the whole trigger call (equivalent to a Solidity `revert`) is undone.

There is no AA-level mechanism to gracefully degrade — e.g., credit an internal ledger and let the user claim later, or reroute the payment to a different, attested address — the way the report's recommendations suggest for the Solidity case. This means once an asset used as collateral/debt in an AA becomes "paused" for a given address (attestor revoked, or condition unsatisfiable), that specific repay/liquidate/withdraw flow is permanently unusable.

### Impact Explanation
Funds held by the AA in the affected asset become frozen: neither the depositor nor a liquidator can trigger a successful payout because every attempt to construct the required payment message fails asset-level validation and bounces the entire trigger. This matches the report's "Medium" impact classes: temporary/permanent freezing of funds, denial of service on repay/liquidation-equivalent flows, and potential protocol insolvency risk if the affected asset is meant to be delivered as debt-clearing consideration to a counterparty.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any AA author who builds a lending/escrow/AMM-style contract accepting a caller-specified asset (a natural, encouraged oscript pattern — see `test/samples/create_an_asset.oscript`) is exposed if that asset's issuer later tightens `spender_attested` attestor lists or the AA doesn't statically restrict itself to assets with `is_transferrable: true` and no `spender_attested`/`transfer_condition`. Because the asset issuer is a legitimate, independent actor (not colluding with the AA), and the trigger sender only needs to be an ordinary depositor whose address later gets deattested or fails a condition, likelihood is realistic for any AA that doesn't explicitly whitelist "safe" assets.

### Recommendation
- AA authors should validate, at deposit time, that any accepted asset has `is_transferrable: true`, `spender_attested: false`, and no `transfer_condition`, before accepting it as collateral/debt, or explicitly document/require this.
- Where flexible assets must be supported, the AA design should avoid a single atomic payout message whose failure reverts the entire trigger; instead it should credit an internal balance/state variable that a user can attempt to withdraw repeatedly (retry pattern), rather than relying on the payout succeeding within the same state-mutating trigger.
- Consider documentation/warnings in oscript/AA developer guidance about the systemic risk of the `spender_attested`/`transfer_condition`/`asset_attestors` features being usable to indefinitely block AA payouts of a given asset, similar to ERC20 pause risk.

### Proof of Concept
1. Asset issuer defines asset `X` with `spender_attested: true`, `fixed_denominations: false`, `is_transferrable: true`, and an initial attestor list including address `A` [10](#0-9) .
2. User at address `A` deposits asset `X` into a lending AA that records the deposit in state vars via a trigger.
3. Asset issuer posts an `asset_attestors` message removing `A` from the attestor list [1](#0-0) .
4. User (or a liquidator) sends a trigger asking the AA to repay/return asset `X` to address `A`.
5. `aa_composer.js` builds the payment message, `loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors`/subsequent validation determines `A` is no longer attested, causing the payment/unit to fail; `sendUnit` calls `bounce(err)` [5](#0-4) , discarding the trigger's state effects [6](#0-5) .
6. Every subsequent repay/liquidate attempt for that user/asset repeats the same bounce — funds remain permanently locked in the AA.
