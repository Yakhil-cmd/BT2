### Title
Pool `repay`/`recapitalize` refund the *declared* excess out of real custody without verifying tokens were ever transferred in — (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The external report's bug class is "an excess/fee refund is pushed to an address that cannot properly receive it, breaking the flow." In XOXNO Lending the same class — an **excess-payment refund computed from a declared amount rather than from verified inbound funds** — appears in the pool's `repay` and `recapitalize` legs, but with a worse outcome than a revert: the refund is paid out of the pool's real token custody even when the caller transferred nothing, letting an unprivileged address drain supplier funds.

### Finding Description
`ops::repay::apply` computes an `overpayment` as `action.amount − min(amount, debt)` via `cache.resolve_repay` and then calls `cache.transfer_out(payer, overpayment)`, sending pool-held tokens back to `payer` (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:25-33, 44-47). The refund is derived purely from the declared `action.amount`; nothing verifies that `amount` tokens actually arrived at the pool. The doc comment states the invariant — "The controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call" (repay.rs:3) — but it is not enforced on-chain, and the refund neither debits `cash` nor passes a reserve check. When the market has no debt, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, `net_repay` is zero, the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct (repay.rs:48-52), and the entire declared amount is refunded. `ops::recapitalize::apply` has the identical shape: `refund = amount − min(amount, backing_shortfall)` is paid to `payer` from custody (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52-55, 34). The in-repo test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` demonstrates a `repay` call with zero inbound transfer refunding `custody_before` to a fresh payer while the cash book is untouched (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3611). The controller's prefund path (`apply_repay_batch` → `pool_repay_call`, contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:209-218) is what makes this safe in the intended flow, but the pool entrypoint itself does not gate on the measured inbound balance.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. An attacker calls `repay` (or `recapitalize` on a market with no backing shortfall) with a declared `amount` equal to the pool's token balance, transfers nothing, and receives the full amount as a "refund" paid from supplier custody. Repeatable across markets until custody is empty; the cash/borrowed books stay consistent, so the loss is silent.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium–High. It requires no privileged role, no timing, and no oracle manipulation — a single call with a large declared amount on any market whose outstanding debt is below pool custody. The only mitigation would be an on-chain check tying the refund to a measured balance delta (as `transfer_amount_measured` does in the controller's liquidation path, contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/apply.rs:58-65), which is absent here. Uncertainty: whether `Pool::repay` additionally requires controller auth in deployment; the test invokes it directly with a generated payer address, indicating it is externally callable, but I could not confirm the exact auth gate in `lib.rs` within the available iterations.

### Recommendation
In `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply`, cap the refundable excess to the measured inbound transfer (record the pool's token balance before/after, or accept only a `transfer_in`-verified amount), or require the refund to be debited from `cash`/pass `require_reserves` so an unfunded refund cannot be paid out of suppliers' principal. Alternatively restrict these entrypoints to the controller, which is the only component that prefunds the declared amount.

### Proof of Concept
See `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611`: on a market with `borrowed == 0` and `cash == custody`, calling `client.repay(&payer, &[action(amount = custody_before)])` credits `actual_amount = 0` and transfers `custody_before` of the real asset to `payer`, leaving the book untouched (`assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody`). The same path exists in `test_bulk_repay_overpayment_refunds_second_entry_surplus` (flows.rs:2825-2871), where a declared overpayment is refunded to the payer. /// Accrues interest, resolves the repay amount into burned debt shares and
/// overpayment, burns the shares, and credits the net repay to cash without
/// transferring the overpayment refund. Panics if a positive net repay would
/// burn zero scaled shares.
pub(crate) fn accounting(env: &Env, action: &PoolAction) -> RepayOutcome {
    let (mut cache, position) = ops::load_leg(env, action);
    let amount = action.amount;

    let (burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position);
    let net_repay = amount
        .checked_sub(overpayment)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));

/// Sizes and books the cash injection without transferring tokens.
///
/// Credits `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to cash and commits. `refund` is
/// `amount - applied`.
pub(crate) fn accounting(
    env: &Env,
    hub_asset: HubAssetKey,
    amount: i128,
) -> RecapitalizationOutcome {
    require_nonneg_amount(env, amount);
    let mut cache = ops::renewed_market(env, &hub_asset);

    let applied = amount.min(guards::backing_shortfall(&cache));
    let refund = amount
        .checked_sub(applied)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));

/// The refund gap is not specific to `recapitalize`. Two pool legs refund an
/// excess derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book:
/// `ops::recapitalize::apply` (the excess over the shortfall) and
/// `ops::repay::apply` (the excess over the debt). Neither refund debits `cash`
/// or passes `require_reserves`.
///
/// A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an
/// overpayment: `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the
/// full-close branch and `net_repay` is zero. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`
/// assert in `ops::repay::accounting` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct,
/// and the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched.
#[test]
fn test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    let token = token::Client::new(&t.env, &t.asset);
    let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);

    let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);
    let before = t.state_snapshot();
    assert_eq!(
        before.cash, custody_before,
        "fixture guard: book and custody must start in sync"
    );
    assert_eq!(before.borrowed, 0, "fixture must carry no debt");

    // Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
    let credited = t
        .client()
        .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
        .get_unchecked(0)
        .actual_amount;
    assert_eq!(credited, 0, "no debt was retired, so nothing is credited");
    assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
}

/// Repays prefunded actions and merges the pool results. The pool refunds
/// excess to `payer`; returns the mutations.
pub(crate) fn apply_repay_batch(
    env: &Env,
    account: &mut Account,
    payer: &Address,
    action: events::PositionAction,
    actions: &Vec<PoolAction>,
    cache: &mut Context,
) -> Vec<PoolPositionMutation> {
    let pool_addr = cache.cached_pool_address();
    let results = pool_repay_call(env, &pool_addr, payer, actions);
    for_each_leg(env, actions, &results, |entry, result| {
        merge_debt_leg(
            env,
            account,
            action,
            &entry.hub_asset,
            LegDirection::Exit,
            &LegOutcome::from(&result),
            cache,
        );
    });
    results
}
