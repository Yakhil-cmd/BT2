### Title
Rounding-down of `remaining-debt-to-repay` causes full collateral seizure via the "dust" fallback in `liquidate` - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar)

### Summary
`liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar` computes a capped, partial collateral seizure (`coll-final-raw`) and then decides whether the liquidation should instead take **all** of the borrower's collateral by checking whether the theoretical debt that could be repaid against the leftover collateral (`remaining-debt-to-repay`) rounds down to `u0`. Because `remaining-debt-to-repay` is derived through a chain of `mul-div-down` operations, small leftover collateral amounts can legitimately round to zero even though they represent real, non-dust value, causing the liquidator to seize the borrower's entire collateral balance instead of the intended, bounded partial amount — the exact "amountPerInterval rounds down, but the code should recognize full completion" failure mode from the report, except here the incorrect full/partial branch benefits the liquidator at the borrower's direct expense.

### Finding Description
In `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`, `liquidate` first computes a bounded, penalty-adjusted collateral amount to seize (`coll-final-raw`) via `scale-debt-for-liquidation` [1](#0-0) . It then computes how much of the *remaining* collateral (`coll-remaining = user-coll-balance - coll-final-raw`) could still be liquidated at the maximum penalty, and stores that in `remaining-debt-to-repay`: [2](#0-1) 

The rounding chain — `normalize`, `div-bps-down`, `mul-div-down` (twice), then `mul-div-up` — rounds toward zero at multiple stages before the final `mul-div-up`. When `coll-remaining` is small (e.g., a few base units of a high-decimal token, or a low-value asset), every intermediate division can floor to `u0`, making `remaining-debt-to-repay` evaluate to `u0` even though `coll-remaining` itself is `> u0` (non-zero, real collateral).

The code then treats `remaining-debt-to-repay == u0` as a signal that "no further debt is repayable against the leftover collateral," and short-circuits to seizing the **entire** collateral balance instead of the intended, bounded amount: [3](#0-2) 

This is structurally identical to the reported bug class: a floor-division used to gate a boundary condition ("is vesting complete?" / "is remaining debt repayable?") produces a false negative due to rounding, and the code's remediation branch (seize-all / vest-all) fires when it should not, transferring more value than intended from one principal (the borrower) to another (the liquidator).

`scale-debt-for-liquidation` already caps `coll-final-raw` at `coll-actual`/`curr-scaled` specifically to bound the liquidator's seizure to the debt actually calculated as repayable [4](#0-3) ; the `remaining-debt-to-repay == u0` fallback undermines that bound whenever rounding produces a false-zero.

### Impact Explanation
This directly harms the borrower (an unprivileged principal) for the direct benefit of the liquidator (a different unprivileged principal): the liquidator seizes collateral beyond the amount their `debt-to-repay` and `liq-penalty` justify, and the market simultaneously treats the position as fully liquidated (`no-collateral-left`), potentially triggering the bad-debt-socialization path prematurely against the supplier pool even though the true remaining collateral had (rounded-away) value. This is a seizure exceeding its intended bound — theft of collateral funds at rest belonging to the borrower — falling under the Critical impact class (direct theft of user funds other than unclaimed yield).

### Likelihood Explanation
The condition is reachable on any ordinary `liquidate` call, not just adversarial ones: any liquidator who partially liquidates a position such that the leftover collateral value, once discounted by `liq-penalty-max` and converted through the debt asset's price/decimals and scaled-index math, floors to zero at any of the four sequential divisions, triggers the full-seizure branch. Low-value/high-decimal collateral tokens or debt tokens with large price/decimals ratios increase the likelihood of hitting this rounding-to-zero boundary in normal operation, and a liquidator could deliberately choose `debt-amount` to land exactly in this rounding gap to maximize seized collateral.

### Recommendation
Do not use `remaining-debt-to-repay == u0` as a proxy for "no collateral value remains." Instead, gate the full-seizure branch on `coll-remaining == u0` (i.e., only take all collateral when the bounded calculation already consumed 100% of the balance), or round `remaining-debt-to-repay`'s intermediate divisions up/conservatively so that any non-zero `coll-remaining` cannot be discounted away to a zero debt-repayable value. This mirrors the report's fix pattern: check the underlying quantity (`milestone.allocation`/`coll-remaining`) directly for completion rather than relying on a rounded derived quantity.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower has a position eligible for partial liquidation (`current-ltv` between `ltv-liq-partial` and `ltv-liq-full`).
2. Liquidator calls `liquidate` with a `debt-amount` sized so that the computed `coll-final-raw` leaves a small `coll-remaining` (e.g., a few base units of the collateral asset).
3. In the `remaining-debt-to-repay` computation, `normalize`, `div-bps-down`, and the two `mul-div-down` calls each floor toward zero; with the chosen inputs the final `mul-div-up rem-scaled rem-borrow-index INDEX-PRECISION` evaluates to `u0` because `rem-scaled` itself was floored to `u0` upstream.
4. `coll-final` is then set to `user-coll-balance` (all collateral) instead of `coll-final-raw` (the bounded amount) [3](#0-2) .
5. The liquidator receives the borrower's full collateral balance for a `debt-to-repay` that was only sized to justify the smaller `coll-final-raw`, and `no-collateral-left` becomes true, potentially triggering socialize-debt-asset prematurely for the shared supplier pool.

;; Scale debt for storage and calculate final execution amounts
;; Converts to scaled units, caps at current debt, calculates final collateral
;; Returns: { scaled-to-remove: uint, debt-to-repay: uint, coll-final: uint }
(define-private (scale-debt-for-liquidation
  (debt-final uint)
  (coll-actual uint)
  (curr-scaled uint)
  (asset-id uint))
  (let (;; convert debt amount to scaled units for storage
        (borrow-index (get index (unwrap-panic (get-cached-indexes asset-id))))
        (scaled-debt (mul-div-down debt-final INDEX-PRECISION borrow-index))
        ;; cap at current debt (prevent over-repayment)
        (scaled-to-remove (if (> scaled-debt curr-scaled) curr-scaled scaled-debt))
        (debt-to-repay (mul-div-up scaled-to-remove borrow-index INDEX-PRECISION))
        ;; If debt was capped, scale collateral proportionally
        (coll-final (if (< scaled-to-remove scaled-debt)
                        (mul-div-down coll-actual scaled-to-remove scaled-debt)
                        coll-actual)))
    {
      scaled-to-remove: scaled-to-remove,
      debt-to-repay: debt-to-repay,
      coll-final: coll-final
    }))
