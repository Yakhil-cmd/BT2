### Title
System-parameter vote counting uses current balance instead of a snapshot at vote-cast time - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` tallies `system_vote` messages (subject `op_list`, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) by weighting each voting address with its **current** stable byte balance at the moment the count runs, not the balance it held when it cast its vote. This mirrors the reported bug class exactly: voting weight is read live instead of being fixed at the time the governance action (here, the vote message) was posted, allowing balance to be inflated right before the count and withdrawn right after.

### Finding Description
When a `system_vote` message stabilizes, `saveSystemVote()` records only `unit, address, subject, value, timestamp` into `system_votes`/`op_votes`/`numerical_votes` — no balance is captured at cast time. [1](#0-0) 

The actual weighting happens later, inside `countVotes()`, which is invoked whenever a `system_vote_count` message stabilizes for a subject (i.e., triggered by any party, not necessarily the voter): [2](#0-1) 

`countVotes()` computes each voter's weight as their **current** stable, unspent balance at the mci being processed: [3](#0-2) 

This balance is then joined against the historical vote records (`op_votes`/`numerical_votes`, filtered only by a `since_timestamp` window) to compute the winning `op_list` or numerical parameter value: [4](#0-3) 

Because the balance lookup is performed at count time rather than at each vote's timestamp, a voter can:
1. Cast a `system_vote` while holding a small balance (satisfying no minimum-balance requirement at cast time).
2. Wait for (or trigger) a `system_vote_count` message to stabilize.
3. Ensure a large balance is present in their address at the exact mci `countVotes()` scans, then move the funds away immediately afterward.

Their old vote — cast at a completely different time and balance — is credited with the inflated weight, exactly as described in the external report where `castVote()` read live voting power instead of a proposal-time snapshot.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` controls the **operator/witness set**, which determines main-chain stability and unit validity ordering; `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier` are consensus-critical network parameters. A manipulated vote can:
- Elect malicious or colluding witnesses into `op_list`, directly affecting node agreement on stability/validity.
- Push `base_tps_fee`/`tps_fee_multiplier`/`tps_interval` to values that price out or block ordinary transactions, impairing the network's ability to confirm new units.

This satisfies the "node disagreement on validity or stability" and "network unable to confirm new units" impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any address can post a `system_vote` at any time with a small balance and is not required to keep that balance until counting. `system_vote_count` triggers are not scheduled deterministically relative to any individual voter's balance, so an attacker fully controls the timing of when they temporarily hold a large balance versus when a count happens to run (they can even be the one to post the `system_vote_count` message themselves once their balance is inflated). No mechanism ties the counted weight to the voter's balance at cast time.

### Recommendation
Snapshot the voter's relevant balance at the time the `system_vote` message stabilizes (in `saveSystemVote()`), storing it alongside `unit, address, subject, value, timestamp` in `system_votes`/`op_votes`/`numerical_votes`. Then have `countVotes()` sum these stored snapshot balances instead of re-querying live balances at count time, consistent with how other time-sensitive consensus values (e.g., stable balances, MC timestamps) are captured once and reused.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker address A posts a `system_vote` unit voting `op_list` (or `base_tps_fee`, etc.) while holding a small balance (e.g., 1 byte above dust) — recorded via `saveSystemVote()` with no balance capture.
2. A waits until any `system_vote_count` message for that subject is about to stabilize (or posts one themselves).
3. Just before the mci that will trigger `countVotes()` becomes stable, A receives a large stable payment into the voting address.
4. `countVotes()` runs, queries `outputs` for A's current stable/unspent balance (large), and weights A's previously-cast vote with this inflated balance: [3](#0-2) 
5. A immediately spends/moves the large balance elsewhere.
6. The result: A's old, small-balance-backed vote decisively swings `op_list`/parameter selection, despite holding negligible balance both before and after the count.
