# Title
Vote tallying uses instantaneous balance snapshots rather than balance held over the voting window, allowing a front-run flash-stake to seize outsized governance weight - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` tallies `system_vote` messages (op_list, threshold_size, base_tps_fee, tps_interval, tps_fee_multiplier) by reading each voting address's **current** stable balance at the moment the count executes, not the balance the address actually held while its vote was cast or throughout the counting window. This is structurally the same bug class as the Reserve report: a reward/weight-bearing accounting value (`payoutLastPaid` / stRSR balance there, voter balance here) is evaluated at a single "unfreeze"/tally instant instead of being time-weighted, letting anyone acquire the underlying asset right before that instant and receive full credit for it.

### Finding Description
`countVotes(conn, mci, subject, ...)` is invoked whenever an mci that carries a `system_vote_count` message becomes stable, i.e. it is reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who submits that app message. It reads the addresses that have cast a `system_vote` for the subject and joins them against the *live* stable-good balance: [1](#0-0) 

The tally then uses this instantaneous `voter_balances` table to weight the vote outcome, for both the `op_list` case: [2](#0-1) 

and the numeric-parameter median case: [3](#0-2) 

The only time-based filter is `since_timestamp`, which restricts *which addresses'* votes are counted (they must have voted within the last N years), not how much balance those addresses held while voting or during the window: [4](#0-3) 

Because the weight is read at count time rather than accrued/held over the voting period, an address that cast a `system_vote` at any earlier point (even with a trivial balance) can have its weight inflated arbitrarily by moving a large balance into it immediately before the vote-count unit stabilizes, and moving the funds back out immediately afterward. This is directly analogous to staking RSR right before `unfreeze()` to capture a reward accrued over the whole freeze period without having borne any of the associated risk/time — here, an attacker captures full governance weight without having held the balance during the period other honest voters were exposed.

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` votes determine the witness/order-provider set, and the numeric votes control consensus-relevant parameters (`threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`). Manipulating these via a flash-balance maneuver lets an attacker with temporarily borrowed/rented capital swing the elected witness list or fee parameters that the whole network depends on for validity/stability determination, i.e. an outcome in the "node disagreement on validity or stability" / "network unable to confirm new units" impact category, achieved with far less capital-at-risk than genuine long-term stakeholders.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering the count only requires posting a `system_vote_count` unit, which is reachable by any unit poster, and the balance manipulation only requires a temporary transfer of funds into a previously-voted address before that unit stabilizes and reversing it afterward — no special privilege or timing beyond normal DAG confirmation delay is needed. Because vote counts happen at a difficult-to-predict but attacker-triggerable moment (posting `system_vote_count` yourself), the attacker fully controls the timing, making the attack straightforward to execute once identified as advantageous (e.g., during an OP-list election).

### Recommendation
Weight votes by a time-weighted or minimum-holding-period balance (e.g., balance the address held continuously since it cast its vote, or an average/minimum balance over the counting window) rather than the balance read at the instant of counting. Alternatively, require that only balance present in the account for at least the full `since_timestamp` window (or since the individual vote's own timestamp) be counted, mirroring the fix pattern used for the Reserve bug (settle/snapshot at each relevant point rather than let value accrue silently and get harvested all at once).

### Proof of Concept
1. Address A casts a `system_vote` for `op_list` (or a numeric subject) with a small balance, early in the voting window, satisfying `since_timestamp` inclusion.
2. Shortly before the attacker (or anyone) posts a `system_vote_count` unit that will trigger `countVotes()`, a large amount of bytes is transferred into address A.
3. `countVotes()` stabilizes, reads A's balance via the `bal_rows` query at that instant, and credits A's full current balance toward the `op_list`/numeric tally, even though A never held that balance during most of the voting period. [1](#0-0) 
4. Immediately after stabilization, the funds are moved out of A again, at the cost of nothing but transfer fees and brief float, having swung the governance outcome (e.g., electing a favorable witness set or fee parameter) disproportionately to the attacker's momentary capital.
