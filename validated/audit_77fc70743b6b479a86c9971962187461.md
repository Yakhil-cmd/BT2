### Title
Unchecked RAY debt accrual permanently bricks an over-scale market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
A large borrower can push total debt value past `i128::MAX` while the borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The next accrual panics before `last_timestamp` advances, so every market operation repeats the same overflowing accrual and the market becomes unusable. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
Every pool mutation loads the market and calls `interest::global_sync` before applying the operation. [3](#0-2)  `global_sync` calls `accrue_step` and only advances `last_timestamp` after all accrual chunks complete. [4](#0-3) 

Inside `calculate_supplier_rewards`, the scaled borrow balance is multiplied by both the old and new borrow indexes. [5](#0-4)  `Ray::mul` calls `mul_div_half_up`, which panics with `MathOverflow` when the resulting debt value cannot fit in `i128`. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The borrow-index cap does not prevent this state: `update_borrow_index` caps only `new_index`, while `calculate_supplier_rewards` subsequently computes the unbounded absolute debt value `borrowed * new_index`. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

An unprivileged user can create the precondition through ordinary controller calls:

1. `supply` a very large amount of a high-decimal debt asset.
2. `supply` sufficient collateral on their account.
3. `borrow` a large share of that market.
4. Submit public `update_indexes` after enough interest accumulates.

`Controller::update_indexes` authenticates only the caller and forwards the selected `HubAssetKey` list to the pool. [10](#0-9)  The pool then loads each market and runs `global_sync`. [11](#0-10) 

The repository contains an explicit regression demonstrating this boundary: a billion-unit, 18-decimal market borrowed at high utilization reaches `MathOverflow` before the borrow-index cap, after which both withdrawal and repayment fail. [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
The affected `(hub_id, asset)` market cannot process `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue`, or `update_indexes`, because each pool mutation performs the overflowing accrual before executing its own logic. [3](#0-2) [13](#0-12) 

Since `last_timestamp` is updated only after accrual completes, the failed call does not advance the market past the overflowing interval. [4](#0-3)  Subsequent calls therefore hit the same overflow indefinitely rather than converging on a bounded index. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay or be liquidated, bad-debt cleanup cannot run, and pool cash associated with that market remains inaccessible. This is at least a persistent freezing of user funds and a market-wide denial of service.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends on a listed market supporting a large enough token quantity and the rate model producing sufficient index growth before other limits intervene. [14](#0-13)  Creating the state does not require privileged access: it uses ordinary supply, collateralized borrowing, and public index accrual. [15](#0-14) [10](#0-9) 

The required capital is substantial and governance-set supply/borrow caps may prevent it on low-cap assets. Where caps and token supply permit the required scale, however, the final trigger is a single unprivileged `update_indexes` transaction.

### Recommendation
Bound market exposure against the maximum representable accrued debt, not merely against the borrow index.

- When listing a market or changing caps, derive a conservative maximum borrow principal such that `scaled_borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` remains below `i128::MAX`.
- Re-check the bound in `borrow`, `create_strategy`, `flash_position`, `multiply`, and migrations that mint debt.
- Alternatively, rework accrual to use a checked intermediate representation and handle representability saturation without poisoning `last_timestamp`; simply clamping `new_total_debt` must not silently undercharge borrowers or break conservation.
- Add a regression that creates the state through public controller entrypoints and verifies repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, bad-debt cleanup, and recapitalization remain callable at the configured worst-case boundary.
