### Title
Stale `Account.owner` grants the NFT seller retained spending authority after a position transfer - (File: contracts/controller/src/account.rs)

### Summary

The NestJS trailing-slash advisory is an authorization bypass caused by two different route identifiers (`/resource` vs `/resource/`) resolving to the same protected handler while the middleware only guards one spelling. The same shape exists in the controller: an account has two ownership identifiers — the live position-NFT owner and the `Account.owner` field stored at creation — and the spending-authority guard consults only the stale one.

### Finding Description

`create_account` stores `owner` inside the `Account` struct at mint time in `contracts/controller/src/account.rs:64-70`. `is_owner_or_delegate` at `account.rs:121` then authorizes spending with `caller == owner`, where `owner` is that stored field, and `require_owner_or_delegate` (`account.rs:130`) is what `process_withdraw` calls before any collateral leaves (`positions/supply.rs:150`) — the same pattern covers `borrow`, `multiply`, `flash_position`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, and `migrate_from_blend` via `load_or_create_account` (`account.rs:101-110`).

Nothing updates `Account.owner` on an NFT transfer. `position-nft` is the stock OpenZeppelin enumerable; its `transfer`/`transfer_from` (`contracts/position-nft`) only require `from`/spender auth and have no hook into the controller, and no controller entrypoint restamps `Account.owner`. Meanwhile `require_account_owner` (`account.rs:143-148`) separately consults `storage::account_owner` (the NFT owner) — the existence of two owner sources confirms the fields can diverge.

So after the victim transfers or sells their position NFT to a buyer:

- The **new NFT owner fails** `caller == account.owner` and is not in the delegate set keyed under the old owner (`get_delegates(env, account_id, owner)` at `account.rs:126`), so every owner-gated flow reverts for them — their collateral is frozen.
- The **previous owner still passes** `caller == account.owner` and can call `withdraw`/`borrow`/`swap_collateral`/`repay_debt_with_collateral` on the account, draining collateral and minting debt against the buyer's position. The previous owner's delegates also remain active.

### Impact Explanation

Permanent freezing of the buyer's funds plus direct theft: the seller (or an old delegate) can withdraw all collateral to themselves or borrow to the solvency limit and let the debt sit on the account. This meets the theft-of-user-funds and permanent-freezing impact criteria, reachable by any unprivileged address that ever owned or was delegated on the account.

### Likelihood Explanation

A position transfer is an in-scope, expected operation (`position-nft::transfer` is a declared caller-auth entrypoint). Any OTC sale, secondary-market trade, or even a user rotating wallets arms the condition. The attack requires no timing, no oracle assumptions, and no privileged role — just calling `withdraw` after the transfer settles.

### Recommendation

Do not keep a second ownership record. Either drop `Account.owner` and have `is_owner_or_delegate`/`require_owner_or_delegate` resolve the current NFT owner via `storage::account_owner` (keying delegates to `account_id` alone, or to the resolved owner), or add a controller-invoked sync step executed by `position-nft` transfer hooks. Delegate grants should additionally be invalidated on transfer, since grants keyed to the seller would otherwise keep authorizing them under the new owner record.

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice calls `supply(caller=alice, account_id=0, spoke_id=S, assets=[(usdc, 1000e7)])` → account 1 created, `Account.owner = alice`, NFT 1 minted to Alice.
2. Alice calls `position_nft.transfer(alice, bob, 1)` — succeeds; NFT owner is now Bob; `Account.owner` remains `alice`.
3. Bob calls `withdraw(caller=bob, account_id=1, withdrawals=[(usdc, 0)], to=None)` → `require_owner_or_delegate` compares `bob == account.owner(alice)` → `NotAuthorized`. Bob's collateral is frozen.
4. Alice calls `withdraw(caller=alice, account_id=1, withdrawals=[(usdc, 0)], to=Some(alice))` → `alice == account.owner` passes → pool pays Alice the full position. Bob owns a worthless NFT.

Caveat: this finding assumes no code path restamps `Account.owner` after mint; `storage::get_account`/`account_owner` internals were not fully verified in the available passes. If `get_account` re-resolves the NFT owner into `Account.owner`, the bug does not exist — but `create_account` persisting `owner` in the stored struct, and `require_account_owner` consulting a separate source, indicate it does not.
