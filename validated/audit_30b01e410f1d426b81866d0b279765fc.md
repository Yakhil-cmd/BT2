The premise is factually incorrect: `get-available-assets` at [1](#0-0)  computes `(- (var-get assets) (var-get total-borrowed))` — it reads the exact same `assets` variable that `redeem` gates on, not a "different quantity" reflecting "real liquidity" via a balance query. There is no separate raw-balance read being smuggled in.

`deposit` at [2](#0-1)  updates `assets` via `(var-set assets (+ current-assets amount))` after receiving underlying funds, which is the normal, expected bookkeeping update every deposit performs — not a bug-induced "priming" of shared state. `redeem` at [3](#0-2)  reads `current-assets` and calls `get-available-assets` consistently against the same `assets`/`total-borrowed` vars, so there is no divergence between what deposit writes and what get-available-assets (or redeem) reads.

Since a deposit increasing `assets` and thus `get-available-assets` for subsequent callers is exactly the ordinary, intended shared-pool accounting (liquidity increasing as deposits occur), this falls under explicitly out-of-scope "ordinary shared-pool economics" per the rules, and no divergent-quantity defect exists to support the claimed insolvency invariant break.
