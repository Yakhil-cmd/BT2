### Title

Batch `claim_revenue` reverts entirely when any one market is above its utilization cap, letting a borrower grief revenue collection for all markets — (File: contracts/controller/src/markets.rs)

### Summary

`controller.claim_revenue(caller, assets)` iterates the supplied `Vec<HubAssetKey>` and calls `pool.claim_revenue` for each market in a single atomic transaction (`markets.rs:133-136`). The pool reverts `claim_revenue` with `UtilizationAboveMax` (127) when a market's utilization exceeds `max_utilization`, and with `PoolInsolvent` (123) on a backing shortfall (contracts/pool/README.md:148). There is no no-throw/per-market isolation: one failing market aborts revenue claims for every other market in the batch. An unprivileged borrower can push a market over its utilization cap, so any batched claim that includes it — e.g. the `claimRevenueAll` ops flow — reverts until the borrower repays, temporarily freezing unclaimed protocol yield.

### Finding Description

In `markets::claim_revenue`, each leg is executed inline:

```rust
// contracts/controller/src/markets.rs:133-136
for hub_asset in assets {
    let amount = claim_revenue_for_asset(env, &caller, &hub_asset, &mut cache);
    results.push_back(amount);
}
```

`claim_revenue_for_asset` calls `pool_claim_revenue_call` (markets.rs:184) and then measures and forwards the receipt to the accumulator (markets.rs:186-196). Because Soroban execution is atomic, a panic in any `pool_claim_revenue_call` rolls back every earlier leg of the same call — identical in shape to the exchange-libs batch-fill griefing: a batched operation with no no-throw variant where a single maliciously-poisoned leg voids the whole batch.

The poisoning lever is permissionless. `max_utilization` is only enforced at borrow entry (`UtilizationAboveMax` on `borrow`, pool README:140); utilization continues to drift upward afterward through `interest::global_sync` accrual. A borrower who draws utilization to just under `max_utilization` and then simply holds the debt lets accrued interest push the market past the cap. From that point `pool.claim_revenue` for that market reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax`, so:

- `controller.claim_revenue([..., poisoned_market, ...])` reverts entirely — revenue in *healthy* markets listed alongside is unclaimable through the batch path.
- The documented `claimRevenueAll` operational flow (configs/script.sh:4844, which builds the asset list from all configured markets) is hard-blocked while the market sits above the cap.

The same structure applies to `update_indexes`/`pool.accrue` (ops/market.rs:68-72 loops `Cache::load` + `global_sync` per asset with no failure isolation) — any market whose accrual panics (e.g., `MathOverflow`) aborts the whole keeper index-refresh batch, though the attacker-controlled trigger there is weaker.

### Impact Explanation

Temporary freezing of unclaimed yield: protocol revenue across all markets bundled with the poisoned market cannot be claimed and forwarded to the accumulator for as long as utilization stays above the cap. The attacker pays only ordinary borrow interest to sustain the grief; the cost to the protocol is blocked fee collection and forced per-market workarounds. Severity is bounded because revenue can still be claimed by submitting a batch that excludes the poisoned market — the freeze applies to the standard batched path, not to per-market calls.

### Likelihood Explanation

Requires a market configured with `max_utilization < RAY` and enough liquidity for the attacker to borrow to the cap boundary. The attacker then does nothing — accrual alone crosses the threshold. No privileged access, oracle manipulation, or timing race is needed. Medium likelihood; the impact is a grief (no direct theft), consistent with the source bug class.

### Recommendation

Give each batch leg its own failure domain: for `claim_revenue`, wrap the per-market claim in a child-contract-call boundary that can tolerate a failed market (e.g., emit a skipped-market result of 0 instead of propagating the panic), or split `claim_revenue` calls so the pool-level guard failure does not cascade. Longer term, treat per-leg panics in any multi-entry loop (`claim_revenue`, `update_indexes`/`accrue`, `seize_positions` batches) as an expected condition and record per-leg outcomes rather than reverting the batch.

### Proof of Concept

1. Market M is configured with `max_utilization < RAY` (e.g., 0.9 RAY per `params_with_max_util`, certora README:112).
2. Attacker supplies collateral in market A and borrows in M until utilization is just below `max_utilization` (`borrow` passes the `UtilizationAboveMax` gate at entry only).
3. Interest accrual raises utilization above `max_utilization` — `pool.claim_revenue(M)` now reverts `UtilizationAboveMax` (pool README:148).
4. Keeper/operator calls `controller.claim_revenue(caller, [X, Y, M, Z])`; the loop at `markets.rs:133` reaches M, `pool_claim_revenue_call` panics, and the entire transaction reverts — claims for X, Y, Z are rolled back.
5. The batch remains unexecutable until the attacker repays or utilization otherwise drops; only per-market batches excluding M succeed.

Uncertainty: I did not read the exact pool-side `claim_revenue`/`guards` code lines that emit `UtilizationAboveMax` on the revenue-claim path; that behavior is asserted from the documented error table in contracts/pool/README.md:148 rather than the guard source, so the precise condition under which the claim is blocked should be confirmed against `contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs` and `guards.rs`.
