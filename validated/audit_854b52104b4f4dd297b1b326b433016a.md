### Title
Unprivileged third-party `supply` top-up keeps an insolvent account's collateral above the dust threshold and permanently blocks `clean_bad_debt` - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
Teller's `commitCollateral` let anyone append collateral to an already-active bid, breaking the later withdraw path. The XOXNO Lending analog is the permissionless third-party `supply` path: any address can top up an asset position a foreign account already holds. For an insolvent account whose residual collateral sits at or below the dust threshold — exactly the precondition for `clean_bad_debt` — an attacker can donate a dust-plus-epsilon top-up, push the remaining collateral value above the threshold, and make every `clean_bad_debt` call revert. Bad debt then cannot be socialized and the residual collateral stays locked in the pool.

### Finding Description
`supply` is deliberately permissionless. After `require_authorized_caller`, `process_supply` loads the target account and applies `require_third_party_existing_supply`, which only restricts a non-owner/non-delegate caller to hub assets the account already supplies — it does not restrict *when* or *how much* may be added [1](#0-0) . The deposit itself is a measured transfer, so the attacker does pay real tokens — but only dust [2](#0-1) .

`clean_bad_debt` is likewise permissionless but gated: it socializes the account's residual debt only "once its remaining collateral is at or below the dust threshold", with the owner-gated `force_socialize_bad_debt` as the only path that omits the cap [3](#0-2) . The lifecycle doc confirms cleanup runs after liquidation leaves sub-dust collateral [4](#0-3) . Because liquidation of dust collateral is intentionally uneconomical (that is the purpose of the dust gate), there is no permissionless fallback once the gate is pushed out of reach — mirroring the Teller bug where `withdraw` on the escrow had no fallback once a phantom collateral entry was committed.

Note: I verified the gate semantics from the documented entrypoint contract and invariant registry, not from the `clean_bad_debt` function body itself (the grep did not return its implementation lines). The reported behavior assumes the dust check reads live collateral value, as documented.

### Impact Explanation
Every `clean_bad_debt` invocation on the targeted insolvent account reverts while the donated top-up holds collateral value above the dust threshold. The account's bad debt is never written into the supply index, the residual collateral stays locked in the pool, and loss recognition to suppliers is frozen. Recovery requires the privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt` path or the abandoned account's owner withdrawing the dust — a temporary/permanent freezing of funds and a liveness failure of the bad-debt resolution mechanism, matching the accepted impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions are narrow but recur naturally: an account liquidated down to sub-dust collateral with residual debt — the standard "dust bad debt" state `clean_bad_debt` exists for. The attacker's cost is a single top-up slightly above the dust threshold in an asset the account already supplies, on any non-paused, non-frozen listing. The attack can be repeated cheaply if the victim owner ever withdraws the dust. A grieving attacker or an interested party (e.g., a large supplier who prefers delaying index write-down, or a competitor) can execute this at will; Medium severity.

### Recommendation
In `clean_bad_debt`, either (a) net out third-party top-ups by treating supply positions created/topped-up after the account became insolvent as protocol revenue rather than collateral toward the dust gate, or (b) fold a seize of remaining collateral into `clean_bad_debt` itself so the dust cap compares debt shortfall rather than gross collateral. Alternatively, restrict third-party `supply` on accounts whose health factor is below 1 or whose collateral is already at/below the dust threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. ALICE supplies USDC and borrows ETH; price moves push her HF below 1.
2. LIQUIDATOR calls `liquidate`, repaying profitable debt and seizing collateral until only dust-value residual collateral and unpaid (bad) debt remain.
3. Attacker MALLORY calls `supply(caller=MALLORY, account_id=alice_id, spoke_id=alice_spoke, assets=[(existing_hub_asset, dust_threshold + ε)])`. This passes `require_third_party_existing_supply` because ALICE still holds that supply position [5](#0-4) .
4. Any keeper calling `clean_bad_debt(caller, alice_id)` now reverts: remaining collateral value exceeds the dust threshold [6](#0-5) .
5. The account's bad debt cannot be socialized and its collateral cannot exit the pool unless governance invokes `force_socialize_bad_debt` or ALICE's (likely abandoned) owner withdraws the donated dust — at which point MALLORY repeats step 3.
