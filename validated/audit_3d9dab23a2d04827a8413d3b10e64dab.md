### Title
Whale-sized market at high utilization permanently freezes because `borrowed × borrow_index` overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap engages — ([File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs](common/src/rates/scaling.rs))

### Summary
The accrual step computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` as `i128` mul-div (`Ray::mul`). For a very large market, the *value* `scaled × index` can exceed `i128::MAX` while `borrow_index` is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (1e36). The mul-div panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`, and because every mutating entrypoint runs `global_sync` first, the market can never be accrued again — no repay, withdraw, borrow, liquidation, or bad-debt cleanup ever succeeds. This mirrors the report's class: a growth transformation (index scaling over time) produces a result larger than the destination type's capacity, overflowing in the middle of iteration rather than at a checked bound.

### Finding Description
- `accrue_step` calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` before computing utilization (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62`).
- `scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` = `mul_div_half_up(scaled, index, RAY)`, which panics with `MathOverflow` when the product `scaled × index` does not fit even the `I256`-widened result back into `i128` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`, `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`).
- `global_sync` runs this on every mutating pool verb, chunked by `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`). The panic aborts the whole call, so `last_timestamp` is never advanced and indexes are never stored — the failure is sticky, not transient.
- The borrow index is only capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36` (`common/src/constants/pool.rs:19`), but the value ceiling is `i128::MAX / scaled`. For `scaled ≈ 1e36` ray (≈1 billion whole tokens at 18 decimals), the value overflows at an index of only ~170 RAY — long before the index cap can engage and stop accrual gracefully.
- The repository's own test pins the behavior: at 98% utilization on the XLM rate curve, `update_indexes` eventually fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls fail identically (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-356`).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and a contract unable to operate: once `scaled × index ≥ i128::MAX`, every controller verb that touches the market (`supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue`) panics inside `global_sync` before doing any work. Suppliers cannot exit, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot act, and protocol revenue is unclaimable. All tokens held by the pool for that market are stuck forever.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses through ordinary `supply`/`borrow` calls — no privileged role, parameter change, or oracle manipulation is required. However, it requires (a) a very large market (on the order of 1e9 whole tokens of an 18-decimal asset, or proportionally less with a higher index, since `scaled` is what matters), (b) sustained high utilization so the index keeps compounding, and (c) enough elapsed time for the index to reach the value ceiling — the test shows this happening at index ~170x on the XLM curve, which takes years at near-max rates. Supply/borrow caps constrain `scaled` but do not prevent the overflow, because `borrowed` (scaled debt shares) is what multiplies the index. Realistically gated by capital and time rather than authorization — Medium likelihood, but the impact is total and irreversible once the cliff is crossed.

### Recommendation
Bound the panic instead of letting the widened mul-div trap: in `accrue_step`, clamp `scaled_to_original` inputs or detect the overflow and saturate `new_borrow_index`/`new_supply_index` at their caps (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`) — the cap check should engage *before* the value computation, e.g. by checking `index` growth against a safe bound `i128::MAX / scaled` rather than only the absolute 1e36 cap. Alternatively, perform utilization and interest computation in `I256` so accrual can always complete; positions' own unscaling at exit can then be handled per-position rather than freezing the entire market.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-360`:

1. Build a market with 18 decimals, XLM rate curve, caps lifted:
   `with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))`, `lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18)`.
2. BOB supplies `1e9 × 1e18` raw units (`principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18)`); ALICE supplies collateral and borrows 98% of it.
3. Advance time year-by-year, calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` until it returns `Err(MATH_OVERFLOW)` — this occurs while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, proving the cap never engages.
4. `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both return `MATH_OVERFLOW` — exits are permanently bricked because `global_sync` panics before any position logic runs.

Root-cause chain: `global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60`) → `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14`) → `Ray::mul` → `mul_div` overflow panic in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`, with the index ceiling at `common/src/constants/pool.rs:19` never reached.
