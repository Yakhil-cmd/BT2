### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` without burning survivors' shares, leaving phantom claims that drain future depositors — (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes unpaid debt by multiplying the market's `supply_index` by `remaining / total_supplied_value`, then clamps the result up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY / 1000`, `common/src/constants/pool.rs:9`). When the socialized debt meets or exceeds total supplied value, the correct write-down would be zero, but the clamp leaves every outstanding scaled supply share with a residual value of 0.1% of face. Those shares are never burned. The clamped index becomes the new baseline: the next depositor mints shares against it, and the wiped supplier can withdraw a real asset amount equal to its residual claim, paid out of the fresh deposit — a direct transfer of funds from the new depositor to the wiped account.

### Finding Description
The vulnerable path is reachable by a single unprivileged address:

1. A market reaches `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`. The 95% `require_utilization_below_max` ceiling only bounds debt at mint time; because the protocol takes a revenue share, `borrow_index` grows strictly faster than `supply_index`, so on a fully-lent market accrued debt eventually exceeds total supplied value. Suppliers cannot exit early because the cash is lent out.
2. Anyone calls `controller::liquidate` (or `clean_bad_debt` for sub-dust accounts). The controller sends `PoolSeizeEntry{side: Borrow}` to the pool (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:35-47`), which executes `seize::apply` → `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` → `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` → `burn_debt` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`).
3. Inside `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, `capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value)` makes `remaining = 0`, `reduction_factor = 0`, `new_supply_index = 0`, and the final `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))` revives the index to `RAY/1000` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:80-88`). Total scaled `supplied` is untouched.
4. Every wiped supplier retains `unscale_supply_floor(scaled) ≈ scaled * RAY/1000` of claim. A fresh `supply` call credits real cash and mints shares at the floored index; the wiped supplier then calls `withdraw`, which passes `require_reserves` (cash now exists) and pays out the residual claim from the new depositor's funds. Pool-side, `withdraw` debits cash against shares whose economic backing was already written off.

The pool unit tests in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369` and `:430-496` demonstrate exactly this mechanics at the raw-cache level: after a floor clamp, a stranded claim withdraws exactly a fresh depositor's full deposit, leaving `cash < honest_claim`. Nothing in `seize::apply` or `commit` runs a solvency guard that would burn the residual shares or block the payout.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Once a market's accrued bad debt reaches the supply value, the first wipeout socialization converts 0.1% of face value per wiped share into an unbacked claim. Any subsequent deposit is immediately capturable by wiped suppliers up to their residual claim size (0.1% of their pre-wipeout deposit — on a whale-scale market this is still material, and the market is permanently insolvent: `unscale_supply_floor(supplied) > cash` forever after). New lenders fund payouts to accounts whose claims were already written down to zero.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an extreme but unprivileged condition: cumulative bad debt on one (hub, token) book reaching 100% of supplied value. This is attainable when a market is fully lent out (suppliers locked), debtors become insolvent, and interest compounds the debt book past the supply book — no oracle manipulation needed, only time plus a price move that makes the debt unrecoverable. The trigger itself is a single `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` call anyone can make. Medium likelihood, high impact.

### Recommendation
In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, when `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, burn the accounting properly instead of clamping: either set the index to the floor **and** reduce `supplied` (and each position's claim) so that residual claims cannot exceed post-wipeout cash, or treat the floor clamp as a full wipe and write total `supplied` to zero with `revenue` handled consistently. At minimum, `seize` should commit behind a solvency assertion (`unscale_supply_floor(supplied) <= cash + recoverable debt`), and `withdraw`/`require_reserves` should compare against backed value rather than raw cash.

### Proof of Concept
The mechanics are already exercised by the repo's own tests:

- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317` `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`: clamps the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, shows the wiped supplier keeps a `stranded > 0` claim, deposits `c = stranded` as user B, then user A's `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a)` pays out exactly `c`, leaving `cash = 0 < bob_claim`.
- `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430` `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`: same drain through the `Borrow`-side seize path (`burn_debt` + index clamp), asserting `gross == deposit` and insolvency (`owed {} > cash {}`).
- Reachability of the seize path is confirmed by `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28` and `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs:93-113`, which shows a real `liquidate` call driving `supply_index` toward the floor.

The only unverified link is a full end-to-end harness run that grows `borrowed * borrow_index` past `supplied * supply_index` before liquidating; the index-growth asymmetry (revenue skim makes borrow index outpace supply index) plus locked supplier cash makes this a matter of accrual time, not attacker privilege.
