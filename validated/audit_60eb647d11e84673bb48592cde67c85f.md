### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Analysis notes:** `get-liquidation-position` (which is just `get-position` called with the enabled-asset bitmap, same as `get-liquidation-position` at [1](#0-0)  is explicitly commented as intentional design "liquidation specific (enabled collateral + all debt)", distinguishing it from `get-position` (enabled only, [2](#0-1) ) and `get-full-position` (all collateral, [3](#0-2) ). This is a deliberate choice to ensure debt from disabled assets is still captured during liquidation, not a state mismatch bug.

In `liquidate-redeem`, the recipient of the redeemed underlying funds (`funds-receiver`) is chosen by the caller themselves via the `receiver` parameter, defaulting to `contract-caller` if unset: [4](#0-3) . This means the liquidator (attacker A) controls where their own liquidation proceeds go — there is no mechanism forcing a distinct victim B's funds through an address B does not control. The borrower being liquidated (the closest candidate for "victim") loses seized collateral to the liquidator/receiver chosen by the liquidator, per `collateral-remove` with `actual-receiver` defaulting to `liquidator`: [5](#0-4) ; the borrower does not receive a "mandatory payout" that could be routed through a rejecting principal — any transfer failure would revert the liquidator's own transaction, not lock or misdirect the borrower's funds.

The described exploit does not identify a concrete two-principal (A writes shared state, B's transaction is harmed by it) fund-theft path; the "vault whose share price the redemption moves" and "route a victim's payout through a rejecting principal" mechanics are not substantiated by the actual code paths in `liquidate`, `liquidate-redeem`, or `vault-redeem`.
