### Title
Attestor-list revocation can be front-run to spend attestation-gated asset funds after removal - (File: validation.js)

### Summary
Ocore assets can be configured with `spender_attested`, meaning only addresses on an issuer-maintained attestor list may hold/spend the asset. An issuer removes a compromised or blacklisted address by posting an `asset_attestors` message. Exactly like the `addBlackList()` front-run in the report, the affected address can watch the network/mempool for the incoming attestor-list update and race to post a competing transfer before the revocation unit becomes stable, moving its attested-asset balance out to a fresh (still-attested or unattested-but-accepted) address before the block-list actually takes effect.

### Finding Description
Payments in an attestation-gated asset are checked against the *stable* attestor list as of `last_ball_mci`, not the latest pending list update: [1](#0-0) 

and again per-input/per-output: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Because the check only consults attestations that are `main_chain_index<=last_ball_mci AND is_stable=1` (the underlying attestor-list lookups in `storage.loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors` / `storage.filterAttestedAddresses` follow this same stability gating used throughout `validation.js`), a newly-posted `asset_attestors` revocation is not enforced until it, and enough units on top of it, become stable and get included in a later last-ball. This creates the same window described in the Sherlock report: an address about to be de-attested (blacklisted) can see the revocation unit propagating and immediately post a transfer of its full asset balance while the old (favorable) attestor list is still the one being enforced by validators, exactly as `removeBlackFunds()` in the referenced report can be front-run because ERC20 balance movement and blacklist application are not atomic.

### Impact Explanation
An asset issuer relying on `spender_attested`/`asset_attestors` to freeze or exclude a malicious/compromised address (the ocore analog of `addBlackList`) can be defeated: the targeted address drains its attested-asset holdings to another address before the revocation is enforced. This is a fund-freezing/compliance bypass for the issuer and can result in continued circulation of funds the issuer intended to lock, i.e., asset-issuance/transfer-condition guarantees are broken — a Medium-severity issue consistent with the class validated in the referenced report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (a) an asset definer to actively use `spender_attested` with `asset_attestors` for compliance/blacklisting, and (b) the targeted address to be watching for its own de-attestation and to race a transfer before the revoking unit and enough of the DAG on top of it become stable. Given ocore's DAG-based finality (units are actionable once broadcast, but only enforced against the *stable* view), the window is real but bounded by how quickly the network reaches stability on the attestor-list unit, similar to the original gas-price race in the ERC20 report.

### Recommendation
Adopt the same idea suggested by Inspex/J4de/0xAgro/gmx for `StakingModule.sol`: don't gate solely on the presence of the address in a "currently-attested" set at validation time; instead, make the asset issuer's revocation apply retroactively/immediately to any *pending* (not-yet-stable) transfer attempts once the `asset_attestors` unit is included in the payment's own parents, or require issuer-side cosigning (`cosigned_by_definer`) for asset classes where blacklisting matters, so a definer can simply refuse to cosign a spend from an address it is in the process of revoking, closing the front-run window regardless of stabilization delay.

### Proof of Concept
1. Issuer defines asset `X` with `spender_attested: true` and posts `asset_attestors` message attesting address `A` (validated via `validateAttestorListUpdate`, `validation.js` case `"asset_attestors"`, `validation.js:2033-2042`).
2. Address `A` accumulates a large balance of asset `X` (payments validated per `validation.js:2115-2123`, `2504-2509`).
3. Issuer decides to revoke `A` (analogous to `addBlackList(A)`) and posts a new `asset_attestors` unit removing `A` from the attestor list.
4. Before that revocation unit and enough successors reach `is_stable=1` with `main_chain_index<=last_ball_mci` for a subsequent payment's `last_ball`, `A` posts a `payment` message transferring its full `X` balance to address `B`.
5. Because `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` (`validation.js:2630-2642`) and `loadAssetWithListOfAttestedAuthors` still see `A` as attested under the last stable ball referenced by `A`'s payment, the transfer validates and is accepted, moving the funds out before the revocation takes effect — mirroring the front-run of `removeBlackFunds()` in the original report.
