### Title
LPs can front-run `liquidate()` bad-debt socialization by calling `redeem()` first to dodge their share of the loss - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` and equivalent vault contracts)

### Summary
Zest's vaults (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) issue shares (`zft`) whose redemption value is a function of the pool's `assets` variable. When `v0-4-market.clar`'s `liquidate()` finds a position with no collateral left to cover its debt, it calls `socialize-debt` on the relevant vault, which writes down `lindex`/`assets` and thereby the exchange rate for every existing shareholder. This is structurally the same public-mempool-visible loss-triggering call as `seizeBadDebt()` in the referenced report, and `redeem()` is the same unrestricted, immediate withdrawal path as `withdraw()` in the report - allowing any LP to see a pending bad-debt-causing `liquidate()` transaction and front-run it with `redeem()` at the pre-write-down exchange rate.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` in `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar` computes `no-collateral-left` and, if true, folds over the borrower's remaining debt calling `socialize-debt-asset`, which calls the vault's public `socialize-debt` function: [1](#0-0) 

`socialize-debt` in the vault (e.g. `v0-vault-ststx.clar`) reduces `total-borrowed`/`assets` and writes `lindex` down proportionally to the loss versus `old-total-assets`: [2](#0-1) 

Any LP's redeemable amount from `redeem()` is computed from the current `assets` var via `convert-to-assets-preview`, executed atomically with no delay, lockup, or queueing mechanism: [3](#0-2) 

Because `liquidate()` is a public function callable by anyone once a position becomes eligible for liquidation, and because vault `redeem()` has no cooldown, an LP watching the mempool for a `liquidate()` call that will trigger `socialize-debt` (i.e., a large enough undercollateralized position with `no-collateral-left`) can submit a higher-gas-fee `redeem()` transaction to withdraw at the current (pre-loss) exchange rate before the `liquidate()`/`socialize-debt` write-down lands. This is exactly the front-run-the-loss pattern from the referenced Hubble `InsuranceFund` finding: `withdraw()`/`seizeBadDebt()` there maps directly to `redeem()`/`socialize-debt` here.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker (an LP who anticipates the socialization event) withdraws their principal at full value and avoids their pro-rata share of the bad debt. The remaining LPs bear a larger share of the write-down than they otherwise would have, since the total loss is now spread over a smaller remaining pool of `total-supply`/`assets`. This is a direct, unclaimed-yield-adjacent loss transfer between two unprivileged principals (the front-runner and the remaining depositors) via the shared state (`assets`/`lindex`/`total-supply` of the vault), which lands in the **High** impact category (temporary/permanent freezing or theft of yield to a third party depositor) since remaining LPs' funds are made to absorb more loss than their fair share, effectively a socialized cost dodged by one party at the others' expense.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (1) an LP holding a meaningful share position in the vault backing the undercollateralized asset, (2) visibility into the mempool of a pending `liquidate()` call that will trigger `no-collateral-left` → `socialize-debt`, and (3) faster/higher-fee inclusion of the `redeem()` transaction. On Stacks, block-level ordering (rather than sub-second mempool MEV as on EVM chains) still allows a user to observe a liquidatable position accruing and to redeem preemptively in the same or an earlier block, similar to the original report's mechanism.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay/queue for large redemptions (request-then-claim with a cooldown), or accrue/reserve for expected bad debt gradually (e.g., via a dedicated buffer funded over time) rather than allowing an instantaneous, full-value exit right up to the block in which `socialize-debt` executes. Alternatively, process `socialize-debt` for a liquidatable position optimistically/pessimistically ahead of any redemption in the same block, or apply a withdrawal fee/haircut proportional to any pending unhealthy positions tied to the vault's exposure.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) has LPs Bob and others providing liquidity; Alice has an undercollateralized position on the market that has fallen enough that a `liquidate()` call will exhaust all her collateral and leave residual debt (`no-collateral-left = true`), per the flow at [4](#0-3) .
2. Charlie broadcasts `liquidate(alice, ...)` which will subsequently call `socialize-debt-asset` → vault `socialize-debt`, reducing `assets`/`lindex`.
3. Bob observes this pending transaction and submits `redeem(shares, min-out, bob)` with higher priority, which computes his withdrawal at the pre-write-down exchange rate (`convert-to-assets-preview`), per [3](#0-2) , and is confirmed before or in the same context as the socialization.
4. Once `liquidate()` executes and `socialize-debt` writes down `lindex`/`assets`, the loss is spread only across the LPs remaining in the pool (excluding Bob, who exited unaffected), confirming the loss-shifting outcome analogous to the Hubble `InsuranceFund` `withdraw()`/`seizeBadDebt()` race.
