### Title
LP-mint tokens can be burned directly via the SPL Token program without updating `PoolState::lp_supply`, permanently desynchronizing pool accounting and freezing LP funds — ([File: programs/cp-swap/src/states/pool.rs])

### Summary
`PoolState.lp_supply` is a program-tracked counter that is supposed to always equal the number of LP tokens actually outstanding for a pool. It is only updated inside the `deposit` and `withdraw` instructions of the `cp-swap` program via `checked_add`/`checked_sub`. However, the LP mint itself is an ordinary SPL/Token-2022 mint whose token accounts are owned by liquidity providers, and a token owner can burn their own tokens by calling the SPL Token program's `Burn` instruction directly — completely outside of the `cp-swap` program and its `withdraw` accounting path. This is structurally the same root cause as the referenced Origami finding: a user-reachable `burn` path that reduces the actual token supply without going through the protocol's bookkeeping function that maintains a "circulating supply"-style counter.

### Finding Description
`lp_supply` is documented as "True circulating supply without burns and lock ups" and is exclusively mutated by:
- `deposit.rs`: `pool_state.lp_supply = pool_state.lp_supply.checked_add(lp_token_amount).unwrap();` [1](#0-0) 
- `withdraw.rs`: `pool_state.lp_supply = pool_state.lp_supply.checked_sub(lp_token_amount).unwrap();` [2](#0-1) 

There is no other place in the on-chain program that adjusts `lp_supply`, and it is used as the denominator in the constant-product pro-rata calculation `CurveCalculator::lp_tokens_to_trading_tokens` for both `deposit` and `withdraw` [3](#0-2) .

The `owner_lp_token` account constrained only by `token::authority = owner` [4](#0-3)  is a standard SPL/Token-2022 account. The mint authority for `lp_mint` is the pool's `authority` PDA (used only for `mint_to`/`burn` calls issued by the program itself, e.g. `token_mint_to`/`token_burn`), but burning tokens from a token account under the SPL Token standard requires only the **token account owner's** signature — not the mint authority. Therefore any liquidity provider can submit a standalone `spl_token::instruction::burn` (or `burn_checked`) transaction against their own `owner_lp_token` account and the `lp_mint`, destroying real LP tokens without ever invoking the `cp-swap` program's `withdraw` instruction and without ever decrementing `pool_state.lp_supply`.

### Impact Explanation
After such a direct burn, `pool_state.lp_supply` stays permanently higher than the tokens truly outstanding. Every future `deposit`/`withdraw` call uses this now-inflated `lp_supply` as the divisor in `lp_tokens_to_trading_tokens`. Since a withdrawer's share of vault tokens is `lp_token_amount * total_token_amount / lp_supply`, an inflated denominator means every remaining LP token entitles its holder to less than its true pro-rata share of `token_0_vault`/`token_1_vault`. Summed across all remaining LP holders redeeming their full balance, the total tokens paid out from the vaults will be strictly less than the total pooled assets, leaving a residual balance permanently stranded in the vaults with no instruction able to retrieve it (protocol/fund/creator fee collection instructions only draw from the dedicated fee counters, not from this residual). This is a permanent freezing of LP funds caused entirely by an unprivileged, single-transaction action (a direct SPL Token burn) reachable by any liquidity provider holding LP tokens.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: any LP holder can trivially construct and submit a standard SPL Token `Burn` instruction against their own token account and the pool's `lp_mint` — no special privileges, no cooperation from the pool authority, and no interaction with the `cp-swap` program's own instruction set is required. This does not depend on any non-default build feature, malicious validator, or off-chain component.

### Recommendation
Do not rely on an externally mutable SPL mint's supply being solely controlled by program logic. Either:
- Track and derive `lp_supply` directly from the LP mint's `supply` field (read from the `Mint` account) at the time of `deposit`/`withdraw`/`initialize` instead of maintaining a separately incrementable/decrementable counter, so any out-of-band burn is automatically reflected; or
- If a separate counter must be kept, reconcile it against the actual mint `supply` on every deposit/withdraw and treat divergence as an error, ensuring `lp_supply` can never drift from the real outstanding token count.

### Proof of Concept
1. Liquidity provider `Alice` deposits into a pool via `deposit`, receiving `N` LP tokens minted to her `owner_lp_token` account; `pool_state.lp_supply` is incremented by `N`.
2. Instead of calling the `cp-swap` program's `withdraw` instruction, Alice directly submits a `spl_token::instruction::burn` (or the Token-2022 equivalent) transaction burning `M <= N` of her LP tokens from her own account, authorized by her own signature over the `lp_mint`.
3. `pool_state.lp_supply` is untouched (still counts the burned `M` tokens as outstanding), while the real total supply of `lp_mint` has decreased by `M`.
4. Any subsequent `withdraw` by other LPs uses the stale, inflated `pool_state.lp_supply` as the divisor in `lp_tokens_to_trading_tokens`, causing every remaining LP token to redeem for less than its true share of `token_0_vault`/`token_1_vault`, permanently trapping the shortfall in the vaults.
