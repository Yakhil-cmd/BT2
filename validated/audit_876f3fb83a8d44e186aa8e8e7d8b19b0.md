### Title
Governance Manipulation: `countVotes` weighs system-parameter and witness (OP-list) votes by balance at count time, not balance at vote time, allowing temporarily-acquired funds to swing consensus votes - ([File: main_chain.js])

### Summary
The GovernorBravo bug is about comparing voting power at two different reference points and taking an inconsistent (max/current) rather than a fully finalized minimum snapshot, letting temporarily-held tokens swing a vote. Ocore's on-chain system-vote mechanism (`system_vote` / `system_vote_count` messages, tallied in `countVotes()`) has an analogous weakness: a voter's ballot recorded via `system_vote` is *not* weighted by the balance the voter held at the time they cast the vote. Instead, at count time the code re-reads whatever balance the address currently holds and applies that weight to a possibly very old vote.

### Finding Description
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` determines the weight of each vote for `op_list` (the witness list) and numerical system parameters (`threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) purely from the voter's **current** stable balance, computed at the moment the MCI stabilizes and vote counting runs: [1](#0-0) 

This balance is joined against `system_votes`/`op_votes`/`numerical_votes`, which store the *choice* the address made and the *timestamp of the vote*, but never freeze the balance that existed when the vote was cast: [2](#0-1) 

The vote-timeframe expansion logic further compounds this: votes can be years old and are still tallied against the address's present-day balance, as the loop simply extends `since_timestamp` backwards until enough current balance is captured under `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Because `countVotes` runs automatically every time an MCI containing a `system_vote_count` message stabilizes, and stabilization/finality delay in ocore is analogous to "block finality" in the audit report, an address holder can:
1. Cast a `system_vote` once from an address (even with a dust balance) - this registers the address as a "voter" for the subject indefinitely (there is no re-voting requirement and no snapshot of the balance held at that moment).
2. Later, shortly before an MCI containing `system_vote_count` becomes stable, acquire a large stable-good balance on that same address (e.g., a large transfer that becomes stable through normal DAG confirmation).
3. Let the vote count happen with the inflated balance, then immediately move the funds away.

This is functionally the same class of bug as the GovernorBravo report: voting weight is derived from a balance snapshot that is not pinned to the moment the ballot was actually cast, so temporarily held funds can dominate the outcome. The emergency `op_list` path (`applyEmergencyOpListChange` / `getUnstableVotes`) has the same property — it reuses `voter_balances` computed from current balances for unstable votes that may be arbitrarily old: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` votes directly determine the set of Order Providers/witnesses used for main-chain ordering and stability determination (`validateWitnesses` / `getOpList` in `validation.js`, consumed by consensus): [6](#0-5) 
Manipulating this election with transient balance lets an attacker (or a coalition with temporary access to large token holdings) push malicious or colluding addresses into the witness set, which is the trust anchor for main-chain stability and the finality of every unit in the DAG — a change here can enable double-spend acceptance or network-wide disagreement on validity/stability. Numerical votes (`threshold_size`, `tps_interval`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) directly control fee/anti-spam economics and can be pushed to unsafe values with the same technique, affecting network throughput/fee-based DoS resistance. This satisfies "node disagreement on validity or stability" / "network unable to confirm new units" impact classes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to acquire and later dispose of a large stable balance around a `countVotes` execution window, which is gated by DAG stabilization delay (not a single-block flash loan), so it is less trivial than an EVM flash loan. However, it does not require any privileged role — any address can post `system_vote`/`system_vote_count`, and the balance check at count time is fully attacker-influenceable via ordinary payments once they are stable-good. Given `since_timestamp` can look back a full year (or more, expanding by multiples of a year) before requiring enough share to have "voted," an attacker's dust vote from long ago remains eligible to be re-weighted by a large balance acquired just before the next count, making this a realistic, unprivileged, medium-likelihood governance-manipulation vector.

### Recommendation
Freeze and store the voter's balance (or a capped/minimum balance observed over the voting-to-counting interval) at the time the `system_vote` unit becomes stable, rather than re-reading current balance at count time. At minimum, take the **minimum** of the balance at vote-cast time and the balance at count time (mirroring the GovernorBravo fix of taking `min(originalVotes, currentVotes)` using only finalized/stable reference points), and require a minimum holding duration between the vote and the count so that transiently-acquired balances cannot be weighted into `op_list`/numerical system votes.

### Proof of Concept
1. Address `A` holds 1 unit of value; it authors a `system_vote` for `op_list` (or a numerical subject) which becomes stable — `saveSystemVote` records `A`'s choice in `system_votes`/`op_votes`/`numerical_votes` with no balance snapshot: [7](#0-6) .
2. Anytime later (up to the expanding `since_timestamp` window, potentially a year+), a large payment is sent to `A` and becomes stable-good just before a `system_vote_count` unit for the relevant subject stabilizes.
3. `countVotes` computes `voter_balances` using `A`'s current (now large) balance: [8](#0-7)  and applies it to `A`'s previously-cast vote to tally `op_list`/numerical outcome: [9](#0-8) .
4. `A` then spends/transfers the large balance away — the vote's influence on the resulting `system_vars` (witness list or fee parameters) persists even though `A` no longer holds that balance.
