### Title
Governance-parameter votes are weighted by an address's *current* balance recomputed at every stabilized MCI, enabling a quick-vote-and-withdraw capture of critical system parameters (op_list/witness list, TPS fee parameters) - (File: main_chain.js)

### Summary
The report describes Aragon voting lacking mitigation for quick vote-and-withdraw, no incentive to vote earlier, and no protection against spam/one-shot governance capture. `ocore`'s on-chain "system vote" mechanism for electing the order-providers list (`op_list`, i.e. witnesses) and network fee parameters (`threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`) exhibits the same class of weakness: vote weight is not a snapshot taken when the vote is cast, but the voter's *live* balance re-queried every time `countVotes()` runs (on every newly stabilized MCI). This lets an address temporarily inflate its balance, cast a `system_vote`, have it counted while the balance is still inflated, and then immediately move the funds away - permanently baking a malicious `op_list`/fee-parameter change into `storage.systemVars` for the interval until the next recount.

### Finding Description
`validation.js` allows any non-AA address (after `constants.v4UpgradeMci`) to post a single `system_vote` message per unit for subjects `op_list`, `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`: [1](#0-0) 

When each MCI stabilizes, `main_chain.js` persists the vote (`saveSystemVote`) and, once per MCI, recomputes the outcome for every subject that has pending votes via `countVotes()`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Critically, the voter's balance used for weighting is not the balance held at the time the vote unit was created/signed — it is the address's aggregate *current* stable, unspent, good-sequence balance, fetched fresh on every count: [4](#0-3) 

This balance is combined with an expanding time window (`since_timestamp`) that only requires a minimum *aggregate share* of `TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` to have voted, not that any individual voter held funds for any minimum duration: [5](#0-4) 

For `op_list`, the top-`COUNT_WITNESSES` addresses by summed voter balance are installed into `storage.systemVars.op_list`, which subsequently determines the network's operator/witness list used for stability calculations: [6](#0-5) 

For fee-related subjects, a median-by-balance value is likewise installed and takes effect immediately from the current `vote_count_mci`: [7](#0-6) 

Because the result is `unshift`-ed into `storage.systemVars[subject]` and takes effect from the exact MCI at which `countVotes` ran, whatever outcome is computed *at that one recount* is locked in and used by the network until the *next* recount corrects it. An attacker who can transiently hold a large balance (e.g., via a large self-payment that becomes stable, a temporary loan/exchange deposit, or coordination among colluding addresses) for the single MCI window in which `countVotes` executes can dominate the weighted vote, install a malicious `op_list` or extreme `tps_fee_multiplier`/`base_tps_fee`, and then immediately spend the balance away in the very next unit — the damage (bad witness list, broken fee parameters) is already committed for that MCI interval. This mirrors the report's "no mitigation for quick vote and withdraw" and "no incentive to vote earlier" issues (there's no benefit to having voted long ago vs. seconds before the count, and the weight measured is not tied to any lock-up).

### Impact Explanation
`op_list` selects the order/witness set relied upon for stability determination throughout the network; corrupting it — even transiently — can let an attacker seed witnesses they control, undermining the network's ability to reach consensus on unit validity/stability (`node disagreement on validity or stability` / potential inability to confirm new units). Corrupting `base_tps_fee`/`tps_fee_multiplier`/`tps_interval`/`threshold_size` directly changes global fee economics and finality thresholds network-wide, which can freeze normal transaction processing or degrade stability guarantees for at least one MCI interval. Because these system variables are consumed globally by every node, a temporary capture has an outsized, non-reversible-in-time effect: even after the attacker withdraws funds, the bad value already took effect and persists until the next successful recount.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the attacker to control enough balance to meet `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE` of `TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` at the exact moment a recount executes — a bar that is non-trivial but achievable by any sufficiently funded actor (or via a self-referential/looped payment that inflates the balance reading for one MCI before immediately being resent elsewhere), especially during periods of low overall voter participation, since the countVotes threshold is measured against *total supply share of ever-voted addresses*, not against total participation integrity. No minimum holding period, no vote-locking, and no incentive structure for early/long-term voters exists in the code, so the attack surface described in the external report directly maps onto this mechanism.

### Recommendation
- Weight votes by the balance held (or a time-weighted/locked balance) at the time the vote unit becomes stable, rather than re-querying the live balance at every subsequent MCI recount.
- Require a minimum holding/lock duration before a balance counts toward vote weight (e.g., balance must have existed unspent since some MCI prior to the count), to prevent quick vote-and-withdraw.
- Consider requiring quorum/participation stability across multiple consecutive counts before committing a change to `op_list` or fee parameters, rather than applying whatever a single `countVotes()` invocation computes.
- Document and formally test the parameter-voting/`op_list` election mechanism against flash-balance and vote-timing attacks before relying on it for safety-critical parameters such as the witness list.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires a large temporary balance at address `X` (e.g., via a large payment that becomes stable, exploiting available liquidity or coordinating multiple addresses) sufficient to satisfy `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE * TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` for the `op_list` (or `tps_fee_multiplier`) subject.
2. Attacker posts a `system_vote` unit from `X` with `subject: "op_list"` and a malicious witness list (or an extreme fee value), which passes validation per `validation.js:1844-1891`.
3. As soon as the vote unit and its containing MCI stabilize, `main_chain.js`'s `handleAATriggers`/`calcCommissions` pipeline calls `countVotes(conn, mci, "op_list")`, which reads `X`'s *current* balance (`main_chain.js:1757-1777`) — still inflated — and, combined with typically low overall voter turnout, computes `X`'s preferred `op_list` as the winning set (`main_chain.js:1850-1871`).
4. `storage.systemVars.op_list` is updated to the attacker-controlled list, effective from that MCI onward until a future recount changes it.
5. Attacker immediately spends/moves the balance out of `X` in the next unit. The next `countVotes()` run will reflect the reduced balance and eventually correct the list, but the malicious `op_list`/fee value already took effect for the intervening MCI(s), during which the attacker-controlled witnesses or broken fee parameters could be leveraged to disrupt stability or fee processing.
