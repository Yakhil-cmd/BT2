### Title
Spoke supply cap silently fails open via saturating index conversion, so the configured bound is never enforced - (File: contracts/controller/src/spoke_usage.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2021-30476 is a "configured restriction never applied" bug: Terraform's Vault provider accepted GCE bound-label configuration but wrote the role without it, so the intended auth constraint was silently absent. The analog in XOXNO Lending is the spoke-level `supply_cap`: it is stored per (spoke, hub asset) and checked on entry, but the entry check converts the asset-denominated cap into scaled shares via the live supply index with a conversion that saturates at `i128::MAX` instead of reverting. Once the conversion saturates, the cap check always passes, so a cap that governance configured is not enforced at all. A single unprivileged address can trigger this via `supply` with a `(spoke_id, hub_asset, amount)` triple. Documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-HALT-03.

### Finding Description
`enforce_spoke_asset_flags` and `require_can_supply` gate supply entry on listing/flag state, and cap enforcement compares stored scaled usage against the configured `supply_cap` converted at the returned live index. Per INV-HALT-03, that conversion "saturates at `i128::MAX` instead of reverting, so the scaled cap can fail open: once saturated, the configured asset-unit limit is not enforced." Saturation becomes reachable because the supply index has a floor of `RAY / 1000` (documented in the same invariant): at the floor, any configured `supply_cap` above roughly 1/1000 of the i128 maximum saturates, and even moderate caps saturate once the index drifts below 1 RAY during the index's initial accrual window. The analog to the CVE is exact: a bound is configured, validated, stored, and emitted in events, but the runtime path that should enforce it converts it into a value that makes the check vacuous — the label exists, the binding does not.

### Impact Explanation
The configured supply cap is the protocol's per-market exposure bound. When it fails open, an unprivileged user can supply amounts far beyond the governance-set limit, inflating the market's share base and the pool's exposure to that asset beyond what risk parameters intended. If the asset later depegs or its oracle quality degrades, the excess uncapped collateral is what becomes bad debt during liquidation, so the silent non-enforcement converts directly into potential protocol insolvency rather than remaining a cosmetic config mismatch. Severity is bounded (Medium) because direct theft is not achieved and the excess position is still subject to normal LTV/liquidation rules.

### Likelihood Explanation
Saturation requires an index below 1 RAY — reachable during early market life or after `clean_bad_debt`/supply-index write-down paths that can push the supply index toward its `RAY/1000` floor. Any unprivileged account calling `supply` then bypasses the cap with no special privileges, keys, or timing. The fail-open behavior is deterministic once the arithmetic saturates.

### Recommendation
Make the cap conversion fail closed: revert (or clamp to zero admissible headroom) when asset-unit cap → scaled-share conversion would saturate at `i128::MAX`, in `spoke_usage.rs` cap accounting. Alternatively, compare usage in asset units by unscaling the stored scaled usage rather than scaling the cap, eliminating the saturation direction entirely.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists asset X in a spoke with `supply_cap = C` (any realistic cap) while the market's supply index is at or driven toward the `RAY / 1000` floor (e.g., freshly listed market before accrual, or post `clean_bad_debt` write-down).
2. Attacker calls `supply(spoke_id, hub_asset=X, amount)` with `amount > C` — far exceeding the configured cap.
3. The cap check converts `C` to scaled shares at the sub-RAY index; the multiplication overflows and saturates at `i128::MAX`, so `usage <= cap_scaled` trivially holds.
4. `supply` succeeds and books supply shares beyond the configured bound; repeated calls compound the excess with no bound ever enforced.

Note: the fail-open behavior is explicitly documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-HALT-03.
