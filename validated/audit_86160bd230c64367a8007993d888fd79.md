## Analysis

The Uniswap `slot0` bug class — pricing a swap from an instantaneous, single-block-observable on-chain value with no manipulation resistance, enabling a sandwich attack — maps onto Obyte's `oscript`/AA engine through the `balance[asset]` formula operator. This operator returns the AA's *current* balance from `objValidationState.assocBalances`, which is exactly analogous to `slot0`: a spot value with no time-weighting, freely readable and — because AAs are permissionless and any address can trigger them with a payment — freely *movable* by anyone willing to send a payment to the AA.

`readBalance()` reads the live balance straight out of `objValidationState.assocBalances` (or `aa_balances` table) with no averaging/smoothing: [1](#0-0) 

Obyte's own reference DeFi AA template, shipped in the repo's test fixtures as the canonical "Uniswap-like market maker" pattern, prices every swap purely off the instantaneous `balance[$asset]` / `balance[base]` ratio, with no TWAP or any other manipulation-resistant oracle: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

AA trigger execution order is fully deterministic and known ahead of time: triggers are collected per stabilized MCI and executed in a fixed order (`ORDER BY level, unit, address`), and a causal descendant of a given trigger is guaranteed to have a higher `level` and thus execute strictly after it: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

This gives any unprivileged AA trigger sender a fully deterministic sandwich primitive: post trigger A (large one-sided swap to skew the pool ratio) → observe/target victim's trigger V once broadcast but before it stabilizes → post trigger D that causally descends from V (so its `level` guarantees it executes after V within the same or a later MCI) reversing A's swap. Because ordering is deterministic and guaranteed by causal/level relationships rather than opaque miner ordering, this is *more* reliably exploitable than a typical Uniswap sandwich, not less.

### Title
Spot-balance-ratio pricing in AA autonomous agents enables deterministic sandwich attacks - (File: `formula/evaluation.js`, `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`)

### Summary
The oscript `balance[asset]` operator exposes the AA's live, un-averaged balance to any formula, and Obyte's own reference market-maker AA design uses this instantaneous balance ratio (`balance[$asset] / balance[base]`) as the exchange rate for every swap, with no TWAP or other manipulation-resistant pricing mechanism — the direct analog of pricing off Uniswap V3's `slot0`.

### Finding Description
`balance[...]` is evaluated by `readBalance()` in `formula/evaluation.js`, returning the current numeric balance of an asset held by an AA address at the moment the triggering unit is processed, with no historical averaging: [1](#0-0) 

The reference AMM-style AA pattern that Obyte ships as the canonical example for building a liquidity-pool style contract computes the swap output purely from this instantaneous ratio (`$p = $asset_balance * $bytes_balance`, then solving for the new balance) on every "exchange bytes to asset" / "exchange asset to bytes" case: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Any address can post a payment that triggers the AA (an "AA trigger sender"), so any address can move the pool ratio at will simply by sending a normal payment. Since AA trigger execution order is deterministic and derived from causal ordering (`level`) once an MCI stabilizes: [4](#0-3) 
an attacker can (1) send unit A that skews `balance[$asset]`/`balance[base]` in their favor, (2) let the victim's trigger V execute against the skewed ratio, then (3) send unit D that causally follows V (guaranteeing it is ordered after V) to reverse the skew and capture the difference. This is functionally identical to the sandwich mechanism described in the Uniswap `slot0` report, just replacing "MEV bot orders transactions within a block" with "attacker controls causal/level ordering across units within an MCI."

### Impact Explanation
Any user who deposits liquidity into, or swaps through, an AA that follows this officially-recommended balance-ratio pricing pattern can have their trade sandwiched, resulting in direct, measurable loss of funds (paying more or receiving less than the fair market rate) and a matching extraction of value by the attacker from the pool/other liquidity providers. This is material loss of funds analogous to the High-severity Maia finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: no privileged role is required — only the ability to send a standard payment to trigger the AA (available to any wallet holder), and the pool ratio is always readable via `balance[]`/`asset[cap]`-style getters before crafting the attack units. The deterministic level-based ordering of AA triggers removes the usual uncertainty MEV searchers face with miner ordering, making exploitation more reliable, not less, than the original Uniswap analog.

### Recommendation
- Do not use the instantaneous `balance[asset]` ratio directly as an exchange rate in AMM-style AAs; instead, maintain and use a state-var-backed time-weighted average price (TWAP) updated incrementally on each trade, with a minimum elapsed-time/adjustment-rate cap between updates.
- Document/flag this risk for AA authors building DeFi primitives on `balance[]`, and update the shipped `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` reference example to demonstrate TWAP-protected or slippage-limited pricing instead of raw spot-ratio pricing, since it is used as a template by third-party AA authors.

### Proof of Concept
1. AA author deploys the reference market-maker AA from `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, seeded with `asset`/`base` liquidity.
2. Attacker sends payment unit A with a large `asset`-input that heavily skews `$asset_balance / $bytes_balance` in the AA (case "exchange asset to bytes").
3. Victim broadcasts trigger V (a normal-sized swap) which, once it stabilizes, executes against the now-skewed ratio and receives an unfavorable amount.
4. Attacker crafts unit D with V as a causal ancestor (parent chain), guaranteeing `level(D) > level(V)`, so per the deterministic ordering in `main_chain.js`'s `handleAATriggers` query D's trigger executes after V's, reversing A's skew and realizing the sandwich profit while the victim is left with the unfavorable trade from step 3.
