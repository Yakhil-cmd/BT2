### Title
Divisible Private-Asset Payments Expose the Sender's Full Change Output (Address, Amount, Blinding) to the Payment Recipient - (File: divisible_asset.js)

### Summary
For divisible private assets, ocore builds a single, unredacted private-payment payload that contains *all* outputs of the payment (the recipient output **and** the sender's change output, each with full `address`, `amount`, `blinding`), and forwards that same payload both to the payment recipient and to the sender's cosigners with no per-recipient redaction. This mirrors the tfplan2md bug class: a rendering/forwarding path that fails hierarchical sensitivity masking, so a value that should stay hidden from a given viewer (the sender's own change address/amount/blinding) is disclosed to a party (the payment recipient) who should not see it.

### Finding Description
When composing a private divisible-asset payment, `composeDivisibleAssetPaymentJoint` builds one payload containing both the destination output and the change output, all with `address`/`blinding`/`amount` filled in and stores it in `assocPrivatePayloads`: [1](#0-0) 

After the joint is saved, `getSavingCallbacks.ifOk` derives `arrChains` from `arrPrivateElements` (each element wraps the same full, unredacted `payload`) and passes the identical array as **both** the recipient chains and the cosigner chains: [2](#0-1) 

This is unlike the indivisible-asset path, where `buildPrivateElementsChain` explicitly redacts the `address`/`blinding` fields of every output *other than* the one addressed to the specific chain holder, replacing them with an opaque `output_hash`: [3](#0-2) 

Because divisible assets have no equivalent per-output hiding, `wallet.js`'s `sendMultiPayment` preCommit logic sends the very same `arrChainsOfRecipientPrivateElements` (== `arrChainsOfCosignerPrivateElements`) to the recipient device via `walletGeneral.sendPrivatePayments`, and to the sender's cosigners via `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfWallets`/`forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfSharedAddresses`: [4](#0-3) 

`validation.js`'s output-format check for private outputs only enforces that a private payment declares exactly one "open" (unhidden) output when using the indivisible/output-hash scheme; for divisible assets (no `output_hash`), every output must carry `address`+`blinding`, i.e., all outputs are always fully open in the payload structure: [5](#0-4) 

`private_payment.js` explicitly documents/accepts this: "divisible outputs are never hidden individually," treating this as expected asset-payment behavior rather than flagging it as a leak: [6](#0-5) 

The net effect: the private-payment recipient (an unprivileged private-payment counterparty who only needs to know their own output) learns the sender's change address, the exact change amount, and the blinding factor used for that change output — information belonging to a UTXO they have no legitimate need to see. This directly parallels the CVE's rendering-path-specific failure of hierarchical sensitivity masking that leaked values that other, correctly-implemented paths (here, the indivisible-asset path) do mask.

### Impact Explanation
Disclosure of the sender's change address, its balance, and its blinding factor to every payment counterparty undermines the confidentiality guarantee of "private" assets (their entire purpose is to hide addresses/amounts from third parties). A single private divisible-asset payment leaks:
- The sender's change address (identity/address-reuse linkage across future transactions),
- The exact remaining balance held at that address,
- The blinding factor, which combined with the address and amount lets the recipient later verify/derive spend proofs for the sender's change UTXO and correlate it in subsequent private-chain messages they may receive.

This is a confidentiality break reachable by any private-payment counterparty (an unprivileged party who merely receives a private-asset payment), consistent with the "private payment chains" attack surface named in the analog rules. It does not by itself cause double-spend or fund loss, but it is a concrete, low-effort information-disclosure impact matching the Medium-severity class of the reference CVE (sensitive value exposure due to incomplete masking across code paths).

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers automatically, without any malicious action needed, on every ordinary divisible private-asset payment made through the standard wallet flow (`sendMultiPayment` → `composeDivisibleAssetPaymentJoint` → `getSavingCallbacks`). Any user who sends a private divisible asset with change will always leak the change output details to the recipient. Likelihood of occurrence is effectively 100% for this transaction type; likelihood of an adversary being positioned to benefit (i.e., being the recipient) is trivial since they are a normal payment counterparty.

### Recommendation
For divisible private-asset payments, build per-recipient views of the private payload analogous to what `buildPrivateElementsChain` does for indivisible assets: strip `address`/`blinding` of outputs that do not belong to the chain's intended holder before sending to the recipient (and, separately, before sending to cosigners who are not entitled to see the counterparty's destination output). If necessary, extend the divisible-asset payload with an `output_hash` commitment scheme (like the indivisible-asset one) so that receivers can validate an output was committed to without revealing its address/amount/blinding to unintended parties. At minimum, stop passing the identical `arrChains` object as both `arrRecipientChains` and `arrCosignerChains` in `divisible_asset.js`'s `getSavingCallbacks`, and construct genuinely distinct, redacted payloads for each audience.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice creates a private divisible asset and holds a single output of 1000 units on address `A`.
2. Alice sends 300 units of this asset to Bob's address `B` via `sendMultiPayment`, letting change (700 units) return to her own address `A2`.
3. `composeDivisibleAssetPaymentJoint` builds one payload: `outputs: [{address: B, amount: 300, blinding: b1}, {address: A2, amount: 700, blinding: b2}]` [1](#0-0) .
4. On save, `getSavingCallbacks.ifOk` calls `callbacks.ifOk(objJoint, arrChains, arrChains)` with the same unredacted chain for both recipient and cosigner delivery [7](#0-6) .
5. `wallet.js`'s `preCommitCb` sends `arrChainsOfRecipientPrivateElements` to Bob's device via `walletGeneral.sendPrivatePayments` [8](#0-7) .
6. Bob's client receives the full payload including `{address: A2, amount: 700, blinding: b2}` — Alice's private change address, exact remaining balance, and blinding factor — none of which Bob needed to validate his own 300-unit output.
