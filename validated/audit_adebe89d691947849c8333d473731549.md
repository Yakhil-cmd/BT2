### Title
AA payment atomicity lets a malicious asset issuer/attestor force perpetual bounce and permanent loss of AA-tracked funds/state - (File: `aa_composer.js`, `validation.js`)

### Summary
An Autonomous Agent's outgoing payment and its state-variable update are committed as a single atomic unit: if the payment message fails ocore's payment validation, `bounce()`/`revert()` rolls back **all** state changes for that trigger. Because a custom asset's `spender_attested`, `transfer_condition`, and `cosigned_by_definer` rules are enforced by a third party (attestor/definer) that the AA cannot control, that third party can perpetually make a specific output address invalid, guaranteeing the AA's payment fails and thus permanently blocking the corresponding state transition (e.g., marking a position/loan closed and returning collateral) - the same "counterparty refuses to accept the transfer to force the victim into a punishable/defaulted state" pattern described in the Cooler report, reachable purely through a normal trigger unit and standard oscript/asset primitives.

### Finding Description
When an AA constructs its response messages, payments are validated the same way as regular payments before the response unit is saved: `sendUnit()` in `aa_composer.js` builds the payment payload and then calls `validateAndSaveUnit`; any failure calls `bounce(err)`. [1](#0-0) 

If the failure happens after some balances/state have already been tentatively applied (or during secondary triggers), `revert()` performs a full rollback (`ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT initial_balances`, clearing `stateVars`) before bouncing: [2](#0-1) 

`bounce()` itself discards the intended state update (`objStateUpdate = null`) and only refunds the bounce fee back to the trigger address - none of the intended payment/state effects occur: [3](#0-2) 

The payment-payload validation that can trigger this bounce is controlled by third parties unrelated to the AA or the triggering user:

- `spender_attested` assets require every output address to be on the attestor's list; the attestor can add/remove addresses at will via `asset_attestors` messages, and validation simply rejects the payment if the recipient isn't attested: [4](#0-3) 

- `transfer_condition` (an arbitrary oscript/definition-style condition, potentially referencing another party's signature) must independently evaluate to true or the whole payment fails: [5](#0-4) 

- `cosigned_by_definer` similarly requires the definer's cooperation on any unit moving the asset: [6](#0-5) 

AAs are explicitly allowed to hold and forward arbitrary externally-defined assets with these flags (only AA-*defined* assets are restricted, per `aa_validation.js`), so any escrow/lending/marketplace-style AA that accepts a counterparty-issued asset as collateral and later needs to pay it back to the depositor is exposed: [7](#0-6) 

Since the payment message and the state-var message (e.g., "loan repaid", "collateral released") are always sent together in one AA response unit, a third party who is the issuer/attestor/definer of the deposited asset can simply keep the depositor's address unattested (or otherwise fail the condition), guaranteeing `bounce()` every single time the depositor tries to trigger the repay/withdraw path. All associated state changes are discarded, so the depositor is left permanently in the "not yet repaid" state, exactly mirroring the Cooler scenario where the borrower is forced into default because the counterparty refuses to accept a transfer.

### Impact Explanation
This lets a malicious asset issuer/attestor/definer (i.e., someone who fully controls how their own asset is transferred) permanently deny state-changing operations of any AA that uses their asset for value transfer between users. In lending/escrow-style AA designs this translates directly into: forced default, permanent freezing of a user's collateral inside the AA, and inability for the AA logic to ever mark the position as closed/repaid - concrete AA fund freezing / loss for the victim while providing leverage for the attacker (e.g., subsequently triggering a "liquidate/claim collateral" path elsewhere in the AA).

### Likelihood Explanation
High for any AA that is designed to accept externally-defined, non-base assets (a documented and supported ocore/AA feature) as collateral or payment and later needs to return/forward that same asset to a specific address. The attestor/definer role is a normal part of the asset's own definition (`spender_attested`, `transfer_condition`, `cosigned_by_definer`), so no additional protocol bug is needed - the attacker just declines to attest/cosign/sign, which is entirely within their rights as asset issuer, and every subsequent trigger will keep failing atomically.

### Recommendation
- Document/require that AAs performing atomic "unlock and forward" flows with counterparty-issued assets treat outgoing payments defensively: e.g., separate the state transition (marking debt/loan as settled) from the payment step, or place the payment in a fail-open branch that still commits state progress even if the transfer bounces, with an explicit later withdrawal step the depositor can retry.
- Consider exposing to AA authors a safer "try-payment" primitive/pattern that does not couple irreversible state progress to third-party-controlled transfer conditions, or add guidance in the AA validation to warn when `messages` include payments of assets whose `spender_attested`/`transfer_condition`/`cosigned_by_definer` are not controlled by the AA itself.
- At minimum, clearly document this atomicity/rollback behavior (`bounce`/`revert` in `aa_composer.js`) as a known risk for AA authors building financial primitives (loans, escrows, marketplaces) on top of externally issued assets, since it is inherent to `sendUnit`'s all-or-nothing execution model.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker/asset issuer defines asset `X` with `is_transferrable: true, spender_attested: true, attestors: [attacker_addr]`.
2. Victim deposits asset `X` into an AA `L` that implements an escrow/lending pattern: `L` records the deposit in a state var and, on a later "repay"/"close" trigger from the victim, sends `X` back to `trigger.address` (the victim) in the same response unit that updates state (`var['status'] = 'closed'`).
3. Attacker (the attestor) never attests, or de-attests, the victim's address via an `asset_attestors` message.
4. Victim posts the "repay"/"close" trigger unit. `sendUnit()` builds the payment message for asset `X`; `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` rejects it with "some output addresses are not attested" because the victim's address is not in `arrAttestedAddresses`. [8](#0-7) 
5. `bounce(err)` fires; `objStateUpdate` is discarded and (if any prior state had been tentatively applied) `revert()` rolls back via `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT initial_balances`. [2](#0-1) 
6. The victim's deposit remains locked in `L`, and `L`'s state var still shows the position as open/outstanding - regardless of how many times the victim retries, since the attestor controls the outcome. If `L` (or another AA) has a separate expiry/liquidation trigger reachable by anyone once a deadline passes, the attacker can then claim the victim's collateral, reproducing the "forced default" outcome from the original report.
