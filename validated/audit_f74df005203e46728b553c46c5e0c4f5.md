### Title
Whale-scale debt book overflows i128 during accrual, permanently freezing all market operations - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
`accrue_step` values the market's debt by computing `borrowed * new_borrow_index` in i128 (`calculate_supplier_rewards`, `common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`). The borrow-index ceiling `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is applied to the index, but the debt *value* (`borrowed` scaled shares times the index) can exceed `i128::MAX` long before the index reaches that ceiling. Once the product overflows, `Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow` (33). Because every pool verb runs `interest::global_sync` first ("Every verb accrues first"), the panic bricks the market: no `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, or `update_indexes` can ever execute again for that `(hub, asset)` book.

### Finding Description
The MySQL CVE is a low-privileged, reachable complete-DoS. The analog here is a state-driven arithmetic trap reachable through ordinary unprivileged entrypoints:

- `update_borrow_index` multiplies `old_index.mul(interest_factor)` and only clamps *after* the product is formed; it caps the index, not the debt valuation (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`).
- `calculate_supplier_rewards` then computes `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` (`index.rs:80-81`). With a book near the admitted cap maximum (~170 billion whole-token equivalents of scaled debt) and a steep utilization curve, this product leaves i128 while the index is still far under `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
- The repo's own stress test proves the freeze: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` drives a `BILLION * 10^18` market to 98% utilization, observes `MathOverflow` in `update_indexes`, and then shows `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-356`).

The docs themselves acknowledge the hole: "Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those operations accrue first. At the borrow-index ceiling, further accrual produces no borrower interest. No dedicated ceiling alarm is emitted" (`docs/reference/formulas.md:432-437`).

An attacker constructs the state with `supply`/`borrow` at scale (or inherits it as organic growth), then any single subsequent `update_indexes`/`supply`/`borrow` call commits the last representable index; the next accrual attempt traps forever. No privileged action can recover it — `update_params` and even `recapitalize` accrue first too, and `upgrade` is the only escape, which is an admin fix, not a code path.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for an entire market: all supplier balances and all borrower collateral routed through that `(hub, asset)` book become unreachable, since `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `claim_revenue` all begin with accrual and all hit the same `MathOverflow` panic. The pool's `cash` bookkeeping and real token balance remain intact but are unspendable through any controller entrypoint. This is the accepted "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate" impact class, matching the CVE's complete-DoS shape.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-moderate likelihood, hence Medium rather than High:

- Requires a debt book approaching the ray-value domain limit (~10^38 ray value), i.e., markets sized near the admitted cap maximum or low-decimal/expensive-asset combinations, plus sustained high utilization on a steep curve so the index compounds before governance intervenes. The stress fixture needed a billion-token 18-decimal market at 98% utilization on a 175% max-rate curve.
- Once the condition is crossed it is permanent and needs no further attacker action — time alone (accrual on the next call) triggers it. There is no on-chain alarm and no self-healing path; the write-down floor (`SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`) does not apply on the borrow side.
- Not exploitable for profit; it destroys value rather than extracting it, so incentive is limited to griefing. A passive drift into the cliff is also possible organically on an unmanaged market ("an untouched market drifts upward… crosses the optimal point after about ten years and runs away," per the test docs).

### Recommendation
- Bound the operation, not just the index: before evaluating `calculate_supplier_rewards`, clamp `new_borrow_index` to `min(computed, MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX-safe divisor for borrowed)` — i.e., compute `safe_index = min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, saturating_div(i128::MAX, borrowed))` in `update_borrow_index` or `accrue_step` so `borrowed * safe_index` always fits i128.
- Alternatively, route the debt valuation through the existing `I256` widened path used elsewhere (`mul_div_floor_saturating` already exists in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`) and saturate the *index* at the largest value that keeps `supplied * index` and `borrowed * index` representable.
- Emit an event when the index hits the value-implied ceiling so integrators/keepers can flag the market before the freeze, and add a governance runbook step to wind down utilization before the cliff.
- Optionally enforce a dynamic effective supply/borrow cap in `validate`/entry checks so `supplied * MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` and `borrowed * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cannot exceed the i128 domain, rather than relying on the loose per-decimal cap maximum (`max_cap_for_decimals`).

### Proof of Concept
The repo ships an executable PoC; an attacker (or natural drift) reproduces it as:

1. Attacker calls `controller.supply` on an 18-decimal market with caps raised to the admitted maximum (`max_cap_for_decimals`), depositing `10^9 * 10^18` base units, then `controller.borrow` to reach 98% utilization against their own collateral — all unprivileged entrypoints.
2. Time advances (or repeated calls compound). Each `controller.update_indexes` (also unprivileged; the keeper calls it with no role check) runs `global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `update_borrow_index`/`calculate_supplier_rewards`.
3. At the first accrual where `borrowed * new_borrow_index > i128::MAX`, `Ray::mul` panics `MathOverflow`. The last committed state still has `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. Every subsequent call touching the market — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `update_indexes`, `claim_revenue`, `recapitalize` — accrues first and panics identically. All supplier and collateral funds in that book are permanently frozen (confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:354-356`).

Note: I verified the panic sites and the freezing test directly, but did not exhaustively trace `accrue_step`'s caller wiring or whether any pool verb skips accrual; the README's flow statement ("Each mutation of an existing market runs this sequence: … `interest::global_sync` …") and the test's demonstrated `withdraw`/`repay` failures support the claim.
