### Title
`liquidate` has no expected-outcome bound: a liquidator's debt repayment can settle while every seizure leg rounds to zero or is dropped - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs)

### Summary
The Mangrove issue class is "no protection for keepers": an execution path accepts a keeper's funds while the outcome the keeper priced off-chain may not materialize, with no on-chain check of an expected result. XOXNO Lending's `liquidate` has the same shape. The liquidator authenticates and submits `debt_payments`, but the entrypoint takes no `min_seized` / expected-seizure argument. The plan is recomputed at execution time from live `Context` prices and indexes, and legs whose seizure rounds to zero are silently dropped. In the edge cases the controller still pulls the accepted repayment and burns the borrower's debt while the liquidator receives nothing.

### Finding Description
`build_liquidation_plan` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs:14-97`) normalizes the offered `debt_payments` against a freshly computed risk snapshot, then calls `calculate_seized_collateral`. In `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs:419-430`, a leg whose `seizure_ray` or `capped_ray` rounds to zero is skipped with `continue`, producing a `seized_collaterals` vector that is an ordered subsequence of the supply positions — possibly empty. `LiquidationPlan::validate` rejects zero-amount entries and negative fees, but nothing in the public `liquidate(liquidator, account_id, debt_payments, seize_mode)` signature lets the caller bind the outcome (no minimum seizure, no maximum payment-WAD, no expected-plan hash).

The protocol's own documentation confirms the reachable loss case: "The contract accepts a liquidation that repays debt and seizes nothing, so the liquidator would pay and receive no collateral" (`skills/xoxno-lending-liquidations/SKILL.md:178-180`), and INV-LIQ-02 states "rounding can leave repayment with no payable seizure" (`docs/reference/invariants.md:421-422`). The unit test `an_expensive_low_decimal_collateral_makes_a_floor_sized_liquidation_seize_nothing` (`contracts/controller/tests/positions/liquidation_math.rs:2361-2402`) demonstrates a listed 3-decimal collateral at a high unit price where a liquidation's whole seizure floors to zero units and every leg is dropped while "the repayment still settles and the debt is still burned". The honeypot dynamics also exist: the plan depends on live collateral balances, prices, and indexes, so between `get_liquidation_estimate` and execution a front-running repayment, withdrawal (where HF permits), price update, or competing liquidation changes which legs exist and what they pay, with no revert bound selectable by the liquidator. Empty-plan cases (all legs dropped with `unbacked_usd > 0`) do revert `InvalidPayments` via `plan.rs:76-79`, but the zero-seizure-with-settlement path above does not.

### Impact Explanation
Theft/loss of liquidator funds: a liquidator's debt-token payment is pulled and the borrower's debt is burned while the liquidator receives zero collateral. More broadly, every liquidation executes an outcome the caller could not bound on-chain, so a keeper relying on an estimate can systematically overpay relative to received seizure in edge cases (whole-unit rounding, fee-equal-to-seizure Credit dust legs, dropped legs).

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged liquidator calling `liquidate`. The loss cases require specific geometry (low-decimal high-unit-value collateral, dust-sized repayments whose seizure rounds to zero, or a state change between estimate and execution), which narrows frequency but does not require privileged action — a borrower can also arrange collateral composition that produces sub-unit or zero-share seizure legs. Mitigations exist off-chain (the protocol instructs liquidators to reject estimates with empty `seized_collaterals`), matching the Mangrove pattern where protection is left to caller-side wrappers rather than enforced by the contract. Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Add an expected-outcome parameter to `liquidate`, e.g. `min_seized: Vec<(HubAsset, i128)>` or a `min_total_seized_wad`, checked after `build_liquidation_plan` and before any transfers, reverting `InvalidPayments` when the realized plan is below the bound. Alternatively (or additionally) reject any plan where `repayment.repay_usd > 0` and `seized_collaterals` is empty, so a repayment can never settle with no payable seizure.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists (or an existing listed asset is priced such that) a collateral with `asset_decimals < MIN_BORROWABLE_ASSET_DECIMALS` and unit value large relative to the liquidation size — see the harness fixture in `liquidation_math.rs:2361-2402` (3 decimals, price at `MAX_REASONABLE_PRICE_WAD`).
2. A borrower supplies only that collateral and borrows to HF < 1.
3. Liquidator calls `get_liquidation_estimate(account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)`; the returned `seized_collaterals` may show legs, or the liquidator submits a dust-sized `debt_payments` leg without checking.
4. `liquidate` executes: `calculate_seized_collateral` floors each seizure below one whole unit / zero shares and drops the legs (`math.rs:419-430`), the repayment is pulled via the authorized token transfers and the borrower's debt shares are burned.
5. Result: liquidator's token balance decreased by the repaid amount; liquidator (or its Credit receiver) received zero collateral; no revert occurs. The same transaction submitted through a wrapper with a `min_seized` bound would revert instead.
