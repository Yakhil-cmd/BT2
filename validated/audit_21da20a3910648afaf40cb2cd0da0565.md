### Title
Attacker-controlled Aquarius LP collateral can block liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs))

### Summary

A borrower can supply an Aquarius LP token while its oracle is valid, then withdraw their own liquidity from that Aquarius pool until its total value falls below the configured `min_pool_value_wad`. The LP price read then returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, and because liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` fetch prices for every supply position before acting, the single unpriceable leg reverts the entire risk calculation and prevents the account from being liquidated or cleaned up.

### Finding Description

`supply` validates only that each submitted asset is collateralizable and unhalted, transfers the measured amount to the pool, and credits the corresponding supply shares; there is no continuing invariant that every collateral leg remains priceable after admission. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

For an Aquarius LP source, the price aggregator resolves both underlying legs, reads live pool reserves and total shares, derives fair LP value, and rejects the read when total pool value is below `lp.min_pool_value_wad`. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`calculate_account_risk_totals` first loads market data for all supply and borrow assets, then dereferences a required price for every supply position. [5](#0-4)  A failed aggregator resolution is converted into a contract error by `force`, so one unusable collateral price aborts the entire calculation. [6](#0-5) 

`liquidate` calls this all-position valuation before checking `health_factor < 1`. [7](#0-6)  `clean_bad_debt` performs the same all-position valuation before testing whether debt exceeds collateral and collateral is below the dust cap. [8](#0-7) 

The borrower cannot simply remove the shielding leg while unhealthy because `withdraw` performs post-pool solvency valuation after the withdrawal mutation, and that valuation again reads all remaining supply and debt prices. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation

An underwater borrower can temporarily prevent all liquidations and permissionless bad-debt cleanup for the duration of the induced Aquarius liquidity failure. While the account remains open, debt can continue accruing and neither its healthy collateral nor the shielding LP collateral can be seized through the normal liquidation path; if the borrower maintains the pool below the configured minimum, the account can remain frozen until the pool is recapitalized or governance uses a privileged recovery path. This is attacker-controlled availability loss and can turn an otherwise liquidatable position into protocol bad debt.

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires the account owner to hold enough liquidity in a listed Aquarius pool to withdraw the pool below `min_pool_value_wad`, which is most feasible for thin pools or pools dominated by the borrower’s own liquidity. It uses only permissionless actions: supplying a listed collateral, ordinary Aquarius liquidity withdrawal, and subsequent liquidation or cleanup attempts by unprivileged callers. The attacker sacrifices access to the deposited collateral while maintaining the condition, so the technique is economically rational primarily when the avoided liquidation loss exceeds that cost.

### Recommendation

Use liquidation-specific valuation that treats an unreadable supply leg as zero-value collateral instead of aborting the entire account valuation, while continuing to require strict prices for debt legs. Apply the same fallback or an equivalent exclusion mechanism to `clean_bad_debt` so one unpriceable collateral position cannot indefinitely block account cleanup. At minimum, add a permissionless path that removes or neutralizes an invalid collateral leg without making protocol recovery depend on the attacker restoring liquidity.

### Proof of Concept

1. The attacker creates or controls account `A`, supplies normal collateral, and calls `borrow(caller=attacker, account_id=A, borrows=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:DEBT}, debt_amount)], to=None)` to establish debt. [11](#0-10) 
2. While the Aquarius LP oracle is valid, the attacker calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=A, spoke_id=S, assets=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:AQUARIUS_LP}, lp_amount)])`, adding the LP token as a collateral leg. [12](#0-11) 
3. Using LP shares retained outside the lending account, the attacker withdraws liquidity directly from the configured Aquarius pool until its reserve-derived pool value is below `min_pool_value_wad`. [13](#0-12) 
4. After the normal collateral price falls enough to make `A` unhealthy, a liquidator calls `liquidate(liquidator=L, account_id=A, debt_payments=[(HubAssetKey{hub_id:H, asset:DEBT}, repay_amount)], seize_mode=SeizeMode::Transfer)`. [14](#0-13) 
5. `build_liquidation_plan` calls `calculate_account_risk_totals`, which requests a strict price for the LP leg; the aggregator returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, causing the liquidation to revert before the health-factor check or repayment. [15](#0-14) [16](#0-15) 
6. Any caller then calls `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id=A)`; it reaches the same all-position risk valuation and reverts on the same unusable LP price before the dust-cap gate can be evaluated. [8](#0-7)
