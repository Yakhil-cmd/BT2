### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Reasoning:** `get-egroup` at [1](#0-0)  takes only a `mask` (a `uint` bitmask derived from the borrower's own asset/collateral bitmap) and resolves it via `.v0-egroup resolve mask` — it has no principal argument and no involvement in fund routing or delivery at all. It cannot be "routed through a principal that always rejects delivery" because it never touches a principal.

In `liquidate` at [2](#0-1) , `get-egroup` is called only to fetch liquidation LTV/penalty parameters for the borrower's asset mask — it is unrelated to who receives collateral. The only principal that can reject a transfer is `collateral-receiver`, an *optional* principal chosen by the liquidator (attacker A) themself: `(actual-receiver (match collateral-receiver recv recv liquidator))` at [3](#0-2) . If that receiver rejects the transfer, the `try!` on `collateral-remove` fails and the whole `liquidate` call reverts — the attacker only harms themselves (a failed liquidation, no funds moved), and the borrower B's collateral position is unaffected since the transaction aborts atomically.

There is no path here where attacker A's transaction changes what a distinct victim B can withdraw: the borrower does not receive any "mandatory payout" through `get-egroup`, and the only externally-supplied receiver is the attacker's own choice, whose failure only reverts the attacker's own call.
