### Title
Bad-debt write-down clamps `supply_index` to a nonzero floor instead of zeroing supplier claims, leaving wiped-out suppliers with phantom claims that drain future depositors - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The GFS2 bug class — flipping a flag/mode without invalidating the stale representation of the underlying objects — maps onto `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in the pool. When socialized bad debt meets or exceeds the total supplied value, the correct end state is "all supply claims are worth zero." Instead, the code computes a zero `reduction_factor` but clamps the new `supply_index` up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`). The `supplied` scaled-share total and every account's `scaled_amount` are left untouched, so each supplier's claim resurrects at the floored index — a stale representation that should have been truncated to zero, exactly the kernel's "cannot mix the two representations" failure.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining = 0`, so `reduction_factor = 0` and `new_supply_index = 0` — but line 88 applies `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`, raising the index back to `RAY/1000`. No shares are burned: `supplied` (including protocol revenue shares) keeps its full scaled amount.

The pool's own test proves the exploit end-to-end: `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:372-427` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`) shows that after such a wipeout, `unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled) > 0`, a fresh deposit mints correctly-priced shares, and `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` returns `gross == fresh_cash` — the stranded claim drains exactly the fresh depositor's tokens, leaving the fresh supplier unbacked.

Reachability is permissionless: `clean_bad_debt` (controller entrypoint, callable by anyone once collateral ≤ dust) routes borrow-side legs through `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:35-47`) into `pool_seize_positions_call`, which reaches `ops/seize.rs:24-27` and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. The deposit side is reclassified as revenue via `absorb_supply_as_revenue`, so the account's own residual collateral also stays inside `supplied` at the floored index.

The trigger requires one market's bad debt to exceed its total supplied value — e.g., a highly utilized market where accrued interest pushes a defaulted debt past `supplied * supply_index`, or a small market fully drained by an insolvent borrower. The docs acknowledge the residual-claim hazard (`docs/reference/invariants.md`, INV-IDX-02: "The lower bound prevents a zero conversion divisor but can preserve an unbacked residual claim"), but do not document or test that the phantom claim pays out real tokens to the wiped supplier.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Any holder of legacy scaled supply shares — including a griefer who deliberately supplies before triggering `clean_bad_debt` — can withdraw real tokens they are no longer entitled to, paid for by whoever supplies the market next. Because `supplied` was never reduced, the phantom claims re-enter every subsequent interest accrual and bad-debt socialization at the floored index, permanently corrupting the market's accounting.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market where a single `clean_bad_debt` (or borrow-side seizure) writes down debt ≥ total supplied value. This is achievable in a thin market with high utilization and accrued interest, or where a borrower was over-leveraged relative to that market's book via cross-collateral. The attacker can farm the position themselves: supply into a near-empty market, wait for/force an insolvency cleanup, then withdraw against the floored index after an honest depositor recapitalizes the pool. `update_indexes` and normal interest accrual can also push `borrowed` above `supplied * index` over time, lowering the bar.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value`, treat the market as fully wiped: burn or zero `supplied`/`revenue` (or store a wipeout flag that forces `unscale_supply_*` to return 0) instead of preserving claims at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alternatively, record a per-market epoch/generation on wipeout so pre-wipeout `scaled_amount`s are unscalable — mirroring the kernel fix's address-space truncation. Add a regression test asserting that after a complete write-down, `resolve_withdrawal` on legacy shares yields zero before any fresh supply is accepted.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market M has `supplied = S` scaled shares, `supply_index ≈ RAY`, cash fully borrowed.
2. Attacker's account accrues debt `D > S * index / RAY` (interest or a fresh flash-grown position) and becomes insolvent with dust-level collateral.
3. Anyone calls `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` → `pool.seize_positions` with `side = Borrow` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000` (interest.rs:88), while `supplied = S` is unchanged.
4. Victim supplies `X` tokens; receives `X * RAY / floor = 1000X` scaled shares.
5. Attacker (or any legacy supplier) calls `withdraw` with `scaled_amount` from step 1: `unscale_supply_floor(S) = S * RAY/1000 / RAY > 0` real tokens are paid out from the victim's `X`, exactly as `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` demonstrates (`gross == fresh_cash`, `cache.cash() < fresh_claim`).
