### Title
Self-inflicted Aquarius LP price outage via `min_pool_value_wad` floor makes a borrower unliquidatable and blocks bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
An Aquarius LP share used as collateral is priced from the underlying pool's live reserves; if the pool's total value drops below the configured `min_pool_value_wad` floor, the aggregator returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` and the controller fails closed. Because liquidation and bad-debt cleanup must price **every** supply leg of the account, any unprivileged liquidity provider of that Aquarius pool — including the borrower — can withdraw pool value below the floor and permanently shield the position from liquidation and `clean_bad_debt`. This is the availability analog of CVE-2018-3282 (induced hang/DoS) mapped onto XOXNO Lending's oracle-dependent liquidation path, and it is explicitly acknowledged in the threat model (DoS.1) and the ops alert `LendingLpPoolValueNearFloor` ("Under 1.0x the LP price is rejected and accounts holding this leg become unliquidatable").

### Finding Description
- `AquariusLpSource` carries `min_pool_value_wad`, a hard liquidity floor for the underlying pool [1](#0-0) .
- In `providers::aquarius::read`, after computing `price_wad` and `total_shares`, the pool's WAD value is compared to the floor and the read errors out below it [2](#0-1) . The error is confirmed by the aggregator tests (`InsufficientAquariusLiquidity`, invalid resolution status) [3](#0-2) .
- The controller prices every collateral leg when building risk totals and the seizure plan (`cached_price` per `supply_positions` entry) [4](#0-3) . A failed leg price reverts the whole call, exactly like a stale feed does (`poc_stale_oracle_blocks_liquidation`) [5](#0-4) .
- `clean_bad_debt` walks all supply/debt legs and seizes positions via `pool_seize_positions_call`, but eligibility and totals still require the same account-wide pricing, so the same poisoned leg blocks socialization [6](#0-5) .
- Crucially, `supply` needs no price (threat model DoS.1), so a borrower can add a dust leg of any listed collateral — including an Aquarius LP share — at near-zero cost [7](#0-6) .

### Impact Explanation
Once the LP pool's value sits below `min_pool_value_wad`, every `liquidate` call against the account reverts, `clean_bad_debt` cannot run, and the governed `force_socialize_bad_debt` path is likewise valuation-dependent. The result is a permanently unliquidatable, unsocializable bad-debt position: suppliers in the debt market cannot recover backing and the protocol accrues unbounded interest on debt that can never be closed — protocol insolvency / permanent freezing of supplier funds. The condition persists as long as any LP keeps the pool under the floor, and the borrower (as a pool LP) can hold it there indefinitely at the cost of their own dust collateral. This maps the CVE's "hang or crash (complete DOS)" class onto a reachable, user-triggered denial of liquidation.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged: the attacker needs only (1) an open borrow position, (2) enough Aquarius LP share to register a supply leg via `supply`/`supply_to_account`, and (3) sufficient LP ownership (or coordination with natural LP exits) to push the pool under `min_pool_value_wad`. Thin pools make step 3 cheap; the floor exists precisely because low-value pools are expected. No privileged role, oracle manipulation within bands, or timing assumption is required — the mechanism is the protocol's own fail-closed floor. The one mitigation is that the attacker must already be underwater (HF < 1) for liquidation to matter, but that is the normal precondition of the attack class.

### Recommendation
- Do not let a single unpriceable collateral leg brick the whole account: skip or floor-to-zero legs whose price resolution fails with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` in `calculate_account_risk_totals`/`calculate_seized_collateral`, so remaining priced collateral can still be seized and debt repaid.
- Allow `clean_bad_debt` to proceed using a last-known-good or zero valuation for the failed leg so the book can be written down rather than frozen.
- Alternatively, give `clean_bad_debt`/governance a per-leg exclusion path (e.g., seize the unpriceable leg as shares at zero value instead of requiring its price), and monitor `LendingLpPoolValueNearFloor` to de-list LP collateral before it can be weaponized.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists Aquarius LP share `LP(A,B)` as collateral in a spoke, with `AquariusLpSource { pool, min_pool_value_wad: F }`.
2. Attacker supplies collateral `C` and borrows asset `D` near max LTV; also adds a dust `supply` leg of `LP(A,B)` (supply requires no price).
3. Attacker (a liquidity provider in `pool`) calls the pool's `withdraw` until `price_wad * total_shares / share_unit < F`; `aquarius::read` now returns `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` on every strict read.
4. Attacker lets the position drift below HF = 1 (interest accrual or a small price move in `D`).
5. Any liquidator calling `liquidate(liquidator, attacker, payments, ...)` reverts during risk-total computation; `get_liquidation_estimate` reports the account unactionable.
6. When the account decays to bad debt, `clean_bad_debt(attacker_account_id)` also reverts because totals require the failed leg price; the debt book keeps accruing and cannot be written down, leaving supplier claims permanently unbacked until someone organically refills the Aquarius pool above the floor — which the attacker can prevent by re-withdrawing.
