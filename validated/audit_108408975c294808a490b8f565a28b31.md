### Title
Interest accrual overflows RAY token value before the borrow-index cap, permanently freezing a market - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
A sufficiently large, highly utilized market can reach the protocol’s `i128` RAY-value limit before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is reached. The next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original`, so every operation that first synchronizes the market—including withdrawal, repayment, and liquidation—reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
`update_indexes` is permissionless and accrues each requested market. [2](#0-1)  The controller also loads market indexes before calculating collateral and debt, so a failed accrual propagates into the risk calculation used by exits, repayment, and liquidation. [3](#0-2)  The deterministic coverage reproduces the cliff on a 1-billion-whole-unit, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization: accrual eventually returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent withdraw and repay calls fail identically. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the value ceiling is reached, suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot liquidate positions touching that market. [5](#0-4)  Because the index cap never engages, the condition is not repaired by waiting for another index update; the market remains bricked unless code or state is changed outside the listed user paths. [6](#0-5)  This is permanent freezing of user funds in the affected market and prevents ordinary insolvency resolution. [7](#0-6) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the condition requires an unusually large supplied book, sustained near-maximum utilization, and extended accrual; the reproduced case uses a 1-billion-token 18-decimal supply and roughly 98% borrowed. [8](#0-7)  No privileged role, leaked key, oracle manipulation, or external service failure is required after market parameters permit the position: the attacker can use `supply` for the collateral and borrowed market legs, `borrow` for the debt, and permissionless `update_indexes` to advance accrual. [9](#0-8)  The practical constraint is whether configured supply/borrow caps and available liquidity admit the required book; the code-level failure itself is deterministic once those values are reached. [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Bound scaled RAY amounts and accrued token values below the `i128` conversion domain, or make accrual saturate gracefully before `scaled_to_original` can panic. Cap checks should account for both the configured token cap and the worst-case index growth required to unscale that amount, rather than checking only the nominal token amount. Add a regression asserting the index cap engages before the value ceiling and that repay, withdraw, and liquidation remain callable at the configured maximum book.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create a large 18-decimal market and a separate collateral market.
2. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` units of the debt asset and borrow approximately 98% of it against sufficient attacker collateral.
3. Advance ledger time and repeatedly call permissionless `update_indexes` for the debt market.
4. Accrual reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while the stored borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
5. Calls to `withdraw` and `repay` on the same market then also revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, demonstrating that neither suppliers nor borrowers can exit. [10](#0-9)
