### Title
Bad-debt wipeout clamps `supply_index` up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving wiped suppliers a phantom claim that drains future deposits — (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When socialized bad debt equals or exceeds the total supplied value of a market, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` should drive every supply claim to zero. Instead it clamps the resulting supply index *up* to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`), so pre-existing supply shares retain a positive residual claim. That claim is masked only while `cash == 0`; once any new supplier deposits, the wiped position can withdraw real tokens against value that no longer exists, stealing the fresh deposit.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` computes `new_supply_index = supply_index * (total_supplied_value - capped_bad_debt) / total_supplied_value` and then applies `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))` at line 88:

```rust
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is 0, `reduction_factor` is 0, `new_supply_index` is 0 — and the `max` lifts it back to `RAY/1000`. Every scaled supply share `S_old` now unscales to `floor(S_old * RAY/1000 / 10^d) > 0` instead of 0. No shares are burned and no compensating bookkeeping occurs.

The function is reachable by any unprivileged address through the permissionless seizure path: controller `clean_bad_debt` / liquidation emits `PoolSeizeEntry` with `side = Borrow`, and `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` lines 24-27 calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` then `burn_debt` and `commit()` — with no `require_backed_market` or share-reset guard on the borrow side:

```rust
AccountPositionType::Borrow => {
    let bad_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position);
    interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, bad_debt);
    cache.burn_debt(position);
}
```

The project's own tests in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`, `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`) demonstrate the mechanics: after a wipeout clamp, a wiped supplier's stranded claim exactly equals a subsequent fresh deposit, and resolving a full withdrawal against the old shares pays out real cash, leaving `cash < fresh_supplier_claim` — insolvency borne by the new depositor.

A full wipeout is attainable: `clean_bad_debt` on a fully-underwater position, or a liquidation seize where collateral is exhausted while debt remains (`AccountPositionType::Borrow` entries socialize the remainder). The deposit-side leg (`absorb_supply_as_revenue`) likewise leaves the survivor's shares unburned, amplifying the residual.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and protocol insolvency. Any holder of supply shares in a wiped market retains a phantom claim `≈ position/1000` in index terms; after fresh liquidity enters, `withdraw` pays it from real cash. The fresh depositor's claim becomes unbacked (`supplied_claim > cash + debt`), permanently freezing or losing their funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market wipeout via bad-debt socialization — an extreme but reachable state (e.g., a low-liquidity spoke whose entire supply is lent to one defaulted borrower, then `clean_bad_debt`). Post-wipeout, the attacker's residual claim is passive: it converts to profit the moment anyone supplies, and `require_reserves` is the only check, which passes once fresh cash exists.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value`, reset all supply shares rather than clamping the index: burn `supplied` and `revenue` to zero (or zero the index and gate all share-based unscaling on a non-zero index). Alternatively, clamp the index only while tracking that the position is wiped — e.g., store a `wiped` flag or rebase shares via `supplied = supplied * reduction_factor` so the floor is never needed. At minimum, skip the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` max when `remaining == 0` and zero out `supplied`/`revenue` in `seize::apply` on the borrow side.

### Proof of Concept
Mirrors `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-494`:

1. Attacker supplies into market X; a borrower borrows the full supply and defaults (or attacker self-liquidates a max-LTV position after a price move within oracle bands).
2. Call `clean_bad_debt` (permissionless): pool runs `seize::apply` with `side = Borrow`; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `remaining = 0`, then clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`. Attacker's shares persist.
3. Victim supplies `D` tokens into market X; pool `cash = D`.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw(0)` (withdraw-all). `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, attacker_scaled)` returns `gross = floor(attacker_scaled * RAY/1000 / 10^d) > 0`; `require_reserves(gross)` passes against `D`.
5. Pool pays `gross` from victim's deposit; victim's `unscale_supply_floor` claim now exceeds `cash` — funds stolen / claim unbacked.

Caveat: I could not fully confirm within available iterations whether `cache.commit()` or the controller's seize batch applies an additional post-wipeout guard (e.g., zeroing `supplied`) beyond the raw-cache path shown; the committed-state behavior in `seize.rs` shows none, and the tests assert the invariant is "masked only by `require_reserves`".
