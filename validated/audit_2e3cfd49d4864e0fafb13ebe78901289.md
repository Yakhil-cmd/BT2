### Title
RAY-scaled value overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market — `update_indexes` panics before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs, exercised via contracts/pool/src/interest.rs `global_sync`)

### Summary
The InnoDB advisory class is a crash/DoS: an operation that panics and wedges the service. The analog in XOXNO Lending is the accrual path in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`: every pool verb first calls `global_sync`, which calls `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26`). When `scaled_shares * index` exceeds the RAY-value domain (i128 fixed-point ceiling), the multiply panics with `MathOverflow`. The borrow index cap `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` never engages because the value overflow fires first, so the panic is terminal: every subsequent `supply`/`borrow`/`withdraw`/`repay`/`liquidate`/`update_indexes` call re-runs accrual and re-panics.

### Finding Description
- `global_sync` runs at the top of every market-touching op; it chunks elapsed time and calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:26-53`).
- `scaled_to_original` multiplies total scaled shares by the index; utilization itself recomputes this product (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-26`), so even the rate fetch panics once the ceiling is crossed.
- The liquidation path is equally bricked: `build_liquidation_plan` recomputes risk totals from indexes (`contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs:34-44`), so bad debt cannot be unwound and suppliers cannot exit — funds are permanently frozen, not merely delayed.
- The protocol's own test demonstrates the cliff: at ~98% sustained utilization on the XLM curve with ~10^27-scale supply (a billion 18-decimal units), accrual panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` after index growth past ~170×, while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — i.e. the safety cap is dead code on this path (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-360).

### Impact Explanation
Once crossed, the cliff is irreversible from unprivileged entrypoints: `update_indexes` (public), `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt` all accrue first and panic. All supplied assets in that market are permanently frozen; the book can neither service exits nor liquidations, matching the "hang/crash → complete DoS" class of ALPINE-CVE-2018-3277 but with permanent rather than restartable impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
Not attacker-on-demand: reaching it requires whale-scale principal (≈10^27 raw units at 18 decimals) at near-max utilization sustained for many years — so it is a latent Medium rather than a high-frequency exploit. However, no privileged action is needed to push it over the edge once market state approaches the bound: a single unprivileged `update_indexes` call crosses it, and the cap that was designed to prevent it does not fire.

### Recommendation
Cap `borrow_index` growth inside `accrue_step`/`accrue_chunk` before it is written back — clamp `step.borrow_index` to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:50`, or apply the cap inside `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates` — and make `scaled_to_original`/utilization saturating (or index-aware capped) so reads and accruals remain total functions. This makes the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` true instead of being preempted by the value overflow.

### Proof of Concept
The harness already encodes it: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360) supplies 10^27-scale principal, borrows 98%, advances time in yearly steps until `try_update_indexes_for` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`, then confirms `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` also revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` and that `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. An unprivileged caller reaches the same panic on-chain via `controller.update_indexes` (or any market verb) once the market approaches the bound.

Caveat: I could not fully verify the exact overflow site inside `common::rates::scaled_to_original`/`accrue_step` (whether it uses i128 or widened intermediate math) within the iteration budget; the panic behavior is nevertheless proven by the protocol's own test and by the `MathOverflow` panics in `fp_core` cited above.
