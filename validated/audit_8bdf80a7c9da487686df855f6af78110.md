### Title
Permanent market freeze: RAY-value overflow in interest accrual bricks every verb on a market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Analog of CVE-2025-47229 (crafted input reaching an assertion/abort that kills the application): an unprivileged user can craft market state — large scaled `borrowed`/`supplied` balances accumulated via ordinary `supply`/`borrow` — such that the next interest accrual panics with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`. Because every pool verb accrues first via `synced_market` → `global_sync` → `accrue_step`, the panic is not transactional-only: it permanently bricks the market. No repay, withdraw, liquidation, bad-debt cleanup, or recapitalize can ever execute again on that (hub, asset) book, freezing all supplier funds.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61`) calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` on every compounding chunk. These multiply a RAY-scaled share count by a RAY index and panic with `MathOverflow` once the product leaves `i128`. Neither the borrow-index cap (`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in `update_borrow_index`, `common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) nor the supply-index cap engages first: the value ceiling `scaled × index < i128::MAX` is hit while indexes are still far below their caps, because admitted scaled balances can reach ~`i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)` per `docs/reference/formulas.md` numeric limits.

The repo's own harness test proves the sequence end-to-end on a real market: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` supplies `BILLION * 10^18` of an 18-decimal asset, borrows 98% of it, advances time, and observes `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all returning `MathOverflow` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The test comment states: "Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages." `contracts/pool/src/ops/mod.rs:30-34` (`synced_market`) confirms accrual precedes every mutation, so the panic is a persistent state condition, not a per-call rejection — the classic fail-*stuck* shape of the PSPP assertion, but with permanent rather than process-level consequences.

Reachability is permissionless: `controller::supply` and `controller::borrow` are declared caller-auth entrypoints (`scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt:49-50`), and spoke caps are the only bound on `supplied`/`borrowed` growth — the admitted cap maximum itself (~170B whole tokens) already exceeds what is needed to reach the value ceiling once the index compounds.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all user funds supplied to the affected market. Suppliers can never withdraw, borrowers can never repay, liquidators cannot clear underwater accounts, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` — which also run through `synced_market` — cannot recover the book. The market is a write-only tomb.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires whale-scale capital (a supply position whose scaled value times a compounded index exceeds `i128::MAX`, i.e. roughly billions of units of a high-decimals asset) and utilization kept high long enough for index growth to push the product over the edge — the test reaches the cliff in under 40 years at 98% utilization on a steep curve segment, faster at larger principal or steeper curves. No privileged action, timing, or oracle manipulation is needed; the attacker only needs to supply and borrow size the protocol's own caps admit. Medium likelihood, matching the report's Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Bound scaled balances so `scaled × MAX_INDEX` cannot overflow `i128` (i.e. clamp effective supply/borrow caps to `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` in scaled-RAY terms at admission time, independent of the token-unit cap), or make `scaled_to_original` saturate/short-circuit accrual rather than panic — e.g. stop index growth and set a "frozen accrual" flag once the product approaches the ceiling, leaving repay/withdraw paths computable against the last good index. Add a regression asserting withdraw/repay remain callable at the value ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
Already encoded in the repo: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` —

```rust
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);            // BILLION * 10^18
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", principal / 100 * 98);
// advance years ...
assert_contract_error(t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

Attacker path: `controller::supply(account_id, BIG18, huge_amount)` then `controller::borrow(account_id, BIG18, ~98% of supplied)` on an account collateralized elsewhere (or the same market if collateralizable), then wait. Once `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX`, `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` panics on every subsequent interaction, permanently freezing the market for all users.

Uncertainty note: the finding is partially documented in `docs/reference/formulas.md` ("Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal") as an arithmetic limit rather than an ADR-endorsed behavior, so it is reported rather than dismissed; severity is tempered by the capital requirement.
