### Title
Permanent market freeze: borrow-index accrual overflows `i128` before `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` engages, bricking all exits - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The bug class in CVE-2024-6994 is an arithmetic write past a fixed bound (heap overflow). On Soroban the analog is `i128` value overflow: the pool's per-millisecond compounding multiplies `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index` in `scaled_to_original` inside every accrual step, and that product overflows `i128` before the borrow index reaches its `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp. Since every user-facing verb accrues first (`update_indexes`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate` settle paths), the market becomes permanently uncallable — supplier principal is frozen forever.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` chunks elapsed time and calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step`, which evaluates utilization via `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` — a `mul_div` of two ray-scaled `i128` values (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs`, backed by `mul_div_*` in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when the result does not fit `i128`). `update_borrow_index` in `common/src/rates/index.rs` multiplies the index by the chunk growth factor and only then clamps to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; the index cap therefore bounds the *index*, not the *product* `borrowed_scaled × index`. For a market whose scaled borrow book is large enough (roughly `> i128::MAX / (170 × RAY)`, e.g. a ~1-billion-unit 18-decimal market at ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve), interest accrual overflows before the index reaches the cap. The protocol's own harness reproduces this: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-356` shows `try_update_indexes_for` failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, after which `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` fail identically because every verb accrues first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of user funds: once the ray-valued debt book crosses the `i128` ceiling, no supply withdrawal, repay, borrow, liquidation, or index sync can execute against that market — all revert in the accrual preface. Supplier principal and borrower collateral held against the market are locked indefinitely; there is no admin escape hatch that skips accrual. This also degrades liquidation: bad debt on the frozen market cannot be cleared, so losses socialize to suppliers while funds stay unreachable — protocol insolvency rather than a temporary DoS.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable entirely by unprivileged calls (`supply`, `borrow`, `update_indexes`) plus ledger time. It needs a very large market (on the order of billions of whole tokens at high decimals) sustained at high utilization for multiple years — the harness reaches the cliff inside 40 years on the XLM curve. This is a real but tail-condition freeze: it requires the market to grow past economic realism before the index cap protects it, so Medium rather than High. Note the codebase already documents the bound in tests, so this may be a known limitation of the fixed-point domain rather than a novel defect; the finding stands on the reachable freeze, not the rate math itself.

### Recommendation
Compute the clamp bound in the value domain: before applying `accrue_step`, check `borrowed_scaled × new_index` against `i128::MAX` and clamp `borrow_index` to `min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX / borrowed_scaled)` so the cap engages before the product overflows. Alternatively, compute utilization/interest via a saturating or widened `I256` path in `accrue_step` (the certora spec already uses an `I256` widened lemma for this exact site), then clamp the stored index — accrual must never panic, even at the edge of the representable domain.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-356`: supply `1e9 × 10^18` BIG18, borrow 98% of it, advance time in yearly steps; `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` eventually returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `try_withdraw_raw` / `try_repay` fail with the same error — demonstrating the permanent freeze reachable from ordinary `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes` calls.
