### Title
Bad-debt write-down floors `supply_index` instead of zeroing surviving supply shares, leaving stranded claims that drain the next depositor - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary

The shared `supply_index` for a (hub, token) market is the denominator that prices every supplier's scaled shares. When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes a loss larger than the total supplied value, it clamps the index **up** to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) rather than letting it reach zero. Surviving scaled supply shares — including an attacker's — keep a nonzero claim against a market whose backing was wiped out, and that claim is later paid from the next depositor's fresh cash.

### Finding Description

The shared-resource destruction in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` works as follows: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `total_supplied_value`, caps the write-down at that value, computes `new_supply_index = supply_index * remaining/total`, then applies `new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))` at `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:88`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero, yet the index is set to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, not zero. Every unburned scaled supply share therefore retains `scaled * SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` of claim even though the write-down implicitly declared the market fully wiped.

The in-repo unit test `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` at `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430-495` demonstrates the exact sequence on `Cache`: after a wipeout seizure the floor clamp "leav[es] unburned shares a residual", a fresh deposit by Bob is booked, and Alice's stranded claim withdraws "exactly Bob's fresh deposit" while `cash < bob_claim`. The pool README confirms `supply_index` is intentionally non-monotone and floored at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`contracts/pool/README.md:188-190`), so nothing downstream reverts the residual.

The reachable path is the controller's permissionless bad-debt cleanup: `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs` collects all of the insolvent account's supply and borrow positions and calls `pool_seize_positions_call` (line 49), which routes through the pool's seizure path that invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. Cleanup seizes only the *target account's* positions; supply shares held by any other address — the attacker's second account — survive in `supplied` and are repriced at the floored index.

The attack shape: in a thin market where `total_supplied_value` is small (attacker supplies dust), keep an underwater account whose residual debt exceeds that tiny supplied value, then call `clean_bad_debt`. The write-down floors `supply_index`, the attacker's surviving dust shares keep a floor-priced claim, and the first honest deposit into the market is withdrawable by the attacker via `withdraw`, since `require_reserves` sees the fresh cash and the withdrawal unscales at the floored index.

### Impact Explanation

Theft of user funds. A stranded supply share created by the floor clamp is redeemable against real cash deposited by the next supplier, up to `scaled * SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. The attacker controls both the stranded share size (by pre-seeding supply) and the trigger (by keeping a qualifying bad-debt account and calling `clean_bad_debt` at will). Repeated across markets or after each new deposit, the residual claim persists because nothing burns the orphaned shares — `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` only touches the index, never `supplied`.

### Likelihood Explanation

Requires a market where an account eligible for bad-debt cleanup carries residual debt at least equal to the market's total supplied value, which is easiest in a near-empty or newly created market, or after large withdrawals. `clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless keeper entrypoint and the pool write-down executes without any floor-vs-shares reconciliation. Constraints: the attacker must produce the bad debt organically (real collateral loss), and profit per cycle is bounded by `stranded_scaled * FLOOR` versus the cost of engineering the bad debt, so the exploit is most attractive in thin markets where small dust debt suffices to floor the index.

Caveat: I could not fully verify the exact dust-threshold gate that makes an account eligible for `clean_bad_debt` in `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs` within the search budget; if eligibility requires debt below a dust threshold *and* that threshold is enforced relative to market size, reachability narrows but does not disappear in low-liquidity markets.

### Recommendation

When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), burn the residual: either set `supply_index` to zero and zero `supplied`, or burn the orphaned supply shares so no claim survives. At minimum, do not floor a fully-wiped index back up — gate the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp behind `remaining > 0`, and make `withdraw`/`supply` treat an at-floor index with zero backing as an empty market (re-mint at `RAY`) rather than honoring stranded claims.

### Proof of Concept

Conceptual, mirroring `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:430`:

1. Attacker account A supplies `S` dust in market `m`; attacker account B deposits collateral, borrows, and becomes bad debt with residual debt `D >= supplied_value(m)` (thin market).
2. Keeper call `clean_bad_debt(B)` → `seize_positions` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`: `total_supplied_value ≈ S`, `capped = S`, `remaining = 0`, `new_supply_index = 0 → clamped to SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`.
3. A's scaled shares survive: claim = `A_scaled * FLOOR > 0`.
4. Victim deposits `X`; `mint_supply` + `credit_cash(X)`.
5. A calls `withdraw`: `resolve_withdrawal` unscales at floor index, `require_reserves` passes against the fresh `X`, pool pays A out of the victim's deposit.
6. Victim's shares now exceed remaining cash — funds stolen.
