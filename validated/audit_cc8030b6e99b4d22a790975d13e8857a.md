### Title
Suppliers can front-run bad-debt socialization (`clean_bad_debt` / `force_socialize_bad_debt`) to dodge the supply-index write-down - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Bad-debt cleanup in XOXNO Lending socializes unpaid debt by lowering the affected market's `supply_index`, which reduces every remaining supplier's claim pro-rata. Because the write-down only hits accounts that still hold supply shares at execution time, any supplier who observes the pending cleanup — whether a permissionless `clean_bad_debt` transaction in flight or a `ForceSocializeBadDebt` operation waiting in the governance timelock — can call `withdraw` first, redeem their shares at the pre-write-down index, and shift their share of the loss onto the suppliers who stay in.

### Finding Description
When an account is cleaned up, `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` (contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:14-60) builds `PoolSeizeEntry` items and calls `pool_seize_positions_call`. On the pool side, `apply` in contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-32 routes borrow-side entries to `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which scales `supply_index` down by `(total_supplied_value - bad_debt) / total_supplied_value` (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89). The loss is therefore borne by whoever holds scaled supply shares at that instant — not by whoever held them when the debt was created.

Withdrawal is a fully permissionless exit path for a debt-free supplier: `process_withdraw` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:140-169) only requires owner/delegate auth, and `require_post_pool_risk_gates` (contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs:29-32) returns early for debt-free accounts, so a pure supplier can always withdraw 100% at the current (not-yet-written-down) index. The only blockers are listing flags: `paused` on the debt asset's listing blocks exits, but `frozen`/`no_seize` do not, and the guardian's immediate flag-tightening is itself a single front-runnable transaction.

The setup is externally visible well before execution:
- `ForceSocializeBadDebt` is a governance operation on the Sensitive delay tier (`docs/reference/runbooks/force-socialize-bad-debt.md`), so the pending write-down — including the exact `account_id` — is public on-chain for the entire delay.
- Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` (contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs:196-243) can be simulated by anyone; eligibility (`total_debt > total_collateral` and `total_collateral ≤ 5 WAD`) is computable from public state and prices, so a supplier can even predict the cleanup before anyone submits it.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds, distributed. The bad debt is a fixed loss; every share withdrawn before the write-down exits at full value, so the remaining suppliers' index reduction is proportionally larger than the pro-rata share they would have borne. A large supplier (or a flash-capable actor holding supply just-in-time) can escape virtually all of the loss, concentrating it on smaller or slower suppliers. In the extreme case, early exits drain the pool's cash, and the last suppliers hold claims written down to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` with insufficient backing.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an actual insolvent cleanup to occur — an infrequent but real event — and a supplier monitoring pending governance operations or simulating `clean_bad_debt` eligibility. The attack needs no privileged role, no oracle manipulation, and no timing precision beyond landing one `withdraw` before one cleanup transaction. Governance's multi-ledger delay makes the `force_socialize_bad_debt` variant trivially front-runnable by any supplier watching the operation queue. Medium likelihood, gated mainly on bad debt actually materializing.

### Recommendation
There is no clean on-chain fix of the same kind as a private mempool, but the design can reduce the window or the incentive:
- Pair `ForceSocializeBadDebt` execution with an atomic guardian `paused` flag on the affected debt listings so withdrawals cannot interpose between the decision and the write-down (note `paused` also blocks exits, so set it in the same transaction batch as execution or via a dedicated cleanup path that locks exits first).
- Alternatively, snapshot supplier shares at proposal time and apply the write-down against that snapshot (i.e., charge withdrawals that occur after the proposal a pro-rata bad-debt fee), so front-running exits still pay their share.
- Document the residual race for operators: `paused` on the debt market is the only existing lever that closes the exit window, and it must be raised before or atomically with cleanup.

### Proof of Concept
1. BOB supplies 1,000,000 USDC; ALICE supplies 100 USDC. An insolvent account holds ETH debt backed by collateral worth less than the debt (per `test_update_account_threshold`-style setup and `force_socialize_does_not_net_collateral_against_debt` in tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs:482-524).
2. Governance proposes `ForceSocializeBadDebt(account_id)`; the operation id and args are public via `get_operation_state`.
3. Before the delay elapses, BOB calls `controller.withdraw(caller=BOB, account_id=bob_id, withdrawals=[(ETH_hub_asset, 0)], to=None)` — the zero amount triggers `WITHDRAW_ALL_SENTINEL` (contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:190-194), and since BOB has no debt, `require_post_pool_risk_gates` returns immediately. BOB receives his full balance valued at the unreduced `supply_index`.
4. After the delay, anyone executes the operation; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` reduces the ETH-market `supply_index` by the full bad debt, but BOB's shares are already gone. ALICE's remaining supply absorbs the entire write-down that should have been split ~10,000:1 against BOB.
5. BOB can re-supply after cleanup if desired; his principal is intact while ALICE's claim is written down by up to `bad_debt / total_supplied_value`.
