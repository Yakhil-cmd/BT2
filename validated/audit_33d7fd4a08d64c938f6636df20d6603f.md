### Title
Flash loan `receiver` is attacker-controlled; pool pulls `principal + fee` via `transfer_from` from any allowance-holding contract - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs))

### Summary
This is the direct analog of the Aave `swapLiquidity` `receiverAddress`/`transferFrom` flaw. In `contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs`, `apply()` accepts a caller-supplied `receiver`, pays the principal out to it, invokes its `execute_flash_loan` callback, and then settles the loan with `transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, principal + fee)` gated only by an allowance check (`contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs:166-180`). The receiver is never required to authorize anything. Any unprivileged caller can therefore name any WASM contract that (a) exposes a non-reverting `execute_flash_loan` entrypoint and (b) holds a live token allowance to the pool, and force the pool to pull `amount + fee` out of that victim contract against the victim's will.

### Finding Description
`flash_loan` reaches `process_flash_loan` in `contracts/controller/src/strategies/flash_loan.rs:14-33`, which only authenticates `caller` (`require_authorized_caller`), checks `amount > 0`, hub active, and `require_wasm_receiver(receiver)` — the receiver merely needs to be a WASM contract. It then calls `pool_flash_loan_call(env, pool, hub_asset, caller, receiver, amount, data)` under the flash guard.

In the pool, `apply()`:
- transfers `amount` from the pool to `receiver` (line 60),
- invokes `receiver.execute_flash_loan(initiator, asset, amount, fee, pool, data)` (lines 150-162) — the victim contract's callback runs, but it is under no obligation to repay or even to know a loan is happening; it only needs to not revert,
- verifies `asset.allowance(receiver, pool) >= total_repayment` and executes `asset.transfer_from(pool, receiver, pool, total_repayment)` (lines 173-179),
- asserts pool balance equals `B + fee` and books `fee` as protocol revenue plus cash (`finalize`/`book_fee`, lines 124-135).

The net effect on the victim: it transiently receives `amount` (which it does not need to spend — the callback can be a no-op) and then loses `amount + fee` through its standing allowance to the pool. Each call therefore strips `fee` from the victim. The fee is credited to cash and protocol revenue, which raises the value of all supply positions — including the attacker's, if the attacker is a supplier. The call is repeatable for every allowance-holding, callback-implementing contract, draining the victim's allowance `fee`-worth at a time (or up to `allowance - amount` per call since `total_repayment` need only fit within the allowance, and `amount` can be chosen small to minimize the per-call floor).

The vulnerability is structurally identical to the report: an arbitrary third-party address plugged into a `transfer_from`-based settlement where the protocol contract is the spender.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. Any contract that has granted the pool a token allowance and exposes a permissive `execute_flash_loan` (e.g., a receiver built for its own legitimate flash-loan use that does not gate `initiator`) loses the flash fee per invocation to the protocol's cash/revenue. An attacker holding the dominant supply share in that market captures the majority of the stolen fee through index appreciation; the remainder accrues to protocol revenue. Repeatable until the victim's allowance or balance is exhausted.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The attack requires a deployed victim contract satisfying two conditions: an outstanding SEP-41 allowance to the pool contract, and an `execute_flash_loan` implementation that does not revert for unsolicited calls. Flash-receiver integrations are the canonical case; a victim with a large/renewed allowance can be harvested repeatedly at zero capital cost to the attacker (no collateral, no account, only `require_auth` on the attacker's own address). The yield capture is indirect (via supply index), which lowers profit efficiency but does not eliminate the theft.

### Recommendation
Bind repayment to consent instead of a standing allowance, or authenticate the receiver:
- Require `receiver.require_auth()` (or have `execute_flash_loan` itself authorize the repayment transfer) so only the receiver's own signature permits the pull; or
- Replace `transfer_from` with a push model — require the receiver to transfer `amount + fee` to the pool inside the callback, then verify `balance == B + fee` (the balance checks in `require_balance` already exist); or
- At minimum, drop the `transfer_from` path and treat any positive post-callback pool balance delta above `balance_after_payout` as repayment.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim contract `V` is a WASM flash-receiver that previously took a flash loan and left `approve(V → pool, X)` outstanding (or maintains one for convenience). Its `execute_flash_loan` does not check `initiator`.
2. Attacker calls `Controller::flash_loan(caller=attacker, hub_asset, amount=A, receiver=V, data=empty)` where `A + fee(A) <= allowance(V, pool)` and `A <= pool cash`.
3. Pool sends `A` to `V`, calls `V.execute_flash_loan` (returns normally), then `transfer_from(pool, V, pool, A + fee)` succeeds against `V`'s allowance.
4. Pool balance check `== B + fee` passes; `fee` is booked via `add_protocol_revenue` + `credit_cash`. `V` is `fee` poorer, having consented to nothing.
5. Repeat until `V`'s allowance/balance is drained; attacker withdraws appreciated supply.

One caveat I could not fully verify within scope: whether any real receiver's `execute_flash_loan` actually skips initiator validation — the exploit depends on that, but nothing in the pool or controller enforces it defensively; the `transfer_from` settlement is unconditional once allowance exists.
