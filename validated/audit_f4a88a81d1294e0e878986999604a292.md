### Title
Accrual-time i128 overflow in `scaled_to_original` permanently freezes a whale market before the borrow-index cap can engage - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The CVE-2016-2519 bug class is "an oversized input drives a routine to an unchecked null/abort path, and the abort is sticky." In XOXNO Lending the analog is the RAY-value ceiling: when a market's scaled debt multiplied by the borrow index exceeds `i128::MAX`, `accrue_step` inside `global_sync` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow`. Every state-changing entrypoint runs accrual first, so once a market crosses the ceiling the panic is permanent — no `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, or `update_indexes` can ever succeed again, and all supplier funds in that market are frozen forever. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never engages because the value overflow fires first.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` runs unconditionally at the head of every pool operation via `cache.needs_accrual()`; each chunk calls `accrue_step` (`interest.rs:39-53`), which computes total borrowed/supplied value through `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` as used in `cache/scale.rs:23-24`. The product `scaled × index` is a RAY-domain value with an `i128` ceiling. Checked math converts the overflow into `panic_with_error!(GenericError::MathOverflow)` (same pattern as `fp_core.rs:117` and `cache/cash.rs:29`). The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`) demonstrates the exact sequence: supply `1e9` whole 18-decimal tokens, borrow 98% of it, and at the steep segment of the XLM rate curve the next accrual panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the index cap "did not engage before the value overflow." After that, `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` because every verb accrues first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every supplier and borrower in the affected market, plus loss of liquidations (protocol insolvency risk since underwater positions cannot be closed). Unlike the CVE's restartable daemon, a Soroban panic is atomic and repeatable: the state is wedged, not crashed-and-recoverable. `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, and governance cannot repair it because they too pass through accrual on that market.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged attacker needs only `supply` and `borrow` on one market. Requirements: a high-decimals listing (18 decimals makes `1e9` whole tokens ≈ `1e36` raw RAY), caps lifted enough to admit the position (attacker supplies the capital themselves), and sustained high utilization on a steep curve. The harness shows the cliff arrives within 40 simulated years at 98% utilization on `xlm_curve()`; higher decimals or a steeper curve reach it sooner. No privilege, oracle manipulation, or third-party cooperation is needed — the attacker merely parks a large borrow and lets compounding do the rest, while every other supplier of that market is frozen alongside.

### Recommendation
Bound the RAY-domain value, not just the index: cap `scaled_to_original` results saturatingly, or clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` to `i128::MAX / scaled` before accrual writes them. Concretely, in `accrue_step` check the post-step `borrowed × borrow_index` product against `i128::MAX` and pin the index at the largest non-overflowing value instead of panicking, so the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap engages *before* the value ceiling. Alternatively reject borrows/supplies whose scaled totals make `scaled × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` overflow at entry time in `SpokeUsageContext::apply_entry`.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing harness test `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`:

1. Register an 18-decimal market on `xlm_curve()` with utilization/max-utilization limits disabled (`with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets`, `lift_caps`).
2. `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", BILLION * 10^18)`; `supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", …)`; `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98% of principal)` — all via unprivileged `supply`/`borrow`.
3. Advance time in 1-year steps calling `update_indexes`; within 40 simulated years `try_update_indexes_for` returns `Error(Contract, MATH_OVERFLOW)` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", …)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is permanently frozen; BOB's full deposit is unrecoverable.
