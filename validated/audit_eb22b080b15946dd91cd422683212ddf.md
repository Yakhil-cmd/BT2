### Title
Accrual `scaled_to_original` overflow permanently freezes a high-utilization market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary
The analog of CVE-2020-24825 (a crafted input crashing the parser → DoS) is an arithmetic-overflow panic in the mandatory interest-accrual step. `accrue_step` computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` = `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` inside `mul_div`-style checked math that panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `borrowed * borrow_index` leaves the i128 range. Every state-mutating and accrual-touching entrypoint runs `Cache::load → interest::global_sync → accrue_chunk → accrue_step` first, so once `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows, every subsequent call touching that market — `repay`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `supply`, `seize_positions` (liquidation), `net_settle`, `recapitalize`, `claim_revenue`, and permissionless `update_indexes` — panics and reverts forever. `update_borrow_index` caps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (1e36), but the multiplication overflows well before the cap engages, so the cap never saves the market. All supplied funds and collateral routed through that market's pool cash are permanently frozen.

### Finding Description
`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62` [1](#0-0)  calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` on raw scaled-share `Ray` values. `scaled_to_original` performs `half_up(scaled * index / RAY)`; for `borrowed ≈ 1e36` (a billion-token 18-decimal market) and `borrow_index ≈ 1.7e29`, the intermediate product exceeds `i128::MAX` (~1.7e38) and panics `MathOverflow` (`common/src/math/fp.rs:12-16`, `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The pool runs this unconditionally in `accrue_chunk` for every elapsed millisecond chunk (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53` [2](#0-1) ), and `global_sync` precedes every mutation per the documented flow (`contracts/pool/README.md:161-168`).

The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` demonstrates the trap end-to-end: after sufficient accrual at 98% utilization, `update_indexes` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with the same error — "the market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic" (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361` [3](#0-2) ).

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once a market crosses the `borrowed * borrow_index > i128::MAX` boundary, no one can repay debt, withdraw supply, liquidate positions, claim revenue, or socialize bad debt on that market — every path panics inside `accrue_step` before any state change. Suppliers' tokens and borrowers' collateral backing that market are bricked, and liquidations of unhealthy accounts borrowing that asset are impossible, pushing the market toward unrecoverable insolvency. This mirrors the report's bug class: a reachable crash (segfault → contract panic) that denies service — here to every user of the market, permanently.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires no privilege: an attacker needs only a whale-scale borrow position (or a whale supplier plus organic borrows) and sustained high utilization, both reachable via the public `supply`/`borrow` entrypoints or `flash_position`-minted debt held open. The borrow index grows monotonically and cannot be reduced by anyone, so once `borrowed` is large enough the cliff is reached by the passage of time alone — at ~98% utilization on the steep segment of the rate curve the index outgrows the safe domain, as demonstrated in the harness test reaching the cliff inside a few simulated years. There is no admin escape hatch: `update_params`, `claim_revenue`, and `recapitalize` all accrue first and hit the same panic, so the freeze cannot be remediated after the fact. Likelihood is gated by capital (a very large market must exist) rather than by permissions, which is why this sits at Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Widen the accrual math so the market caps out instead of trapping:

- In `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-62`), compute `scaled_to_original` through the existing widened `I256` path (as `try_mul_div_half_up` in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:122-143` already does for large products) or clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` to `MAX_*_INDEX_RAY` *before* the multiplication so `update_borrow_index`'s cap (`common/src/rates/index.rs`) actually engages first.
- Alternatively, gate `global_sync`/`accrue_chunk` to skip accrual and clamp the index once `borrow_index` reaches `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so repay/withdraw/liquidate remain operable at the capped index.
- Add a regression test mirroring `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` asserting that `update_indexes`, `repay`, and `withdraw` keep succeeding (at the capped index) rather than reverting with `MathOverflow`.

### Proof of Concept
Reachable entirely through unprivileged entrypoints:

1. On an 18-decimal market with a steep interest curve (e.g., `xlm_curve()` in the harness), attacker calls `supply`/`borrow` to reach ~98% utilization at ~1e9-token scale: `supply(BOB, BIG18, 1e27)` and `borrow(ALICE, BIG18, 0.98e27)` via the controller `supply`/`borrow` verbs.
2. Wait while `borrow_index` compounds (anyone may poke `update_indexes`). At `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY > i128::MAX` the next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original` — the test shows this happens while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, i.e., before the intended cap engages.
3. From that point every call reverts with `MathOverflow` (error #33): `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate` (via `seize_positions`), `claim_revenue` — demonstrated by `t.try_withdraw_raw` and `t.try_repay` both failing with `MATH_OVERFLOW` in the harness test. The market, all its supplied cash, and all positions depending on it are permanently frozen.

pub(crate) fn global_sync(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache) {
    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }

    cache.mark_accrued();
}

/// Applies one compound step of `delta_ms` to indexes and protocol revenue.
///
/// The arithmetic lives in [`accrue_step`], shared with the read-only
/// `simulate_update_indexes` so the view and the mutator cannot drift.
fn accrue_chunk(env: &Env, cache: &mut Cache, delta_ms: u64) {
    let step = accrue_step(
        env,
        cache.params(),
        cache.borrowed(),
        cache.supplied(),
        cache.borrow_index(),
        cache.supply_index(),
        delta_ms,
    );

    cache.set_borrow_index(step.borrow_index);
    cache.set_supply_index(step.supply_index);
    cache.accrue_revenue(step.revenue_shares);
}

/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    std::println!(
        "ray-value cliff reached after {years} years at 98 percent utilization on the XLM curve; last index x{:.1}",
        last.borrow_index as f64 / RAY as f64
    );
}
