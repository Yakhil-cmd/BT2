### Title
Borrower can permanently block liquidation by holding collateral in a low‑activity asset whose oracle price goes stale, causing `get-assets` to `unwrap-panic` and abort the entire `liquidate` call - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar)

### Summary
`liquidate` (and every other health-check path) values a borrower's **entire** collateral set through `get-assets`, which calls `price-multi-resolve` over *all* assets in the account's mask and then does `(unwrap-panic (price-multi-resolve ...))`. If price resolution fails for even one asset in the borrower's mask — e.g. because that asset's oracle feed is stale (`oracle-timestamp-fresh` fails) — the whole call panics and the transaction aborts. A borrower who deliberately holds a small/dust amount of collateral in an illiquid, rarely-updated asset can therefore make their position permanently unliquidatable by any liquidator, even though their debt in an unrelated, healthy-priced asset is deeply underwater.

### Finding Description

which calls `get-assets(mask)`: [2](#0-1) 

`get-assets` resolves prices for **every** asset in `safe-mask` (the intersection of the account's own entered-collateral mask and the globally enabled bitmap) via `price-multi-resolve`, and hard-panics on any failure: [3](#0-2) 

`price-resolve` requires the oracle timestamp to be fresh relative to `max-staleness`: [4](#0-3) 

If the underlying price feed (Pyth or DIA) for a low-activity asset has not been updated recently — which is exactly the scenario the referenced report describes ("low supply and borrow activity") — `oracle-timestamp-fresh` returns `false`, `price-resolve` returns `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT`, `iter-price-multi` marks the fold `valid: false`, `price-multi-resolve` returns `(err ERR-ORACLE-MULTI)`, and the outer `(unwrap-panic ...)` in `get-assets` **aborts the entire transaction** rather than returning a recoverable error.

Because `get-assets` is invoked unconditionally inside `liquidate` before any liquidation-specific logic runs, and it iterates over the borrower's **full** collateral mask (not just the specific `collateral-ft`/`debt-ft` pair the liquidator is targeting), a single stale/illiquid collateral asset in the borrower's position is enough to make the *entire* position "unevaluable," blocking liquidation of collateral that is completely unrelated to the stale asset.

- **Attacker**: the borrower (Alice), who chooses to deposit a small amount of collateral into an enabled but rarely-traded/rarely-priced asset.
- **Victim**: any liquidator attempting to call `liquidate`, and by extension the protocol/other suppliers who bear the risk of Alice's now-unliquidatable bad debt.
- **Shared state**: the account's collateral `mask` and the resulting `get-assets` valuation loop that all liquidation/health-check paths depend on.

### Impact Explanation
Once triggered, no liquidator can call `liquidate` against the account until the stale oracle updates (which may never happen for an inactive asset) or until Alice voluntarily removes the collateral. Meanwhile Alice's debt in other, healthy-priced assets can continue to accrue as bad debt, since the debtor's LTV can far exceed `LTV-LIQ-FULL` without recourse. This results in **temporary (potentially indefinite) freezing of funds** for the protocol/suppliers, since bad debt cannot be repaid via liquidation and the collateral cannot be seized — matching the in-scope "temporary freezing of funds" impact class. It could also degrade into insolvency if the position accrues losses beyond what remaining collateral (once eventually liquidated) can cover.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires (a) an enabled collateral asset whose oracle updates infrequently enough to exceed `max-staleness` under low trading/reporting activity, and (b) a borrower willing to add a small collateral position in that asset specifically to grief liquidation. Both are within reach of any DAO-enabled long-tail asset, and the action (adding trivial collateral) is a normal, permissionless operation — no privileged access or oracle compromise is required, only naturally occurring staleness for a thinly-tracked asset.

### Recommendation
- In `get-assets`/`price-multi-resolve`, do not hard-`unwrap-panic` on a single asset's price failure; instead, either exclude assets with 0 amount effectively (already partially done via `find-collateral-amount`), or restructure valuation for `liquidate` so that price failures on assets *not involved* in the liquidation (`coll-aid`, `debt-aid`) do not abort the transaction — e.g. valuing the requested collateral/debt pair independently, or falling back to a "worst case" valuation (e.g. treat unpriceable collateral as zero value) for the liquidatable-position calculation only.
- Consider allowing the DAO/impl to force-disable an asset as collateral globally (already possible via `disable`) but that requires a DAO transaction; add a permissionless "prune stale collateral" path or make liquidation resilient to per-asset price staleness so unrelated collateral cannot be held hostage.

### Proof of Concept
1. DAO enables an infrequently-traded asset `X` as collateral with a Pyth/DIA feed and a bounded `max-staleness`.
2. Alice supplies her main collateral (e.g., sBTC) and borrows USDC against it near the liquidation threshold.
3. Alice also deposits a trivial amount of `X` as additional collateral via `collateral-add`, entering `X`'s bit into her mask.
4. `X`'s market sees little activity; its oracle price is not refreshed and eventually exceeds `max-staleness`.
5. USDC price moves (or interest accrues) such that Alice's LTV crosses `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`.
6. Charlie (liquidator) calls `liquidate(alice, sbtcToken, usdcToken, amount, ...)`. Internally this calls `get-liquidation-position` → `get-assets(mask)` → `price-multi-resolve` over Alice's full mask, including asset `X`.
7. `price-resolve` for `X` fails `oracle-timestamp-fresh`, returns `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT`; `price-multi-resolve` returns `(err ERR-ORACLE-MULTI)`; the `(unwrap-panic ...)` in `get-assets` aborts Charlie's transaction.
8. Alice's sBTC-collateralized, deeply-underwater USDC debt remains un-liquidated indefinitely, as long as `X`'s price stays stale, even though `X` has nothing to do with the sBTC/USDC pair being liquidated.

(define-private (oracle-price-legal (p uint))
  (> p u0))

(define-private (oracle-timestamp-fresh (ts uint) (prev uint) (max-staleness uint))
  (let ((delta (if (> ts stacks-block-time)
                   u0
                   (- stacks-block-time ts))))
    (and
      (<= delta max-staleness)
      (>= ts prev))))

(define-private (price-resolve
  (data { type: (buff 1), ident: (buff 32), callcode: (optional (buff 1)), max-staleness: uint }))
  (let ((type (get type data))
        (ident (get ident data))
        (key { type: type, ident: ident })
        (resolution (try! (resolve-price-feed type ident)))
        (price (get value resolution))
        (callcode (get callcode data))
        (final-price (try! (resolve-callcode price callcode)))
        (last-update-time (oracle-last-update key))
        (timestamp (get timestamp resolution))
        (max-staleness (get max-staleness data)))

    ;; validate price and timestamp using max-staleness from oracle data
    (asserts! (and (oracle-price-legal final-price) (oracle-timestamp-fresh timestamp last-update-time max-staleness))
              ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT)

    ;; update timestamp if newer
    (if (> timestamp last-update-time)
        (map-set last-update key timestamp)
        false)

    (ok final-price)))
