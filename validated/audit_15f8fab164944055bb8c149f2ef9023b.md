### Title
Supply-index floor clamp on bad-debt socialization leaves phantom supplier claims that drain fresh deposits — (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`)

### Summary
When unpaid debt is socialized via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, the code clamps the new supply index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) rather than zeroing out the share supply. All pre-existing scaled supply shares therefore retain a positive claim of `shares × RAY/1000` even though the underlying value was fully wiped out by bad debt. Once fresh cash enters the market, those stranded shares pay out real tokens, leaving the pool unable to cover the fresh suppliers' claims — silent accounting corruption directly analogous to memory corruption: the bookkeeping state no longer reflects reality.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89` computes `remaining = total_supplied_value − capped_bad_debt`, derives `reduction_factor = remaining / total`, and sets `new_supply_index = supply_index × reduction_factor`, then applies `.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW))`. On a near-total wipeout (`bad_debt ≈ total_supplied_value`), the true index should be ~0, but the clamp restores it to `RAY/1000`. Since share balances (`scaled_amount`) are untouched, every wiped supplier keeps a claim valued at the floor index. This is reachable from the unprivileged path: `seize::apply` (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`) calls it on the `Borrow` side whenever a liquidation or `clean_bad_debt` seizes unpaid debt — an attacker can manufacture underwater debt (borrow, let collateral value drop or simply walk away) and trigger the cleanup themselves.

The codebase's own test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-427`) demonstrates the exploit end-to-end: after the floor clamp, `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)` pays the stranded position `gross > 0`, draining exactly the fresh deposit (`gross == fresh_cash`), and the final assertion shows `cache.cash() < fresh_claim` — the fresh supplier's funds are lost.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: phantom claims of wiped-out suppliers (attacker-controlled) are paid from new depositors' cash, leaving a backing shortfall `supplied_claim > cash + debt`. The attacker effectively converts worthless wiped shares back into real tokens.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a near-total bad-debt wipeout so `reduction_factor` pushes the index below `RAY/1000` — achievable by letting a large position go fully bad (or many incremental `clean_bad_debt` calls on dust positions). Any unprivileged address can originate the debt and call the seize path. One caveat I could not fully verify within the iteration budget: whether `cache.commit()` or the calling op runs `require_backed_market`/`require_reserves` after the withdrawal leg, which would revert the drain. The test's own comment ("invariant only masked by require_reserves") indicates the drain succeeds where that guard is absent or ordered after the payout; the stranded-claim state itself is unconditionally committed by `seize::apply`.

### Recommendation
When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), reset `supplied` and `revenue` shares to zero alongside the index, or store a wiped flag that zeroes `unscale_supply` results, instead of clamping the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alternatively, floor-clamp only when `remaining > 0` and explicitly zero share supply when `remaining == 0`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies `S` tokens to a hub market, borrows the maximum permitted, and lets the position go fully bad (price move or self-liquidation path).
2. Attacker calls `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate`; `seize::apply` on the borrow side invokes `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` while the attacker's `S/index_old` scaled shares persist.
3. A victim supplies `fresh_cash` to the same market.
4. Attacker calls `withdraw(amount=0)` (withdraw-all sentinel); `resolve_withdrawal` unscales the stranded shares at the floored index and transfers `gross = fresh_cash`, leaving the victim's claim unbacked (demonstrated in `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:414-426`).
