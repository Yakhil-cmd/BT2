### Title
Donating tokens to an Aquarius pool inflates the fair-value LP price, letting LP-backed positions over-borrow and leave bad debt — (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
Aquarius LP collateral is priced from the pool's *live* `get_reserves()` and `get_total_shares()`. Both `fair_lp_price_wad` (`2·sqrt(vA·vB)/supply`) and `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (`D·min(price_a, price_b)/supply`) grow monotonically when a token balance is donated to the pool without minting shares. An attacker holding LP shares can donate to the pool, inflate the oracle price of their collateral within the sanity band, borrow against the inflated value, then redeem their LP (recovering most of the donation) and abandon the position as bad debt.

### Finding Description
`read()` in `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs:90-113` pulls `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` — a direct `get_reserves` cross-contract call on the pool (`common/src/oracle/providers/aquarius.rs:45-57`) — and feeds those balances into `fair_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp.rs:57-87`) or `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` (`common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs:80-110`) each time a price is resolved. There is no TWAP, smoothing, or historical comparison for LP sources: per ADR-0014, LP oracles are sole-source and waive smoothing/tolerance checks (`docs/explanation/decisions.md:177-190`).

For the constant-product formula the price scales with `sqrt(reserve_a·reserve_b)`; for the stable formula the invariant `D` scales roughly linearly with donated reserves while `min_price` is unchanged. A raw `token.transfer(pool, X)` — a plain direct token transfer, reachable by any unprivileged address — raises the computed LP share price with no offsetting cost, because the donation is distributed pro-rata to LP holders and the attacker holds the shares. Multiple production markets use this source as collateral assets (e.g., `XLMAQUA_LP`, `USTRYUSDC_LP`, `xSolvBTCSolvBTC_LP` in `configs/mainnet/markets.json`), with sanity bands as wide as 10x (`min_sanity_price_wad` 5.36e15 / `max_sanity_price_wad` 5.36e16 for XLMAQUA_LP).

The attack in one transaction or short sequence:
1. Acquire/mint LP shares of an Aquarius pool whitelisted as collateral.
2. `supply` the LP token to the controller as collateral.
3. Donate a large amount of the cheaper underlying directly to the pool (`token.transfer`), pushing `get_reserves` up and inflating the LP fair price toward the `max_sanity_price_wad` ceiling.
4. `borrow` the maximum permitted by the inflated LTV-weighted collateral value.
5. Burn the LP position (or transfer the position NFT to a fresh account) to recover the donated funds pro-rata; the open borrow now exceeds real collateral value → bad debt for the pool.

### Impact Explanation
Protocol insolvency: the borrowed assets are real and withdrawn, while the collateral's true value reverts after the attacker removes liquidity. The shortfall becomes bad debt absorbed by suppliers via supply-index write-down in `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize`. The sanity band caps the inflation (up to ~10x on configured markets), but even a 2-3x inflation against a 50-80% LTV makes the borrow exceed recoverable collateral.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only unprivileged actions: a direct token transfer to the pool (explicitly in scope), `supply`, and `borrow`. The capital cost is the donation times other LPs' share fraction plus fees; since the attacker recovers their pro-rata donation by redeeming LP shares, net cost can be well below the extracted borrow, especially on shallow pools (only `min_pool_value_wad` floors pool size). Feasible on any market whose collateral is priced by `AquariusLp`/`AquariusStableLp`.

### Recommendation
Use manipulation-resistant reserves: read cumulative/TWAP reserves or the pool's virtual price if exposed, instead of spot `get_reserves`; alternatively value LP shares as `min(spot-derived value, donation-resistant lower bound)` — e.g., clamp reserves to the smaller implied balance given pool invariant and oracle prices, or require pool-value *and* share-price deltas vs. a stored reference to stay within a tight band across successive reads, tightening the LP sanity band well below the current 10x ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker mints LP shares in whitelisted Aquarius stable pool P, supplies them via `controller.supply`.
2. `TokenClient(token_a).transfer(attacker, pool, N)` where `N ≈ reserve_a` doubles `D` in `solve_stable_d` → `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` nearly doubles (still inside the configured sanity band).
3. `controller.borrow` draws up to the inflated collateral value.
4. Attacker redeems LP shares, recovering ~their share of the donation; the borrow position is left undercollateralized and later written down as bad debt.
