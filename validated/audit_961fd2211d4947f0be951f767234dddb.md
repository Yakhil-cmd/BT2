### Title
Suppliers can front-run bad-debt socialization by redeeming before `liquidate` writes down `lindex`, then re-depositing at the discounted price - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar, mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar)

### Summary
Zest v2 vaults price zTokens via a liquidity index (`lindex`) that is written down immediately and in full whenever bad debt is socialized. Any current zToken holder can watch the mempool for a pending `liquidate` call that will trigger `no-collateral-left` / bad-debt socialization, front-run it with `redeem` to exit at the pre-loss price, let the socialization transaction land (which slashes `lindex` for everyone still in the vault), and then `deposit` again immediately afterward to re-enter at the discounted price. This is the same "notifyLoss can be frontrun by redeem" MEV pattern from the Frankencoin report, and unlike Frankencoin's `MintingHub`, Zest's vaults have **no withdrawal cooldown at all**, making the attack strictly easier here.

### Finding Description
`liquidate()` in `market.clar`/`v0-4-market.clar` computes whether a borrower position has no remaining collateral (`no-collateral-left`) and, if so, calls `socialize-debt-asset` for each remaining debt entry: [1](#0-0) 

`socialize-debt-asset` in turn calls the vault's `vault-socialize-debt`/`socialize-debt` entry point directly, in the same transaction as the liquidation, with no delay or private-relay mechanism: [2](#0-1) 

Inside the vault, `socialize-debt` reduces `total-assets` by the bad debt and immediately rewrites `lindex` proportionally to the loss versus `old-total-assets`, which is the shared per-share pricing state used by every `deposit`/`redeem` call: [3](#0-2) 

Share pricing is computed from this same state via `convert-to-shares-preview` / `convert-to-assets-preview`, which use `total-assets-preview()` (derived from `lindex`/`index`) and `total-supply-preview()`: [4](#0-3) 

Critically, `redeem` and `deposit` have no cooldown, no time lock, and no queueing - a caller's balance and slippage bound are the only checks: [5](#0-4) 

A repo-wide search confirms there is no `cooldown` concept anywhere in the vault or market contracts, so even Frankencoin's mitigating 90-day lock (which the report shows is already insufficient) is absent here - any depositor can redeem and re-deposit at will in the same block window as a pending liquidation.

**Attacker / victim / shared state:**
- Attacker: any existing zToken holder in the vault backing the debt asset about to take a loss (e.g., `zUSDC` holder).
- Victim: other zToken holders of the same vault who remain deposited through the bad-debt socialization.
- Shared state: `lindex`/`index` and `total-assets`/`total-supply`, which determine the shares-to-assets conversion rate (`convert-to-shares-preview`, `convert-to-assets-preview`) used by every depositor and redeemer of that vault.

### Impact Explanation
Without the attacker's front-run: the bad-debt loss (`debt-reduction`) is socialized proportionally across all zToken holders in the vault at the time of `socialize-debt`, including the attacker, per the standard `mul-div-down current-lindex (- old-total-assets debt-reduction) old-total-assets` formula.

With the attacker's front-run: the attacker's `redeem` transaction executes before `liquidate`/`socialize-debt`, extracting their assets at the pre-loss `lindex` and removing their share of `total-supply` and `total-assets` from the base that will absorb the loss. This means the exact same absolute `debt-reduction` is now divided by a smaller `old-total-assets`, making `new-lindex` proportionally lower for the remaining, non-front-running holders than it would have been had the attacker stayed in. The attacker then re-deposits (`deposit`) immediately after the price has dropped, minting more shares per unit of underlying than they would have gotten pre-loss, and fully participates in any subsequent recovery/yield. Net effect: the attacker pays none of the socialized bad debt while remaining/late suppliers absorb a strictly larger relative loss than they would have otherwise - this is theft of value from other unprivileged suppliers via an ordering dependence within/around the same liquidation transaction, i.e., temporary/permanent freezing/loss of unclaimed yield or principal value for the victims. This lands on the in-scope **High** impact class (temporary freezing/theft of unclaimed yield or funds shared among suppliers), since the loss transfer is a proportional redistribution rather than direct external theft.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is elevated relative to the original Frankencoin report because:
- There is no cooldown restricting redemption at all in `v0-vault-*.clar`, so any current supplier can execute this at will, at any time, without having first waited out a lock period.
- `liquidate()`/`liquidate-multi()` are fully permissionless public functions, visible in the mempool before inclusion, giving any watcher an opportunity to sandwich them with `redeem` + `deposit` on the corresponding vault.
- The attack only requires the attacker to already hold zTokens in the affected vault and have capital to redeploy after redeeming, both of which are trivial capital/logistics requirements for any active liquidator or bot monitoring positions for `no-collateral-left`.

### Recommendation
Adopt one of the standard MEV-resistant loss-accounting mitigations proposed in the original report, adapted to Zest's vault design:
- Amortize `socialize-debt`'s `lindex` write-down over a short window (e.g., via a decay-rate cap) instead of applying it atomically in the same transaction as `liquidate`.
- Introduce a minimum holding period / cooldown between `deposit` and `redeem` (mirroring, or improving upon, Frankencoin's 90-day model) so a same-block or same-mempool-window redeem-then-redeposit cannot be used to dodge a pending socialization.
- Alternatively, snapshot/queue redemptions such that the final redemption price incorporates any bad-debt socialization already pending against the same borrower/position in the same block.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower's position under asset `debt-aid` becomes fully undercollateralized; a liquidator submits `liquidate` (or `liquidate-multi`) which will hit the `no-collateral-left` branch and call `socialize-debt-asset` → vault `socialize-debt`.
2. Attacker, holding zTokens of the vault for `debt-aid`, observes this pending transaction in the mempool and submits (front-run) `redeem(amount, min-out, recipient)` on the same vault, exiting at the current (pre-loss) `lindex`/share price: [6](#0-5) 
3. `liquidate` executes, invoking `socialize-debt`, which writes down `lindex` based on `old-total-assets` that no longer includes the attacker's redeemed principal, so the loss is spread thinner across remaining holders: [7](#0-6) 
4. Attacker immediately submits `deposit(amount, min-out, recipient)` back into the same vault, minting shares at the new, discounted `lindex`-derived price via `convert-to-shares-preview`: [8](#0-7) 
5. Attacker now holds more zTokens for the same principal than they would have without step 2-4, while never having absorbed any portion of the socialized bad debt - the remaining/late suppliers bear a proportionally larger loss than intended.

                (acc { borrower: principal, success: bool }))

  (if (not (get success acc))
      acc
