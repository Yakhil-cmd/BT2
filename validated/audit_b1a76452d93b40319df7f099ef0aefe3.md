### Title
`flash_loan` (and `flash_position`/`swap_debt`/`multiply` strategy fees) books 100% of the fee as protocol revenue, bypassing `reserve_factor` — suppliers receive no flash-loan yield - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs)

### Summary
In XOXNO Lending, ordinary borrow interest is split between suppliers and the protocol via `reserve_factor`: `calculate_deposit_rate` multiplies `rate * utilization` by `BPS - reserve_factor`, and `accrue_step` only mints the protocol's fraction as revenue shares while the rest accrues to the supply index (suppliers). Flash-loan and strategy fees skip this split entirely: `flash::book_fee` converts the **entire** fee into protocol revenue shares via `interest::add_protocol_revenue`, and credits the entire fee to cash. The supply index is unchanged, so suppliers earn exactly zero of every flash fee, even though they provide the liquidity being lent out. This is the same bug class as the SushiSwap Trident `flashSwap` finding (entire fee routed to `barFeeTo` instead of the configured split).

### Finding Description
- `flash::apply` computes the fee via `Bps::flash_loan_fee_on` and then calls `finalize`, which calls `book_fee` (contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs:69, 124-128).
- `book_fee` calls `interest::add_protocol_revenue(cache, protocol_fee)` with the **full** fee and `cache.credit_cash(fee)` — no `reserve_factor` is consulted anywhere in this path.
- `add_protocol_revenue` mints the full fee as scaled revenue shares (`revenue` and `supplied` both grow, supply index untouched) — contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:59-66.
- Contrast with the normal interest path in `common/src/rates/curve.rs:79-96`: the supplier share is `BPS - reserve_factor`, and only `reserve_factor` of the yield becomes protocol revenue. The same asymmetry is verified by the Certora fixture: `post.revenue - pre.revenue == expected_shares.raw()` and `post.cash - pre.cash == fee` with `supply_index` unchanged (certora/pool/spec/flash_loan_accounting_rules.rs:94-98, 168-173).
- The same full-fee-to-revenue path is used for strategy fees: `ops::strategy::compute_fee` (contracts/pool/src/ops/strategy.rs:94-100) computes the fee for `multiply`, `swap_debt`, `flash_position`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, etc., and the fee booked flows through the same protocol-revenue minting, again bypassing `reserve_factor`.
- Any unprivileged address triggers this via `pool.flash_loan(hub_asset, initiator, receiver, amount, data)` or by running a fee-charged strategy via the controller (`multiply`, `swap_debt`).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of unclaimed supplier yield. On a market configured with, e.g., `reserve_factor = 10%`, suppliers are entitled (by the protocol's own fee-splitting model applied to all other yield) to 90% of fee income. Every flash loan and every fee-charging strategy transaction diverts 100% of the fee to protocol revenue shares, which are claimable only via `claim_revenue`. Suppliers permanently lose the LP share of all flash/strategy fee income; the economic incentive to supply the liquidity backing flash loans is materially reduced. With `flashloan_fee` up to `MAX_FLASHLOAN_FEE_BPS = 500` bps of principal, the diverted amounts are substantial on flash-heavy markets.

### Likelihood Explanation
Deterministic: it triggers on every `flash_loan` and every strategy call where `charge_fee` is true, requiring only a flashloanable market with `flashloan_fee > 0` and cash reserves — all reachable by a single unprivileged caller. No privileged action, oracle condition, or edge case is needed; the accounting path always books the entire fee to revenue.

### Recommendation
Route the fee through the same `reserve_factor` split used by interest accrual: mint only `fee * reserve_factor / BPS` as protocol revenue shares via `add_protocol_revenue`, and accrue the remaining `fee * (BPS - reserve_factor) / BPS` to suppliers — e.g., by distributing it through the supply index (or equivalently crediting it to `supplied`/index so all suppliers share pro-rata) rather than into `revenue`. Apply the same split in the strategy-fee path (`ops::strategy::compute_fee` / its booking call site). Update `certora/pool/spec/flash_loan_accounting_rules.rs` and `fee_strategy_accounting_rules.rs` to assert the supplier share accrues to suppliers.

### Proof of Concept
```text
Setup (mirrors contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs::test_flash_loan):
1. Admin configures market: is_flashloanable = true, flashloan_fee = 100 bps,
   reserve_factor = 1000 bps (10% protocol / 90% suppliers).
2. Supplier calls controller.supply -> pool books 10_000_000_000 units; supply_index = RAY.
3. Attacker/user calls pool.flash_loan(hub_asset, initiator, receiver,
   amount = 100_000_000, data) with a WASM receiver that approves repayment
   of amount + fee.
4. Pool computes fee = 1_000_000. apply() -> finalize() -> book_fee():
     - add_protocol_revenue(fee) mints ~RAY-scaled fee shares into `revenue`
     - credit_cash(1_000_000)
     - supply_index stays exactly RAY; suppliers' position value unchanged.
5. Observed: revenue grows by the full 1_000_000; suppliers receive 0.
   Expected (consistent with reserve_factor model): revenue grows by 100_000,
   suppliers accrue 900_000.
Assert (as in certora spec): post.supply_index == pre.supply_index and
post.revenue - pre.revenue == full fee shares — demonstrating 100% diversion.
```

Caveat: if routing 100% of flash/strategy fees to revenue is a documented ADR decision rather than an oversight, this is intended behavior; no explicit documentation of that intent was found in `docs/` or `contracts/pool/README.md`, which describe revenue generically, so the divergence from the `reserve_factor` split appears to be an inconsistency in the fee accounting rather than a stated design choice.
