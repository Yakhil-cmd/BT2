### Title
Unbounded index accrual overflows and permanently freezes a market - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
A sufficiently large borrowed market can reach the `i128` RAY-value limit before `borrow_index` reaches `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which every operation that synchronizes the market panics with `MathOverflow`. Because all market exits, repayments, liquidations, bad-debt handling, and explicit index updates accrue interest before mutating state, all funds in that market become permanently frozen and no recovery path can re-enter the market. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
Pool debt is stored as scaled RAY shares and converted to outstanding value as `borrowed.mul(borrow_index)` during every interest accrual. `update_borrow_index` multiplies the stored index by the next interest factor, but `calculate_supplier_rewards` separately computes `borrowed * new_borrow_index`; this product can overflow before the index itself reaches its configured cap. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

`global_sync` invokes this calculation before `last_timestamp` is advanced and before any later mutation can commit. A failed chunk therefore leaves the same overdue accrual range pending for every subsequent call, while `synced_market` is shared by supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, net-settle, seize, flash, strategy, revenue, recapitalize, and market-update paths. [2](#0-1) [6](#0-5) 

This is reachable through permissionless `Controller::borrow` and `Controller::update_indexes`; ordinary suppliers and borrowers then cannot exit because `withdraw` and `repay` call `ops::load_leg`, which first synchronizes the same market. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation
A crossed market cannot process withdrawals, repayments, liquidations, bad-debt cleanup, revenue claims, or recapitalization because each entrypoint reaches the same accrual overflow. Supplier principal, borrower collateral backing the frozen market, protocol revenue, and recovery contributions are permanently inaccessible for that market unless the contracts are upgraded, and the frozen accounting can preserve or amplify protocol insolvency. [3](#0-2) [6](#0-5) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering this requires a very large debt book and sustained interest growth; the repository's long-horizon test uses a one-billion-whole-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization and demonstrates failure before the borrow-index cap. The exploit does not require privileged access, malformed XDR, or oracle manipulation, but it does require a market whose caps and configured interest curve permit enough debt and sustained growth. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Recommendation
Bound the accrual state transition before committing an index whose total-debt value cannot be represented. In `accrue_step`, cap `new_borrow_index` at `min(MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY, i128::MAX / borrowed.raw() * RAY)` when `borrowed > 0`, or otherwise compute outstanding debt through a saturating path and persist a safe index. Also add a permanent invariant test proving that every admitted `(borrowed_scaled, borrow_index)` pair can complete `calculate_supplier_rewards` without overflowing. [4](#0-3) [12](#0-11) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Create a high-decimal market and set caps/max-utilization limits high enough to admit a one-billion-whole-token supply and approximately 98% utilization.
2. A permissionless account supplies sufficient collateral and calls `Controller::borrow`; another account or the attacker separately provides the market liquidity being borrowed.
3. Advance ledger time while the configured interest curve compounds the debt.
4. Call permissionless `Controller::update_indexes` for the market.
5. Accrual computes `borrowed * new_borrow_index`, overflows `i128`, and panics before `last_timestamp` advances.
6. Call `withdraw` or `repay` for the market; both call `ops::load_leg` → `synced_market` → `global_sync` and panic on the same overdue interval, so neither suppliers nor borrowers can exit. [11](#0-10) [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13)
