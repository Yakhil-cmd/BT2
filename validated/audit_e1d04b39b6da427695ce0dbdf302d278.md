### Title
Front-running a "repay all" call with a borrow forces a third-party repayer to pay more than the observed debt - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
`Controller::repay(caller, account_id, payments)` is permissionless: any caller may pay down any account's debt, authorizing a fixed token transfer of the declared `amount` to the pool. The pool always pulls the full declared amount, repays `min(amount, current_debt)`, and refunds the difference to the payer. XOXNO has no `uint(-1)` sentinel, so the "repay the whole loan" idiom is to pass an amount ≥ the observed debt and rely on the refund. Because the pull is fixed while the debt burned is resolved at execution, a borrower who observes an incoming oversized repay can front-run it with `borrow` on their own account; the newly minted debt absorbs what would have been the repayer's refund, and the repayer ends up paying up to their entire authorized amount rather than the debt they saw. There is no `max_acceptable`/`expected_debt` parameter and no revert when the debt has grown — the exact mitigation the source report recommends is absent.

### Finding Description
- `process_repay` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:69-91` only calls `validation::require_authorized_caller(env, caller)` — it never checks `require_owner_or_delegate`, unlike `process_borrow` at `debt.rs:43`. Third-party repay is intentional and confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/repay.rs:139-197` (`test_repay_permissionless_payer_auth_only`, where Bob repays Alice's account with only Bob's auth).
- The pool side, `apply`/`accounting` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:25-67`, resolves `resolve_repay(amount, position)` → `(burned, overpayment)` where overpayment is `amount − ceiled_debt` (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:111-119`), credits only `net_repay` to cash, and `transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` refunds the excess to whoever funded the pull.
- Debt is measured at execution time against the accrued `borrow_index` and current scaled shares. `borrow` (`process_borrow`, `debt.rs:33-66`) requires only owner/delegate auth on the borrower's own account, so the debtor can increase the debt between the repayer's signing and inclusion.
- The documented integration pattern (`skills/xoxno-lending-contracts/abi.md:54`, `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/extreme_amount_inputs.rs:122-136`) is precisely "send more than the debt, get the excess refunded" — i.e., the functional equivalent of Aave's `uint(-1)` repay-all, but with a hard pull instead of a computed pull.

### Impact Explanation
The repayer's net outflow equals `min(authorized_amount, debt_at_execution)`, not the debt at signing. A debtor who sees an incoming full-repay can submit `borrow(caller=self, account_id=own, borrows=[(hub, extra)], to=self)` — a normal, authorized call — and capture the repayer's intended refund as fresh borrowed liquidity repaid by the victim's funds. Concretely: Alice signs `repay` with `amount = X` (covering Bob's 1000-unit debt plus headroom, or a large "repay all" value). Bob front-runs a borrow of `X − 1000`. Alice's full `X` is pulled, the pool burns all `X` of debt, refund = 0. Alice pays `X` for a 1000-unit loan she intended to clear; Bob walks away with the borrowed tokens. This is theft of the difference between observed and executed debt, bounded only by the repayer's authorized amount — matching the accepted impact class (theft of user funds).

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Preconditions: (1) a third party repaying the borrower's account using the oversized-amount idiom — a documented, tested pattern rather than an edge case; (2) the debtor (or a delegate) observing the pending repay and landing a `borrow` first, i.e., transaction ordering control on Stellar/Soroban. It requires no privileged role, no oracle manipulation, and no contract bug beyond the fixed-pull/refund shape combined with permissionless repay. It cannot be executed atomically in one transaction (the borrow must strictly precede), so it is a true ordering race rather than a same-tx sandwich, which caps the severity at Medium.

### Recommendation
Add an expectation parameter so the repayer can bound execution-time debt, e.g. `repay(caller, account_id, payments, max_burn_per_leg)` or an `expected_debt` leg field, and revert (not refund) when `ceiled_debt > expected`. Alternatively, since the refund already goes to `payer`, bound the *burned* amount rather than the pulled amount: pull `amount` but cap `net_repay` at a caller-specified `max_repay`, refunding `amount − min(net_repay, max_repay)`. Short term, document that a large repay amount authorizes payment up to that full value against whatever the debt is at inclusion, and steer integrators toward submitting exactly the quoted debt plus minimal rounding headroom.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bob owns `account_id` B with 1_000 USDC debt in hub H (position shares accrued at `borrow_index`).
2. Alice calls `Controller::repay(caller=alice, account_id=B, payments=[((H, USDC), 1_500_0000000)])` intending to fully clear the debt (overpay-and-refund idiom per `tests/.../lifecycle.sh:261-264`), authorizing `token.transfer(alice → pool, 1_500)`.
3. Bob sees the pending tx and submits `Controller::borrow(caller=bob, account_id=B, borrows=[((H, USDC), 500_0000000)], to=None)` — authorized as owner; debt becomes ~1_500 USDC.
4. Alice's repay executes: pool pulls 1_500 USDC, `resolve_repay` computes `overpayment = 1_500 − 1_500 = 0`, `net_repay = 1_500` credited to cash, no refund.
5. Result: Alice paid 1_500 USDC for a loan that was 1_000 USDC when she signed; Bob holds the extra 500 USDC borrowed against Alice's payment.

Relevant code: `contracts/controller/src/positions/debt.rs:69-91` (permissionless repay entry), `contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:30-47` (overpayment resolution and refund to payer), `contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:111-119` (`resolve_repay` caps burn at execution-time debt).
