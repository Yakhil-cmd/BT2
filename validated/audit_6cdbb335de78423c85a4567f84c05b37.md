### Title
Utilization-cap monopolization lets a single borrower freeze all non-liquidation withdrawals - ([File: contracts/pool/src/guards.rs](contracts/pool/src/guards.rs))

### Summary

The pool gates every non-liquidation `withdraw` on a post-state utilization check, `require_utilization_below_max` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`). Because burning supply shares raises utilization, any borrower who pushes a market to its `max_utilization` cap makes *every* withdrawal revert with `UtilizationAboveMax` (127). There is no withdrawal queue or partial-fill fairness: exit capacity is first-come-first-served on the remaining `1 - max_utilization` headroom, and a single unprivileged address can consume that headroom via `controller.borrow` (or leveraged `multiply`/`flash_position`, which mint debt through the same path). Once at the cap, millisecond interest accrual (`interest::global_sync`) monotonically grows the ceiled debt numerator, pinning utilization at or above the cap with no action required from the attacker.

### Finding Description

`require_utilization_below_max` computes `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied) <= max_utilization` *after* the withdraw has burned supply shares (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:57-89` → `gate_and_debit`). Burning supply strictly lowers the denominator, so once `borrowed/supplied == max_utilization`, no withdrawal of any size passes — a property the README states explicitly: "once utilization reaches the cap no withdrawal of any size passes, and interest accrual alone pushes it there" (`contracts/pool/README.md:292-297`).

The cap is reachable by an unprivileged caller: `borrow` accepts post-state utilization up to `max_utilization` (same guard, applied to debt mint), and `max_utilization` may be set below `RAY` (`common/src/types/pool.rs:212-214` allows `optimal <= max <= RAY`). The only releases are borrower repayments or liquidations. A griefer who borrowed against well-collateralized positions simply maintains HF — a healthy position cannot be liquidated, so liquidation's guard bypass (`is_liquidation` skips the utilization check, `withdraw.rs:79`) does not help other suppliers. This is the direct analog of the report's buffer-drain DoS: an attacker converts a fungible exit capacity (here, utilization headroom instead of an ETH buffer) into a denial of exit for everyone else, and there is no queue to order redemptions.

### Impact Explanation

All suppliers' funds in that (hub, asset) market are frozen for as long as the attacker holds utilization at/above the cap — indefinite, since the attacker controls the release (repay) and can keep the position healthy forever. Impact class: temporary freezing of user funds, escalating to effectively permanent while the attacker's debt stands. Suppliers also cannot exit into the interest-accrued value of their positions; any liquidation-driven capacity released is again first-come-first-served with no QoS.

### Likelihood Explanation

Medium. Cost is bounded by the borrow interest on the pinned debt and the collateral locked; no privileged access, timing race, or oracle manipulation is needed — a single `borrow` sized to hit the cap suffices, and `multiply`/`flash_position` lower the upfront capital via leverage. The attack requires a market with `max_utilization < RAY` and enough attacker capital to occupy the headroom, which on thin markets is small.

### Recommendation

Implement withdrawal queuing/partial fills analogous to the report's suggestion: when a withdrawal would breach `max_utilization`, fill it up to the cap (partial burn) instead of reverting, and/or enqueue the remainder FIFO, released by repayments, liquidations, or new supply. Alternatively, reserve utilization headroom the same way `require_liquidation_buffer` reserves 2% of supply for seizures (`guards.rs:39-47`) — e.g., a `WITHDRAW_BUFFER_BPS` of headroom that borrows cannot consume but withdrawals can use.

### Proof of Concept

1. Market `(hub, A)` created with `max_utilization = 0.9 * RAY`. Suppliers deposit; utilization below cap.
2. Attacker `supply` collateral in market B (or same hub), then `controller.borrow` / `multiply` on market A sized so post-state `borrowed/supplied = max_utilization`. `require_utilization_below_max` permits equality.
3. Any user's `controller.withdraw` on market A → pool `withdraw` → `gate_and_debit` → `require_utilization_below_max` → `UtilizationAboveMax` (127), for any amount.
4. Accrual pushes utilization strictly above cap; attacker adds collateral if HF drifts, never repays; healthy position is unliquidatable. Withdrawals remain frozen until the attacker repays or a repay/liquidation from third parties happens to free headroom — which the attacker can re-consume by borrowing again (no queue).
