### Title
Accrual-time `i128` overflow panics permanently freeze a market — no repay, withdraw, or liquidation - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
The memory-corruption class maps onto XOXNO Lending's fixed-point arithmetic domain: every mutating and permissionless entrypoint runs `global_sync` accrual first, and accrual multiplies RAY-scaled share totals by the accrued indexes in `i128`. Once a market's book (scaled shares × index) approaches `i128::MAX`, the next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original` / `accrue_step` **before** the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` clamp can protect it, and because the panic happens at the head of every verb the market is frozen forever: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) runs `accrue_step` for every chunk; `accrue_step` computes `borrowed × borrow_index`, `supplied × supply_index`, and interest deltas through `scaled_to_original`/`update_borrow_index` in `common/src/rates/{scaling,index}.rs`. `update_borrow_index` multiplies before it clamps, and the product `borrow_index × factor` plus `borrowed × new_index` must fit `i128`. The borrow-index ceiling is `10^36` raw RAY, but a whale-scale book is `~1e36` raw shares, so `supplied × supply_index` overflows `i128` (~1.7e38) well before indexes reach their caps. The dedicated cliff test confirms the failure mode verbatim: "the index grows past 170x … and the next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes: no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation" (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-319`), and `docs/reference/invariants.md` concedes "Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached. Bounded indexes do not guarantee representable position or market values" (INV-IDX-01, lines 235-236).

Reachability for a single unprivileged address: the attacker supplies up to the market's supply cap, takes a large borrow to pin utilization in the steep (post-optimal) slope-3 segment, and lets `update_indexes` — a permissionless entrypoint (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:370`) — accrue at `max_borrow_rate` (capped only at 200% APR). Accrual is chunked at one-year steps (`MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS`), so the index compounds at up to e²≈7.4× per year; a book near the caps crosses the `i128` cliff deterministically, after which the panic is permanent — there is no admin escape that skips accrual, since even `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `seize_positions` all call `ops::renewed_market`/`global_sync` first.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every participant of the affected market: supplier principal, unclaimed yield, and the liquidation/recapitalization recovery paths are all unreachable once `scaled_to_original` overflows, because the panic precedes every state mutation. This is not a transient fail-closed gate — no sequence of valid calls restores the market, and bad-debt cleanup itself is unreachable.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires a very large book (shares × index near `i128::MAX`) and sustained high utilization, so it is not triggerable on demand on a small market. However every ingredient is reachable by unprivileged calls (`supply` up to cap, `borrow` to push utilization, permissionless `update_indexes` to accrue), the curve permits up to 200% APR in the steep segment, and once reached the state is irreversible. The protocol's own long-horizon test suite acknowledges this is "the one cliff the domain has" rather than a prevented bound.

### Recommendation
Make accrual saturate instead of panicking: clamp index-valued products at `i128::MAX` (or the index ceilings) inside `scaled_to_original`/`accrue_step` before the multiplication, e.g. pre-check `supplied > i128::MAX / supply_index` and pin the index/value at the bound rather than aborting; alternatively bound market `supplied`/`borrowed` share totals at a hard constant that guarantees `shares × MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` fits `i128` with headroom, enforced at supply/borrow time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supply `~1e9` whole tokens (1e36 raw RAY shares, within `max_cap_for_decimals`-style caps exercised by `large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs`) to a market on a steep curve (e.g. the XLM stress curve, `max_borrow_rate` = 175% APR, optimal 75%).
2. Borrow to hold utilization in the slope-3 segment (~95–98%).
3. Call `update_indexes` periodically; each one-year chunk multiplies the borrow index by up to `e²`. After the index grows ~170×, `supplied × supply_index` (and `borrowed × borrow_index`) exceeds `i128::MAX` and the accrual panics with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`.
4. From that point every call — `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, even `update_indexes` — reverts at the same accrual step, permanently freezing all funds in the market. This mirrors exactly the scenario pinned in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs` (GH-06 cliff test).
