### Title
Unchecked scaled-debt multiplication permanently freezes an oversized market before the borrow-index cap - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The pool’s interest-accrual path multiplies total scaled debt by the borrow index before applying the configured borrow-index ceiling. For a sufficiently large market, that product exceeds `i128::MAX` while the index itself remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, making every subsequent operation that accrues interest fail with `MathOverflow`. This is a medium-severity permanent market-freeze vulnerability because it requires a very large funded market and sustained borrow utilization, but only ordinary lending entrypoints are needed to establish the state.

### Finding Description
Each accrual chunk starts by calculating `borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` before calculating utilization, the interest factor, or the capped borrow index. [1](#0-0)  `scaled_to_original` performs a checked `Ray::mul`, so `borrowed * borrow_index` panics when their RAY product exceeds `i128`. [2](#0-1)  The borrow-index cap is applied only later by `update_borrow_index`, after the already-overflowing debt-value multiplication has completed. [3](#0-2)  `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is `1e36` raw RAY, but the value ceiling is reached much earlier when total scaled debt is also large. [4](#0-3) 

`global_sync` executes this unchecked accrual for every elapsed chunk before a market mutation is committed. [5](#0-4)  The public controller paths for `supply`, `borrow`, `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation, and bad-debt cleanup all enter the corresponding pool mutation flow, so they cannot bypass the accrual panic. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Even the explicit controller-driven `update_indexes` path invokes `ops::market::accrue`, which reaches the same arithmetic. [9](#0-8) 

The repository’s long-horizon test explicitly identifies this cliff: a one-billion-token, 18-decimal market at 98% utilization reaches the RAY-value ceiling before the index cap, leaving the index below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [10](#0-9)  The test establishes the state through normal raw supply and borrow actions, advances ledger time, receives `MathOverflow` from `update_indexes`, and confirms that both withdrawal and repayment then fail for the same reason. [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation
Once `borrowed * borrow_index` crosses the `i128` range, the affected market cannot accrue interest without reverting. Because accrual is mandatory before state-changing pool operations, suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and bad-debt cleanup cannot execute for that market. This causes permanent freezing of pool funds and blocks the protocol’s normal insolvency-recovery path.

### Likelihood Explanation
The condition is difficult to reach because it requires an extremely large token book—demonstrated with `1_000_000_000` whole 18-decimal tokens, 98% utilization, and enough collateral elsewhere to support the borrow—followed by sustained high interest accrual. [12](#0-11)  It does not require privileged calls once a market’s configured caps and liquidity permit that size, and any unprivileged caller can later trigger the freeze through `update_indexes`, repayment, withdrawal, or liquidation. The practical dependency on an exceptionally large market and prolonged utilization makes this Medium rather than High or Critical.

### Recommendation
Prevent the pre-cap debt-value multiplication from overflowing. In particular, the accrual logic should detect `borrowed.raw() > i128::MAX / borrow_index.raw()` before calling `scaled_to_original`, and handle that boundary deliberately rather than trapping. A safe design could use wider intermediate arithmetic for utilization and reward calculations, or enforce a market-level maximum scaled-debt value that is coordinated with `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Add boundary tests proving that accrual, repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and `clean_bad_debt` remain executable when the unscaled debt approaches the representable ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance has listed an 18-decimal market whose caps permit approximately `1e36` raw units of supply.
2. A supplier calls `Controller::supply` with that amount, and a sufficiently collateralized account calls `Controller::borrow` for 98% of it; these are the same ordinary actions used by the repository test. [13](#0-12) 
3. Time passes while utilization remains high. Each yearly call to `Controller::update_indexes` invokes pool accrual until the next chunk computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and overflows `i128`. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 
4. The stored index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, confirming that the configured cap did not prevent the overflow. [16](#0-15) 
5. Subsequent `Controller::withdraw` and `Controller::repay` calls fail with `MathOverflow`, demonstrating the permanent freeze. [17](#0-16)
