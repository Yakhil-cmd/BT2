### No vulnerability found for this question.

This describes intended debt-cap behavior, not a defect. `system-borrow` enforces `(asserts! (<= (+ debt amount) CAP-DEBT) ERR-DEBT-CAP-EXCEEDED)` against the global `cap-debt` variable and current `total-debt` [1](#0-0) . A debt cap is, by design, a shared ceiling across all borrowers — it is not meant to guarantee simultaneous borrowing capacity to every principal, and headroom naturally depends on outstanding debt being repaid by whoever holds it, exactly as in any lending protocol with a global borrow cap (Aave, Compound, etc.). Attacker A holding debt and choosing not to repay is not exploiting a bug: A pays real economic cost (accruing interest via the `index` accrual in `accrue`) to occupy that headroom [2](#0-1) , and gains no profit from denying B — this is ordinary shared-pool resource consumption, explicitly listed as out-of-scope ("liquidity being consumed"). There is no missing forced-deleverage mechanism defect here; requiring a cap that shrinks headroom as usage grows and requires eventual repayment by *some* borrower to free capacity is the intended mechanism of a debt cap, not a NON-INTERFERENCE violation between two unprivileged principals.
