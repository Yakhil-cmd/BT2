### Title
Interest accrual overflow permanently freezes an oversized high-utilization market - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Market accrual converts total scaled debt and supply to RAY-denominated values before applying the interest-index cap. Once `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` or `supplied * supply_index / RAY` no longer fits `i128`, `scaled_to_original` raises `MathOverflow`. Because every pool mutation accrues before acting, a market that reaches this state cannot be repaid, withdrawn, liquidated, recapitalized, or index-synced again.

### Finding Description
`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs::global_sync` invokes `accrue_step` for each elapsed accrual chunk. `common/src/rates/simulate.rs::accrue_step` first calculates:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
```

`scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(index)`, which performs half-up `x * index / RAY` arithmetic and returns an `i128` `Ray`. Although `update_borrow_index` later clamps the index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, that cap protects only the index—not the already-overflowing market-value product.

The existing deterministic regression test demonstrates the boundary: an 18-decimal market with `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` raw supplied units and `98%` borrowed reaches `MathOverflow` after sustained accrual, while `borrow_index` is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The test then confirms both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with `MathOverflow` because they run the same accrual first.

The strongest permissionless path is:

1. Call `controller::supply` with `account_id = 0` and an asset entry such as:
   `assets = [((hub_id, debt_asset), 1_000_000_000 * 10^18)]`.
2. From a second owned account, supply sufficient collateral through `controller::supply`, then call:
   `controller::borrow(caller, collateral_account_id, borrows = [((hub_id, debt_asset), 980_000_000 * 10^18)], to = attacker_address)`.
3. Maintain high utilization while normal interest accrues.
4. Call `controller::update_indexes(hub_assets = [(hub_id, debt_asset)])`. Once the RAY market value exceeds `i128::MAX`, this call raises `MathOverflow` before committing the next accrual.
5. Any subsequent `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, `flash_loan`, or strategy operation touching that market reaches `global_sync` first and fails on the same product.

The required caps and rate parameters are inside the protocol's accepted domains: `max_cap_for_decimals(18)` permits approximately `170.14` billion whole tokens, and the demonstrated curve uses a `175%` maximum borrow rate, below the `200%` protocol maximum. The token amounts are economically extreme, but they are representable and admitted by the listed market domain.

### Impact Explanation
This is permanent freezing of all funds and debt accounting for the affected `(hub_id, asset)` market. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and recapitalization or bad-debt cleanup cannot recover the market because those controller paths call into pool operations that accrue first.

It can also create protocol insolvency in practical terms: outstanding debt and supplier claims continue to exist, but the market can no longer execute the operations needed to settle or socialize them.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is low-to-medium rather than high. The exploit requires a very large token position—on the order of one billion whole tokens at 18 decimals—and sustained high utilization under a steep valid rate curve. It also requires governance to list such a market with sufficiently large caps and a permissive utilization ceiling.

However, no privileged action is needed after listing. An unprivileged whale can provide the liquidity and collateralized borrow through ordinary `supply` and `borrow` calls, and any unprivileged caller can then trigger the fatal transition through permissionless `update_indexes`. The condition is deterministic once market totals approach the representable RAY-value ceiling.

### Recommendation
Accrual should bound the scaled totals and index products before calling non-saturating multiplication. In particular:

- Add a checked/saturating safe path for `scaled_to_original` during market-level utilization and accrual.
- Clamp `borrow_index` before the market debt value becomes unrepresentable, not only after multiplying the index.
- Consider storing a market-level `max_ray_value` or deriving one from `i128::MAX / index`, then rejecting new supply/borrow before the market can enter the unrecoverable region.
- At minimum, make rescue paths such as `repay`, liquidation debt repayment, bad-debt cleanup, and `recapitalize` use a saturating or pre-clamped accrual mode so repayment and recovery remain possible after ordinary accrual becomes unrepresentable.
- Emit or expose a warning metric when `scaled * index` approaches the arithmetic ceiling.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains a deterministic reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`.

Relevant sequence:

```rust
let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);
```

The test advances time until `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`, verifies `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and then confirms:

```rust
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

The root cause is the unguarded market-value multiplication in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs::accrue_step` through `common/src/rates/scaling.rs::scaled_to_original`, reached unconditionally by `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs::global_sync`.
