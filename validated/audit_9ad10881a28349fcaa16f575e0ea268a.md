### Title
Renewable "challenging period" timer in the 51% attack game AA allows the leading team to indefinitely stall game finalization, freezing all contributed funds - (File: test/samples/51_attack_game.oscript)

### Summary
The `51_attack_game.oscript` autonomous-agent (AA) template shipped in ocore implements a "challenging period" pattern that is structurally identical to the Tigris `limitClose`/`updateTpSl`/`addToPosition` exploit: a state field that gates a time-locked action (`finish`) can be reset for a trivial cost by the same unprivileged party who benefits from delaying that action, allowing indefinite postponement of finalization and payout.

### Finding Description
The AA tracks a candidate winner and a challenge deadline: [1](#0-0) 

Whenever *any* contribution pushes a team's cumulative amount above 51% of the AA's base balance, `var['winner']` and `var['challenging_period_start_ts']` are (re)set to the current trigger timestamp: [2](#0-1) 

The `finish` case only succeeds once the challenging period has elapsed without the timer being renewed: [3](#0-2) 

Because the state-updating branch ("contribute to a team") has no restriction preventing the *already-winning* team from contributing to itself, and because contributing to the winning team while it is already above the 51% threshold keeps the ratio above 0.51 (adding the same amount to both numerator `team_amount` and denominator `balance[base]`), the winning team's own members can repeatedly submit a minimal qualifying contribution (`trigger.output[[asset=base]] >= 1e5`, i.e., 100,000 bytes — a negligible fraction of the total pool) purely to overwrite `challenging_period_start_ts` with the current timestamp. This never lets `timestamp >= var['challenging_period_start_ts'] + $challenging_period` become true, so `finish` (line 101-102) will always bounce with `'challenging period not expired yet'`.

This is the same three-part exploit shape described in the Tigris report:
1. A cheap, unrestricted action (`contribute`) can renew a delay/timer.
2. A privileged final action (`finish` → payout) is gated purely on that timer having expired, with no check on whether the renewal was legitimate/competitive.
3. The party who benefits from staying "in the lead" is the same party who can cheaply renew the timer, letting them lock in their current advantageous state indefinitely.

### Impact Explanation
Because payouts (`pay out the winnings`) are only reachable once `var['finished']` is set, an unprivileged member of the currently-leading team can grief the AA by perpetually renewing the challenge timer with negligible-cost contributions. This permanently prevents `finish` from succeeding, which in turn permanently blocks the payout case, freezing all bytes and team-share assets held by the AA for every contributor (winning and losing teams alike). This matches the "AA fund loss/freezing" impact category — funds become permanently locked because the finalization condition can never be satisfied by an adversarial insider.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only sending a standard "contribute" trigger to the already-winning team with the minimum required amount (1e5 bytes) at any point before the challenging period elapses — no privileged access, cosigning, or coordination with other actors is needed. Any single member of the leading team (or the leading team itself, self-funding) can execute this unilaterally and repeatedly, making the likelihood high once a team has taken the lead.

### Recommendation
The `finish` condition (and/or the timer-renewal condition) should not allow the currently-winning team to reset `challenging_period_start_ts` merely by contributing to itself. For example: only reset the timer when a *different* team overtakes the current `var['winner']`, or require the timestamp check on `finish` to depend on the last time a *different* candidate became the leader rather than on the last contribution of any kind to the incumbent leader.

### Proof of Concept
1. Team A contributes enough to cross 51% of the pool; `var['winner']='A'` and `challenging_period_start_ts=T`.
2. Before `T + 24h`, a member of team A sends another `{team:'A'}` trigger with exactly `1e5` bytes. Since `team_A_amount` and `balance[base]` both increase by the same amount and the ratio remains above 0.51, the branch at line 88 fires again, resetting `challenging_period_start_ts` to the new `timestamp`.
3. Repeat step 2 just before each 24-hour window expires.
4. `finish` (line 101) always bounces with `'challenging period not expired yet'`, so `var['finished']` never becomes true and the payout branch (line 116 onward) is unreachable, permanently freezing all funds in the AA.
