### Title
Arbiter contract "appeal" mechanism does not prevent immediate fund withdrawal by the initial dispute winner - (File: arbiter_contract.js)

### Summary
The Obyte arbiter-contract escrow protocol lets two parties lock funds in a shared multi-signature address whose spending conditions are defined by `deriveSharedAddress()` [1](#0-0) . When a dispute is opened, the arbiter is expected to resolve it by posting a data-feed value `CONTRACT_<hash> = <winner_address>` (or `CONTRACT_DONE_<hash>` for private assets), which is checked directly by the address definition to authorize the winner to spend the escrowed funds. The wallet/app layer additionally exposes an "appeal" workflow (`appeal()`, and status transitions `dispute_resolved → in_appeal → appeal_approved/appeal_declined`), suggesting a losing party can contest the arbiter's initial decision before funds are finally awarded. However, this appeal state is only tracked in the local `wallet_arbiter_contracts` table/off-chain protocol messages and is never encoded into the on-chain address definition, so it has no effect on what can actually be spent.

### Finding Description
`deriveSharedAddress` builds the escrow address as an `or` of five branches [2](#0-1) :
1. mutual signature of both parties,
2/3. either party unilaterally paying the other party the contractually correct amount,
4/5. the party named by the arbiter's data feed `CONTRACT_<hash>` (or `CONTRACT_DONE_<hash>` for private assets) spending unilaterally.

Branches 4 and 5 only check `["in data feed", [[contract.arbiter_address], "CONTRACT_" + contract.hash, "=", offeror_address/acceptor_address]]` [3](#0-2) . As soon as the arbiter's first-instance decision unit is posted and becomes stable, this condition is permanently and irrevocably true — nothing in the address definition references any subsequent appeal outcome.

Meanwhile, the app-level flow treats the dispute as still contestable: `appeal(hash, cb)` is only reachable when `objContract.status === "dispute_resolved"` [4](#0-3) , and the arbstore later replies with `appeal_approved` or `appeal_declined`, which is only permitted through the wallet message handler `wallet.js` case `'arbiter_contract_update'` for the `in_appeal` status [5](#0-4) . None of this ever updates or re-derives the shared address, and the original `CONTRACT_<hash>` data feed value is immutable once posted (a data feed is just a message on a unit; it cannot be "revoked").

Consequently, the party who wins the arbiter's first-instance decision can immediately compose and broadcast a payment spending from the shared address using branch 4/5 the moment the resolution unit is stable, regardless of whether the losing party requests (or has already requested) an appeal, and regardless of whether the arbstore later reverses the decision via `appeal_approved`. There is no mechanism analogous to "the liquidity provider cannot withdraw until the claim is created and voted on" — here, "the claim can be reversed on appeal" is advertised by the protocol but not enforced on-chain, so an appeal that succeeds cannot recover funds already withdrawn by the initial winner.

### Impact Explanation
This allows unauthorized/definitive loss of escrowed funds for the party who should have won on appeal: the initial (possibly wrong) arbiter ruling can be exploited by the temporarily "winning" party to drain the shared address before an appeal can even be filed or concludes, defeating the entire purpose of the appeal/arbstore dispute-review process and permanently misallocating the disputed funds (base coins or non-fixed-denomination assets) held in the shared address.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any party to an arbiter contract who wins the arbiter's initial ruling can trivially trigger this by simply composing a spending unit off the `CONTRACT_<hash>` data feed as soon as it stabilizes — this requires no special privilege beyond being a party to the contract, and there is a direct incentive to do so (grab funds before an unfavorable appeal outcome), making exploitation straightforward for any dishonest counterparty in a disputed contract.

### Recommendation
Encode the appeal window/appeal state into the on-chain shared-address definition itself, e.g., require the winner's spending branch to also prove (via an additional data feed condition or timelock) that the appeal period has elapsed without an appeal being filed, or make the arbstore's final (post-appeal) decision the only value the address definition trusts, rather than gating appeals purely at the application layer while leaving the underlying funds immediately spendable off the first data-feed post.

### Proof of Concept
1. Two parties open an arbiter contract for a public asset and fund the shared address per `pay()` [6](#0-5) .
2. A dispute is opened via `openDispute()`, status becomes `in_dispute` [7](#0-6) .
3. The arbiter posts a `CONTRACT_<hash> = winner_address` data-feed unit; once stable, the app sets status to `dispute_resolved` (`arbiter_contract.js:894`).
4. Before the losing party can call `appeal()` (`arbiter_contract.js:321-355`), or even immediately after they do, the "winner" composes a unit spending from the shared address using the definition branch 4/5 (`arbiter_contract.js:473-480`) that only checks the already-posted, immutable `CONTRACT_<hash>` data feed.
5. This spend is valid per the address definition and gets included/stabilized in the DAG, transferring the funds out of the shared address.
6. Even if the arbstore subsequently issues `appeal_approved` in favor of the original loser, there is no on-chain mechanism to reverse step 5 — the funds are already gone, so the appeal process is unable to correct the outcome.
