### Title
AA "randomness" derived from `trigger.unit` can be freely precomputed and grinded by the trigger sender before broadcast - ([File: formula/evaluation.js])

### Summary
Autonomous Agents (AAs) can source "randomness" from `trigger.unit` (the hash of the unit that triggers the AA) directly, or indirectly through `number_from_seed(trigger.unit)` or similar formulas that mix in `trigger.unit`. Because the unit hash is a deterministic function of content that the trigger's author fully controls before signing/broadcasting, an attacker can locally enumerate many candidate trigger units (varying `data`, `timestamp`, or other free fields), compute the resulting "random" outcome for each off-chain, and only ever broadcast the one unit whose derived outcome favors them. This is the same class of issue as the gold-card `_commit`/`recommit` grinding bug, except here the attacker doesn't even need to pay to "retry" — retries are free until the final favorable unit is actually broadcast and paid for.

### Finding Description
`trigger.unit` is set from the exact hash of the posted trigger unit: `getTrigger()` sets `trigger.unit = objUnit.unit` [1](#0-0)  and the evaluator exposes it verbatim to oscript formulas via the `trigger.unit` case [2](#0-1) .

The unit hash itself (`objUnit.unit`) is a pure, attacker-computable function of the unit's content — `getUnitHash`/`getNakedUnit` in `object_hash.js` compute it deterministically from fields the author controls (parents, data payload, timestamp, outputs, etc.) [3](#0-2) , and unit validation simply checks that the claimed hash matches this deterministic recomputation [4](#0-3) .

The oscript language provides `number_from_seed(seed[, min[, max]])`, which derives a "random" number by SHA-256-hashing the seed and mapping the digest into a numeric range [5](#0-4) . If an AA author uses `trigger.unit` (or any expression combining `trigger.data`, `trigger.address`, etc., that the sender also controls) as the seed to `number_from_seed`, or otherwise branches AA logic (payouts, lottery wins, card/item rarity, etc.) on `trigger.unit`, the outcome is entirely predictable and freely re-rollable by the very party sending the trigger, before they ever commit to broadcasting it.

This mirrors the reported gold-card bug: there, the "randomness" depended on a future block/DAG state that the buyer could keep re-committing against until desired; here, the "randomness" is derived from data the trigger sender picks and hashes itself before broadcasting, so they can iterate for free, entirely off-chain, and only submit the one unit (and pay its fee) that yields the outcome they want.

### Impact Explanation
Any AA that uses `trigger.unit` (or similarly sender-controlled trigger fields) as an entropy source for anything of value — game-of-chance payouts, item/card rarity, lottery winner selection, fee/reward randomization — can be gamed deterministically by the calling address. The attacker can guarantee winning outcomes, causing direct fund loss/drainage from the AA to the attacker at the expense of other honest participants or the AA's reserves. This falls under "AA fund loss" impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any AA design that naively uses `trigger.unit` or other trigger-sender-controlled fields as a randomness seed, since this is a natural, low-friction pattern for oscript authors implementing games/lotteries with `number_from_seed`, and the exploit requires no special privileges beyond being an ordinary AA trigger sender who can locally hash arbitrary candidate unit content before choosing what to broadcast.

### Recommendation
AA developers must not use `trigger.unit`, `trigger.data`, `trigger.address`, or any other value fully known/chosen by the trigger's author as the sole/primary randomness seed. Prefer values unknown to the sender at trigger-composition time, such as a future `mc_unit` value that stabilizes after the trigger, oracle-provided data feeds, or `vrf_verify`-checked externally supplied randomness that the sender cannot bias, combined with a commit-reveal scheme where the "commit" cannot be discarded without cost (e.g., forfeiting a bond) if the reveal is unfavorable.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker drafts an AA-trigger unit that includes a payment to a lottery/gambling AA using `number_from_seed(trigger.unit)` (or similar) to decide the payout.
2. Before broadcasting, the attacker locally varies non-critical fields of the draft unit (e.g., `data` payload contents, timestamp, parent selection) and, for each variant, computes `objectHash.getUnitHash(objUnit)` exactly as `validate()` would [4](#0-3) , then evaluates what `number_from_seed(candidate_hash)` would yield using the same SHA-256-based algorithm [6](#0-5) .
3. The attacker only signs and broadcasts the one variant whose derived value produces the maximal payout/favorable branch in the AA logic.
4. The AA, upon receiving this trigger, computes the identical `trigger.unit`/`number_from_seed` result and pays out according to the attacker-predetermined "random" outcome, causing systematic fund loss to the AA/other participants.
