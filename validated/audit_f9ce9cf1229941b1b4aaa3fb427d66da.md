### Title
Accrual overflow in `accrue_step` permanently freezes a market — a whale-sized book at sustained high utilization bricks repay/withdraw/liquidate — (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The Virtuoso CVE is a DoS reachable by a crafted request. The lending analog is a state-driven DoS: the pool's accrual path multiplies scaled balances by the borrow index, and when the product `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX`, accrual panics with `MathOverflow` before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap can engage. Because `global_sync` runs first inside every mutating entrypoint, the market becomes permanently unusable: no repay, withdraw, supply, borrow, or liquidation touches it again.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33` unconditionally accrues elapsed time in chunks of `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` via `accrue_chunk` (`interest.rs:39-53`), which calls the shared `accrue_step` in `common/src/rates.rs`. The index multiplication panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled_amount * index` overflows `i128` (see `mul_div_half_up` → `GenericError::MathOverflow` in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). There is no try/catch and no clamp path before the multiplication: the repo's own test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:315-361`) demonstrates that on an 18-decimal market holding 1 billion tokens at 98% utilization on a steep curve, the borrow index grows past ~170× and the next `update_indexes` panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` — the cap "never engages." The same test asserts `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` then both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, since every verb accrues first.

Reachability: `update_indexes`, `supply`, `borrow`, `repay`, `withdraw`, and `liquidate` are all unprivileged. The attacker path is: supply a very large principal to a high-decimal market, borrow to drive utilization into the steep segment of the configured curve, and let time accrue (the victim here is the market's other suppliers — even without an attacker, a legitimately large market drifts upward in utilization over time because debt compounds faster than supply, as the test header notes at lines 10-13). Once `borrowed_scaled_ray * borrow_index` crosses `i128::MAX`, any subsequent accrual panics and the market's state can never be advanced again.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: all suppliers' tokens in that market become unwithdrawable, borrowers cannot repay (so their collateral in other markets cannot be freed via repay either — `repay` accrues the debt market first), and liquidations touching the market revert. This satisfies the "permanent freezing of funds" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a market whose raw supplied value is within ~170× of the `i128` ceiling (i.e., a whale-scale book on a high-decimals asset) combined with a steep admin-configured rate curve and years of sustained high utilization. It is not triggerable by a single cheap transaction — the capital and time requirements are real — but the failure mode is deterministic, unrecoverable, and hits a book that can grow into the trap organically through normal supply/borrow demand. No governance action can rescue it because `recapitalize`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` paths also go through pool state that accrues first. Likelihood is therefore low-to-moderate; severity high (permanent freeze of an entire market's funds).

### Recommendation
Bound the multiplication in `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`: clamp `borrow_index` at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (and `supply_index` at `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY`) *before* computing `scaled * index`, so the cap actually engages instead of the product overflowing first. Alternatively compute the accrued value via a saturating `mul_div` (as `mul_div_floor_saturating` already exists in `common/src/math/fp_core.rs`) and stop accruing interest once the value ceiling is reached, keeping the market operable for repay/withdraw.

### Proof of Concept
The repo's own harness test is the PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`. It supplies `1_000_000_000` tokens of an 18-decimal asset, borrows 98% of it on the XLM stress curve, then calls `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` in a yearly loop until it returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`; it then asserts `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (cap never engaged) and that `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, proving the permanent market freeze.
