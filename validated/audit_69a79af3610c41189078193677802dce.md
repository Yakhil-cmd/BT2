### Title
Single failing secondary-AA payout bounces the entire trigger response, blocking unrelated already-valid reward/payout branches in the same unit - (File: aa_composer.js)

### Summary
In ocore's AA engine, a primary trigger response can contain several independent payment branches (e.g. an "internal" payout handled directly by state vars, and an "external" payout forwarded to another AA acting as a reward/asset distributor). All these branches are packed into one response unit and settled atomically. If any one of the resulting secondary AA triggers bounces for reasons unrelated to the other branch, `handleSecondaryTriggers` discards and reverts the *entire* primary response — including the fully valid, independent reward/payout that had nothing to do with the failure.

### Finding Description
`handleSecondaryTriggers` fires a child `handleTrigger` call for every AA address that received an output from the current response unit, via `async.eachSeries`: [1](#0-0) 

If any single secondary AA in that series bounces, the callback receives an error and the whole flow reverts the primary (non-secondary) response: [2](#0-1) 

`revert()` then rolls back the entire unit to the savepoint, discards `arrResponses`, clears the batch, and re-runs `bounce()` on the *original* trigger, which recomputes a brand-new bounce-only response, refunding only `trigger.outputs - bounce_fees` back to the original sender: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Consequently, if a single trigger unit was meant to settle two independent claim types in one AA call — e.g. one message paying out an "internal" reward directly to the trigger address, and another message routing a payment to a second AA that performs an "external" distribution/verification step — a failure confined to the second (external) leg causes `revert()`/`bounce()` to discard the first (internal) leg too, even though it was already validated, funded, and would have succeeded standalone. The AA composer does not offer any mechanism to settle the independent branches separately or to retry only the failing branch; the entire trigger is treated as one all-or-nothing unit tied to the fate of the least-reliable secondary AA it happens to touch.

### Impact Explanation
A user (or any unprivileged AA integrator) who is entitled to two independent, unrelated payouts from one trigger can be denied both because of an unrelated failure in the leg that forwards to another AA. Funds that were correctly computed and would have been paid out on the "internal" branch never leave the AA and are effectively withheld/returned-with-fee-deduction instead of delivered, because the entire response — not just the failing leg — is rolled back and replaced with a bounce. This is a fund-availability/DoS impact reachable purely by an ordinary trigger sender interacting with any AA design that fans out to more than one downstream AA in a single response, matching the reported bug class of "claim logic X's failure blocks claim logic Y's success" but at the protocol engine level rather than in application script logic.

### Likelihood Explanation
This is triggered any time a primary AA's response unit contains outputs to more than one AA address (a common composition pattern for reward-splitting, or forwarding to a helper/verifier AA) and one of those secondary AAs bounces for any reason (insufficient balance, its own logic bounce, `max_aa_responses`/`MAX_RESPONSES_PER_PRIMARY_TRIGGER` limits, etc. per [5](#0-4) ). No malicious actor is required — an ordinary, unprivileged trigger sender simply needs to interact with an AA that fans out payouts to two independent destinations, one of which is itself an AA subject to failure conditions outside the trigger sender's control.

### Recommendation
Either (a) document and enforce that AA authors must not combine unrelated, independently-claimable payout branches with a downstream-AA leg in the same response if partial success is required, or (b) provide an engine-level mechanism to isolate secondary-trigger failures so that a bounced secondary AA only cancels its own leg (and its specific output) rather than reverting the entire primary response and all sibling payments/state changes that had no dependency on it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy AA `X` whose response to a trigger produces two payment messages in the same unit: one direct payment to `trigger.address` (internal reward) and one payment to AA `Y` (external reward distributor), as in the flow validated at [6](#0-5) .
2. Craft conditions so AA `Y`'s own trigger logic bounces (e.g., unrelated balance/logic condition), matching test patterns such as [7](#0-6) .
3. Observe via `handleSecondaryTriggers` that the bounce of `Y` propagates to `revert()` on the primary response of `X` [8](#0-7) , discarding the payment that was already destined for `trigger.address`, and the entire unit is replaced by a bounce-only response computed in `bounce()` [9](#0-8) .
