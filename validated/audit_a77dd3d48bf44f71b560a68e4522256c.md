This confirms `pool_state.pool_creator` is the sole gate for who can ever call `collect_creator_fee` (`address = pool_state.load()?.pool_creator` constraint on the `creator` signer) [1](#0-0) , and it also drives who receives permissionless payouts via ATA authority in `collect_creator_fee_permissionless` [2](#0-1) . That field is set once, permanently, at pool creation time by `PoolState::initialize` [3](#0-2) .

In `initialize_with_permission`, the account that becomes `pool_creator` is a completely unconstrained `UncheckedAccount<'info>` named `creator`, distinct from the `payer: Signer<'info>` that actually authorizes and funds the transaction [4](#0-3) . The `Permission` PDA that gates who may call this instruction is derived from `payer.key()`, not from `creator` [5](#0-4) . `creator` never signs and is never checked against `payer` or any authorization data; it is passed straight into `pool_state.initialize(..., ctx.accounts.creator.key(), ...)` [6](#0-5) .

The `pool_state` PDA address itself is derived only from `amm_config`, `token_0_mint`, and `token_1_mint` — it does not encode `creator` at all [7](#0-6) . This is the exact analog of the reported bug class: the salt/identity used to predict/deploy the resource (here, the pool PDA) is decoupled from the party that should own the resulting state (here, `pool_creator`), and the actual "owner" field is chosen unilaterally by whoever submits the transaction (the permissioned `payer`), exactly matching Scenario 3 in the report — a relayer/payer can deploy the pool "for" someone while unilaterally setting privileged state (here, permanent fee-recipient rights) to any address they choose, and the intended creator has no way to validate or contest this beforehand since the pool address doesn't depend on the creator field.

### Title
Permissioned `payer` can set an arbitrary, unverified `pool_creator` in `initialize_with_permission`, permanently redirecting all future creator-fee revenue - (File: `programs/cp-swap/src/instructions/initialize_with_permission.rs`)

### Summary
`initialize_with_permission` accepts `creator` as an unchecked, non-signing account fully controlled by the whitelisted `payer`, and stores it verbatim as `pool_state.pool_creator`. This field permanently and irrevocably determines who is entitled to withdraw all accumulated `creator_fees_token_0`/`creator_fees_token_1` for the life of the pool.

### Finding Description
`InitializeWithPermission` requires `payer` to be a `Signer` gated by a `Permission` PDA keyed on `payer.key()` [5](#0-4) , but `creator` is declared as `UncheckedAccount<'info>` with no signature requirement and no constraint tying it to `payer`, a client-provided allowlist, or any other verifiable identity [4](#0-3) . Inside `initialize_with_permission`, `ctx.accounts.creator.key()` is written directly into `PoolState.pool_creator` via `pool_state.initialize(...)` [6](#0-5) [3](#0-2) . This field is never revisited or made mutable after creation.

Because the pool's PDA address is derived only from `amm_config`, `token_0_mint`, and `token_1_mint` [7](#0-6) , an intended pool creator who requests a permissioned relayer/`payer` to create a pool "for them" (e.g., in a launchpad flow) has no on-chain mechanism to guarantee that the recorded `pool_creator` matches themselves — the payer can freely substitute any pubkey (including their own) as `creator` in the same instruction, producing an identical, expected-looking pool state and address, but with permanently hijacked fee rights. This mirrors Scenario 3 of the referenced report: the party executing the deployment transaction can silently alter privileged on-chain state (here, the fee-beneficiary) that the intended recipient cannot detect or validate beforehand.

### Impact Explanation
`pool_creator` is the sole authority accepted by `CollectCreatorFee` (`address = pool_state.load()?.pool_creator` on the signer) [1](#0-0)  and the sole ATA-authority target in `CollectCreatorFeePermissionless` [2](#0-1) . `creator_fees_token_0`/`creator_fees_token_1` accumulate from real swapper trading fees over the pool's lifetime [8](#0-7) . A permissioned `payer` can therefore permanently and irrevocably divert this entire revenue stream to any address of their choosing at pool-creation time, with no possibility of correction, constituting theft of funds legitimately owed to the true intended pool creator.

### Likelihood Explanation
The `initialize_with_permission` instruction is explicitly in scope and reachable by any account holding the `Permission` PDA (a designated pool-creator role) in a single transaction with attacker-chosen `creator` data; no additional privilege beyond the intended "permissioned pool creator" role is required, and the flaw is a straightforward missing-constraint bug (no signer/authorization check on `creator`), making exploitation trivial and requiring no race conditions or complex preconditions.

### Recommendation
Require `creator` to either be a `Signer` (proving consent of the actual intended creator) or add an explicit constraint binding `creator` to `payer` (e.g., `creator.key() == payer.key()` when the payer is meant to also be the creator) or to a pre-registered mapping/allowlist entry established by the intended creator before pool creation. Additionally, expose a view/simulation path so an intended creator can verify the `pool_creator` that will be recorded before finalizing a delegated deployment, analogous to the recommendation in the referenced report of predicting/validating addresses based on the true owner rather than the transaction executor.

### Proof of Concept
1. A permissioned account `P` (holding the `Permission` PDA derived from `P.key()`) is asked to create a pool on behalf of intended creator `Alice` via `initialize_with_permission`, supplying `token_0_mint`, `token_1_mint`, `amm_config`.
2. `P` submits the transaction as `payer`, but sets the `creator` account field to their own pubkey (or any pubkey they control) instead of `Alice`'s pubkey.
3. `pool_state.initialize(...)` records `pool_creator = P` [6](#0-5) ; the pool address itself is unaffected since it doesn't depend on `creator` [7](#0-6) , so Alice has no way to detect the substitution from the address alone.
4. As swaps occur, `creator_fees_token_0`/`token_1` accrue in `pool_state`.
5. `P` calls `collect_creator_fee` (passing the `address = pool_state.load()?.pool_creator` check since `P` is the recorded creator) [1](#0-0)  and withdraws all creator fees that were intended for Alice, permanently and with no recourse.
