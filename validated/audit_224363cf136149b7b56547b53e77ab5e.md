### Title
An unprivileged third-party `supply` can inject a `no_seize` collateral leg into a victim's `supply_positions`, permanently reverting every `liquidate`/`get_liquidation_estimate` plan against that account — ([File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs])

### Summary
The reported bug class is: a user-reachable path pushes an entry into a stored list; a later batch/finalize routine iterates that list and one bad entry reverts the whole loop (LidoVault's `fixedOngoingWithdrawalUsers` → `finalizeVaultEndedWithdrawals`). The XOXNO analog is the per-account collateral list `account.supply_positions`: anyone can `supply` into *another* user's account (`supply` is permissionless and credits `account_id`), and the liquidation planner iterates *all* nonzero supply legs of the seized account, reverting with `SpokeAssetSeizureHalted` (`controller #318`) if any planned leg's listing has the `no_seize` flag. An attacker can therefore deposit a small amount of a `no_seize`-flagged listed asset into an unhealthy victim account, poisoning the iterated collateral set and DoSing liquidation of that account's debt.

### Finding Description
- `process_supply` / the `supply` entrypoint only requires an authorized caller and credits positions to `account_id` — the harness confirms third-party top-ups are allowed: `try_supply_to_account(BOB, ALICE, "USDC", 1.0)` succeeds (`tests/test-harness/.../security_audit.rs:489`).
- Liquidation builds a pro-rata seizure plan over every supply position in `account.supply_positions`. Per the threat model and liquidator skill docs: "Seizure uses its own `no_seize` flag across nonzero planned collateral legs; one such flag can abort a pro-rata liquidation" (`docs/explanation/threat-model.md:271-273`), failing with `SpokeAssetSeizureHalted` (`skills/xoxno-lending-liquidations/SKILL.md:61-62`). `plan.rs`/`apply.rs` invoke `enforce_spoke_asset_flags`/`no_seize` checks during planning.
- Unlike the dust leg-drop path (`a_sub_unit_leg_is_dropped_while_its_siblings_are_still_seized`), a leg whose planned seizure is nonzero is kept and its flag halts the entire plan — so the attacker merely needs a deposit large enough that its pro-rata share does not round to zero, still a trivial cost.
- Preconditions: the victim has a free supply slot (position limit) and the spoke lists an asset carrying the `no_seize` flag (e.g., RWA/liqvid-style collateral). The attack is repeatable: if the victim withdraws the dust leg, the attacker re-deposits, and a bot can front-run any `liquidate` transaction with a fresh dust supply.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of a core protocol function: while the injected leg is present, **every** `liquidate` call (both `SeizeMode::Transfer` and `Credit`, which share the same planner) against the victim reverts. An underwater account cannot be liquidated on demand, so debt keeps accruing while collateral may keep falling — direct path to protocol insolvency / bad-debt accumulation. Medium severity: per-account and reversible (victim or `clean_bad_debt` can remove the leg; cleanup ignores listing flags per INV-LIQ-04), but the attacker can re-arm cheaply or time the injection to sandwich a liquidation, making the DoS effectively continuous for motivated griefing.

### Likelihood Explanation
High where a `no_seize` listing exists: `supply` is unauthenticated with respect to the target account, the cost is one dust deposit, and the flag-check ordering means the injected leg is never silently skipped. Requires the victim to have a spare position slot and a listed `no_seize` asset in its spoke — both common for liqvid-type collateral listings.

### Recommendation
In the seizure planner, treat `no_seize` legs the same way zero-rounded legs are treated: drop them from the planned collateral set instead of aborting the whole plan (i.e., check the flag per-leg during leg construction in `positions/liquidation/plan.rs`, skipping flagged legs rather than reverting), or gate `supply` to existing positions / require owner-or-delegate consent for new collateral slots on an account, mirroring `require_owner_or_delegate` used by `withdraw` (`positions/supply.rs:150`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Spoke lists assets USDC (normal collateral) and LIQ (`no_seize` flag set), plus debt asset ETH.
2. Alice supplies USDC and borrows ETH; price moves so Alice's `health_factor < 1` and `is_liquidatable(account_id)` returns true.
3. Attacker Bob calls `supply(caller=Bob, account_id=Alice, payments=[(LIQ, small_amount)])`. Alice's `supply_positions` now contains a nonzero LIQ leg.
4. Liquidator calls `get_liquidation_estimate`/`liquidate(caller=LIQUIDATOR, account_id=Alice, payments=[(ETH, amount)], seize_mode=Transfer)` → the planner iterates Alice's supply legs, hits the LIQ leg's `no_seize` flag, and the call reverts with `SpokeAssetSeizureHalted` (#318).
5. Every subsequent liquidation attempt reverts identically until the LIQ leg is removed; Bob re-deposits after any removal, sustaining the block.

Caveat: I could not execute code to confirm the exact ordering between the dust-drop filter and the `no_seize` check in `plan.rs`; if the flag check is applied only to legs surviving planning, a sufficiently tiny injection may be dropped before the check — the attack then needs a slightly larger (still cheap) deposit whose planned seizure is nonzero.
