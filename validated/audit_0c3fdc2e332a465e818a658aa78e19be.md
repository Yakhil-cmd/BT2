### Title
Suppliers can frontrun the timelocked `ForceSocializeBadDebt` execution and withdraw at par, shifting the entire write-down onto passive suppliers - (File: contracts/governance/src/op.rs)

### Summary
`AdminOperation::ForceSocializeBadDebt` is a timelocked governance operation that eventually triggers a supply-index write-down on the debt market, socializing the loss across that market's suppliers. Because the operation sits publicly visible in the timelock for its full delay, and because the pool deliberately does not gate withdrawals on market backing, informed suppliers can call `withdraw` at the pre-write-down index before execution lands, draining cash at par. The loss is then concentrated on suppliers who did not exit — a sophisticated-supplier advantage mirroring the reported "claim before removal" frontrun class.

### Finding Description
- `ForceSocializeBadDebt(account_id)` is resolved in `contracts/governance/src/op.rs` into a scheduled controller operation, so it is publicly queued for its delay tier before execution, and `propose` restricts it to the owner via `contracts/governance/src/timelock/lifecycle.rs` [1](#0-0) .
- On execution, `socialize_bad_debt` runs `execute_bad_debt_cleanup`, which seizes positions and writes the bad debt into the market's supply index via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, reducing every remaining supplier's claim per the documented two-floor formula [2](#0-1) .
- Pool exits are intentionally ungated by backing: `require_backed_market` fires only on `supply`, so `withdraw` on an under-backed (or about-to-be-written-down) market is allowed; the only exit checks are `require_reserves` (cash on hand) and `require_utilization_below_max` [3](#0-2) .
- During the timelock window the market's books still show the pre-write-down supply index, so a supplier's full claim can be withdrawn against existing cash. After execution the index write-down lands on whoever did not exit, and reduced cash plus a lower index leaves late withdrawers underpaid or blocked by `require_reserves` [4](#0-3) .
- The same race exists at mempool/ordering level for the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path, but the timelocked governance path gives a deterministic, multi-ledger window — no latency race needed.

### Impact Explanation
Remaining suppliers bear more than their pro-rata share of the socialized loss: early exiters receive full principal while cash lasts, and the write-down plus depleted cash magnifies the haircut on passive suppliers, in extreme cases approaching total loss when a large supplier drains cash first. This is a direct transfer of user funds from passive suppliers to informed ones, triggered by a publicly observable pending governance action.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an insolvent account that only the owner-gated `ForceSocializeBadDebt` gate can clear (collateral above the ~$5 dust cap, so permissionless `clean_bad_debt` is ineligible), plus suppliers actively monitoring the governance queue. Bad-debt events of this size are uncommon but not rare in lending markets, and monitoring a timelock queue is trivial. The exit path requires only an ordinary `withdraw` call by an unprivileged supplier — fully within reachable surface. The "backing shortfall blocks entry, not exit" policy is documented as intentional, which weakens novelty, but the documented rationale does not address advance-notice exit races against a queued write-down; I could not fully verify whether maintainers consider this specific frontrun accepted, so Medium confidence on acceptance.

### Recommendation
Apply a withdrawal restriction or flag on a market once a socialization operation targeting it is queued, or execute the write-down in the same transaction that announces it (e.g., an owner-immediate cleanup entrypoint analogous to `revoke_role_immediate`). Alternatively, snapshot a per-market "socialization pending" timestamp at propose time and value withdrawals at the post-write-down index computed at queue time, so all suppliers share the loss pro-rata regardless of exit timing.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 100_000 USDC to hub 0; Bob supplies 100_000 USDC. Charlie's account accrues bad debt in the USDC market exceeding the dust cap (e.g., collateral $10_000 vs debt $50_000 after a price move).
2. Owner proposes `AdminOperation::ForceSocializeBadDebt(charlie_account_id)` via `governance.propose`; the operation is publicly enumerable through `get_operation`/`get_operation_ledger` during its Sensitive-tier delay.
3. Alice observes the pending op and calls `controller.withdraw` for her full USDC supply. Pre-write-down, `supply_index` is unchanged and `require_backed_market` does not gate exits, so she receives ~100_000 USDC cash at par.
4. The timelock delay elapses; `execute` runs cleanup: Charlie's debt is burned and `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` lowers the USDC `supply_index` proportionally to `min(bad_debt, total_supply) / total_supply`.
5. Bob's claim is cut by the write-down on the shrunken supplier base, and pool cash was reduced by Alice's par exit, so Bob's realizable recovery is strictly worse than the pro-rata loss he would have borne had Alice not exited — Alice profited at Bob's expense purely from advance notice.

```rust
pub(crate) fn require_backed_market(env: &Env, cache: &Cache) {
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        backing_shortfall(cache) == 0,
        CollateralError::PoolInsolvent
    );
}

/// Asset units by which supplier claims exceed cash + debt (0 if solvent).
pub(crate) fn backing_shortfall(cache: &Cache) -> i128 {
    let supplied_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let outstanding_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil(cache.borrowed());
    let backing = cache.cash().saturating_add(outstanding_debt);
    supplied_claim.saturating_sub(backing).max(0)
}
```
