### Title
Repeated dust donations to an insolvent account's collateral leg indefinitely block permissionless bad-debt cleanup - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs))

### Summary
`clean_bad_debt` is permissionless only while the insolvent account's remaining collateral is at or below `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` (5 USD); above it the call reverts with `CannotCleanBadDebt` (114). `supply` lets any third party top up an existing supply leg of an account it does not own. An attacker can therefore keep re-donating just over the dust threshold into a bad-debt account's collateral leg after every partial liquidation or cleanup attempt, holding collateral above 5 USD forever and preventing `clean_bad_debt` from ever succeeding. This mirrors the anti-sniping extension class: a cheap, repeatable, permissionless action that indefinitely postpones a terminal protocol operation, with only privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt` as the fallback.

### Finding Description
The permissionless cleanup gate is collateral-denominated:

- `clean_bad_debt` rejects with `CannotCleanBadDebt` (114) whenever the account's collateral value exceeds `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`; only the governance-owned `force_socialize_bad_debt` path omits the dust cap (`docs/reference/errors.md` row 114; `scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt` line 72).
- `controller::supply` accepts third-party top-ups into asset slots the account already holds: "Anyone may top up an account they do not own, but only for hub assets it already holds a supply position in" (`scripts/permissionless_entrypoints.txt` line 69), confirmed by the harness helper `t.supply_to(CAROL, account, "USDC", 0.0000001)` used in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/third_party_supply_and_risk_restamp.rs` line 26.
- Supply mints shares to the target account, raising its collateral NAV measured by `calculate_account_risk_totals`, which is what the dust check reads.

Attack loop reachable by a single unprivileged address:

1. Wait for (or create) an insolvent account whose collateral has been liquidated down to ≤ 5 USD, making it eligible for permissionless `clean_bad_debt`.
2. Before/in front of any `clean_bad_debt` call, `supply` ~5–6 USD worth into an existing collateral leg of that account. The dust check now fails and cleanup reverts.
3. A liquidator (or natural price/interest drift) eventually seizes or erodes the donated collateral below the threshold again; repeat step 2. Each iteration costs only the dust amount plus fees, and can be repeated an arbitrary number of times, exactly like the 15-minute bid-extension loop.

### Impact Explanation
While the loop is maintained, the market's bad debt is never socialized: the supply index is not written down, `borrowed` stays on the books, and `PoolInsolvent`-adjacent accounting persists. Supplier withdrawals in the affected market remain blocked by the actual cash shortfall anyway, so the concrete effect is temporary freezing of funds and a forced dependency on the privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt` governance path (delay + execute) every time the attacker is active — a griefing delay bounded only by the attacker's willingness to burn ~5 USD per cycle plus the liquidation churn it induces.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-medium. The attack is permissionless and cheap per iteration, but it is a pure grief: the donated collateral belongs to the insolvent account and is capturable by liquidators, so the attacker cannot profit directly and must continuously spend to sustain the delay. Unlike the original report, there is no compounding "late deposit" payoff primitive — the donation earns no shares for the attacker. The realistic motive is delaying loss recognition on a market where the attacker is a borrower or where a competitor's liquidation strategy depends on cleanup.

### Recommendation
Gate the cleanup eligibility on net collateral attributable to the account's own deposits, or exempt dust amounts credited via third-party supply from the `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` check (e.g., track per-leg third-party donations, or evaluate the threshold against collateral excluding shares minted by non-owner/non-delegate suppliers in the account's lifetime). Simpler alternative: cap the threshold check to collateral present before the most recent block/ledger of cleanup attempts, or allow `clean_bad_debt` to first seize remaining collateral into revenue and then socialize the residual regardless of the dust reading.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A (attacker-created or victim) is insolvent: debt D > collateral C, with C liquidated down to ~4 USD. `clean_bad_debt(caller, A, ...)` would now succeed.
2. Attacker calls `supply(caller=attacker, account_id=A, spoke_id, assets=[(hub_asset, 6 USD)])` on an existing collateral leg of A — permitted per the third-party supply rule for existing slots.
3. A keeper's `clean_bad_debt` transaction reverts with `CannotCleanBadDebt` (114) because C ≈ 10 USD > `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`.
4. Any liquidator repays part of D and seizes the donated leg (SeizeMode::Transfer), driving C back under 5 USD; attacker repeats step 2.
5. The only resolution is the privileged `force_socialize_bad_debt` path, which the attacker can front-run perpetually, keeping the market's bad debt unsocialized for as long as they fund the loop. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4)
