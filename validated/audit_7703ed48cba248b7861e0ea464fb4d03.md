### Title
RAY value overflow in accrual permanently freezes a market before the borrow-index cap engages - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but every accrual first computes `borrowed * new_borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` as `i128` RAY products. On a large, heavily-utilized market these products exceed `i128::MAX` and panic with `MathOverflow` while the index is still below its cap. Because every state-changing entrypoint accrues first, the panic permanently freezes the market: no repay, withdraw, borrow, or liquidation can execute, locking all supplier and collateral funds in that market.

### Finding Description
`scaled_to_original` delegates to `Ray::mul`, which panics on overflow (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`). Inside the per-chunk accrual loop this product is computed unconditionally:

- `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies `borrowed` by both indexes (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`)
- `update_supply_index` multiplies `supplied` by `old_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:34`)
- `supply_index_reward_shortfall` multiplies `supplied` by both indexes (`common/src/rates/index.rs:60-62`)
- `Cache::calculate_utilization` unscales both books each op (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:23-24`)

`update_borrow_index` caps the index only after `old_index.mul(env, interest_factor)` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-18`), so the cap protects the index value, not the `shares × index` product. The repo's own harness test drives this cliff: a ~10^27-base-unit 18-decimal market at ~98% utilization on a steep curve panics inside `scaled_to_original` after a few years of accrual, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and `withdraw`, `repay`, and `update_indexes` all revert with `MathOverflow` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-356`).

The triggering inputs are all reachable by an unprivileged address: `supply` and `borrow` build the book, and `update_indexes` is permissionless accrual. No privileged call is needed once the market's configured caps admit a book large enough that `scaled_shares × index > i128::MAX`; for an 18-decimal asset that bound is ~1.7e11 whole tokens scaled by index, well under `max_cap_for_decimals(18)`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and an effectively bricked market. Every pool verb (`supply`, `withdraw`, `borrow`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash` paths against the book) runs accrual first, so all of them panic on the same product. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot close bad positions, and accrued protocol revenue is unreachable. There is no recovery path: the panic is inside the accrual itself, so `recapitalize` or governance restamping cannot unwind it without a contract upgrade.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. It needs a whale-scale book (hundreds of billions of base units on a high-decimals asset) sustained at high utilization for the compounding index to push `shares × index` past `i128::MAX` — the harness test reaches it within the asserted horizon only on a stress curve with caps raised to the domain maximum. Markets listed with restrictive supply caps or low-decimal assets never reach the corner. However, nothing in the listing validation prevents a cap large enough to admit the cliff (`calculate_scaled_cap` even saturates rather than rejecting), so any high-cap, high-decimal market is exposed over long horizons, and the state is irreversible once reached.

### Recommendation
Bound the value product, not just the index. Options: compute accrual with the saturating/`try_mul` variants and clamp debt-growth or index updates at the largest representable RAY value (analogous to `calculate_scaled_cap` and `protocol_fee_shares`, which already saturate); or enforce `scaled_shares × MAX_*_INDEX_RAY ≤ i128::MAX` as an additional effective supply/borrow cap at listing and at each deposit, so the market can never enter the domain where accrual overflows. Either preserves liveness: accrual clamps instead of panicking, keeping repay/withdraw/liquidate executable.

### Proof of Concept
The in-repo test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) is an executable PoC:

```rust
t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", BILLION * 10i128.pow(18));   // unprivileged supply
t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);                      // ~98% utilization
// advance years; permissionless update_indexes eventually reverts:
assert_contract_error(failure, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert!(book.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY);       // cap never engaged
assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
```

The market is permanently frozen: suppliers' ~10^27 base units and all accrued interest are unrecoverable through any unprivileged entrypoint.
