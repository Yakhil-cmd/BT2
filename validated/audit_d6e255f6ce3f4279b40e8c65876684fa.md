### Title
Overpayment refund is paid from declared, not received, amounts — unfunded repay/recapitalize drains pool custody while the cash book is untouched - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The pool's `repay` and `recapitalize` legs implement a "push" refund of the excess over what is actually owed/needed, but the refunded amount is derived from the *declared* `action.amount` / `amount` argument rather than from a measured inbound balance delta, and the refund is never debited from `cash` nor gated by `require_reserves`. As in the Tapioca report (a refund mechanism that misbehaves around the surrounding call), the refund path here is decoupled from the accounting: a caller can obtain a full refund for a payment that never happened, leaving the pool's real token custody reduced while its books still report the funds as present.

### Finding Description
In `ops::repay::accounting`, `cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` splits the declared `amount` into `net_repay` (credited to `cash`) and `overpayment`; `apply` then executes `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` unconditionally — a push refund of custody with no check that `amount` was ever received [1](#0-0) . When a market carries no debt, `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is zero, the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct, and the *entire declared amount* is treated as overpayment and refunded out of real custody [2](#0-1) . The same pattern exists in `recapitalize`: `refund = amount - min(amount, backing_shortfall)` and `cache.transfer_out(&payer, outcome.refund)` [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . The in-repo tests document the result: `token.balance(payer) == declared` while `token.balance(pool) == 0` and `after.cash == before.cash` — "the pool's entire custody has left the contract" with "the cash book untouched" [5](#0-4) .

The pool assumes the controller has already transferred `amount` in before the call ("The controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call") [6](#0-5) , but nothing in these legs *measures* the inbound transfer — unlike the controller side, which uses measured balance deltas for its own refund of `refund_controller_balance_delta` [7](#0-6) . Any entry path that reaches `pool.repay` / `pool.recapitalize` with a declared amount exceeding what was actually transferred converts the difference into a free withdrawal of suppliers' custody.

### Impact Explanation
Each unfunded call transfers real underlying tokens to the payer without debiting `cash` or `borrowed`. Custody falls below the book: `Cache::require_reserves` reads the book, not the balance, so the market "reports itself solvent and cannot pay" — later withdrawals/borrows/seizure payouts fail inside the SAC transfer, i.e. permanent freezing of supplier funds and direct theft of pool custody up to the pool's balance [8](#0-7) . This maps to the accepted impacts: theft of user funds and a contract unable to operate from lack of token funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering requires only a call whose declared `amount` exceeds the tokens actually delivered: a `repay` against a market with no debt makes the entire amount an overpayment [9](#0-8) , and `recapitalize` with `amount > backing_shortfall` refunds the difference. Whether an unprivileged address can invoke these pool ops directly (rather than only through the controller) is the open question — the pool test harness calls `client().repay(&payer, ...)` directly, and I could not confirm within the available iterations whether `pool/src/lib.rs` restricts these ops to the controller. If the controller is the only caller and always performs a measured inbound transfer first, the exploitability narrows to paths where the declared amount can exceed the delivered amount (e.g., fee-on-transfer debt tokens, where `transfer_amount_measured` shows under-delivery is a handled case [10](#0-9) ). Severity is High if directly callable; otherwise it is a latent under-delivery refund flaw.

### Recommendation
Measure the refund against custody, not the argument:
- Snapshot the pool's token balance before crediting and refund only `min(declared_overpayment, measured_inbound_excess)`, or have the controller pass the *measured received* amount into the pool action.
- Debit the refund from `cash` (or route it through `require_reserves`) so the book cannot overstate custody.
- For `recapitalize`, reject `amount > backing_shortfall` instead of refunding, or pull the exact `applied` amount.

### Proof of Concept
Already encoded in the repo's own test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody`: with zero borrowed debt and `custody_before` tokens in the pool, calling `repay(payer, [(hub_asset, custody_before)])` with no inbound transfer credits `0`, returns `custody_before` to `payer`, leaves `token.balance(pool) == 0`, and leaves `cash` unchanged [9](#0-8) . Steps: (1) pick a market with no debt (repay) or a surplus above `backing_shortfall` (recapitalize); (2) call the op declaring `amount` equal to the pool's balance without funding it; (3) the push refund transfers the balance to `payer` while `cash` still reports the funds.
