### Title
Suppliers can exit atomically around `clean_bad_debt` to dodge the supply-index write-down and shift the entire bad-debt loss onto remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
In the source report, a vesting recipient front-runs `revokeAll()` with `claim()` to rescue unclaimed tokens before the owner can seize them. The XOXNO analog is a supplier front-running — or simply ordering within a single transaction — the permissionless bad-debt cleanup path: `withdraw` before the `supply_index` write-down, then optionally re-`supply` after it. Because bad-debt socialization works by lowering `supply_index` pro-rata over whoever still holds supply shares (`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`), any supplier that exits first keeps 100% of their balance and concentrates the whole loss on the suppliers who remain. The repo's own test `tests/test-harness/tests/composition/supplier_exit_before_socialization_is_bounded_by_utilization.rs` (GH-15) pins this: `exit_then_clean_then_re_enter_dodges_the_write_down_when_utilization_allows` executes `withdraw → CleanBadDebt → supply` in one script and asserts "the runner kept its whole stake" while "BOB absorbed the entire write-down". No privileged role, mempool observation, or leaked intent is required — `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless (INV-LIQ-04), so the attacker bundles the whole sequence themselves.

### Finding Description
- Bad-debt write-off path: `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs` `apply` on `AccountPositionType::Borrow` calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` then `burn_debt`. The write-down formula in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` reduces `supply_index` by `1 − min(bad_debt, supplied_value)/supplied_value`, so every still-held scaled supply share loses the same fraction. A supplier who burns shares before this call escapes the haircut entirely.
- Exit gate asymmetry: `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs` shows `require_backed_market` gates only `supply` — "you cannot supply into an under-backed market but you can withdraw from one" (`contracts/pool/README.md`, Guards/Notes). Non-liquidation `withdraw` in `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs` (`gate_and_debit`) enforces only `require_reserves`, `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt`. So while cash remains and post-exit utilization stays under `max_utilization`, a supplier can fully withdraw at the pre-write-down index even though the market is carrying known-insolvent debt.
- Atomic composition: `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless (per INV-LIQ-04 in `docs/reference/invariants.md`, requiring only ceil debt > half-up collateral and collateral ≤ the $5 dust threshold, plus no active flash guard). Nothing prevents a caller's own transaction from ordering `withdraw` before `clean_bad_debt` — confirmed by the GH-15 test where the script runner does exactly that and re-enters afterward.
- Scale: the exit is bounded only by `cash` and the utilization ceiling (`bad_debt_netting_and_exit_timing.rs::withdrawal_ceiling_tracks_one_minus_utilization_over_max` pins `f ≤ 1 − u/max_utilization` of total supply). At low utilization — the common case when bad debt is dust relative to a large pool — a whale holding nearly all supply can exit ~fully, leaving the residual suppliers to absorb a write-down amplified proportionally (in the limit, to the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` = RAY/1000 clamp).

### Impact Explanation
Theft/unfair transfer of value between users: the protocol's intended loss allocation is pro-rata across suppliers, but the exiting supplier realizes the pre-impairment value of shares whose backing is already impaired. Whatever fraction of supply exits converts directly into a larger write-down for non-exiting suppliers (formula in `interest.rs` divides by post-…-pre state `supplied_value`, which the early exit shrinks). On a mostly-idle market with dust bad debt this is close to a full dodge for a dominant supplier, i.e., a Medium-severity loss-shifting attack under the "theft of user funds / unfair pro-rata loss" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
- No timing race needed: unlike the Ethereum original, the attacker does not need to observe a privileged transaction — `clean_bad_debt` is callable by anyone once the account qualifies, so the attacker deterministically bundles `withdraw` → `clean_bad_debt` → `supply` in one transaction (the GH-15 test does this via `script_runner`).
- Conditions: pending dust-insolvent debt, cash covering the exit, and utilization below `max_utilization` after exit. High-utilization markets block it (`the_exit_is_capped_by_max_utilization_and_the_script_reverts_whole` reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax`), which is a partial mitigation, not a fix.
- Cost: one transaction plus temporarily forgoing yield; a flash loan can fund the supply/withdraw leg if the attacker lacks capital.

### Recommendation
Make the socialization indifferent to exit timing, e.g.:
- Compute the write-down against a snapshot/mark of supply taken before exits are processed in the same window, or apply the bad-debt reduction to each supplier's share value at withdraw time (track a per-market "impaired index" the moment cleanup becomes permissionless-eligible).
- Simpler: when `clean_bad_debt` conditions are met, allow the cleanup call itself to first settle pending accrual and treat the exiting fraction's escaped loss as additional bad debt (iterative re-write-down is impossible post-hoc, so prefer:) block or haircut withdrawals while a qualifying insolvent account exists — e.g., have `withdraw` re-check eligibility for bad-debt cleanup and either revert or internally trigger the write-down before honoring the exit.
- At minimum, document that `require_utilization_below_max` is the only bound and that suppliers at low utilization can dodge socialization.

### Proof of Concept
Sequence (all unprivileged, single transaction or adjacent ledgers):
1. Market (hub, ETH): attacker supplies a dominant share, BOB supplies the rest; ALICE holds tiny USDC collateral with ETH debt. USDC price crashes → ALICE's account is dust-insolvent (debt > collateral, collateral ≤ $5).
2. Attacker calls `controller.withdraw` on their ETH supply — `gate_and_debit` passes since `require_backed_market` does not apply to exits and utilization stays under the cap.
3. Attacker calls `controller.clean_bad_debt(ALICE)` → pool `seize` writes the debt off via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; BOB alone absorbs the index drop.
4. Attacker calls `supply` again; their re-minted shares are valued at the written-down index but their principal is intact.

Executable evidence already in-repo: `tests/test-harness/tests/composition/supplier_exit_before_socialization_is_bounded_by_utilization.rs` — `exit_then_clean_then_re_enter_dodges_the_write_down_when_utilization_allows` (lines 52–79) asserts the runner retains `≥ 50 * U − 1` while BOB's balance strictly decreases; `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs::test_bad_debt_reduction_matches_formula` (lines 247–277) confirms the loss accrues exactly as balance × index write-down to whoever remains.

Uncertain/unverified within the iteration limit: the exact controller `withdraw`/`clean_bad_debt` function signatures in `contracts/controller/src/lib.rs` were located by grep but not line-cited; the mechanics are fully pinned by the pool-side code and the GH-15 test above.
