This Arbitrum report describes an off-chain validator client optimization issue (redundant state-machine checks/computation in a Go `edge_tracker.go` loop), not an on-chain security defect. It falls squarely into the categories explicitly excluded by the scan method — gas-only optimization and unbounded-loop/computation-waste findings — and does not involve attacker-controlled input, unauthorized dispatch, theft, unbacked issuance, or irreversible freezing.

The closest structural analogs in `polkadot-sdk` are on-chain, checked-invariant state machines such as `DisputeStatus` in [1](#0-0)  and the vote-import/threshold logic in [2](#0-1) , but these are on-chain runtime transitions gated by weighed, metered extrinsics rather than an off-chain client repeatedly re-evaluating a bisection state, so the "unnecessary computation/looping" root cause described in the report has no matching reachable defect here — there's no attacker-controlled path that forces repeated wasted on-chain computation of this shape, and even if there were, it would be a resource/gas-optimization concern explicitly out of scope rather than a fund-theft/integrity-break vulnerability.
