### Title
Attacker-defined "trap" assets can be deposited into generic-purpose AAs and can never be withdrawn because AAs never check `is_transferrable`/`transfer_condition`/`spender_attested` before crediting a balance - (File: `aa_composer.js`)

### Summary
The Putty finding is a class of bug where an asset can be accepted as payment collateral by a contract even though its type/definition makes any later attempted transfer of that same asset impossible for anyone but the original issuer, permanently freezing the value inside the accepting contract. The Obyte analog is: an attacker defines a "trap" asset whose transferability rules only ever succeed when the sender **is** the asset's definer, deposits it into a generic swap/bank/exchange AA (a normal, common oscript pattern), and the AA — which never inspects `is_transferrable`, `transfer_condition` or `spender_attested` on deposit — credits it as valid balance. Every subsequent attempt by the AA to pay this asset back out (to the depositor or anyone else) fails full unit validation and bounces, permanently freezing that portion of AA state/funds.

### Finding Description
An asset issuer (an ordinary, unprivileged unit poster) fully controls the spending rules of an asset they define, via `is_transferrable`, `transfer_condition`, `spender_attested`, and `cosigned_by_definer`, validated in `validateAssetDefinition` [1](#0-0) . In particular, when `is_transferrable` is `false`, `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` allows a payment **only** if the sole input address is the asset's own `definer_address` (or the output is solely the definer, or a definer+self-change pattern): [2](#0-1) 

This means the definer (attacker) can always send their own non-transferable asset to *any* arbitrary address as a first transfer (since `arrInputAddresses[0] === objAsset.definer_address` is satisfied), but that recipient — unless it is also the definer, or sends back only to the definer / itself — can never move the asset onward: any later payment message carrying this asset will fail with `"the asset is not transferrable"`.

When an AA receives a trigger payment, `validateAATriggerObject` only checks that referenced assets exist and that amounts are positive integers — it never inspects `is_transferrable`, `transfer_condition`, or `spender_attested`: [3](#0-2) 

The trigger's incoming coins (of any asset, including such a trap asset) are then unconditionally credited to the AA's internal balance: [4](#0-3) 

Many common AA patterns (banks/exchanges/bounty pools) explicitly accept arbitrary assets as deposits and record ownership in state vars for later withdrawal, e.g. the bank sample that stores `var[$asset_key]` on deposit and pays `trigger.data.asset` back on withdraw: [5](#0-4) 

When the AA later composes a response that pays the same asset back out (e.g. a withdrawal, a swap settlement, or a refund), `sendUnit`/`completePaymentPayload` only pre-checks `fixed_denominations` and `is_private` before building the payment — it never checks `is_transferrable`, `transfer_condition`, or `spender_attested` of the outgoing asset before composing: [6](#0-5) 

The composed response unit is only checked for validity afterward, via a full re-validation pass in `validateAndSaveUnit`; if that fails (as it will for a non-transferrable trap asset being sent from a non-definer AA to a non-definer address), the whole response is discarded and the AA bounces: [7](#0-6) 

Because the AA is not the asset's definer, and the destination (the depositor or any third party) is neither the definer nor "the same input address" in the required pattern, the withdrawal payment permanently fails `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs`'s transferability check [2](#0-1)  (or, if the attacker instead sets an unsatisfiable `transfer_condition`, the `evaluateAssetCondition` check at [8](#0-7) ) every single time it is attempted. The AA's recorded balance/state for that asset is permanently stuck: no valid payment can ever move it out of the AA to anyone but the original attacker-definer.

### Impact Explanation
This directly causes **AA fund freezing**: any AA implementing a generic deposit/withdraw, swap, or escrow pattern (a very common and encouraged oscript idiom, as shown in the bundled sample contracts) can have its internal accounting/state polluted with an asset balance that can never actually be paid out. Depositors who send such a trap asset (believing it to behave like any transferable token) will have their share of the AA's ledger permanently unspendable, and any AA logic gating other operations on successfully completing that payment (refunds, settlement, unlocking of paired base-asset funds in the same response) will also permanently fail/bounce, since the whole response unit is validated and saved atomically. Repeated user attempts to trigger the stuck withdrawal only burn bounce fees. This matches the accepted "AA fund loss or freezing" impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: any unprivileged user can define an asset (`asset` message) with `is_transferrable: false` and no special exception, then, as the definer, freely send it into any target AA's trigger the same as any normal asset (this initial "definer-sends" transfer always validates). The griefing requires no special privilege or race condition — only that the target AA's design accepts and records balances of arbitrary attacker-nominated assets without restricting to a known whitelist (a design shown to be encouraged/common in the shipped oscript samples).

### Recommendation
- AAs that accept deposits of arbitrary assets should explicitly check `asset[...].is_transferrable`, presence of `transfer_condition`, and `spender_attested`/`cosigned_by_definer` via the formula language (`asset[$asset].*` getters) before crediting a balance that the AA later intends to pay back out, and reject/bounce non-transferable assets on deposit rather than after a failed withdrawal.
- Consider providing an oscript-level helper/warning (or a documented best practice) making it clear that "definer can always send once" is a special-case bypass of `is_transferrable`, so that generic financial AAs are aware they can be griefed by definer-restricted assets unless explicitly filtering by asset properties on deposit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker defines Asset X with `is_transferrable: false`, `issued_by_definer_only: true`, no `transfer_condition` needed — validated per `validateAssetDefinition` [1](#0-0) .
2. Attacker, as sole input and also the `definer_address` of Asset X, sends a trigger payment of Asset X to a target "bank"/exchange AA (e.g., the sample in `test/samples/a_bank_without_percent.oscript`). This is valid per the `is_transferrable` special case (`arrInputAddresses[0] === objAsset.definer_address`) [2](#0-1) .
3. The AA credits its internal balance for Asset X, as it never validates `is_transferrable` on deposit (`aa_composer.js` trigger validation and balance update) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) .
4. Attacker calls the AA's `withdraw` action requesting Asset X back. The AA composes a payment message for Asset X to the attacker's (non-definer, since payer used a fresh address, or any third-party) address; `completePaymentPayload`/`sendUnit` do not pre-check transferability [6](#0-5) .
5. `validateAndSaveUnit` runs full validation on the composed response; `validatePaymentInputsAndOutputs` rejects it with `"the asset is not transferrable"` because the AA is not the definer [2](#0-1) , causing the response to bounce [7](#0-6) .
6. Every subsequent withdrawal attempt for Asset X from this AA bounces identically — the AA's recorded balance/state entry for Asset X is permanently frozen.

Note: I was unable to fully trace the exact rollback semantics of `bounce()`/`SAVEPOINT initial_balances` within the available context (the `bounce` function body itself was not returned by search), so the precise interaction between balance rollback and state-var persistence across a bounced response is not 100% confirmed from the retrieved snippets; a Devin session with full file access should verify this before finalizing severity.
