### Title
Supply-index floor clamp leaves phantom claims that drain fresh deposits after bad-debt socialization - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
Analog to the Yearn yETH exploit: a corrupted accounting state (claims exceeding backing) is left live in the system instead of being zeroed or rejected, and a later ordinary deposit/withdrawal round trip lets the residual unbacked claim extract real tokens. In Yearn the broken Σ/Π invariant plus a dust deposit minted infinite LP tokens; here `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt` writes the supply index down but clamps it at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving wiped suppliers a phantom claim that can withdraw a later depositor's cash.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89` socializes bad debt by scaling `supply_index` down, but clamps the result to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of letting it reach zero:

```rust
let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);
let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
let new_supply_index = cache.supply_index().mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
``` [1](#0-0) 

When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is 0 and the mathematically correct index is 0 — but the clamp leaves every supply share with a claim worth `shares × RAY/1000`, backed by nothing. This is exactly the "impossible state that the code doesn't check" shape: shares outstanding, positive index, zero backing. `docs/reference/formulas.md:391-401` documents that "the non-zero floor can leave residual claims without backing" and asserts that "Supply then fails the backing gate" — but the backing gate only blocks *new* `supply`; it does not block `withdraw` of the phantom claim. `withdraw` only calls `cache.require_reserves(gross)`, which checks `cash`, not backing [2](#0-1) .

The in-repo test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` proves the full kill chain end to end: after a wipeout writes the index to the floor, a fresh deposit (`mint_supply` + `credit_cash`) is drained in full by the stranded position via `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled)` + `debit_cash`, leaving the honest supplier's claim unbacked [3](#0-2) .

Attack path for a single unprivileged address:
1. Supply to the market that will absorb the bad debt (attacker holds supply shares `s`).
2. Create/locate a dust-collateral insolvent account whose debt is in that market (permissionless `clean_bad_debt` requires `total_debt > total_collateral` and `total_collateral ≤ $5` — cheap to manufacture by borrowing against a sub-3-decimal whole-unit collateral leg or letting a tiny position accrue past its collateral; alternatively any naturally occurring dust bad debt works).
3. Call `clean_bad_debt` (controller entrypoint, permissionless for dust accounts) → pool `seize` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000`. Attacker's shares now hold a phantom floor claim.
4. Wait for — or sandwich — a victim `supply` into that market (the backing gate blocks supply only while `backing_shortfall > 0`; note `recapitalize` lets anyone, including the attacker at cost bounded by the shortfall, clear the gate, and any cash donation to the pool raises `cash` toward clearing it).
5. Call `withdraw` (amount `0`/`i128::MAX` sentinel = withdraw-all) on the attacker's supply position → `require_reserves` sees the victim's cash → phantom claim is paid in real tokens.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: the wiped supplier's residual claim — which economically should be zero since the bad debt met or exceeded the entire supplied value — is paid out of a subsequent depositor's principal. The test demonstrates the drained amount equals the fresh deposit exactly, leaving the victim's claim permanently unbacked (`cash < claim` thereafter).

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requires a market with bad debt ≥ total supplied value (a full wipeout, not a partial write-down) — most plausible in thin/young markets or via a manipulated price crash on a dust account — and fresh cash entering the market afterwards. The permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path (collateral ≤ $5) makes triggering the write-down cheap; the attacker must hold pre-wipeout supply shares in that market. `force_socialize_bad_debt` is owner-gated and out of scope, but `clean_bad_debt` suffices.

### Recommendation
When `capped == total_supplied_value` (full wipeout), zero out `supplied` and `revenue` shares (or record the market as dead) rather than clamping the index to `RAY/1000`; a non-zero index on wiped shares is what mints the phantom claim. Alternatively/additionally, make `withdraw`/`require_reserves` enforce the backing invariant (`payout ≤ cash` is insufficient; require the post-withdrawal `supplied × supply_index` to remain covered), or burn the unbacked residual: pay `min(floor_claim, cash − outstanding_debt − remaining_backed_claims)`.

### Proof of Concept
`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-370` (`test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard`) and `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-428` (`test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`) already execute the exploit against the real cache functions: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` → clamp to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` → stranded claim `> 0` with `cash == 0` → fresh `mint_supply`/`credit_cash` → `resolve_withdrawal` + `require_reserves` + `debit_cash` drains the fresh deposit exactly. The controller-reachable version is: `supply` → (price crash on dust account) → `clean_bad_debt` → victim `supply` → attacker `withdraw`.

fn test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let scaled_a_raw = 1_000_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: scaled_a_raw,
            borrowed: 0,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let scaled_a = Ray::from(scaled_a_raw);

        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(2_000_000 * RAY));
        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "wipeout must clamp supply index UP to the floor, not reset the base"
        );

        let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a);
        assert!(stranded > 0, "floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim");
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "empty market: no cash to extract yet");

        let c = stranded;
        let scaled_b = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(c);
        cache.mint_supply(scaled_b);
        cache.credit_cash(c);

        let b_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b);
        assert_eq!(b_claim, c, "userB's honest claim equals their deposit");

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "stranded position pays out non-zero");
        assert_eq!(
            gross, c,
            "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool"
        );

        assert!(
            cache.cash() < b_claim,
            "pool cash ({}) can no longer cover userB's claim ({}): honest supplier lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            b_claim
        );
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "userA drained the pool to empty");
    });
```
