### No vulnerability found for this question.

The described scenario has two disqualifying issues under the audit rules:

1. **`socialize-debt` is privileged, not caller-triggerable by an ordinary principal A.** It requires `check-caller-auth`, i.e., `contract-caller` must be in the `authorized-contracts` map (only the market contract, not arbitrary principals), so A cannot cause "A's own socialize-debt write-off" to land as part of this attack sequence. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

2. **The remaining mechanism — A redeeming first and consuming `get-available-assets` so B's later redeem in the same block reverts with `ERR-INSUFFICIENT-LIQUIDITY`** — is exactly the ordinary shared-pool liquidity-consumption behavior the audit rules explicitly instruct to reject ("liquidity being consumed" is normal pool economics, not a defect). `get-available-assets` is simply `assets - total-borrowed`, and `redeem` checks `available-assets >= inkind` before paying out; any withdrawal by any depositor reduces the liquidity available to subsequent redeemers in the same or later blocks — this is inherent ERC4626-style vault design, not a bug. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

No shared-state defect specific to a `socialize-debt` interaction exists here that A (an unprivileged principal) can trigger, and the liquidity-race itself is standard pool economics explicitly out of scope.
