### Title
Any unprivileged caller can forcibly restamp worse liquidation terms onto other users' debt positions - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
`update_account_threshold` is permissionless (any authenticated `caller`, any `account_ids`), yet with `has_risks = true` it mutates other accounts' stored liquidation threshold, liquidation bonus and liquidation fees to the current spoke listing — including changes that strictly favor liquidators (lower LT, higher bonus, lower fee). The only protection is a post-update health-factor floor of 1.05, which does nothing to prevent the degradation itself: it merely guarantees the account stays solvent at the instant of the restamp. This is the XOXNO Lending analog of CVE-2024-0439: a configuration-modifying operation is reachable by a caller whose legitimate privilege does not include deciding other users' risk parameters.

### Finding Description
Every supply position stores its own risk tuple (`liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus`, `liquidation_fees`, `loan_to_value`) copied at creation time. `contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:124-144` exposes `update_account_threshold(env, caller, has_risks, account_ids)`, which requires only `require_authorized_caller` — no ownership, no role, no per-account authorization.

With `has_risks = true`, `sync_account_thresholds` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple`, which reaches `apply_gated_liquidation_params` (`params.rs:68-93`). `favors_liquidator` (`params.rs:96-100`) detects exactly the changes that harm the account holder — lower `liquidation_threshold`, higher `liquidation_bonus`, lower `liquidation_fees` — and for accounts with debt the sole gate is `clears_min_hf`: HF computed with the new LT must be ≥ 1.05 (`params.rs:102-119`). If the gate passes, the adverse tuple is written to the victim's position (`params.rs:90-92`, `params.rs:218`).

The result: after governance raises a market's liquidation bonus (or lowers its LT/fees) via `edit_asset_in_spoke`, a stranger can pick any specific borrower whose HF ≥ 1.05 and pin the harsher terms onto their position, while other accounts keep grandfathered terms until they voluntarily supply/withdraw. The victim had no say: the position's stored terms persist precisely so that listing edits do not retroactively harm existing borrowers (the code deliberately stamps terms at creation), yet this entrypoint lets any address override that protection selectively against chosen victims.

### Impact Explanation
- **Earlier liquidation**: a restamped lower `liquidation_threshold` lowers the victim's HF numerator, so the account becomes liquidatable (`HF < 1`) at a higher collateral price than under its original terms. Positions opened under a more conservative listing are pushed into liquidation sooner.
- **Larger seizure**: a restamped higher `liquidation_bonus` directly increases the collateral seized from the victim in any subsequent liquidation — value transferred to the liquidator (who can be the attacker themselves) that the victim would not have lost under its grandfathered tuple.
- A lower `liquidation_fees` restamp marginally reduces protocol revenue; the dominant harm is the first two.
- Because the attacker can bundle any `account_ids` in one call and target only accounts where the gate passes, they can mass-downgrade the risk terms of the borrower base immediately after a governance listing edit, then wait — or liquidate near-threshold accounts once price moves. This constitutes theft of user funds via forced adverse parameter changes the users never authorized.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Requires a governance `edit_asset_in_spoke` that makes listing terms strictly worse for borrowers in one dimension (lower LT, higher bonus, or lower fee). Such edits are a routine part of risk management — the runbook in `docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md` describes exactly this LT 6000 → 5300 change as a normal operation.
- Once the listing is edited, the attack is a single permissionless call (`update_account_threshold(caller, true, [victim_ids])`) — no capital, no flash loan, no oracle manipulation. Any HF ≥ 1.05 account qualifies, which covers essentially all non-liquidatable accounts.
- Severity bounded to Medium: the harm only materializes if the account later becomes liquidatable, the HF ≥ 1.05 gate prevents driving an account to the edge itself, and degraded accounts are still solvent immediately after the restamp.

### Recommendation
Restrict who may restamp liquidation terms on an account they do not control:
- Gate `has_risks = true` refreshes to the account owner or an active delegate (reuse `require_owner_or_delegate`), leaving the `LtvOnly` path permissionless for keeper LTV syncs; or
- Keep it permissionless but make `favors_liquidator` changes opt-in per account (owner signature/flag), since adverse-to-holder tuple changes are a one-way ratchet on the user's position; at minimum, refuse to apply a strictly `favors_liquidator` tuple unless the caller is the account owner.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice opens an account and supplies collateral in market M; her supply position stamps the current listing (`LT = 6000`, `bonus = B`).
2. Governance timelock executes `edit_asset_in_spoke` raising M's `liquidation_bonus` to `B' > B` (or lowering `liquidation_threshold` / `liquidation_fees`).
3. Attacker Bob (any address) calls `controller.update_account_threshold(bob, has_risks = true, [alice_account_id])`. `apply_gated_liquidation_params` sees `favors_liquidator == true`, Alice's HF with new params is ≥ 1.05, so her position is overwritten with the worse tuple (`params.rs:90-92`, persisted at `params.rs:218`).
4. Market price drifts down. Under her original terms Alice's HF would still be above 1; under the restamped lower LT she is liquidatable. Bob liquidates her via `controller.liquidate` and captures the enlarged `B'` bonus — collateral value Alice would not have forfeited had a stranger not been able to restamp her position's risk configuration.
