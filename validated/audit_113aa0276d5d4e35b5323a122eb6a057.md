### Title
Supply-index floor resurrects wiped claims after bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary

A permissionless bad-debt cleanup can reduce a market’s supply index to zero but then incorrectly clamps it to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. This leaves all suppliers with a positive residual claim even when the write-down exceeded the market’s entire supplied value. The first supplier to withdraw can consume the market’s remaining cash while other suppliers retain unpayable claims. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

`Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` is permissionless and requires only caller authentication and the dust-capped insolvency condition. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Cleanup sends each borrow position to `pool_seize_positions_call`; the pool converts the position to `bad_debt`, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debt. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Inside `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, the write-down is capped at `total_supplied_value`, so a sufficiently large debt produces `remaining == 0` and a zero reduction factor. [7](#0-6) 

The resulting supply index should remain zero, but the code replaces it with `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. [8](#0-7) 

This incorrectly preserves roughly 0.1% of every existing scaled supply position even though the socialized debt exhausted the entire supply-side claim. [9](#0-8) 

A subsequent `Controller::withdraw` resolves those preserved shares through the pool withdrawal path, which checks only the requested cash reserve, utilization, and whether debt remains after the burn. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Impact Explanation

This can permanently freeze supplier funds and transfer the market’s remaining cash to whichever supplier withdraws first. [2](#0-1) [11](#0-10) 

After a complete wipeout, all scaled supply shares remain in storage but represent only the floor-clamped claim. If remaining cash is below the aggregate floor claim, the first withdrawal succeeds while later withdrawals fail for lack of cash. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

The economic error is not merely dust: the floor is proportional to total scaled supply, so a sufficiently large market can have a material aggregate residual claim after cleanup should have removed it entirely. [2](#0-1) 

Severity is Medium because the attacker must wait for or help create a qualifying insolvent account and can extract only the remaining cash, but the action is permissionless and directly determines which supplier receives the residual backing. [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The trigger is reachable by any authenticated address through `Controller::clean_bad_debt` once an account has open debt, is insolvent, and its remaining collateral is at or below the dust cap. [3](#0-2) [14](#0-13) 

The overflow condition requires one cleaned debt leg’s unscaled RAY value to be at least the market’s total supplied RAY value. [6](#0-5) [2](#0-1) 

That condition can arise when accrued debt exceeds the original supplied value while the market still retains a small amount of unborrowed cash. [15](#0-14) [16](#0-15) 

The attacker only needs to hold supply shares in the affected market before cleanup and then submit `clean_bad_debt` followed by `withdraw`; no owner, delegate, liquidator credit authorization, governance action, or privileged pool call is required. [17](#0-16) [18](#0-17) 

### Recommendation

Do not clamp the post-write-down supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` when `remaining == 0`. [2](#0-1) 

The correct fix is to make the bad-debt write-down burn or zero the outstanding scaled supply representation when the capped loss exhausts the full supplied value, rather than preserving a fixed floor claim. [9](#0-8) [6](#0-5) 

At minimum, preserve the floor only when `remaining > 0`, and add an end-to-end regression test proving that a complete bad-debt wipeout leaves no withdrawable supply claim. [2](#0-1) [10](#0-9) 

### Proof of Concept

Assume a market with total scaled supply `S`, initial index `RAY`, `cash = 1`, and a bad-debt position whose `unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` exceeds `S * RAY`. The attacker owns at least enough scaled supply that `attacker_scaled * RAY / 1000 >= 1 * RAY`. [1](#0-0) [6](#0-5) 

1. The attacker calls `Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller = attacker, account_id = victim)`, where the victim’s debt exceeds collateral and remaining collateral satisfies the dust gate. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 
2. Cleanup emits a borrow-side `PoolSeizeEntry`; `ops::seize::apply` converts that leg to `bad_debt`, socializes it, and burns it. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 
3. `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `capped = total_supplied_value` and `remaining = 0`, yielding a zero index, but then writes `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead. [2](#0-1) 
4. The attacker calls `Controller::withdraw(caller = attacker, account_id = attacker_account, withdrawals = [(hub_asset, 1)], to = Some(attacker))`. [18](#0-17) 
5. The pool burns the attacker’s floor-preserved shares, accepts `cash >= 1`, debits the last unit of cash, and transfers it to the attacker. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 
6. Other suppliers retain positive scaled positions at the floored index, but the market now has `cash = 0`, so their withdrawals cannot pay out. [8](#0-7) [12](#0-11)

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
