### Title
Accrued-position value overflows `i128` before the borrow-index cap, permanently freezing the market - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
Analogous to Pillow's integer overflow while tracking an accumulating position, XOXNO Lending keeps a running RAY-scaled valuation of each market (`borrowed × borrow_index`, `supplied × supply_index`) inside `accrue_step` / `scaled_to_original`. These products are checked `i128` arithmetic with no saturating or widened fallback on the production path. Because every state-changing verb accrues first, once `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index` exceeds `i128::MAX` the whole market permanently panics — before the `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` ceiling can stop index growth. An unprivileged attacker who supplies and borrows whale-scale positions within the admitted cap domain can drive the market to this cliff deliberately.

### Finding Description
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs::scaled_to_original` does `scaled.mul(env, index)`; `Ray::mul` → `fp_core::mul_div_*` uses an `i128` fast path and only widens to `I256` for the *intermediate product*, so a result exceeding `i128` raises `MathOverflow` (docs/reference/formulas.md:20–24).
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` runs `accrue_step` (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs`) on every mutation; it calls `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` to compute utilization each chunk (tests/fuzz mirror at rates_and_index.rs:294–296).
- The borrow index is capped at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` (10^9 × RAY) *after* multiplication, but the value product `borrowed × index` overflows `i128` (~1.7e38) far earlier whenever scaled debt ≳ 170 RAY-units. `update_borrow_index` has no guard against the value-domain overflow.
- Confirmed by `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321–361`: a market with ~1e9 × 10^18-token supply at 98% utilization panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; subsequently `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert with the same error. Liquidation and `update_indexes` fail identically since they also accrue.
- Reachability for a single unprivileged address: `supply`/`borrow`/`update_indexes` are permissionless; caps are the only gate, and `docs/reference/formulas.md:425–430` admits caps up to ~170 billion whole tokens (`i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)`). On an 18-decimal market with a large admitted cap, one attacker can `supply` ~cap-max tokens, `borrow` ~98% of them (utilization drives the steep curve segment), then let compounding — accelerated by permissionless `update_indexes` calls — push `borrowed × borrow_index` past `i128::MAX`. No governance action or privileged call is needed after listing.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all user funds in the affected (hub, token) book and protocol insolvency: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` paths that touch the book also accrue first and revert. All deposits and accrued yield in that market are unrecoverable.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires (a) a market listed with a cap large enough for scaled totals near ~170 RAY-domain units (admitted but not default), and (b) sustained very high utilization so the index compounds ~170× — the harness shows this on the steep segment of a production-shaped curve. The capital cost is high but the attack is fully deterministic and one-sided: the attacker's own borrowed position is what overflows, and they sacrifice nothing they intended to recover.

### Recommendation
Widen the accrual valuation to `I256` (as the Certora widened rule already does for `borrowed * new_index`) or clamp/saturate `scaled_to_original` at the RAY domain bound and halt further index growth gracefully at a *value* ceiling, rather than trapping. Emit an alarm/flag when a market approaches the value ceiling so caps can be lowered before the cliff.

### Proof of Concept
See `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321–361`: supply `10^9 × 10^18` units of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98% against a second collateral market, advance time in yearly chunks calling `update_indexes`; `try_update_indexes_for(["BIG18"])`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all revert with `errors::MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
