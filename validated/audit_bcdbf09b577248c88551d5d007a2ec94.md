### Title
Permanent market freeze from RAY debt-value overflow during accrual - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
A sufficiently large, heavily borrowed market can cross the representable RAY debt-value bound before the borrow-index cap is reached. Once that happens, every state-changing path that touches the market first runs `global_sync`, and `accrue_step` repeatedly panics while calculating `borrowed * borrow_index`, permanently blocking repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, recapitalization, and further index updates for that market. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
Every pool leg loads the market through `load_leg`, which calls `synced_market`; `synced_market` unconditionally executes `interest::global_sync` before the requested operation. [3](#0-2)  `global_sync` processes all elapsed time in bounded chunks, but each chunk calls `accrue_step`. [4](#0-3) 

Inside `accrue_step`, utilization is calculated by first unscaling the market-wide scaled debt and supply with `scaled_to_original`. [5](#0-4)  `scaled_to_original` performs a non-saturating `Ray::mul`, and `Ray::mul` panics with `MathOverflow` when the resulting RAY value does not fit in `i128`. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The borrow index is capped only after this debt-value multiplication has already been evaluated. [8](#0-7)  Therefore, a large `borrowed` scaled total can reach `i128` overflow in `borrowed * borrow_index` while `borrow_index` remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`; the repository’s regression test demonstrates that exact ordering and confirms that `withdraw` and `repay` subsequently revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`. [9](#0-8) 

The controller exposes the vulnerable path through ordinary `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)`, `borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)`, and `update_indexes` operations; controller borrows forward position legs to `pool.borrow`, whose accounting path calls `load_leg` and thus accrues before minting debt. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the market crosses the arithmetic boundary, no operation that requires accrual can succeed. Suppliers cannot withdraw because `withdraw` loads a synced market before resolving the position; borrowers and liquidators cannot repay because `repay` does the same; and `update_indexes` cannot rescue the market because it also runs the overflowing accrual. [13](#0-12) [14](#0-13) [1](#0-0) 

This is a permanent freezing of all user funds associated with the affected pool market, not a temporary availability failure. The borrowed cash remains outstanding, supplier claims cannot be exited through normal or liquidation paths, and the market cannot resume without an upgrade or other privileged recovery mechanism. The existing regression test explicitly labels the resulting state as “the market is frozen” and shows both withdrawal and repayment failing. [15](#0-14) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering the condition requires an unusually large debt book and sustained high utilization, so it is not exploitable on every deployment. Nevertheless, it is reachable by unprivileged protocol actions: any user can supply collateral, borrow through their own account, and later call the public index-update path once enough time has elapsed. A whale or a market that grows organically to the documented RAY-domain boundary can therefore put the market into a state where the next accrual permanently bricks it. [16](#0-15) [10](#0-9) [17](#0-16) 

The practical threshold is high: the documented bound is approximately `i128::MAX / 10^(27-decimals)`, and the checked-in scenario uses a 1-billion-token, 18-decimal market at roughly 98% utilization. However, neither the borrow-index cap nor the utilization cap prevents the later debt-value overflow, and the repository’s numeric documentation acknowledges that value overflow can occur before either index reaches its configured ceiling. [18](#0-17) [19](#0-18) 

### Recommendation
Representability must be checked before accrual rather than relying only on the index ceiling.

- Bound admitted scaled `borrowed` and `supplied` so their products with `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` cannot overflow `i128`.
- Add equivalent post-accrual headroom checks in `accrue_step`, before `scaled_to_original` or `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies scaled totals by indexes.
- Prefer a saturating or bounded result only where it preserves accounting correctness; do not silently clamp debt values used for utilization or repay accounting.
- Enforce tighter market caps based on `asset_decimals`, current index, and remaining index headroom during `supply`, `borrow`, and strategy debt minting.
- Emit or expose a market risk warning before the hard overflow boundary so keepers and governance can deleverage before the market becomes immutable.

### Proof of Concept
A reachable sequence is:

1. Supply collateral through `supply(caller, 0, spoke_id, [(collateral_hub_asset, collateral_amount)])`.
2. Borrow the vulnerable asset through `borrow(caller, account_id, [(debt_hub_asset, amount)], to)`, where `amount` is within cash, cap, and solvency checks but large enough to create a very large scaled `borrowed` total.
3. Maintain high utilization while time elapses, either by borrowing most of the market or by the market reaching that state through normal demand.
4. Call the controller’s public `update_indexes` path for the `HubAssetKey`, or submit `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, or `recapitalize` for the same market.
5. `load_leg` calls `global_sync`; `accrue_step` evaluates `borrowed * borrow_index`; the RAY product exceeds `i128::MAX`; the transaction panics with `MathOverflow`.
6. Repeat step 4 with repayment or withdrawal. Both fail at the same accrual before any state mutation, leaving debt and supplier cash permanently locked.

The checked-in regression `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` reproduces this with a 1-billion-unit, 18-decimal asset, approximately 98% borrowed, repeated one-year advancement, and confirms `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while the borrow index remains below its cap. [20](#0-19)

    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;

        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
    }
