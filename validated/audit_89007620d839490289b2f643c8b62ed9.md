### Title
Stale accumulator governance execution redirects unclaimed protocol revenue - (File: contracts/governance/src/timelock/lifecycle.rs)

### Summary
A ready `SetAccumulator` governance operation can be executed by any address and unconditionally overwrites the controller’s current accumulator, even after a newer accumulator operation has already executed. Subsequent permissionless `claim_revenue` calls forward accrued protocol revenue to the stale address rather than the current accumulator.

### Finding Description
`AdminOperation::SetAccumulator` resolves to the controller `set_accumulator` entrypoint with the destination encoded in the scheduled arguments. [1](#0-0) 

Governance execution accepts `executor = None`, checks only that the submitted operation hashes to a ready scheduled operation, invokes it, and clears it; it does not check that the scheduled destination remains current or that no newer accumulator operation has executed. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The controller handler blindly stores the supplied address as the singleton accumulator. [4](#0-3)  `claim_revenue_for_asset` then reads that singleton at claim time and forwards the measured pool receipt to it. [5](#0-4) 

An unprivileged executor can therefore execute an older ready `SetAccumulator(A)` after `SetAccumulator(B)` has already become effective, restoring `A` as the revenue recipient despite governance’s latest committed intent being `B`.

### Impact Explanation
This can permanently redirect or freeze unclaimed protocol yield: once the stale accumulator is restored, any caller can invoke `claim_revenue`, and the controller sends the measured revenue receipt to the outdated address. [6](#0-5)  If the stale address is controlled by an undesired recipient this is theft of protocol revenue; if it is an obsolete or unreachable accumulator, the claimed funds are frozen there. The amount is bounded by revenue currently claimable plus future claims until governance installs another accumulator.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is low because it requires governance to leave an obsolete `SetAccumulator` operation ready alongside, or after, a newer accumulator change. Once that precondition exists, however, `execute(None, ...)` is intentionally callable by anyone, so no executor role or signature is needed to restore the stale destination. [3](#0-2) 

### Recommendation
Bind singleton configuration operations to execution-time state. For example, add an `expected_accumulator` or monotonically increasing accumulator epoch to `AdminOperation::SetAccumulator`, pass it through the scheduled controller arguments, and have `set_accumulator` revert if the live value/epoch differs. Alternatively, make proposing or executing a newer accumulator operation automatically cancel or invalidate older pending accumulator operations, similar to the existing spoke-flags epoch guard used for delayed flag relaxation. [7](#0-6) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance schedules `AdminOperation::SetAccumulator(A)` and later schedules `AdminOperation::SetAccumulator(B)`.
2. Both operations become `Ready`.
3. `execute(None, controller, "set_accumulator", [B], predecessor, salt_B)` commits `B` as the accumulator.
4. Any unprivileged caller then invokes `execute(None, controller, "set_accumulator", [A], predecessor, salt_A)`; because the operation is still ready and carries no accumulator epoch/current-value precondition, `set_accumulator` overwrites `B` with `A`.
5. The same or another caller invokes `claim_revenue(caller, [hub_asset])`.
6. `claim_revenue_for_asset` reads `A` from controller storage and transfers the measured pool revenue receipt to `A`, even though `B` was the most recently committed accumulator.
