### Title

A borrower can plant dust collateral on an already-stale feed to block liquidation, withdrawal, and bad-debt cleanup until the feed recovers - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs))

### Summary

`supply` accepts and creates a new collateral position without requiring a usable oracle price for that asset, but liquidation and bad-debt cleanup value every supply and debt position before proceeding. An unprivileged borrower can therefore add a minimal amount of an asset whose feed is stale and make all subsequent risk calculations revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, temporarily preventing liquidation and bad-debt socialization while debt continues accruing. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description

`process_deposit` validates entry gates, transfers the offered amount, calls the pool, and merges the resulting supply shares; none of the shown supply path establishes that the newly added asset has a currently valid price. [1](#0-0) 

In contrast, `calculate_account_risk_totals` loads market data for the union of all supply and borrow positions, and `load_markets` fetches prices for every distinct token in that union. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

`liquidate` reaches this all-position valuation before checking the account’s health factor, while permissionless `clean_bad_debt` performs the same calculation before deciding whether the account can be socialized. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The attacker only needs to call `supply(caller, account_id, spoke_id, assets)` on their own borrowing account with a dust amount of a listed asset whose oracle is already stale. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation

A stale dust leg causes liquidation and `clean_bad_debt` to revert even after the account becomes underwater, so debt can remain open and accrue while lenders cannot execute the intended risk-reduction paths. [9](#0-8) 

The borrower also cannot withdraw the poisoned leg while debt remains because withdrawal still runs the post-pool solvency calculation over the stale position. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

If the feed remains unavailable through further collateral depreciation or interest accrual, liquidation may eventually recover less than the debt and cleanup can socialize the residual loss to suppliers. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires an asset admitted as collateral whose oracle can enter a stale or otherwise unusable state, plus enough token dust to mint a nonzero supply position. [14](#0-13) 

The borrower does not need governance access, oracle access, leaked keys, or a contract callback; they only authorize their own `supply` call. [15](#0-14) 

The denial lasts only while the required price remains unusable: the deterministic harness restores the WBTC price and the identical liquidation succeeds afterward. [16](#0-15) 

### Recommendation

Require a fresh, valid price for every newly created supply position before crediting it, or make liquidation and cleanup tolerate economically negligible unpriceable collateral. A conservative implementation should price-check the new leg during `process_deposit` when the account has debt, and reject the supply if the returned feed is invalid. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Alice supplies USDC and borrows ETH, leaving a healthy account. [17](#0-16) 
2. The WBTC feed is made stale, then Alice successfully supplies `0.001` WBDC/WBTC-class collateral as a new dust leg. [18](#0-17) 
3. USDC crashes, making the account eligible for liquidation. [19](#0-18) 
4. `liquidate(liquidator, alice_id, [(ETH, amount)], SeizeMode::Transfer)` reverts with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`. [20](#0-19) 
5. `clean_bad_debt(caller, alice_id)` also reverts with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, and withdrawing even part of the dust leg fails for the same reason. [21](#0-20) 
6. Restoring a fresh WBTC price makes the identical liquidation succeed, proving the temporary denial is caused by the newly added stale collateral leg. [16](#0-15)
