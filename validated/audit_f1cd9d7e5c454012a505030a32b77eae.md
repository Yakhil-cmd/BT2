Based on extensive analysis, the closest reachable analog to the Bludit "ghost session" bug class (revoked/deleted authorization state remaining trusted because a positive fact is checked without checking for later revocation) is the attestation-based spending-authorization check in `storage.js`.

### Title
Attestation-gated spending never re-validates for revocation, letting a permanently "ghost-attested" address keep spending rights - ([File: storage.js])

### Summary
`spender_attested` assets and `["attested", [address, [attestors]]]` address-definition conditions authorize spending purely on the *historical fact* that an attestor once posted an `attestation` for the address. Neither `storage.filterAttestedAddresses()` nor the `'attested'` opcode evaluator in `definition.js` ever check whether that attestation has since been superseded/revoked by the attestor — they only check that *some* attestation exists before `last_ball_mci` and (only) that it postdates the address's *own* last key-rotation. This mirrors the Bludit flaw: authorization is derived from a stale positive record instead of the current authoritative state.

### Finding Description
`filterAttestedAddresses()` runs: [1](#0-0) 
This query returns an address as "attested" if **any** row exists in `attestations` from one of the trusted attestors before `last_ball_mci`, gated only by a check that the attestation is newer than the address's last `address_definition_change` (key rotation) — not by whether the attestor has since revoked/changed the attestation.

This is consumed by the asset-spending authorization path: [2](#0-1) 
and by the address-definition opcode used to gate signing authority for regular payments: [3](#0-2) 

Compare this to `'in data feed'`/formula-level attestation lookups elsewhere in the codebase, which explicitly pick the **latest** value with `ifseveral: 'last'` semantics: [4](#0-3) 
No equivalent "latest wins" or revocation-field mechanism exists for the `'attested'` opcode used in address/asset spending conditions — it is a pure existence check over all time. Once an attestor (e.g., a KYC/compliance oracle) has attested an address once, that address is permanently authorized to spend under any `attested`-gated definition or `spender_attested` asset, even if the attestor later determines the identity should be revoked (sanctioned, fraud, compromised key handed back to original owner, etc.) — there is no on-chain way to remove the earlier `attestations` row's effect, since the check is "was ever attested," not "attestor's current/last verdict."

### Impact Explanation
Any address or asset whose spending condition relies on `["attested", ...]` (a documented, commonly used building block for KYC-gated multisigs and regulated/attested assets) permanently grants signing/spending authority to a previously-attested address. An attestor cannot revoke access once granted through this mechanism — the address retains authorization to move funds under attested-gated conditions, which is a form of unauthorized/uncontrollable spending authority persisting against the intent of the definer/attestor, directly analogous to Bludit's persistent access for a deleted/revoked account.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged unit poster who was attested even once by a trusted attestor can rely on this forever in their own address definition or when spending a `spender_attested` asset; no special privilege beyond being attested once is required, and the condition is straightforward to construct (`["attested", ["this address", [attestor_addr]]]`) as part of a normal address definition or asset filter, which are both fully user/asset-issuer controlled constructs reachable from a single posted unit.

### Recommendation
Change `filterAttestedAddresses()` to consider only the attestor's most recent attestation for the address (per attestor, "last wins," matching the formula-engine's `ifseveral: 'last'` semantics) and support/require an explicit revocation record (e.g., a later attestation from the same attestor with a `revoked`/`verified=false` field) to be honored by the `'attested'` opcode, rather than treating any historical attestation as permanent proof of current status.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attestor A posts `attestation` for address X (fact recorded in `attestations` table).
2. X's owner uses `["attested", ["this address", ["A"]]]` in an address definition, or issues/holds units of an asset with `spender_attested=1` and A in `asset_attestors`.
3. Attestor A determines X should be de-authorized (compromise, sanctions, KYC failure) and has no on-chain mechanism to revoke the original attestation's effect — `filterAttestedAddresses()` will keep returning X as attested indefinitely because it only checks for existence of *a* pre-last-ball attestation, not currency/validity of that attestation.
4. X continues to satisfy the `attested` condition and can sign/spend indefinitely, contradicting the attestor's intended revocation — the "ghost" authorization persists exactly as in the Bludit ghost-session case.
