### Title
Bad-debt wipeout preserves phantom supply shares that drain later deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When unpaid debt equals or exceeds a market’s total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the wiped-out supply index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of eliminating the pre-existing share base. [1](#0-0)  Because those old shares remain in `supplied`, they retain a positive claim equal to roughly 0.1% of their former value and can consume cash deposited after the cleanup. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)` is permissionless apart from `caller.require_auth()`. [3](#0-2)  It admits an account with open debt when `total_debt > total_collateral` and `total_collateral <= BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD`. [4](#0-3) 

Cleanup sends every remaining deposit and borrow position to the pool’s `seize_positions` entrypoint. [5](#0-4)  For a borrow leg, the pool converts the debt shares into RAY-denominated debt, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and then burns the debt. [6](#0-5) 

Inside `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, `bad_debt` is capped at total supplied value and `remaining` becomes zero on a full wipeout. [7](#0-6)  The calculated index is therefore zero, but the subsequent `max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` resurrects it to `RAY / 1000`. [8](#0-7)  The existing `supplied` balance is not burned or moved to a new share generation, so every pre-wipeout scaled share remains redeemable at that floor.

Deposit-side cleanup worsens this by reclassifying seized supply as revenue while leaving total `supplied` unchanged. [9](#0-8)  Those reclassified shares can later be claimed through the normal revenue path, which burns claimable revenue, debits cash, and transfers the proceeds to the controller. [10](#0-9) 

### Impact Explanation
A full bad-debt write-down leaves lenders or protocol revenue with phantom claims backed by no cash. [11](#0-10)  The next supplier deposits real tokens at the floor index; the stranded shares can then withdraw or claim that cash first, leaving the new supplier’s valid claim unbacked. [12](#0-11) 

This is theft of user funds and can make the market insolvent: the deposited cash is paid to holders of shares that were supposed to have been wiped out. [13](#0-12)  If the stranded shares were reclassified as revenue, permissionless `Controller::claim_revenue(caller, assets)` forwards the drained amount to the configured accumulator. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged action is required for the cleanup itself: any authenticated caller can invoke `clean_bad_debt` once the dust-cap insolvency predicate is satisfied. [16](#0-15)  A heavily liquidated account can naturally reach that state when remaining collateral falls to dust while debt remains.

The vulnerable condition occurs whenever the unpaid debt in the affected market reaches its remaining supplied value, which is expected for a market whose cash has been borrowed and whose debt is then socialized. [17](#0-16)  Afterward, exploitation requires only an ordinary later supply and a withdrawal or revenue claim by a holder of the preserved shares.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp a fully wiped index upward while retaining the old share base. When `remaining == 0`, either:

- keep the index at zero and prevent new supply until explicit recapitalize/reinitialization, or
- introduce a new supply-share generation so pre-wipeout shares cannot claim post-wipeout deposits, or
- reject socialization that would consume the entire supplied value and require recapitalization instead.

The floor should apply only to newly minted shares after a reset boundary; it must not resurrect claims for shares included in the wiped base.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies `S` shares worth `V` to market `B`; the market’s cash is borrowed by account `X`.
2. Liquidations or price movement leave `X` with debt in `B` greater than remaining collateral, while collateral is at most the bad-debt dust threshold.
3. Any caller invokes:
   `Controller::clean_bad_debt(attacker, account_id_X)`
   satisfying `caller.require_auth()` and the dust-cap gate. [16](#0-15) 
4. The borrow seizure computes `bad_debt = X.borrow_scaled * B.borrow_index`, applies it to `B`’s supply index, and burns the debt. [6](#0-5) 
5. Because `bad_debt >= supplied * supply_index`, `remaining = 0`, but `supply_index` is set to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Alice’s `S` shares therefore still have claim `floor(S * floor_index)` despite the wipeout. [1](#0-0) 
6. Bob supplies `C` tokens to `B`; his shares mint at the floor index and `cash` increases by `C`. [18](#0-17) 
7. Alice submits a full withdrawal for her preserved `S` shares. The pool burns them and transfers `gross = stranded_claim`; with `C = stranded_claim`, the transaction drains Bob’s entire deposit while Bob’s valid claim remains unbacked. [19](#0-18) 

The in-tree test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` demonstrates the exact accounting outcome: the floor creates a positive stranded claim, a fresh deposit funds it, and the stranded position withdraws the pool to zero. [20](#0-19)

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));

fn test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    t.as_contract(|| {
        let scaled_a_raw = 1_000_000 * RAY;
        let mut cache = t.fresh_cache(PoolStateRaw {
            supplied: scaled_a_raw,
            borrowed: 0,
            revenue: 0,
            borrow_index: RAY,
            supply_index: RAY,
            last_timestamp: 0,
            cash: 0,
        });
        let scaled_a = Ray::from(scaled_a_raw);

        apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(2_000_000 * RAY));
        assert_eq!(
            cache.supply_index().raw(),
            SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW,
            "wipeout must clamp supply index UP to the floor, not reset the base"
        );

        let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a);
        assert!(stranded > 0, "floor clamp leaves userA a phantom claim");
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "empty market: no cash to extract yet");

        let c = stranded;
        let scaled_b = cache.calculate_scaled_supply(c);
        cache.mint_supply(scaled_b);
        cache.credit_cash(c);

        let b_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(scaled_b);
        assert_eq!(b_claim, c, "userB's honest claim equals their deposit");

        let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a);
        cache.require_reserves(gross);
        cache.burn_supply(burn);
        cache.debit_cash(gross);

        assert!(gross > 0, "stranded position pays out non-zero");
        assert_eq!(
            gross, c,
            "userA drains exactly userB's fresh deposit out of the pool"
        );

        assert!(
            cache.cash() < b_claim,
            "pool cash ({}) can no longer cover userB's claim ({}): honest supplier lost funds",
            cache.cash(),
            b_claim
        );
        assert_eq!(cache.cash(), 0, "userA drained the pool to empty");
    });

/// Authorizes permissionless dust-gated cleanup outside flash loans.
pub(crate) fn process_clean_bad_debt(env: &Env, caller: &Address, account_id: u64) {
    caller.require_auth();
    validation::require_not_flash_loaning(env);
    clean_bad_debt_standalone(env, account_id);
}

/// Admission condition for bad-debt socialization.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
enum BadDebtGate {
    /// Permissionless: insolvent *and* collateral at or below the dust threshold.
    DustCapped,
    /// Owner-only: insolvent alone, with no cap on the collateral left behind.
    InsolventOnly,
}

/// Requires open debt and the selected insolvency gate, then cleans up the account.
fn socialize_bad_debt(env: &Env, account_id: u64, gate: BadDebtGate) {
    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    let account = storage::get_account(env, account_id);

    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        !account.borrow_positions.is_empty(),
        CollateralError::DebtPositionNotFound
    );

    let totals = risk::calculate_account_risk_totals(
        env,
        &mut cache,
        &account.supply_positions,
        &account.borrow_positions,
    );

    let admits = match gate {
        BadDebtGate::DustCapped => {
            is_socializable_bad_debt(totals.total_debt, totals.total_collateral)
        }
        BadDebtGate::InsolventOnly => totals.total_debt > totals.total_collateral,
    };
    assert_with_error!(env, admits, CollateralError::CannotCleanBadDebt);
