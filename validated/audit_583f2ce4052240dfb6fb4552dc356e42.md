### Title
Borrowed liquidity can sandwich and temporarily block collateral withdrawals - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs](contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs))

### Summary
An unprivileged attacker can front-run a victim's `withdraw` by borrowing enough of the same asset to leave the pool with less cash than the withdrawal amount. The victim's transaction reverts with `InsufficientLiquidity`; the attacker can then repay the debt and recover their collateral, restoring the pool to its prior state. This mirrors the NFT-withdrawal grief: temporarily remove the resource required by the victim's action, let their transaction fail, then restore it at only interest and transaction-cost expense.

### Finding Description
The controller allows any authenticated account owner to supply collateral and borrow against it through `supply` and `borrow`. [1](#0-0)  Pool borrowing transfers real token liquidity to the borrower after minting debt and debiting market cash. [2](#0-1)  A withdrawal is routed to the pool and must pass `require_reserves`, which reverts when market cash is below the requested amount. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Borrowing cannot consume the entire reserve because `require_liquidation_buffer` requires the post-borrow balance to remain at least `ceil(supplied × LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS / BPS)`. [5](#0-4)  Consequently, every withdrawal larger than that reserved buffer can be forced to fail by reducing free cash below the withdrawal amount.

The attacker can unwind the position by calling the permissionless `repay` entrypoint. Repayment credits cash back to the pool and refunds any excess payment. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)  The collateral backing the borrow is not consumed and can be withdrawn after repayment.

### Impact Explanation
This is a temporary freezing-of-funds condition. A supplier attempting to withdraw more than the liquidation buffer can have their transaction deliberately reverted while the attacker controls enough free liquidity. The attack is repeatable whenever the attacker retains sufficient collateral to reopen the borrow, and users cannot distinguish this induced liquidity shortage from ordinary utilization without inspecting pending transactions. During the interval, the victim's supplied assets remain unavailable even though no funds are stolen.

### Likelihood Explanation
The path is fully unprivileged: the attacker supplies collateral to their own account, borrows the target asset, repays it, and later withdraws the collateral. The attack does not require privileged roles, leaked authorization, oracle manipulation, malicious tokens, or access to the victim's account. Its main cost is borrowing interest and transaction fees, while the collateral and principal remain recoverable. Feasibility depends on the victim's requested amount exceeding the protocol's liquidation buffer and on the attacker having enough collateral and debt capacity to reduce cash below that amount.

### Recommendation
Add an anti-griefing mitigation for liquidity-exhaustion sandwiches, such as:

- reserving a configurable withdrawal-only liquidity tranche that ordinary borrows cannot consume;
- allowing withdrawals to execute partially and return the available amount when full liquidity is temporarily absent;
- introducing a short settlement delay or withdrawal-request queue for amounts above the liquidation buffer;
- charging a minimum borrow-open or short-duration fee so immediately reversed borrows are not economically free.

A withdrawal-only reserve is the most direct fix, but it should be sized carefully so it does not materially reduce legitimate borrowing capacity.

### Proof of Concept
Let:

- `V` be the victim account supplying `W` units of asset `A`;
- `A` also be borrowable by the attacker;
- `R` be the pool's current cash balance of `A`;
- `B = ceil(total_supply(A) × LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS / BPS)`;
- `W > B`.

1. The attacker supplies sufficient collateral `C` to their own account using `supply(attacker, 0, spoke_id, [(C_key, C)])`.
2. The attacker obtains account ID `attacker_id`.
3. Immediately before the victim's withdrawal, the attacker calls `borrow(attacker, attacker_id, [(A_key, D)], Some(attacker))`, choosing `D` such that:
   - `D > R - W`, leaving less than `W` cash;
   - `D <= R - B`, satisfying the liquidation-buffer check;
   - the resulting utilization and attacker health factor remain valid.
4. The victim submits `withdraw(victim_owner, V, [(A_key, W)], recipient)`. The pool calls `require_reserves(W)`, observes `cash < W`, and reverts with `InsufficientLiquidity`.
5. The attacker calls `repay(attacker, attacker_id, [(A_key, repayment)])`, where `repayment` is at least the accrued debt. Excess is refunded, cash is restored, and the attacker can withdraw collateral `C`.
6. The attacker can repeat the sequence whenever another large victim withdrawal is pending.

```rust
/// Panics with `InsufficientLiquidity` if drawing `draw` leaves cash below the liquidation buffer.
///
/// Every debt mint checks it, borrows and strategy openings alike (INV-ACCT-07). Exits do not.
pub(crate) fn require_liquidation_buffer(env: &Env, cache: &Cache, draw: i128) {
    let supplied = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let reserved = mul_div_ceil(env, supplied, LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS, BPS);
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        cache.cash().saturating_sub(draw) >= reserved,
        CollateralError::InsufficientLiquidity
    );
```
