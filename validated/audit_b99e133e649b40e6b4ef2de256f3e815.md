### Title
Full bad-debt socialization preserves phantom supply claims and freezes the market - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes a full supplier write-down correctly but then clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` without burning or epoch-resetting the outstanding `supplied` shares. After debt equal to the entire supplier claim is socialized and burned, those shares still represent a positive claim, while cash and debt are both zero. This leaves an unbacked market that cannot resume normal operation until a third party recapitalizes the residual claim. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
Bad-debt cleanup sends each account position to the pool as a `PoolSeizeEntry`; for a `Borrow` entry, the pool computes the debt in RAY terms, calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and then burns only the debt shares. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The write-down caps the loss at `total_supplied_value`, computes `remaining = total_supplied_value - capped`, and multiplies the current index by `remaining / total_supplied_value`. When bad debt reaches the entire supplier claim, the mathematically correct index is zero, but line 88 clamps it to `RAY / 1000` instead. [4](#0-3) 

The analogous flaw is the stale index state surviving an operation that logically emptied the book: `borrowed` is reduced to zero, but `supplied` and `revenue` remain populated and now decode to positive value at the nonzero index floor. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

A market’s backing check compares the floored supplier claim with `cash + ceiled outstanding debt`; after a complete wipeout this becomes a positive supplier claim backed by zero cash and zero debt. [7](#0-6) 

The repository’s own unit test demonstrates this exact state: the floor leaves an old position with a positive claim, a subsequent deposit can fund that claim, and paying it leaves insufficient cash for the new supplier. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged caller can invoke permissionless bad-debt cleanup on an eligible account, causing the affected market to retain an unbacked residual supplier claim after all economically meaningful supply was written down. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

The market then remains insolvent until `recapitalize` injects enough cash to cover `backing_shortfall`; recapitalization credits only the measured shortfall and does not mint compensating shares. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

This is at least a temporary freezing of market funds and normal market operation; if a new deposit is admitted before the shortfall is repaired, an old wiped-out position can consume cash that economically belongs to the new depositor. [13](#0-12) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs an account whose debt exceeds its collateral while its collateral is at or below the `$5` permissionless-cleanup threshold, which can occur after borrowing pool liquidity and experiencing a collateral-price decline. [9](#0-8) 

The severe state additionally requires the socialized debt value to be at least the affected market’s total supplier claim; this is most plausible in a near-fully-utilized market where reserve-factor-adjusted debt has grown beyond supplier claims. [4](#0-3) 

No privileged action, oracle manipulation outside normal price movement, leaked key, or upgrade is required because `clean_bad_debt` is part of the permissionless liquidation surface and socialization is performed by the production pool path. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

### Recommendation
Do not represent a complete supplier wipeout by retaining the same share supply at a nonzero index floor. When `capped == total_supplied_value`, either move the market to a new share/index epoch that invalidates all pre-wipeout positions, or explicitly burn the corresponding total `supplied` and `revenue` bookkeeping and ensure old position records cannot later be valued against the floor. [16](#0-15) [17](#0-16) 

Until an epoch mechanism exists, the safer fallback is to prevent cleanup from committing a state whose floored index creates a positive `backing_shortfall`, and require atomic recapitalization of the residual claim before the market can process further share-bearing operations. [7](#0-6) [18](#0-17) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Victims supply a debt market until it has `S` scaled supply shares at index `I`, with supplier value `S * I`. [6](#0-5) 
2. An attacker borrows essentially all available cash so the market’s remaining cash approaches zero, and lets interest make outstanding debt greater than or equal to `S * I`. [15](#0-14) 
3. The attacker’s collateral falls below the debt and to at most `$5`, making the account eligible for permissionless cleanup. [9](#0-8) 
4. Any caller invokes `clean_bad_debt` for the account; the controller creates a `Borrow` seize entry containing the attacker’s debt shares and submits it to the pool. [19](#0-18) 
5. `seize::apply` converts the position to `bad_debt`, socializes it, and burns the debt; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` observes `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, calculates a zero reduction factor, but writes `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead. [20](#0-19) [21](#0-20) 
6. The original `S` supply shares remain in market state and now value at `S * RAY / 1000`, so `backing_shortfall` is positive even though borrowed debt and pool cash are zero. [22](#0-21) 
7. The market cannot clear its backing invariant until a payer calls `recapitalize`; if fresh liquidity is admitted without that repair, the stale shares can withdraw cash belonging to the fresh supplier, as demonstrated by `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard`. [23](#0-22) [24](#0-23)

pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
}

/// Asset units by which supplier claims exceed cash + debt (0 if solvent).
pub(crate) fn backing_shortfall(cache: &Cache) -> i128 {
    let supplied_claim = cache.unscale_supply_floor(cache.supplied());
    let outstanding_debt = cache.unscale_borrow_ceil(cache.borrowed());
    let backing = cache.cash().saturating_add(outstanding_debt);
    supplied_claim.saturating_sub(backing).max(0)
}

/// Sizes and books the cash injection without transferring tokens.
///
/// Credits `min(amount, backing_shortfall)` to cash and commits. `refund` is
/// `amount - applied`.
pub(crate) fn accounting(
    env: &Env,
    hub_asset: HubAssetKey,
    amount: i128,
) -> RecapitalizationOutcome {
    require_nonneg_amount(env, amount);
    let mut cache = ops::renewed_market(env, &hub_asset);

    let applied = amount.min(guards::backing_shortfall(&cache));
    let refund = amount
        .checked_sub(applied)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));

    cache.credit_cash(applied);
    cache.commit();
