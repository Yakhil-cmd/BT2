### Title
Permanent market freeze via uncaught i128 overflow in interest accrual (`scaled_to_original`) - (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
The bignum advisory is a DoS from an unchecked runtime exception reachable through a public entrypoint. The XOXNO analog is an uncaught `i128` overflow panic inside interest accrual: `accrue_step` calls `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)` to compute utilization *before* the borrow-index ceiling can engage, and `Ray::mul` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `scaled * index` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Any unprivileged user can push a high-decimals market to this state simply by supplying and borrowing a very large amount and waiting, after which every pool verb on that market (supply, borrow, withdraw, repay, liquidate, update_indexes) accrues first and panics forever — permanently freezing all supplier funds.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` in `common/src/rates/simulate.rs` unscales both books before computing the interest factor:

```rust
let borrowed_original = scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index);
let supplied_original = scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index);
```
(common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61)

`scaled_to_original` is `scaled.mul(env, index)` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), which bottoms out in `mul_div_half_up` and panics with `MathOverflow` when the product does not fit `i128` (`common/src/math/fp_core.rs:108-118`). The intended guard, `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 1e36`, is applied only *after* this multiplication inside `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), so for a market whose `borrowed` (scaled, RAY-precision) is large enough, `borrowed * borrow_index` overflows `i128` while `borrow_index` is still far below the cap. Once `borrowed_scaled * borrow_index >= i128::MAX` (≈1.7e38), every subsequent call that triggers `global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` panics (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`), and since accrual runs first in every pool mutation, the market is bricked.

This is not hypothetical: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360` reproduces it — a whale supplies ~1e9 units of an 18-decimal asset, a borrower takes 98% utilization on the steep XLM-style curve, and after enough years `update_indexes` panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, after which `withdraw` and `repay` also panic with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and the test confirms `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engages).

Reachability for an unprivileged caller: `controller.supply` (or `recapitalize`/`migrate_from_blend`) grows `supplied`/`borrowed` shares; `controller.borrow` raises utilization into the steep segment of the rate curve; `update_indexes` is permissionless and advances accrual in `MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS` chunks. Time does the rest — no privileged call is required once the market exists and caps allow the size.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the scaled-debt × index product crosses the `i128` ceiling, every entrypoint that touches the market reverts during the mandatory pre-accrual, so suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and revenue cannot be claimed — for all users of that market, forever (no governance action rewrites the scaled share balances). The physical tokens remain locked in the pool.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a whale-scale position on a high-decimals token (the test uses ~1e9 whole tokens at 18 decimals, i.e., `borrowed` on the order of 1e45 raw units, so overflow occurs when `borrow_index ≈ 170×RAY`) combined with sustained high utilization so the index actually grows that far before hitting `1e36`. On steep curve segments this takes years of compounding, and a single attacker must fund both the supply and borrow legs (or coexist with organic borrowing). Capital and time requirements make it a deliberate, expensive attack rather than an accident, but it is fully permissionless and irreversible once reached — Medium/High likelihood-adjusted severity; I rate it Medium given the multi-year accrual horizon and capital cost, High if a real high-decimals market with large TVL exists.

### Recommendation
Make the unscale step saturating or pre-clamped inside `accrue_step`: e.g., clamp `borrowed`/`supplied` so `scaled * index` cannot exceed `i128::MAX` before calling `scaled_to_original`, or compute utilization via `mul_div` on a widened (`I256`) path that degrades gracefully instead of panicking. Alternatively, enforce a per-market bound on `borrowed` scaled shares (`borrowed <= i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) at mint time in `contracts/pool/src/cache/shares.rs`/caps validation, so the index cap provably engages before the value ceiling does. The existing test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` should then be updated to assert the market stays operable.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create a market for an 18-decimal token with a steep interest-rate curve (e.g., `xlm_curve`) and supply/borrow caps lifted.
2. Unprivileged attacker calls `controller.supply` with ~`1e9 * 1e18` units, then `controller.borrow` for 98% of it (funded by collateral supplied on another market).
3. Advance ledger time (anyone can call `controller.update_indexes` periodically) until `borrow_index * borrowed_scaled` exceeds `i128::MAX` — in the reference scenario, ~10–20 years at sustained 98% utilization, before `borrow_index` reaches `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. Every subsequent `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `claim_revenue`, and `flash_loan` on that market panics in `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original` with `GenericError::MathOverflow`; supplier funds are permanently locked. This exact sequence is encoded and asserted in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`.
