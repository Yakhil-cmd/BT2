### Title
Permissionless `update_account_threshold` lets anyone restamp a victim's liquidation parameters (lower threshold, higher bonus, lower fee) at an attacker-chosen time - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs)

### Summary
`Controller::update_account_threshold` is a permissionless entrypoint that rewrites any listed `account_id`'s stored per-position LTV, liquidation threshold, liquidation bonus, and liquidation fees to the currently listed spoke values. The only protection for a deteriorating tuple is a post-update health-factor floor of 1.05; there is no check that the caller owns or is a delegate of the account. An attacker can therefore force another user's account onto worse liquidation terms (e.g. a higher `liquidation_bonus` or lower `liquidation_threshold` newly listed by governance) at the precise moment that maximizes the victim's loss — the same class as `BalancedVault::syncAccount`, where anyone settles/rebalances a foreign account at a predictable, unfavorable time.

### Finding Description
The entrypoint accepts an arbitrary `caller` and a batch of `account_ids`, requiring only `caller.require_auth()`:

```rust
// contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:124-144
pub(crate) fn update_account_threshold(env, caller, has_risks, account_ids) {
    validation::require_authorized_caller(env, &caller);   // any authenticated address
    ...
    for account_id in account_ids {
        cache.reset_spoke_context();
        sync_account_thresholds(env, account_id, scope, &mut cache);  // no owner check on account_id
    }
}
```

Each supply position caches its own `loan_to_value`, `liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus`, and `liquidation_fees` at creation (`get_or_create_supply_position`), and those stored values — not the live listing — drive the health factor and the liquidation bonus curve (`docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md`:362-374). The stored tuple is only refreshed on the owner's own supply/withdraw/borrow actions, or via this permissionless call (`sync_account_thresholds` → `refresh_supply_risk_params` → `apply_gated_liquidation_params`, lines 149-237, 25-40, 68-93).

When `has_risks = true`, adverse tuples pass through `apply_gated_liquidation_params`, which gates only on the *hypothetical* health factor with the new threshold being ≥ 1.05 (`clears_min_hf`, lines 103-119, and the batch-level assert at 229-233). Nothing ties the caller to the account:

- If governance raises `liquidation_bonus` (or lowers `liquidation_fees`), `favors_liquidator` (lines 96-100) is true, and any account whose HF ≥ 1.05 under the new threshold silently takes the worse tuple.
- If governance lowers `liquidation_threshold`, the same applies — the victim's stored HF numerator shrinks, moving them closer to liquidation, and the only floor is 1.05, not the previous HF.

Crucially, the parameter change is predictable: `edit_asset_in_spoke` is a timelocked governance operation, so the new listed values and their effective moment are known in advance. The attacker chooses both the timing (e.g. immediately before an expected price update that will move the account toward HF 1) and which accounts get restamped. A victim who would otherwise have kept the old, safer tuple until their next voluntary action is forced onto the new terms on someone else's schedule.

### Impact Explanation
For an account that is later liquidated, a restamped higher `liquidation_bonus` directly increases the collateral a liquidator seizes beyond what the victim's stored terms allowed — a real, quantifiable loss of user funds. A restamped lower `liquidation_threshold` lowers the account's HF to as far as 1.05, so a subsequent adverse price move (whose timing the attacker knows) liquidates the account earlier than it would have under its stored tuple, again with the larger bonus. The victim never authorized the refresh and may have been deliberately relying on the insulation the cached parameters were designed to provide between governance changes and their own next interaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance listing change that worsens at least one of LT/bonus/fee for an asset the victim supplies, plus a victim account with HF ≥ 1.05 under the new LT — a routine condition, since most healthy accounts sit well above 1.05. The call is permissionless (only `caller.require_auth()`), callable with the attacker as `caller` and the victim's `account_id`, and takes a batch so many victims can be restamped in one transaction. The adverse listing change itself is timelocked and publicly observable, satisfying the "predictable update" element of the bug class. The attacker gains indirectly (or via a cooperating liquidator capturing the larger bonus), so this is griefing/extraction at Medium severity rather than direct theft.

### Recommendation
Enforce ownership on the adverse path: either require `caller` to be the account owner or an active delegate (`require_owner_or_delegate`) whenever `has_risks = true` would apply a `favors_liquidator` tuple, or skip (rather than apply) any tuple change that worsens the position for accounts the caller does not control. Keep the permissionless path only for strictly neutral-or-beneficial restamps (higher LT, lower bonus, higher fee), mirroring the BalancedVault recommendation that only the account holder may trigger their own settlement.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim holds a supply position in asset A with stored tuple `{ltv: 8000, lt: 8000, bonus: 500, fees: 100}` and a borrow position giving HF ≈ 1.20.
2. Governance proposes and executes `edit_asset_in_spoke` for A with `{lt: 7500, bonus: 1000}` — worse for suppliers, timelocked and publicly visible.
3. The victim does nothing, expecting to keep the stored `{lt: 8000, bonus: 500}` until their next supply/withdraw/borrow.
4. Attacker calls `update_account_threshold(caller: attacker, has_risks: true, account_ids: [victim_id])`. `favors_liquidator` is true (LT 7500 < 8000, bonus 1000 > 500); HF under LT 7500 is ≈ 1.125 ≥ 1.05, so `apply_gated_liquidation_params` writes the worse tuple and the batch assert passes.
5. A price dip the attacker anticipated drops collateral value ~10%. Under the old tuple HF would have been ≈ 1.08 (not liquidatable); under the restamped tuple HF falls below 1.0 and the account is liquidated, with the liquidator claiming the 1000 bps bonus instead of 500 — all without any action or consent by the victim.
