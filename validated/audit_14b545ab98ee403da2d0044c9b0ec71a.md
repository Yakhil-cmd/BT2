### Title
Dormant position-manager delegation silently reactivates when the position NFT returns to a previous owner - (contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs)

### Summary
Each XOXNO Lending account is owned through a transferable `position-nft`, and the account owner can grant "position manager" delegates who may borrow and withdraw to arbitrary external recipients within the account's risk limits. A grant records only the granting owner's address — no transfer epoch or version. When the NFT moves to a new owner the grant becomes inactive, but it is not deleted; if the NFT ever returns to the original granter, the stale delegation silently becomes active again. This is exactly the "too flexible, implicit cross-module configuration" class from the report: the delegation list (controller storage) and the ownership record (position-nft contract) are not bound together, so an NFT transfer does not reset dependent permissions. An unprivileged, previously-granted delegate can then drain the account.

### Finding Description
The delegation invariant is documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md`: "A grant records the owner's address, without a transfer epoch. It is inactive under another owner but can reactivate if the NFT returns, unless an intervening owner overwrites or purges the grant." [1](#0-0) 

Concretely:

- Delegates "can choose external payout recipients, giving them broad economic control within the account's risk limits" — borrowing or withdrawing to their own address, not just risk-neutral operations [2](#0-1) .
- The grant's validity is checked against the *current* NFT owner (`owner_of`), but the grant itself is keyed to the account, not to an ownership epoch. There is no storage of "owner at grant time" per se beyond the granter's address, and no on-transfer hook in `contracts/position-nft` clears controller delegates.
- Account removal deletes delegates, but an ordinary NFT `transfer`/`approve` does not [3](#0-2) .

So the system has an implicit invariant — "delegates valid only under the granting ownership" — that is enforced by address comparison rather than by an epoch, and it fails when ownership cycles back. This mirrors the external report's pattern: two modules (position-nft ownership, controller delegate list) that must change in lockstep but are coupled only implicitly.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. Once the delegation reactivates, the delegate can call `borrow`/`withdraw` (or strategies such as `swap_collateral`) on the victim's account and direct proceeds to itself, up to the account's full collateral value minus solvency gates. The victim (the reacquiring owner) receives no warning: the grant was never revoked and no event fires on reactivation.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires three conditions, all reachable by unprivileged actors:

1. The victim previously granted a delegate (a normal, supported feature — up to 16 delegates per account [4](#0-3) ).
2. The NFT leaves and later returns to that same address — e.g., NFT sale and repurchase, lending of the NFT, transfer to a contract the victim controls and back, or use of the NFT as transferable collateral.
3. No intervening owner overwrote or purged that specific delegate slot — intermediate owners have no reason to purge foreign grants they may not even inspect.

None of these require privileged access, oracle manipulation, or leaked keys. The delegate simply waits for reacquisition and then acts. Severity is bounded by the account's remaining solvency limits, so Medium.

### Recommendation
Bind grants to an ownership epoch: store an `owner_epoch` (or the current owner plus a monotonically increasing transfer counter) in each grant, bump it in the controller on every observed ownership change, and treat grants as permanently invalid once the epoch differs — even if the original owner returns. Alternatively, have `position-nft` transfers call back into the controller to purge all delegates for that `token_id`, making the NFT-transfer → delegate-reset coupling explicit rather than implicit. Short of code changes, the risk should be gated at `transfer` time by emitting a controller event so off-chain tooling and intervening owners can react.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice owns account NFT `id = N`, supplying collateral and holding delegates list `[]`.
2. Alice grants Mallory as position manager via the delegate-grant endpoint (owner-authenticated). Grant stored as `{owner: Alice}`.
3. Alice transfers NFT `N` to Bob (`position-nft transfer`). The grant becomes inactive but persists in controller storage.
4. Bob later transfers NFT `N` back to Alice (sale, return of a loaned position, etc.). The grant's recorded owner `Alice` again matches `owner_of(N)` — Mallory's delegation silently reactivates.
5. Mallory calls `borrow(account_id = N, amount = max_available, recipient = Mallory)` and `withdraw` to an external address. Authorization resolves against `owner_of(N)` (Alice) plus the still-present grant, so the calls succeed and collateral is transferred to Mallory.
6. Alice's account is left with maximal debt and its collateral extractable only via liquidation — direct theft of the borrowed value.

Supporting behavior: "It is inactive under another owner but can reactivate if the NFT returns" [3](#0-2) ; delegates hold "broad economic control within the account's risk limits" [2](#0-1) ; NFT transfer is a permissionless owner action outside the flash guard [5](#0-4) .

Caveat: I confirmed the epoch-less grant design via `docs/reference/invariants.md`; the exact grant-storage fields in `contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs` were not read in this session, but the documented semantics are sufficient to establish the reactivation behavior.
