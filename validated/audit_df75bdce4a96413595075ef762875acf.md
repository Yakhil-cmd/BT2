### Title
Flashloan-based LP sandwiching lets an attacker capture disproportionate trading-fee revenue via same-transaction deposit/withdraw - (File: `programs/cp-swap/src/instructions/deposit.rs`, `programs/cp-swap/src/instructions/withdraw.rs`, `programs/cp-swap/src/instructions/swap_base_input.rs`)

### Summary
`deposit` and `withdraw` price LP tokens purely off the pool's *instantaneous* vault balances and `lp_supply`, with no minimum holding period, vesting, or per-block restriction. Because the LP-retained portion of every swap's trade fee is added directly into the vault balance that backs LP share value, an attacker who flash-loans a large deposit immediately before a fee-generating swap and withdraws immediately after (all in one transaction/block) captures a pro-rata share of that fee with essentially zero holding-time risk, at the expense of genuine liquidity providers.

### Finding Description
`deposit` computes the token amounts owed for `lp_token_amount` using `CurveCalculator::lp_tokens_to_trading_tokens` against the pool's *current* `vault_amount_without_fee` and `lp_supply`, then mints LP tokens for the caller: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

`withdraw` performs the symmetric operation, redeeming LP tokens for a pro-rata share of the *current* vault balances and burning them: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Neither instruction records a timestamp/epoch commitment for the depositor, checks a minimum holding duration, nor restricts withdrawal to a later slot/block — both simply operate against whatever the vault balances are at call time.

Meanwhile, `swap_base_input` computes the trade fee, splits out `protocol_fee`, `fund_fee`, and `creator_fee`, and adds the remaining `input_amount_less_fees` (which still contains the LP-retained fee portion) straight into the vault balance: [5](#0-4)  The protocol/fund/creator fee shares are tracked separately in `protocol_fees_token_0/1`, `fund_fees_token_0/1`, and `creator_fees_token_0/1` via `update_fees`, so they are excluded from `vault_amount_without_fee`, but the LP-fee remainder is not tracked separately — it stays commingled in the vault and immediately backs every LP token equally, regardless of how recently that LP token was minted: [6](#0-5) 

Because deposit/withdraw pricing is instantaneous and proportional, and the LP-fee portion of a swap is folded into the vault the moment the swap executes, an attacker can:
1. Flash-loan a large amount of token_0/token_1 and call `deposit`, acquiring a large fraction of `lp_supply` at the pre-swap price.
2. Let (or trigger) a swap execute in the same transaction/block, which adds LP-fee revenue into the vault.
3. Immediately call `withdraw` for all newly minted LP tokens, redeeming a pro-rata share of the vault that now includes a slice of the fee just generated — a slice sized according to the attacker's inflated, momentary share of `lp_supply`.
4. Repay the flash loan, keeping the extracted fee share as risk-free profit, which is fee revenue diverted away from LPs who held their position over time and bore the pool's price risk.

This is analytically the same bug class as the reported PoC: value that should accrue only to committed liquidity providers is instead captured by a flash-loan actor who deposits and withdraws atomically, because there is no delay/lock between `deposit` and `withdraw`.

### Impact Explanation
Genuine, long-term LPs receive a diluted share of trading fees whenever a flash-loan actor sandwiches a fee-generating swap with a deposit/withdraw pair, since the LP-fee portion is distributed pro-rata to whoever holds LP tokens at withdrawal time — including tokens minted and burned within the same transaction. This constitutes an insolvency/accounting-fairness impact on the fee ledger for genuine LPs (unbacked extraction of yield that should be theirs), reachable by any unprivileged user who can submit a single transaction combining `deposit`, a swap, and `withdraw` with attacker-chosen amounts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Low-to-Medium in practice: the attack requires (a) access to a large flash loan of the pool's underlying tokens, (b) a fee-generating swap occurring in the same transaction/block, and (c) sufficient swap volume/fee rate for the pro-rata capture to exceed flash-loan costs and transaction fees. Larger `trade_fee_rate` pools with high swap volume are more attractive targets, similarly to how the reported PoC scales with lower `_targetCoverageRatio`.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum holding delay (e.g., enforce that `deposit` and `withdraw` for the same `owner`/pool cannot occur within the same slot/transaction, or require a cooldown of N slots between the two), or alternatively account for LP-fee accrual with a time-weighted/checkpoint mechanism (e.g., track fee growth per LP share similar to Uniswap V3's `feeGrowthGlobal`) so that only LPs present during the fee-generating swap receive that fee, rather than whoever holds LP tokens at withdrawal time.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence within a single transaction (or same-block bundle):
1. Attacker flash-loans a large amount of `token_0`/`token_1`.
2. Attacker calls `deposit` with a large `lp_token_amount`, minting LP tokens against the current vault ratio: [2](#0-1) 
3. A swap (`swap_base_input`) executes, adding the LP-fee remainder into the vault balances: [7](#0-6) 
4. Attacker calls `withdraw` with the same `lp_token_amount`, redeeming a pro-rata share of the now-larger vault (including a slice of the just-added fee) and burning the LP tokens: [8](#0-7) 
5. Attacker repays the flash loan, retaining the captured fee share as profit, all within one transaction, with no delay mechanism in the code to prevent it.
