No vulnerability found for this question.

Rationale: The reported issue is specific to ZKPay's application-level query/fulfillment/cancellation contract logic (a user-initiated `cancelQuery()` racing against an oracle's `fulfillQuery()`). NEAR's transaction/receipt model has no analogous "submit a request, let a third party fulfill it, but the submitter can cancel to front-run the fulfillment and both get a refund and see the result" pattern. Transactions and receipts in nearcore are deterministically ordered and processed by the runtime and protocol itself [1](#0-0) , not by a merchant/oracle relationship, and there is no user-controlled cancellation path for a submitted transaction/receipt that would let its own submitter both reclaim funds and observe a result that a counterparty had already computed. The closest constructs found—`PrepareTransactionsJob::cancel()` [2](#0-1)  and `prepare_transactions_extra`'s `cancel` flag [3](#0-2) —are internal node-side async job cancellation mechanisms used by the block/chunk producer to abort an in-progress preparation task, not a user-facing capability, and are unrelated to the front-running/cancel-to-steal-result bug class described in the report.
