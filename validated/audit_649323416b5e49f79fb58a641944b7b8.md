### Title
Accrued market value overflows `i128` before the index ceiling engages, permanently freezing all repay/withdraw/liquidate paths — ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
The borrow and supply indexes are nominally bounded by `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` / `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` (10^36 raw RAY), but the accrual step computes the *unscaled* market value (`borrowed * borrow_index`, `supplied * supply_index`) with a panicking multiply **before** the index is clamped. On a large, highly utilized market, accrued debt value crosses the `i128::MAX` RAY-value domain while the borrow index is still far below its cap. From that point every verb on the market — repay, withdraw, liquidate, seize, clean_bad_debt — reverts with `MathOverflow`, because they all accrue first. Funds in that market are permanently frozen.

### Finding Description
In `common/src/rates/simulate.rs`, `accrue_step` unconditionally computes `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)` at the top of each chunk. `scaled_to_original` in `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14` is `scaled.mul(env, index)`, which panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` once the product leaves `i128`. The intended bound is the index ceiling in `update_borrow_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`) and `update_supply_index` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:29-45`), but those clamps run **after** the value multiply, so the representable-value limit is reached first. There is no pre-check that `borrowed * new_index` fits, no early clamp of `supplied`/`borrowed`, and no emergency path that skips accrual — `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`) runs at the head of every mutating pool call.

The repo's own harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-361`) demonstrates this end-to-end: an 18-decimals market supplied with ~1e9 whole tokens and borrowed at 98% utilization panics inside `scaled_to_original` after enough years, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` at failure, and subsequent `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` calls revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds for every supplier and borrower in the affected market, plus permanent freezing of unclaimed protocol revenue shares booked into `supplied`. Because accrual is a prefix of every operation, there is no reachable code path — including liquidation, bad-debt cleanup, or recapitalization — that can unwind the market once the value domain is exceeded. Note: this corner is explicitly acknowledged as an arithmetic limit in `docs/reference/formulas.md#numeric-limits` ("Value overflow can occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal") and ADR-0016; if the program treats documented limits as out of scope, this finding should be deprioritized accordingly.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses: `supply` and `borrow` admit amounts up to `max_cap_for_decimals` (~1.7e11 whole-token equivalents), so a whale or coalition can book a market near the domain ceiling and borrow it to high utilization on a steep curve segment. From there, time alone — chunked `accrue_step` calls triggered by any user calling `update_indexes` or any verb — drives the index up until the RAY-value product overflows. No governance action is required after the market is listed.

### Recommendation
Bound the computed value, not just the index. In `accrue_step`, use a saturating/headroom-aware multiply for `borrowed * new_borrow_index` and `supplied * new_supply_index` (or check `borrowed <= i128::MAX / index` before the multiply) and clamp the effective index to the largest value that keeps the unscaled market value inside `i128`, analogous to how `protocol_fee_shares` caps revenue shares at `i128::MAX - supplied` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:94-99`). Additionally, keep exit paths (repay, withdraw, seize) operable when accrual would overflow — e.g., accrue with a clamped index rather than reverting — so the ceiling is a real bound instead of a freeze point.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`:
1. List an 18-decimals market; lift supply/borrow caps via `lift_caps` to `max_cap_for_decimals(18)`.
2. `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1e9 * 10^18)`; `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 0.98 * principal)` against `COL` collateral.
3. Advance time year by year, calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` — it reverts with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
4. `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", ...)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW`: the market is bricked.
