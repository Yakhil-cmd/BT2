This confirms the vulnerability. `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses` in [1](#0-0)  aggregates the recipient addresses across the **entire batch** of `arrChains` into `assocOutputAddresses`, then passes the **whole unfiltered `arrChains` array** to `walletDefinedByKeys.forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfWallets` and `walletDefinedByAddresses.forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfAddresses` for delivery to devices that control **any one** of those addresses [2](#0-1) . Those downstream functions send `arrChains` unmodified to every matched device [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) , with no per-chain filtering by which address that specific device actually controls.

### Title
Private-payment-chain forwarding discloses unrelated recipients' confidential payment data to third-party wallet members - (File: wallet.js)

### Summary
When a device receives a batch of private-payment chains (`private_payments` message, possibly containing chains for several different final addresses in a single bundle), `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses` computes the union of all output addresses in the whole batch and forwards the **entire, unfiltered batch** to every device that is a member/cosigner of *any one* of those addresses.

### Finding Description
`handlePrivatePaymentChains` accepts `body.chains`, an array of private-element chains, from a peer device [5](#0-4) . After validation, if the message was not itself a forward, it calls `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses(arrChains, true)` [6](#0-5) .

That function walks every chain in `arrChains`, collects each chain's designated output address into one shared set `assocOutputAddresses`, and looks up which local wallets/shared addresses match *any* of those addresses via `readWalletsByAddresses` [1](#0-0) . It then forwards **the whole `arrChains` array**—not just the chain(s) belonging to the matched address—to:
- every device sharing a wallet with any matched address, via `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfWallets(arrChains, arrWallets, ...)` [3](#0-2) 
- every device sharing a multisig/shared address with any matched address, via `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfAddresses(arrChains, arrOutputAddresses, ...)` [4](#0-3) 

The same unfiltered-forwarding pattern exists in `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfSharedAddresses`, used when a payer's shared-address cosigners are notified of a completed payment, which bundles the payee's chain together with the payer's own change chain and hands the combined bundle to `forwardPrivateChainsToDevices` for every cosigner [7](#0-6)  and [8](#0-7) . This mirrors the underlying flaw class in GHSA-grwx-4p5v-9g2g: content intended for restricted access (private payment amounts, sender/change addresses, blinding factors, and the full spend-proof chain up to coin issuance) is bundled together and distributed as a unit without per-item access checks, so a party legitimately entitled to only one item in the bundle also receives the others.

Since any shared/multisig address can independently be a member of unrelated wallets on other devices (a device is often a cosigner on multiple, otherwise-unrelated shared addresses), a device that legitimately controls only one address referenced in the batch will still receive and can access the private financial details—amount, blinding, chain of custody—of chains destined for a completely different, unrelated address in the same batch.

### Impact Explanation
This exposes confidential information (payment amounts, addresses, blinding factors, and full spend-proof history back to issuance) of a private/indivisible asset transfer to device(s)/cosigners that are not a party to that specific output, purely because they happen to co-control a different address referenced in the same forwarded batch. This is an unauthorized disclosure of sensitive private-asset transaction data (CWE-200 analog), potentially revealing a counterparty's change amounts and wallet linkage to unrelated third parties, undermining the confidentiality guarantee of ocore's private asset payment feature.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering this requires that a device be a legitimate member/cosigner of at least one address referenced in a batch of private chains that also includes chains for unrelated addresses—a configuration that arises naturally whenever devices participate in multiple shared/multisig wallets, or whenever a payment produces a bundle mixing a payee chain with the payer's own change/cosigner chain forwarded onward. No malicious network behavior is required; the disclosure results purely from ordinary, expected private-asset payment and forwarding activity between legitimately paired devices/wallets.

### Recommendation
Filter chains per recipient before forwarding: for each target device/wallet/shared address, only include the chain(s) in `arrChains` whose designated output address (`objHeadPrivateElement.output.address` or the divisible-asset output matching that address) actually belongs to that specific wallet/shared address, instead of forwarding the full unfiltered `arrChains` array to every device matched from the aggregated address set in `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses`, `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfWallets`, `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfAddresses`, and `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfSharedAddresses`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Device D is a cosigner on shared address S1 (unrelated wallet A) and also a cosigner on shared address S2 (unrelated wallet B).
2. A sender pays a private indivisible asset amount to output address S1 while change returns to the sender's own address; the resulting `arrChainsOfCosignerPrivateElements` bundle contains both the S1 chain and the sender's change chain, sent to the sender's own cosigners.
3. Independently, S2 (wallet B, controlled in part by D) receives a `private_payments` message whose batch of chains happens to include a chain destined for S1 (e.g., because both chains were bundled together when relayed by an intermediate cosigner acting on `new_direct_private_chains`/`forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses`).
4. `forwardPrivateChainsToOtherMembersOfOutputAddresses` on that intermediate device unions S1 and S2 into one address set, finds D controls S2, and forwards the **entire** unfiltered batch (including the S1 chain) to D.
5. D, who has no legitimate interest in the S1 payment, now possesses the amount, blinding factor, and full spend-proof chain of the S1 output, disclosing confidential financial data belonging to unrelated parties.
