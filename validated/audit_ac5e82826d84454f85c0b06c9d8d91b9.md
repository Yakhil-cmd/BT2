### Title
Supply-index floor clamp leaves wiped-out suppliers a residual claim that drains fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache.rs / interest.rs)

### Summary
When bad-debt socialization would push a market's `supply_index` below `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (10^24), `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index *up* to the floor instead of zeroing supply claims. Scaled supply shares minted while the index was ~RAY therefore retain a residual claim of roughly 1/1000 of their original value. The pool's own test suite proves that once any fresh cash enters the emptied market, the stranded claim can be withdrawn through the normal `withdraw` path and pays out real tokens — extracting a later supplier's deposit and leaving the honest supplier unbacked. This is the same class as the GoonFi incident: a program logic/config edge (the floor clamp) that misprices share claims and lets an unprivileged account drain pool cash.

### Finding Description
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` is invoked on the permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path and on the liquidation/seizure bad-debt write-down path. Instead of resetting surviving scaled shares to zero when the write-down exceeds total supplied value, it sets `supply_index = SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. Because `unscale_supply_floor` computes `floor(scaled * index / RAY)`, every pre-wipeout scaled share still claims `scaled * 10^24 / 10^27` units — a phantom claim backed by nothing (`cash = 0` at wipeout). The only thing masking it is `require_reserves`, which merely checks the pool has cash; it does not check that claims are backed.

The production tests spell out the exact sequence:
- `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` (contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:373-428): wipeout clamps index to the floor, stranded claim > 0 while cash = 0; a fresh deposit of the same size is then fully drained by the old scaled shares via `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled)`, leaving `cash < fresh_claim`.
- `test_raw_cache_seizure_residual_would_drain_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (interest.rs:431-495): same outcome when the write-down arrives through the seizure/liquidation path — `total_owed > cash` immediately after the next deposit.

The reachable call chain is: unprivileged `clean_bad_debt` / `liquidate` (bad-debt legs) → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (index clamped to floor, shares never zeroed) → later `supply` by any victim → `withdraw(i128::MAX)` by the stranded holder pays gross > 0 out of the victim's cash.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent loss / theft of supplier funds: the first supplier(s) into a market that was previously written down to the index floor can have their deposits withdrawn by holders of wiped-out positions whose claims should be worthless. The books become insolvent the moment fresh cash arrives (`owed > cash`), and the stranded holder exits whole at the new depositor's expense. Maps to "theft of user funds / protocol insolvency."

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Triggering the floor requires cumulative bad-debt write-down approaching the market's total supplied value, which is a tail event (deep undercollateralized liquidations or repeated dust cleanups); `clean_bad_debt` is capped by the ~$5 `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` per account, so reaching the floor through dust cleanup alone is impractical on a large market. However, the liquidation seizure path writes down unbounded bad debt when collateral is exhausted, and no supply-side guard zeroes the residual. Once the condition exists, extraction is a single `withdraw` call by any unprivileged holder of pre-wipeout scaled shares — no timing race needed. The pool's own tests confirm the mechanism end-to-end at the cache layer.

### Recommendation
When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` hits `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, treat the market as wiped: either reset surviving scaled supply to zero (burn/rebase shares so stranded claims are exactly 0), or gate `supply`/`withdraw` on the floor state so no fresh cash can be credited against a floored index until the market is re-initialized. At minimum, make `resolve_withdrawal`/`require_reserves` account for total owed (`unscale_supply_floor(supplied)`) rather than raw cash when the index is at the floor.

### Proof of Concept
The in-repo tests already execute the full exploit sequence against production cache logic:

1. Market state: `supplied = S` (scaled), `supply_index = RAY`, `cash = 0`. A bad-debt write-down `> S` is applied via `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` sets `supply_index = SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (interest.rs:332-340, 388-396, 448-459).
2. Stranded claim: `unscale_supply_floor(S) > 0` while `cash == 0`.
3. Victim calls controller `supply` of `C = stranded` → `mint_supply` + `credit_cash(C)`; victim's claim equals `C` (interest.rs:403-412).
4. Stranded holder calls `withdraw(i128::MAX)` → `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = C`, `require_reserves` passes (cash ≥ C), tokens transfer out; `cash` drops to 0 < victim's claim `C` (interest.rs:414-427, 479-494).

Net effect: the wiped-out holder extracts exactly the fresh deposit; the honest supplier's claim is unbacked.

Note: I verified the mechanism through the pool cache tests; whether a higher-level caller adds a guard that fully blocks this sequence could not be confirmed within the available iterations, which is why the likelihood is rated Medium rather than High.
