### Title
`claim_revenue` drains market cash without honoring the liquidation buffer, so permissionless revenue claims can freeze withdrawals and borrows - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs)

### Summary
`revenue::accounting` pays out up to `min(cash, revenue_claim)` and enforces only `require_utilization_below_max` and `require_supply_for_debt`. It never calls `require_liquidation_buffer`, the guard every debt mint (`borrow`, strategy openings) must satisfy, which reserves `ceil(supplied × 200 bps)` of cash for seizure liquidity. An unprivileged caller invoking `claim_revenue` can therefore push the market's cash below — or arbitrarily close to zero relative to — the reserved amount that debt-mint and liquidity checks assume remains.

### Finding Description
The liquidation buffer is an invariant that every cash draw is supposed to preserve (`guards.rs::require_liquidation_buffer`, lines 39-47): `cash − draw ≥ ceil(floor_supply × LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS / BPS)`. `claim_revenue` is a cash draw — `burn_claimable_revenue` computes `amount = cash.min(treasury_actual)` (`cache/shares.rs:54-56`) and `accounting` then calls `cache.debit_cash(net_transfer)` (`ops/revenue.rs:42-46`) — but the two guards it applies (`require_utilization_below_max`, `require_supply_for_debt`) check share-value ratios, not the cash floor. Utilization is `debt_value / supply_value`; it does not constrain `cash` directly, so a post-claim market can pass both guards with `cash < reserved` or even `cash ≈ 0` while debt and supply remain large.

`claim_revenue` is reachable by any unprivileged address through the controller (`claim_revenue(caller, assets)` is listed with caller `None`, paying only the configured accumulator — the payout destination is fixed but the trigger is permissionless), so a single caller can submit the strongest path: `controller.claim_revenue` → `pool.claim_revenue` → `ops::revenue::accounting`.

The omitted reserve produces concrete downstream breakage rather than a cosmetic shortfall:

- Every subsequent `borrow` and strategy opening (`multiply`, `flash_position`, `swap_debt`, `create_strategy`) must satisfy `require_liquidation_buffer` and reverts `InsufficientLiquidity` until repayments refill cash. The market is unable to operate its core entry path.
- `withdraw` (non-liquidation) has no buffer check and `debit_cash` rejects insufficient reserves, so once claims have drained cash to near zero, supplier withdrawals revert — a temporary freezing of user funds until borrowers repay.
- Transfer-mode liquidation legs depend on the pool actually holding collateral/debt-side liquidity; the buffer exists precisely to keep that payout capacity, and the claim path consumes it.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds and a market unable to operate: suppliers cannot withdraw while cash is drained below their claims, and all new borrowing reverts until repayments restore the reserve. The protocol's own treasury payout is the instrument that causes it, so no privileged action or leaked key is needed — anyone can fire `claim_revenue` on a market with accrued revenue.

### Likelihood Explanation
Revenue accrues continuously (`accrue_revenue` mints on interest, flash, liquidation and strategy fees), so `treasury_actual` grows without bound relative to cash in thin markets. One permissionless call per market is sufficient whenever `treasury_actual` approaches available cash; no timing window, race, or counterparty cooperation is required.

### Recommendation
Apply the same reserve accounting to the revenue claim that debt mints use: either cap `burn_claimable_revenue`'s payout at `cash − ceil(floor_supply × LIQUIDATION_BUFFER_BPS / BPS)`, or call `require_liquidation_buffer(env, &cache, net_transfer)` inside `ops::revenue::accounting` after `debit_cash`. If leaving seizure liquidity below the buffer is ever intended, document it explicitly and bound the claim to keep post-claim `cash` sufficient for at least pending withdrawal demand.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market X has supplied S, borrowed B, and revenue shares worth R. Cash C satisfies `C ≥ reserved = ceil(S_floor × 2%)` from prior borrows.
2. Time passes; interest accrual grows R until `min(C, R) > C − reserved` (e.g., R ≥ C).
3. Any address calls `controller.claim_revenue([X])`. `burn_claimable_revenue` computes `amount = min(C, treasury_actual)`, `debit_cash` pays it to the owner/accumulator. Post-state `cash = C − amount < reserved`; both applied guards still pass.
4. Call `borrow` on X → `require_liquidation_buffer` reverts `InsufficientLiquidity` for any positive draw.
5. A supplier calls `withdraw` for an amount above remaining `cash` → `debit_cash` reverts. Withdrawals stay frozen until repayments restore cash above the requested amount plus nothing extra — the buffer guard then also blocks re-borrowing, prolonging the freeze.
