### Title
Accrual-time `scaled_to_original` overflow permanently freezes a saturated market before the borrow-index cap engages — (File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs)

### Summary
Analog of CVE-2019-6473 (a reachable assertion that kills the service): `accrue_step` unconditionally calls `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and `scaled_to_original(supplied, supply_index)`, both panicking `Ray::mul` multiplications. On a large, highly utilized market the product `scaled × index` can overflow `i128` while the borrow index is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, so the clamp in `update_borrow_index` never engages. Because `interest::global_sync` runs before every market mutation, the `MathOverflow` panic bricks every subsequent supply/withdraw/repay/liquidate/`clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` touch of that market.

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` computes utilization from unscaled values first:

- `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-61` — `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)` / `scaled_to_original(env, supplied, supply_index)`
- `common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16` — `scaled_to_original` is a plain `scaled.mul(env, index)`; `Ray::mul` panics on overflow (`MathOverflow`, error 33)
- `common/src/rates/index.rs` — `update_borrow_index` clamps at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, but the overflow happens *before* the clamp is reached, in the utilization read
- `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53` — `global_sync` runs `accrue_chunk` (which calls `accrue_step`) on every entrypoint before any mutation

The harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`) demonstrates the exact failure: a ~1e9·1e18-supplied market at ~98% utilization grows the borrow index past ~170× within a few years on the XLM-style steep curve segment; the next accrual panics inside `scaled_to_original` with `MathOverflow`, and the test confirms `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (the cap never engages) while `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail with `MathOverflow`. The test also flags that the documented bound in `docs/reference/formulas.md` is wrong — the claimed safety bound does not hold.

An unprivileged attacker only needs `controller.supply` and `controller.borrow` — both in the allowed set — plus time. Caps (`calculate_scaled_cap`) bound position size but are governance-set per market; on any market whose supply cap admits a book large enough that `scaled × index` can reach `i128::MAX` before the index ceiling (any cap ≥ ~i128::MAX/(170·RAY) scaled units), a whale or a patient attacker driving utilization to the steep end of the curve reaches the cliff.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds in the affected market: once the pre-clamp product overflows, every verb accrues first and reverts — suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and `recapitalize`/`clean_bad_debt` cannot operate on that market. No recovery path exists short of an upgrade. This is not a fail-closed input rejection; it is persistent state corruption of the market's arithmetic domain — the assertion fires on the *stored* state, not on the caller's arguments, identical in shape to Kea's stored-option assertion killing the daemon.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Requirements: (1) a market whose caps admit a book within ~170× of the `i128` RAY ceiling, (2) sustained high utilization so the borrow index compounds steeply, (3) years of elapsed accrual on default curves — or less on steeper configured curves. The attacker needs substantial capital but no privilege; alternatively the condition arises organically on a large successful market. The protocol's own test proves the cliff is reachable in ≤ a few years at 98% utilization on an existing curve shape.

### Recommendation
Compute utilization on saturating arithmetic (use `mul_div_floor_saturating`-style paths or cap `scaled × index` at a representable maximum), or clamp `borrow_index`/`supply_index` growth *before* unscaling, so that `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`/`MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` are true hard stops. Alternatively enforce supply caps so `scaled × MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` stays within `i128`, and correct the bound documented in `docs/reference/formulas.md`.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-360`: supply ~1e27 raw units of an 18-decimal asset, borrow ~98% of it, advance ledger time in 1-year steps; `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MathOverflow` (33) while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequently `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both fail identically. In production the trigger path is `controller.update_indexes(caller, [hub_asset])` → `pool.update_indexes` → `interest::global_sync` → `accrue_step` → `scaled_to_original`.
