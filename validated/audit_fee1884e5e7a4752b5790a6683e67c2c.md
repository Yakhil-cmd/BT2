### Title
RAY-scaled debt valuation overflow permanently freezes an overgrown market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs])

### Summary
A sufficiently large, heavily utilized market can grow its borrow index until the `borrowed * borrow_index` RAY valuation no longer fits in the protocol’s checked fixed-point domain. `accrue_step` calculates this value before the borrow-index ceiling is applied, and every pool mutation syncs interest first, so the first accrual that crosses the value ceiling reverts and leaves every later repayment, withdrawal, liquidation, and index update reverting on the same calculation. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` calls `scaled_to_original(env, borrowed, borrow_index)`, which executes `borrowed.mul(env, index)` before calculating the new capped index. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4)  Although the stored borrow index is capped at `1e36`, the product of the scaled debt and the current index can exceed the representable RAY-value domain before that cap is reached. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

The pool’s shared `synced_market` helper loads market state and calls `global_sync`; position legs call that helper before withdrawal, repayment, borrowing, supply, settlement, and other mutations. [8](#0-7)  `global_sync` then executes `accrue_chunk`, which reaches the overflowing valuation. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

An unprivileged address can create the state by using ordinary `supply` and `borrow`, then trigger it through permissionless `update_indexes`; the controller forwards that call to the owner-gated pool accrual entrypoint. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the market crosses the representable-value threshold, user funds in that market are permanently frozen under the deployed implementation: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot settle debt because each operation accrues first and hits `MathOverflow`. [3](#0-2) [13](#0-12)  The repository’s regression test demonstrates that the borrow-index cap does not engage before the valuation failure and that both withdrawal and repayment revert afterward. [13](#0-12) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: exploitation requires a market whose configured caps and available liquidity admit a very large scaled position and sustained high utilization, but it requires no privileged access once such a market exists. [14](#0-13)  The attacker can keep the market at high utilization by borrowing against their own collateral and then call `update_indexes`; if the market reaches the cliff naturally, any unprivileged caller can trigger the irreversible state. [11](#0-10) [15](#0-14) 

### Recommendation
Bound the RAY-valued market debt before multiplication, or make accrual detect an unrepresentable product and clamp progression at a safe index instead of reverting. At minimum, `accrue_step` should check `borrowed <= i128::MAX / borrow_index` before `scaled_to_original` and transition the market into a capped, withdrawable state rather than repeatedly attempting the same overflowing calculation. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Caps should also be enforced against the eventual representable market value, not merely the initial token amount, because the current admission bound permits positions whose accrued RAY value can later overflow. [16](#0-15) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Create or reuse two accounts controlled by the same caller: supply a very large amount of the target asset through `supply`, supply collateral to a second account, and borrow enough target asset through `borrow` to hold the market at high utilization. [17](#0-16) 
2. Allow accrual to increase the borrow index until `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` exceeds the representable fixed-point result. [18](#0-17) [2](#0-1) 
3. Call `update_indexes(caller, vec![target_hub_asset])`; pool accrual reverts with `MathOverflow`. [11](#0-10) [19](#0-18) 
4. Attempt `withdraw`, `repay`, or `liquidate`; each path first syncs the market and reverts on the same overflowing valuation, leaving supplier principal inaccessible. [3](#0-2) [20](#0-19)

    if !cache.needs_accrual() {
        return;
    }

    let mut remaining = cache.elapsed_ms();
    while let Some(nonzero) = NonZeroU64::new(remaining) {
        let chunk = nonzero.get().min(MAX_COMPOUND_DELTA_MS);
        accrue_chunk(env, cache, chunk);
        remaining = remaining.saturating_sub(chunk);
