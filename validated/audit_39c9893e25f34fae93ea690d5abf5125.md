## Title
Fundraising‑proxy AA lets shareholders drain the "refund" case for bytes that have already been forwarded, instead of receiving the team asset - ([File: test/samples/fundraising_proxy.oscript])

### Summary
The `fundraising_proxy.oscript` sample AA issues a transferable share asset 1:1 for contributed bytes while the fundraiser is `'open'`. Once the funding target is reached, the AA marks the campaign `'raised'` and forwards the entire pooled `total_raised` bytes to `$destination_aa`, expecting to later receive the team asset in return and hand it out to shareholders in exchange for their shares. However, the `cases` array is evaluated in order and the "refund" case is matched **before** the "pay the obtained team asset" case and does not check that the campaign is still `'open'`, so a shareholder can still redeem shares for base bytes 1:1 after the funds have already left the AA. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
The contract's cases are:
1. start
2. contribute (mints `$asset` shares 1:1 for bytes, and once `$bDone`, forwards `total_raised` bytes to `$destination_aa`, sets status `'raised'`)
3. "received team asset" (sets status `'done'` when the team asset arrives)
4. **refund** — `if: {$asset AND trigger.output[[asset=$asset]] > 0}` — pays back `trigger.output[[asset=$asset]]` bytes for any amount of `$asset` received, with **no check on the fundraiser status**
5. "pay the obtained team asset" — only reached if case 4 does not match first, gated on `var[$in_asset || '_status'] == 'done'` [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Because cases are checked sequentially and the first satisfied `if` wins, sending the fundraising share asset (`$asset`) back to the AA at any time — including after the target is reached (`'raised'`) or after the team asset has been received (`'done'`) — matches case 4 first, not case 5. The AA will pay out bytes 1:1 for the shares even though:
- the underlying bytes (`total_raised`) were already sent out to `$destination_aa` at the moment `$bDone` became true (see the `contribute` case), so the AA's actual byte balance no longer represents the shares outstanding, and
- `var['total_raised']` is decremented as if bytes were still held, silently desynchronizing the AA's internal accounting from its real balance.

This is directly analogous to the Astaria `WithdrawProxy` bug: a claim (shares) representing a future/expected pool of assets is redeemed using logic that assumes the underlying reserve is still present, when in fact it has already been moved elsewhere (to the destination AA / a later epoch), causing depletion of funds meant for other, legitimate claims and never entering the state that hands out the correct correspoding asset.

### Impact Explanation
Once the campaign is `'raised'` or `'done'`, any shareholder can bypass the intended "exchange shares for team asset" path and instead extract bytes 1:1 through the refund case, even though those bytes were already forwarded to `$destination_aa`. This:
- drains the AA's remaining byte balance (funds belonging to other, still-unredeemed shareholders or unrelated deposits) to pay users who should instead be entitled only to the team asset,
- can leave later, legitimate shareholders unable to redeem their shares for the team asset (case 5 becomes unreachable for anyone able to trigger case 4 first) or unable to get any payout at all once the AA's bytes are exhausted,
- corrupts `var['total_raised']` accounting relative to actual on-chain balance.
This is a direct AA fund-loss/fund-misallocation issue reachable by any unprivileged AA trigger sender holding the share asset.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. Any address holding the `$asset` share tokens (obtained legitimately during the `'open'` contribution phase) can trigger this at will by simply sending those tokens back to the AA after the status changes to `'raised'` or `'done'` — no special privileges, timing races, or cooperation from other parties are required.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit status guard to the "refund" case so it can only fire while the fundraiser is still `'open'` (e.g., `if: {$asset AND trigger.output[[asset=$asset]] > 0 AND var[$asset || '_status'] != 'raised' AND var[$asset || '_status'] != 'done'}`), and/or reorder the cases so the more specific "pay the obtained team asset"/status-aware handling is evaluated before the generic refund case.

### Proof of Concept
1. User A sends `1e5` bytes to the fundraising AA while `$asset` is unset → case 1 defines `$asset`.
2. User A (or others) contribute bytes; once cumulative contributions cross the 51% target, `$bDone` becomes true in the `contribute` case: the AA forwards `total_raised` bytes to `$destination_aa` and sets `var[$asset || '_status'] = 'raised'` (case 2). [5](#0-4) 
3. Before the destination AA responds with the team asset (i.e., status is still `'raised'`, not yet `'done'`), User A sends back the `$asset` shares they were minted in step 1.
4. The trigger matches case 4 ("refund") because its `if` only checks `$asset AND trigger.output[[asset=$asset]] > 0` — it does not exclude `'raised'`/`'done'` status. [3](#0-2) 
5. The AA pays out bytes 1:1 for the shares from whatever balance it has left (which should have all been forwarded to `$destination_aa`), either causing a bounce due to insufficient balance (denying other shareholders access altogether once the AA is out of funds) or draining funds intended for other contributors, while `var['total_raised']` is decremented to reflect bytes that are no longer actually being tracked correctly against the forwarded amount.
