### Title
Wrapped-BTC collateral (SolvBTC) is priced from the BTC quote via a fundamental-NAV factor floored at 1.0, so a market depeg cannot be reflected in its price - (File: `common/src/types/composable_oracle.rs`)

### Summary
The price-aggregator prices bridged/wrapped assets with a `Scaled` source that multiplies a RedStone **fundamental** factor feed by the underlying asset's `Ref` quote (e.g., `SolvBTC_FUNDAMENTAL × BTC`). The configured `min_factor_wad` equals exactly `1.0` WAD, so any factor reading below parity is rejected, and neither configured source is a market price. If the wrapped asset depegs from its underlying, the protocol keeps valuing collateral at (or above) the underlying's price, enabling over-collateralized-on-paper borrows that become real bad debt — the same bug class as pricing WBTC directly off BTC/USD.

### Finding Description
`ScaledSource` prices an asset as `factor_feed_price × resolve(quote)`, rejecting factor readings outside `[min_factor_wad, max_factor_wad]` [1](#0-0) . The engine resolves the nested `quote` key recursively, so a wrapped asset's entire USD value tracks the underlying's quote [2](#0-1) .

The deployed mainnet oracle for SolvBTC (`CAUP7NFA…O772J`) uses exactly this shape [3](#0-2) :

- Leg 1 (`Scaled`): `SolvBTC_FUNDAMENTAL` (a NAV/fundamental feed, `nature: "Fundamental"`) × `Ref("BTC")`, with `min_factor_wad = 1e18` and `max_factor_wad = 1.05e18`. Because the lower bound is exactly 1.0, the composed price can **never** go below 1× BTC even if the fundamental feed would report a discount — it is rejected instead.
- Leg 2 (`Feed`): `SolvBTC_FUNDAMENTAL/USD`, again `Fundamental` nature — the same NAV methodology, not a market price.

Both legs therefore derive from fundamental NAV rather than any market venue where a depeg would manifest, and the wide dual-leg tolerance (`upper_ratio_bps: 10500`, `lower_ratio_bps: 9524`) cannot detect a market-vs-NAV divergence since the two legs never disagree [4](#0-3) . The sanity band (`min_sanity_price_wad = 20000e18`) only bounds absolute price, not depeg from BTC. Other mainnet ops files configure additional assets with the same `Scaled` pattern, so this is a systemic configuration class, not a one-off.

### Impact Explanation
If SolvBTC (or another `Scaled`-priced wrapped asset) depegs from BTC — e.g., the issuer bridge/reserve is compromised, mirroring soBTC post-FTX — the aggregator continues reporting ~1× BTC (the factor floor at 1.0 WAD guarantees it). An unprivileged borrower can then `supply` depegged collateral and `borrow` real assets against a valuation the market no longer supports; the position is unprofitable to liquidate because the oracle overvalues the collateral, so the protocol accumulates bad debt that must later be absorbed via `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize`, i.e., protocol insolvency borne by suppliers.

### Likelihood Explanation
Wrapped-asset depegs are a documented recurring event (soBTC, WBTC premiums/discounts during market stress). The design guarantees the failure mode rather than merely permitting it: the factor feed is fundamental (NAV), which does not track secondary-market price, and `min_factor_wad = 1.0` explicitly forbids any sub-parity reading. Likelihood is bounded by the (low) probability of a SolvBTC reserve/bridge failure, consistent with a Medium severity as judged for the analogous WBTC case.

### Recommendation
Mirror the USSD remediation: add a market-based leg. Concretely, include a `PriceSource::Feed` (or `AquariusLp`-derived market observation) for the wrapped asset that reflects actual secondary-market pricing, tighten the dual-source `tolerance` so a market-vs-fundamental divergence halts fresh valuations/borrows, and reconsider flooring `min_factor_wad` at exactly 1.0 WAD so that a fundamental discount can propagate into the price.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires SolvBTC at a steep market discount after a depeg event.
2. Attacker calls controller `supply` with SolvBTC into a hub where it is accepted as collateral.
3. `borrow` resolves the collateral price: `Scaled` leg computes `SolvBTC_FUNDAMENTAL` (still ~1.0 NAV, and clamped at `min_factor_wad = 1e18` regardless) × `BTC` quote → ~full BTC value; leg 2 agrees, tolerance passes.
4. Attacker borrows stablecoins/XLM up to the cached LTV against the overvalued collateral and walks away.
5. Liquidators cannot profitably seize the depegged collateral at the oracle's inflated valuation; the position deteriorates into bad debt settled by suppliers through supply-index write-down.
