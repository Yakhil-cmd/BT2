### Title
Sustained high utilization drives a Ray-value i128 overflow panic inside `accrue_step`, permanently freezing the market — (`File: common/src/rates/index.rs`)

### Summary
The NimBLE report's bug class is a reachable assertion: a remote-triggerable code path hits an `assert`/`panic` on attacker-influenced state, yielding denial of service. In XOXNO Lending the same shape exists in interest accrual. `accrue_step` computes `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` inside `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:73-89`) and `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`). `Ray::mul` panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` when `borrowed * borrow_index / RAY` exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every monetary verb accrues first via `global_sync` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-33`), once the product crosses the i128 ceiling the market is permanently bricked — repay, withdraw, liquidate, borrow, supply and `update_indexes` all trap before touching state. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap never engages because the panic fires on `borrowed × index` well below the index cap.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` clamps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), but `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies `borrowed` (scaled debt) by the new index with a panicking `i128` multiply (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`). The widening path in `fp_core` only widens the intermediate `I256` product; the final result must still fit `i128`, and `to_i128()` / `checked_*` panic on overflow. With a large book (e.g., ~1e9 tokens at 18 decimals → scaled `borrowed ≈ 1e45` Ray) the Ray-value ceiling `borrowed × index / RAY < i128::MAX ≈ 1.7e38` is crossed once the index reaches roughly 170× — reachable in a few years on a steep curve at sustained ~98% utilization, per the repository's own pinned test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-362`), which asserts `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` and `last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. Any unprivileged caller triggers the panic via `update_indexes` (or any verb) once state crosses the threshold — a state an attacker can deliberately create by supplying and borrowing the market to maximum utilization.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once `borrowed × borrow_index` overflows `i128`, every entrypoint that runs `global_sync` reverts, so suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, and liquidators cannot liquidate — the entire market's funds are locked forever, matching the "permanent freezing of funds" acceptance criterion. It is the direct analog of NimBLE's reachable-assert DoS, but with persistence: the assert bricks protocol state rather than just the connection.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. It requires a whale-scale book (scaled debt near `1e45` Ray) and sustained high utilization on a steep configured curve over a multi-year horizon; governance-set rate params and supply/borrow caps bound but do not eliminate it (the test uses an in-range 175% max rate). An attacker with sufficient capital can push utilization to the steep segment and keep it there; once crossed, no governance action can unfreeze the market because accrual panics before any admin path completes. No privileged action or oracle manipulation is needed to trip the panic itself.

### Recommendation
Make the value-domain overflow non-fatal at the accrual boundary: in `calculate_supplier_rewards` (and `scaled_to_original`/`update_supply_index` call sites inside `accrue_step`), compute `borrowed × index` via `mul_div_floor_saturating` or an `I256` widening path that clamps the index/steps back instead of panicking — e.g., when `borrowed × new_borrow_index` does not fit `i128`, clamp `new_borrow_index` to the largest index for which the product fits (still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`) so the index cap semantics engage rather than trapping. Add a regression test asserting `update_indexes` succeeds (saturating) after the cliff instead of `MATH_OVERFLOW`.

### Proof of Concept
Pinned by the existing harness test (GH-06): supply `1e9` 18-decimal tokens, borrow at 98% utilization under the 175%-max-rate XLM curve, advance time in yearly chunks calling `update_indexes`; the call reverts with `GenericError::MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`/`calculate_supplier_rewards` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw`/`repay` revert identically — the market is permanently frozen. See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`, `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`, `common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-81`, `common/src/rates/index.rs:80-83`.

Caveat: the cliff is documented in `docs/reference/formulas.md` and pinned as a known bound, so it may be treated as a documented limitation rather than a novel finding; the analog is nonetheless the strongest reachable-assert → permanent-DoS shape in the in-scope code.
