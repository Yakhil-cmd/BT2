### Title
`repay`/`recapitalize` refunds are computed from the declared amount, not measured receipt, and are paid out of the pool's shared token custody without debiting the cash book - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The pool computes the "excess" to return to a payer from the caller-declared `amount` (`amount - debt` in `repay`, `amount - backing_shortfall` in `recapitalize`) and then executes `transfer_out` of that refund against the real token balance held in shared custody. The refund leg never measures what actually arrived and never debits `cash`, so a declared-but-unfunded amount pays real supplier money out of the shared balance while the `cash` book still reports it as present — the same bug class as checking `pool.amount` while funds actually live in a `shared_escrow`: the payout decision reads a field that does not reflect where the money is.

### Finding Description
In `ops::repay::accounting`, `resolve_repay` splits the declared `action.amount` into a net repay and an `overpayment`; `apply` then calls `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)`, which performs a raw `token::Client::transfer` from the pool address. Neither step measures the inbound receipt (no `token::balance` delta is taken in `repay`), and the refund branch does not call `debit_cash`, so `require_reserves` is bypassed entirely (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:25-67, contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs:34-53). [1](#0-0) 

The identical shape exists in `ops::recapitalize`: `applied = amount.min(backing_shortfall)`, `refund = amount - applied`, `credit_cash(applied)`, then `transfer_out(payer, refund)` — again paying the refund from custody with no receipt measurement and no cash debit (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:44-67). [2](#0-1) 

The repo's own test suite proves the concrete consequence: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` calls `repay` with a declared amount equal to the pool's entire custody and no debt; `current_debt_ceil` is zero so the full declared amount becomes "overpayment" and is transferred back out, draining the pool balance to zero while `cash` is untouched (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3611). `test_unfunded_recapitalize_leaves_the_cash_book_overstating_custody` shows the aftermath: the `cash` book still passes `require_reserves`, so a supplier withdraw clears the pool's own liquidity guard and then reverts inside the SAC transfer for want of custody — the market reports itself solvent and cannot pay (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3404-3477). [3](#0-2) 

This is the direct analog of the reported bug: just as `try_autoclose_pool` consulted `pool.amount` while the real funds sat in `shared_escrow`, the refund logic consults the declared `amount`/book fields while the real funds sit in the pool's shared token balance that backs all sibling (hub, token) markets and all supplier claims.

### Impact Explanation
If the refund is paid in excess of what was actually received, the shortfall is borne by the shared pool balance: supplier withdrawals revert inside the SAC `transfer` after passing `require_reserves`, producing a temporary-to-permanent freeze of supplier funds until the gap is recapitalized. Whether this is reachable with a declared amount greater than actual receipt depends on the caller path — pool entrypoints are `#[only_owner]` (controller), and the controller normally transfers the repay/recapitalize amount in first; any controller path (e.g., a routed `repay_debt_with_collateral` or batched repay) that forwards a declared `amount` not backed by an equal measured inbound transfer lets an unprivileged account trigger the drain. At minimum, the refund accounting is provably inconsistent with custody: `cash` overstates the balance after any refund that exceeds the real surplus received.

### Likelihood Explanation
The mechanism requires a repay/recapitalize call whose declared `amount` exceeds the tokens actually moved into the pool in that transaction — e.g., an over-declared repay on a zero- or low-debt market, or a recapitalize on a market whose `backing_shortfall` is smaller than declared, through any entrypoint that does not strictly bind the transfer to measured receipt. Such calls are reachable by unprivileged addresses via `controller::repay`/`recapitalize`. Note: if every controller path pulls exactly `amount` from the payer before invoking the pool, the pool's unmeasured refund is benign by construction rather than by internal check — but the pool contract itself contains no defense, and its own tests demonstrate the drain-and-stuck-exit sequence once the assumption is violated.

### Recommendation
Bind the refund to measured receipt: snapshot `token::Client::new(env, &asset_id).balance(pool)` before the mutation (or receive the measured inbound from the controller) and cap `overpayment`/`refund` at `min(computed_excess, actual_received_surplus)`, never at `min(computed_excess, declared_amount)`. Alternatively, route refunds through `debit_cash` so any refund exceeding the received surplus reverts via `require_reserves` instead of silently overdrawing shared custody, and assert `sum of cash across sibling markets <= token balance` after each op.

### Proof of Concept
```rust
// From contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs — demonstrates the drain.
let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);           // e.g. 10_000_000_000
// Market with zero outstanding debt; declared amount == whole custody.
let credited = t.client()
    .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
    .get_unchecked(0).actual_amount;
assert_eq!(credited, 0);                               // no debt retired
assert_eq!(token.balance(&t.pool), 0);                 // custody fully refunded out
assert_eq!(t.state_snapshot().cash, before.cash);      // book still claims funds present
// A later supplier withdraw passes require_reserves, then reverts in the SAC
// transfer with BalanceError(10) — funds frozen although the book is "solvent".
```
The same shape via `recapitalize`: declare `amount` far above `backing_shortfall(&cache)` (guards.rs:61-66); `refund = amount - applied` is transferred out while only `applied` is credited to `cash`, leaving `cash` overstating custody by the unfunded portion of `refund`.

/// The refund gap is not specific to `recapitalize`. Two pool legs refund an
/// excess derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book:
/// `ops::recapitalize::apply` (the excess over the shortfall) and
/// `ops::repay::apply` (the excess over the debt). Neither refund debits `cash`
/// or passes `require_reserves`.
///
/// A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an
/// overpayment: `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the
/// full-close branch and `net_repay` is zero. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`
/// assert in `ops::repay::accounting` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct,
/// and the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched.
#[test]
fn test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    let token = token::Client::new(&t.env, &t.asset);
    let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);

    let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);
    let before = t.state_snapshot();
    assert_eq!(
        before.cash, custody_before,
        "fixture guard: book and custody must start in sync"
    );
    assert_eq!(before.borrowed, 0, "fixture must carry no debt");

    // Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
    let credited = t
        .client()
        .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
        .get_unchecked(0)
        .actual_amount;
    assert_eq!(credited, 0, "no debt was retired, so nothing is credited");
    assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
}
