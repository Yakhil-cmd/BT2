### Title
Unmeasured overpayment refunds in pool `repay`/`recapitalize` drain real custody without debiting the cash book - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The codebase uses measured-receipt settlement everywhere else (`transfer_amount_measured`, `balance_delta_since`), but the pool's `repay` and `recapitalize` legs refund an "excess" computed from the *declared* `action.amount`/`amount`, not from tokens actually received. This mirrors the libsixel bug class: two cleanup strategies for the same object, where one path (`refund_controller_balance_delta` / measured pulls) respects the real balance and the other (`ops::repay::apply` / `ops::recapitalize::apply`) frees custody based on a trusted declared figure. An unfunded refund pays out of pre-existing pool custody while `cash` is left untouched, silently desynchronizing book and balance.

### Finding Description
`ops::repay::accounting` resolves the declared `action.amount` into `burned` debt shares plus `overpayment`, credits only `net_repay = amount - overpayment` to cash, and then `ops::repay::apply` unconditionally executes `cache.transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` with no check that `amount` was ever transferred in [1](#0-0) . `ops::recapitalize::accounting` has the identical shape: `applied = min(amount, backing_shortfall)`, `refund = amount - applied`, then `cache.transfer_out(&payer, refund)` [2](#0-1) .

Everywhere else the protocol deliberately measures receipts rather than trusting declared amounts: `transfer_amount_measured` is used for liquidation repayments, supply, debt, and strategy legs [3](#0-2) , and `refund_controller_balance_delta` refunds only the measured controller balance increase, never a declared figure [4](#0-3) . The pool refund legs are the inconsistent path: they neither measure the inbound transfer nor debit `cash` for the refund.

The existing test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` proves the exploit primitive directly on the pool client: a `repay` against a market with zero debt makes the entire declared amount an overpayment, `net_repay == 0` passes the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert on its zero disjunct, and the full declared amount is transferred out of real custody while `cash` still reports the funds as present [5](#0-4) . `assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody` confirms the pool balance goes to zero and the cash book is untouched [6](#0-5) .

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. Each unfunded refund transfers real tokens out of pool custody without reducing `cash`. `Cache::require_reserves` and all withdrawal/borrow accounting read the book, not the balance, so the pool reports itself solvent while custody is drained; subsequent legitimate withdrawals and borrows revert inside the SAC transfer, permanently freezing remaining supplier funds. The same primitive exists in both `repay` (any over-declared amount on a zero-dust-debt market) and `recapitalize` (amount exceeding the backing shortfall).

### Likelihood Explanation
The drain requires only submitting a `repay`/`recapitalize` call whose declared amount exceeds what is actually transferred in. The pool legs themselves contain no receipt measurement — the only thing standing between the refund and custody is the caller honoring the "controller transfers amount first" convention documented in the module headers [7](#0-6) . Any path that reaches `PoolAction::Repay` or `recapitalize` without a preceding full-amount transfer — including a direct call to the pool entrypoints as exercised in `flows.rs` — converts the entire declared amount into a custody payout. This is a single-call, unprivileged-shaped operation requiring no price manipulation, no timing, and no privileged role.

### Recommendation
Make the refund legs consistent with the rest of the codebase's measured-receipt strategy: snapshot the pool's token balance before the leg, and cap the refund at `min(declared_excess, measured_inbound_delta)`, or require the controller to pass only the net amount and move refund computation entirely to the caller. At minimum, debit the refund from `cash` (or run it through `require_reserves`) so the book can never overstate custody. Add an invariant check `token.balance(pool) >= cash` after `commit()`.

### Proof of Concept
Reproduced by the existing test in `contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3590`: with a pool holding `custody_before` tokens and `borrowed == 0`, call `repay(payer, action(amount = custody_before))`. `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `overpayment = custody_before`, `net_repay = 0` (assert passes), `transfer_out` pays the payer the entire custody, and `after.cash == before.cash` — the pool is empty while its books still show full reserves. A follow-on `withdraw` by any supplier then panics inside the token transfer despite `require_reserves` having approved it.

```rust
/// Accrues interest, resolves the repay amount into burned debt shares and
/// overpayment, burns the shares, and credits the net repay to cash without
/// transferring the overpayment refund. Panics if a positive net repay would
/// burn zero scaled shares.
pub(crate) fn accounting(env: &Env, action: &PoolAction) -> RepayOutcome {
    let (mut cache, position) = ops::load_leg(env, action);
    let amount = action.amount;

    let (burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position);
    let net_repay = amount
        .checked_sub(overpayment)
        .unwrap_or_else(|| panic_with_error!(env, GenericError::MathOverflow));
    assert_with_error!(
        env,
        net_repay == 0 || burned.raw() > 0,
        GenericError::RepayRoundsToZeroShares
    );
```

```rust
/// The refund gap is not specific to `recapitalize`. Two pool legs refund an
/// excess derived from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book:
/// `ops::recapitalize::apply` (the excess over the shortfall) and
/// `ops::repay::apply` (the excess over the debt). Neither refund debits `cash`
/// or passes `require_reserves`.
///
/// A repay against a market with no debt makes the entire declared amount an
/// overpayment: `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the
/// full-close branch and `net_repay` is zero. The `RepayRoundsToZeroShares`
/// assert in `ops::repay::accounting` passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct,
/// and the whole amount is refunded out of real custody with the book untouched.
#[test]
fn test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody() {
    let t = TestSetup::new();
    let token = token::Client::new(&t.env, &t.asset);
    let payer = Address::generate(&t.env);

    let custody_before = token.balance(&t.pool);
    let before = t.state_snapshot();
    assert_eq!(
        before.cash, custody_before,
        "fixture guard: book and custody must start in sync"
    );
    assert_eq!(before.borrowed, 0, "fixture must carry no debt");

    // Nothing transferred in, no debt to retire: the whole amount is "excess".
    let credited = t
        .client()
        .repay(&payer, &t.ract(0, custody_before))
        .get_unchecked(0)
        .actual_amount;
    assert_eq!(credited, 0, "no debt was retired, so nothing is credited");
    assert_unfunded_refund_drained_custody(&t, &payer, custody_before, &before);
}
```
