### Title
Pool flash loan lets any caller name a third-party `receiver` and drain its pre-approved allowance as fees - ([File: contracts/pool/src/ops/flash.rs])

### Summary
`flash_loan` is permissionless and takes an arbitrary `receiver` contract address. The pool pays principal to `receiver`, invokes its `execute_flash_loan` callback, then repossesses principal **plus fee** via `transfer_from` drawn against `receiver`'s standing token allowance to the pool. Nothing requires `receiver == initiator` or proves the receiver consented to this specific loan — its pre-existing `approve` is treated as consent, the same class as the WithdrawPeriphery report where a victim's approval is consumed by an unrelated caller.

### Finding Description
In `collect_repayment`, the pool only checks `asset.allowance(receiver, pool) >= total_repayment` and then pulls `amount + fee` out of `receiver` [1](#0-0) . The principal paid to `receiver` is `amount`, so the receiver nets `-fee` on a loan it never requested [2](#0-1) . `receiver` is a caller-supplied parameter: `apply(env, hub_asset, initiator, receiver, amount, data)` uses `initiator` only as a callback argument and `receiver` as the repayment source [3](#0-2) . The controller entrypoint forwards an attacker-chosen `receiver` [4](#0-3) . The only constraints on `receiver` are that it is a WASM contract [5](#0-4)  and that its `execute_flash_loan` callback does not revert [6](#0-5) .

Any flash-receiver contract that pre-approves the pool (the documented repayment flow — the pool repossesses funds via allowance rather than an inbound transfer) is a standing victim: an attacker repeatedly initiates flash loans naming it, the victim keeps no principal, and loses `fee` each call until its allowance or balance is exhausted.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: a contract's token balance is drained in `fee`-sized increments by an unprivileged attacker through the permissionless `flash_loan` entrypoint, without the contract's owner initiating anything.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a victim contract that (a) exposes a non-reverting `execute_flash_loan` function and (b) holds an outstanding allowance to the pool. Both are precisely the standard integration pattern for this pool's flash receivers, so any deployed receiver with a persistent or pre-staged allowance is exploitable on demand at attacker-chosen `amount`/`data`.

### Recommendation
Bind repayment consent to the initiator: e.g., require the callback to return a signed/explicit repayment acknowledgment, have the receiver `transfer` repayment itself during the callback rather than relying on standing `transfer_from` allowance, or restrict `receiver`/`allowance` spending to receivers opted into a per-loan nonce recorded by the pool. At minimum, document that any allowance granted to the pool can be consumed by third-party-initiated flash loans.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim flash-receiver contract `V` approves the pool for a large amount in preparation for its own flash loans (or retains residual allowance).
2. Attacker calls `controller.flash_loan(caller=attacker, asset=X, amount=A, receiver=V, data=…)`.
3. Pool transfers `A` of `X` to `V`, invokes `V.execute_flash_loan(initiator=attacker, …)`; `V`'s callback completes (it has no way to reject — reverting only aborts this attempt, and a permissive receiver executes normally).
4. Pool checks `allowance(V, pool) >= A + fee` and `transfer_from`s `A + fee` from `V` [1](#0-0) .
5. `V`'s net position: `+A − (A + fee) = −fee`. Repeat to drain `min(allowance, balance)` in fee increments.

Caveat: I could not fully verify whether in-scope flash-receiver contracts validate `initiator` inside `execute_flash_loan` or whether the allowance pattern is used transiently (approved and consumed within one tx). If all real receivers use just-in-time allowance inside their own initiated transaction, the exploitability window narrows to allowance races, but the missing `receiver`↔`initiator` binding in `collect_repayment` remains the concrete defect.
