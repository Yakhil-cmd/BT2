### Title
Borrower can permanently shield bad debt from liquidation and socialization by self-inflicting an Aquarius LP price outage on a dust collateral leg — (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary

Analog of CVE-2018-8409 (improper request handling → denial of service): an unprivileged borrower can make their own account permanently unliquidatable and un-cleanable by holding a dust supply position in an Aquarius LP market whose price feed they can force into an error state. `providers::aquarius::read` returns `OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` whenever the pool's total value falls below the configured `lp.min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) . Since the protocol's own threat model documents that a single missing price on any collateral leg blocks liquidation, permissionless `clean_bad_debt`, **and** owner-only `force_socialize_bad_debt`, a borrower who is also an LP in that pool can rug their own feed: withdraw enough of their own liquidity to push pool value under the floor, after which no valuation of the account can ever succeed [2](#0-1) .

### Finding Description

The kill chain stands entirely on in-scope mechanics:

1. **Supply requires no price.** The attacker creates an account, supplies a meaningful primary collateral (priced, healthy), then adds a *dust* supply leg of an Aquarius LP share token in a second `(hub_id, asset)` market. Supply itself does not need a valuation, so the leg is admitted freely. The attacker controls the account, so no third-party top-up restriction applies.

2. **Borrow to insolvency threshold.** The attacker borrows (`Controller::borrow`) against the good collateral and lets HF drift below 1 (or pushes it there via ordinary price movement / accrued interest).

3. **Self-inflicted feed outage.** The LP collateral corresponds to a shallow Aquarius pool in which the attacker is a liquidity provider. Via their own pool withdrawal (an "own trade on Aquarius" — explicitly in scope), they shrink reserves until `pool_value_wad < lp.min_pool_value_wad`. Every subsequent price read of that collateral errors with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` [3](#0-2) .

4. **Fail-closed valuation bricks every rescue path.** Risk totals iterate all supply legs through the Context-cached strict prices; one unpriceable leg makes the account unvaluable. `liquidate` reverts (planner cannot compute HF/seizure), `clean_bad_debt` reverts (`is_socializable_bad_debt` needs `total_collateral`), and even `force_socialize_bad_debt` — which drops the collateral cap but still values the account — reverts [4](#0-3) . Pro-rata seizure iterates `account.supply_positions` over every leg, so there is no path that prices only the healthy collateral [5](#0-4) .

Unlike a passive feed outage, the attacker causes and can indefinitely sustain the condition (re-withdraw whenever LPs refill the pool, since `min_pool_value_wad` is a fixed governance constant).

### Impact Explanation

Permanent freezing of funds / protocol insolvency: the account's debt can never be liquidated nor socialized while the outage holds. Interest keeps accruing on the unpayable debt; suppliers of the debt market bear the loss when the collateral floor path (index write-down via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`) is unreachable. If the attacker times the outage before HF < 1 becomes profitable to act on, the bad debt is locked in the books permanently. Cost to the attacker is one dust LP position plus capital temporarily parked in a shallow pool they already control — asymmetric against arbitrarily large borrowed exposure secured by the *other* collateral legs.

### Likelihood Explanation

- Fully unprivileged: `supply`, `borrow`, and Aquarius pool withdrawal are all permissionless.
- No reliance on oracle dishonesty, admin flags (`no_seize`, pause), or timing luck — the attacker deterministically triggers the error branch at line 120–122.
- Precondition: a listed collateral market priced by an Aquarius LP source with a `min_pool_value_wad` floor, and a pool shallow enough for one LP's withdrawal to cross it. Mainnet `configs/markets.json` configures multiple Aquarius LP sources with nonzero `min_pool_value_wad`, so the attack surface exists in the deployed configuration.
- The same mechanism generalizes: any borrower can pick *which* listed collateral's feed to break, making the shield robust even if one pool deepens.

### Recommendation

- Make per-leg price failure non-fatal to the aggregate paths: treat an unpriceable collateral leg as zero-value (or skip it) in `risk::totals` and the liquidation planner rather than aborting the whole valuation, and let seizure plan over the priceable legs only. A zero-valued dust leg contributes nothing to `total_collateral`, preserving `is_socializable_bad_debt` eligibility.
- Alternatively/additionally: enforce a minimum *value* (USD) per supply position at deposit time priced by a non-LP leg, so a dust leg cannot ride an outage; or cap the number of supply legs priced by pool-derived (manipulable-availability) sources per account.
- For `force_socialize_bad_debt`, allow an owner-specified collateral valuation override so governance retains a last-resort path when a feed is attacker-broken.

### Proof of Concept

```text
Setup (all permissionless):
  1. Attacker provides liquidity to shallow Aquarius pool P (share token LP_P),
     receiving LP shares.
  2. Attacker creates account A:
       controller.supply(USDC, 10_000)            // priced, liquid collateral
       controller.supply(LP_P, dust_amount)       // second supply leg
  3. controller.borrow(A, ETH, max) → HF ≈ 1.

Trigger:
  4. Price move / accrual pushes HF(A) < 1.
  5. Attacker calls Aquarius pool P.withdraw(their shares):
       reserves shrink → pool_value_wad < min_pool_value_wad.

Result:
  6. price_aggregator.read(LP_P) → Err(InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)
     (aquarius.rs:118-122).
  7. controller.liquidate(liquidator, A, ...)      → reverts (no valuation).
  8. controller.clean_bad_debt(A)                  → reverts (needs totals).
  9. controller.force_socialize_bad_debt(A)        → reverts (still values A).
 10. Attacker re-adds/removes liquidity at will to keep P below the floor.
     Debt and seized collateral remain permanently locked; suppliers absorb
     the accruing loss.
```

Supporting evidence: `providers::aquarius::read` hard-errors below `min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) ; threat-model DoS.1 confirms the dust-LP-leg shield blocks liquidation, cleanup, and force-socialization and names the LP-withdrawal trigger [4](#0-3) ; liquidation/seizure iterates every supply leg [5](#0-4) .
