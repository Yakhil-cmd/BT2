### Title
Accrued debt-value overflow permanently freezes a large market - ([File: common/src/rates/index.rs](common/src/rates/index.rs))

### Summary
Accrual multiplies the total scaled debt by the updated borrow index before that index is clamped, so a sufficiently large market can exceed the `i128` RAY-value domain even while the borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)  The failing multiplication occurs inside every elapsed-time accrual, and every pool mutation synchronizes interest before performing its requested operation. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3)  Once this boundary is crossed, permissionless `update_indexes`, borrower `repay`, supplier `withdraw`, and liquidation paths touching that market all revert with `MathOverflow`. [5](#0-4) 

### Finding Description
`accrue_step` first converts stored scaled `borrowed` and `supplied` balances to their index-adjusted RAY values and calculates utilization. [6](#0-5)  It then updates the borrow index and calls `calculate_supplier_rewards`, which multiplies the unchanged scaled debt by both the old and new borrow indexes. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Although `update_borrow_index` clamps the resulting index to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, no equivalent saturation or bounded-debt guard protects `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The project’s own boundary test demonstrates that an admitted billion-token market at sustained 98% utilization reaches the RAY-value ceiling before the index cap. [9](#0-8)  After `update_indexes` fails, both `withdraw` and `repay` fail for the same reason because `ops::load_leg` performs the mandatory accrual before either mutation can reduce the overflowing balance. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

### Impact Explanation
This is permanent freezing of market funds and protocol insolvency risk rather than an ordinary rejected transaction. [5](#0-4)  Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers or third parties cannot repay, liquidators cannot reduce the debt, and keeper accrual cannot advance the market timestamp. [3](#0-2) [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10)  Because the panic occurs before debt shares are burned or cash is credited, even an oversized repayment intended to close the position cannot rescue the market. [13](#0-12) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the condition requires a very large admissible market, high utilization, and enough elapsed accrual for debt value to exceed the `i128` RAY domain. [14](#0-13)  Those are substantial economic and temporal requirements, but all operations involved—supplying, borrowing, and later calling `update_indexes`—are reachable by unprivileged users under their existing account and cap gates. [15](#0-14) [16](#0-15)  The issue is therefore most credible for high-supply or high-decimal assets operating near their configured borrow caps at elevated utilization. [17](#0-16) 

### Recommendation
Make accrual fail safely before the unchecked value multiplication becomes necessary. [8](#0-7)  Specifically, calculate a market-level debt-value ceiling that guarantees `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` remains representable, reject new supply or borrow that could reach it, and add a recovery path that can reduce debt or supply using the last committed indexes without first performing a doomed full accrual. [13](#0-12) [11](#0-10)  A complementary mitigation is to compute interest with a saturating mul-div helper or reduce the borrow index before the value multiplication, while preserving the documented index ceiling semantics. [1](#0-0) [18](#0-17)

At the failure point, `update_indexes` returns contract error `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index` is still below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, proving that the overflow occurs in debt-value calculation rather than in the intended index ceiling. [20](#0-19)

    let (mut cache, position) = ops::load_leg(env, action);
    let amount = action.amount;

    let (burned, overpayment) = cache.resolve_repay(amount, position);
    let net_repay = amount
        .checked_sub(overpayment)

        GenericError::RepayRoundsToZeroShares
    );

    let position = position.checked_sub(env, burned);
    cache.burn_debt(burned);

    cache.credit_cash(net_repay);

| Bound | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Asset decimals 0..=18 | Exact token-to-RAY upscaling. Below 3: collateral only, no flash loans, no liquidation fee, its account's only supply position, at least 2 whole units while in debt |
| Both indexes initially RAY; ceiling 10^36 | 10^9 times initial index; protocol constants |
| Supply-index floor 10^24 | At most 1,000 times the shares minted at index one for the same deposit |
| Borrow APR maximum 2 RAY | 200% annual rate; not a bound on balance growth alone |
| Token-to-RAY input maximum `i128::MAX / 10^(27-d)` | About 170.14 billion whole tokens, before other limits |
| Deposit conversion at the supply-index floor | About 170.14 million whole tokens before scaled-share overflow |

The token-to-RAY maximum is also the admitted cap maximum. Accrued position
values and market totals must independently fit the RAY domain; valid caps and
bounded indexes do not guarantee that future accrual fits. Value overflow can
occur before the index ceiling and block repayment/withdrawal because those
operations accrue first. At the borrow-index ceiling, further accrual produces
no borrower interest. No dedicated ceiling alarm is emitted.
