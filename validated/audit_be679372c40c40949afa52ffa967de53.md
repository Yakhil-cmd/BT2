### Title
Accrual overflows i128 before the borrow-index cap, permanently freezing the market - (File: common/src/rates/index.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2023-30087 is a numeric-overrun → denial-of-service class bug. The analog in XOXNO Lending is the i128 overflow inside RAY-scaled index multiplication during interest accrual. `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` caps the index, but the accrual computes `borrowed * borrow_index` (and `supplied * supply_index`) in raw RAY terms *before* the cap can matter; that product overflows `i128` and panics with `MathOverflow`. Because every mutating entrypoint runs `interest::global_sync` first, once the market crosses this threshold all verbs revert forever — suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate. An adversarial flow test in the repository already documents exactly this end state.

### Finding Description
`update_borrow_index` multiplies `old_index` by the `interest_factor` and clamps the *index* to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` [1](#0-0) . The cap, however, does not bound the *value* terms the accrual multiplies next. `calculate_supplier_rewards` computes `borrowed.mul(env, new_borrow_index)` and `borrowed.mul(env, old_borrow_index)` where `borrowed` is a scaled RAY share count proportional to market size [2](#0-1) , and `update_supply_index` computes `supplied.mul(env, old_index)` [3](#0-2) . `Ray::mul` is checked arithmetic — the pool README states every market entrypoint panics with `MathOverflow` (33) on checked-arithmetic overflow [4](#0-3) .

With `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY = 10^36` [5](#0-4) , the product `scaled_shares × index` reaches `i128::MAX` (~1.7×10^38) for a market holding ~10^28 raw units (e.g., ~10^10 whole tokens at 18 decimals) well before the index approaches the cap. The utilization calculation in the cache multiplies `borrowed × borrow_index` and `supplied × supply_index` the same way [6](#0-5) , so even the rate-model read inside accrual traps.

Every market mutation runs `Cache::load → interest::global_sync → mutate → guards → commit` [7](#0-6) , so the overflow in accrual gates supply, withdraw, borrow, repay, seize/settle, and bad-debt cleanup alike. The in-repo adversarial test proves the exact scenario: a whale market at ~98% utilization on a steep rate curve hits the RAY-value ceiling (`MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`) while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which `withdraw` and `repay` both revert with `MathOverflow` [8](#0-7) .

The unprivileged path is: `controller.supply` a large position in a high-decimal market, `controller.borrow` to sustained high utilization (with `max_utilization` at or near `RAY`, a config the harness uses), then let `update_indexes` accrue — anyone can call `update_indexes` to advance the market into the frozen state, and no one can move it back.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds in the affected (hub, token) market book: all supplier principal and accrued yield become unreachable, borrower collateral can never be recovered or liquidated through that market, and protocol revenue shares in the book are unclaimable. Because the panic occurs inside the mandatory accrual preface, the condition is irreversible — there is no admin path that skips `global_sync`, and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` accrue first too. The freeze is permanent rather than budget- or revert-scoped, matching the accepted impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is constrained by capital requirements: pushing a market's scaled-share base high enough for `shares × index` to approach `i128::MAX` requires an extremely large principal (on the order of 10^28 raw units in the demonstrated configuration), and accrual must compound for many years at high utilization. The rate curve, `max_utilization`, and borrow caps are governance-set, so the window depends on a high-decimal market being listed without tight utilization/supply caps. No oracle manipulation, privileged call, or leaked key is needed — only `supply`, `borrow`, and permissionless `update_indexes`. Given the capital scale and multi-year compounding, this is a Medium-severity finding.

### Recommendation
Bound the *value* product, not only the index:
- In `update_supply_index` and `calculate_supplier_rewards` (common/src/rates/index.rs), compute `total_supplied_value`/`total_debt` via `mul_div_floor_saturating`-style saturating helpers, or short-circuit accrual when `borrowed`/`supplied` exceed a precomputed safe bound `i128::MAX / MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
- Alternatively clamp the index growth inside `global_sync` using the saturating path so the index cap engages before the raw product overflows, keeping exit paths (repay, withdraw, liquidation) live even after the cap binds.
- Add a listing-time or governance-time check that rejects a configuration where `max_utilization ≈ RAY` combined with `asset_decimals` and cap sizes that permit the unsafe product domain.

### Proof of Concept
The repository's own adversarial test is the executable PoC: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs::a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` supplies `10^9 × 10^18` raw units of an 18-decimal asset, borrows 98% of it, advances time in yearly chunks until `update_indexes` fails with `MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`, then asserts `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` and that both `withdraw` and `repay` revert with `MathOverflow` — i.e., the market is permanently frozen [9](#0-8) . Call sequence for an unprivileged actor:

1. `controller.supply(hub, whale_2, BIG18, huge_amount)` — seeds the market.
2. `controller.supply(hub, attacker, COL, collateral)` then `controller.borrow(hub, attacker, BIG18, ~98% of supply)` — pins utilization on the steep curve segment.
3. Anyone calls `controller.update_indexes` periodically; each call grows `borrow_index` via `interest::global_sync`.
4. Once `borrowed_scaled × borrow_index ≈ i128::MAX`, the next `update_indexes` panics with `MathOverflow` (`Error(Contract, #33)`); thereafter `withdraw`, `repay`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, and `recapitalize` all revert in the accrual preface — funds are permanently frozen while the index cap was never reached.

```rust
/// `i128::MAX`, 170 times that. At the XLM curve's steep segment the index
/// grows past 170x in a few years, and the next accrual panics inside
/// `scaled_to_original`. Every verb accrues first, so the market freezes:
/// no repay, no withdraw, no liquidation. The index cap never engages.
#[test]
fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    std::println!(
        "ray-value cliff reached after {years} years at 98 percent utilization on the XLM curve; last index x{:.1}",
        last.borrow_index as f64 / RAY as f64
    );
```
