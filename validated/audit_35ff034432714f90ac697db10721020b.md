### Title
Unchecked RAY-denominated accrual permanently freezes oversized high-utilization markets - (File: `common/src/rates/index.rs`)

### Summary
The interest-accrual path can compute a total supplied or borrowed value that exceeds `i128` before the borrow or supply index reaches its configured cap. Because `Controller::update_indexes` is permissionlessly reachable and every monetary market operation accrues first, crossing this numeric boundary makes subsequent repayments, withdrawals, liquidations, and further index updates fail with `MathOverflow`, permanently freezing the affected market absent contract intervention.

### Finding Description
`global_sync` divides elapsed time into bounded accrual chunks and calls `accrue_step` for each chunk. [1](#0-0) 

Inside accrual, `calculate_supplier_rewards` multiplies total scaled debt by both the old and new borrow indexes. [2](#0-1) 

Likewise, `update_supply_index` multiplies total scaled supply by the old supply index before applying its cap. [3](#0-2) 

Those products are RAY-denominated asset values rather than indexes; therefore, a market can remain below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` or `MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` while `scaled_amount * index` no longer fits in `i128`. [4](#0-3) 

`scaled_to_original` performs the unchecked semantic product through `Ray::mul`, and unrepresentable multiply-divide results raise `MathOverflow`. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

The regression test demonstrates this cliff: after a billion 18-decimal tokens are supplied and 98% is borrowed under sustained high-utilization conditions, `update_indexes` fails with `MathOverflow` while the stored borrow index is still below its cap. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Once the boundary is crossed, the market cannot repay debt or withdraw supplied funds because those operations accrue the market before settling the requested action. [8](#0-7) 

The same arithmetic is shared by liquidation-relevant market reads and index updates, so suppliers can be left with permanently frozen claims and borrowers with permanently frozen obligations. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

This meets the permanent-freezing-of-funds impact class rather than fail-closed DoS: the arithmetic domain accepted by the market does not provide a recovery path that can unwind the state once total scaled value outgrows `i128`. [2](#0-1) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged caller can trigger the failing transition through `Controller::update_indexes`, which forwards the selected markets to the owner-restricted pool accrual. [9](#0-8) [11](#0-10) 

The precondition is a very large live market whose RAY-denominated total value approaches `i128::MAX`, combined with enough debt and time for the indexes to grow; the demonstrated scenario uses a billion 18-decimal supplied tokens at 98% utilization. [12](#0-11) 

Likelihood is therefore conditional on a listed market permitting that scale and on token supply or attacker-controlled collateral being sufficient to build it, but the triggering call itself needs no privilege and the resulting state cannot be repaired by ordinary user operations. [4](#0-3) [13](#0-12) 

### Recommendation
Bound market state by the maximum representable RAY-denominated total value before accrual, not merely by the index caps.

Specifically:

1. Compute the largest scaled supply and debt amounts for which `scaled_amount * MAX_SUPPLY_INDEX_RAY` and `scaled_amount * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` remain representable.
2. Enforce those scaled-position ceilings in `supply`, `borrow`, strategy debt creation, liquidation credits, and any other position-growing path.
3. In accrual, calculate `old_total_debt`, `new_total_debt`, and `total_supplied_value` with a saturating or widened representation and clamp the resulting accrual rather than trapping.
4. Add an invariant test asserting that every listed market remains operable from the highest admitted scaled position through `MAX_*_INDEX_RAY`.

The key invariant should be `scaled_amount <= i128::MAX / index` for every index the protocol permits, rather than `index <= MAX_INDEX` alone.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure or select an 18-decimal market whose caps admit a billion whole tokens, alongside sufficient collateral market capacity.
2. An ordinary supplier calls `supply` with `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` base units of the market asset.
3. An ordinary borrower supplies enough collateral and calls `borrow` for 98% of that market’s liquidity.
4. Let the market accrue under high utilization until `borrowed * borrow_index` approaches the `i128` boundary.
5. Any address calls `update_indexes(caller, vec![hub_asset])`; accrual reaches `calculate_supplier_rewards`, whose `borrowed.mul(new_borrow_index)` computation overflows.
6. The transaction fails with `MathOverflow` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`.
7. Subsequent `withdraw`, `repay`, liquidation, and further `update_indexes` calls hit accrual first and fail identically, leaving user funds frozen.

The in-tree reproduction performs these steps and observes `MathOverflow` from `update_indexes`, `withdraw`, and `repay`. [14](#0-13)

fn a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap() {
    let mut t = LendingTest::new()
        .with_market(big("BIG18", 18, xlm_curve()))
        .with_market(col())
        .with_max_utilization_disabled_all_markets()
        .build();
    lift_caps(&t, "BIG18", 18);
    lift_caps(&t, "COL", 7);
    let principal = BILLION * 10i128.pow(18);
    t.supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", principal);
    let debt = principal / 100 * 98;
    t.supply_raw(ALICE, "COL", BILLION * 10_000_000 * 3);
    t.borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", debt);

    let mut years = 0u32;
    let failure = loop {
        years += 1;
        assert!(
            years <= 40,
            "no cliff within 40 years; the bound in docs/reference/formulas.md is wrong"
        );
        t.advance_time(YEAR_SECS);
        if let Err(e) = t.try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"]) {
            break e;
        }
    };
    let failed: Result<(), soroban_sdk::Error> = Err(failure);
    assert_contract_error(failed, errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    let last = book(&t, "BIG18");
    assert!(
        last.borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY,
        "the index cap did not engage before the value overflow"
    );
    // The market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic.
    assert_contract_error(t.try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    assert_contract_error(t.try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0), errors::MATH_OVERFLOW);
    std::println!(
