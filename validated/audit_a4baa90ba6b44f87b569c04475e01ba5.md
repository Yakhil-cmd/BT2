### Title
Ordinary withdrawals permanently revert once utilization drifts above `max_utilization`, freezing all supplier funds - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to the Derby issue — where `redeemRewardsGame()` was gated by `onlyWhenVaultIsOn` and reverted for a benign state unrelated to the user's redemption — XOXNO Lending's ordinary `withdraw` leg enforces `require_utilization_below_max` on the post-withdrawal market state (`gate_and_debit`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`, calling `guards::require_utilization_below_max` at `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:19-34`). Only liquidation withdrawals and footprint-only closes skip the check (`is_liquidation || empty_close` at `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:78-79`).

### Finding Description
Utilization is computed as `ceil(debt value) / floor(supply value)` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:24-31`). Because the borrow index accrues interest continuously, `borrowed.div_ceil(supplied)` grows over time even with zero user activity. Once it crosses `params.max_utilization`:

- Every withdrawal burns supply shares first (`burn_position`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:67`), which only *raises* utilization, so no ordinary `withdraw` — partial or full — can ever satisfy the gate afterward. The `assert_with_error!` reverts, rolling back the burn and the cash transfer.
- `borrow` cannot lower utilization (it adds debt), and is itself gated by the same check in `contracts/pool/src/ops/borrow.rs`.
- The only ways out are `repay` or liquidation (`is_liquidation` skips the check), which depend on borrowers/liquidators, not on the suppliers whose funds are locked.

A single unprivileged user can also *trigger* the cliff directly: supply a large amount, borrow up to (but under) `max_utilization`, then withdraw a small slice of a *second* account's collateral in the same market — or simply wait for interest accrual — pushing utilization above the cap. From that moment, every supplier's `withdraw(account_id, [(hub_asset, amount)])` reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax`, regardless of how much cash sits in the pool (`require_reserves` at `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:112` passes independently of the utilization gate).

The same over-broad gate also freezes `claim_revenue`: `ops::revenue::accounting` runs `require_utilization_below_max` and `require_supply_for_debt` unconditionally, even when nothing is claimable (`contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs:39-55`), so accrued protocol revenue cannot be claimed while utilization is above the cap.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of user funds: all suppliers of the affected hub asset are unable to withdraw any of their collateral — including suppliers with no debt whose withdrawals are pure exits — for as long as utilization remains above `max_utilization`. If borrowers are insolvent or inactive, the freeze persists until liquidation or `clean_bad_debt` reduces debt, which can be arbitrarily long. Unclaimed protocol revenue is simultaneously frozen via the same guard in `claim_revenue`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Interest accrual alone moves utilization upward monotonically between repays, so any market parameterized near its cap will cross it with no adversary required. An adversary can accelerate this by opening a maxed-out borrow and letting the index drift, or by supplying-then-borrowing to pin utilization just above the threshold. No privileged action is needed; `supply`, `borrow`, and `withdraw` are all unprivileged entrypoints.

### Recommendation
Apply the utilization gate only when the operation worsens it, or skip it for withdrawals of debt-free positions — mirroring how `is_liquidation` already skips it:

```rust
// contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs, gate_and_debit
if !skip_utilization_check {
    // Only gate if the account still carries debt, or compare
    // pre/post utilization and allow strictly-improving exits.
    guards::require_utilization_below_max(env, cache);
}
```

A simpler fix: check utilization against the *pre-withdrawal* state, or require `post <= max(pre, max_utilization)` so exits that reduce absolute supply but were already above the cap are not blocked.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier S calls `supply` with 1000 USDC; borrower B supplies collateral and `borrow`s 900 USDC on a market with `max_utilization = 0.95 RAY`.
2. Time passes; `borrow_index` accrues so `ceil(borrowed)/floor(supplied)` becomes `0.951 > 0.95` — or B borrows slightly more before the check would trip.
3. S calls `withdraw(account_id, [(USDC, 100_000_000)])`. `burn_position` reduces `supplied`, `require_utilization_below_max` computes utilization ≈ 0.96 and reverts with `UtilizationAboveMax` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:29-33`).
4. Every subsequent `withdraw` by any supplier reverts identically, even though the pool holds 100 USDC cash and `require_reserves` would pass. Only a `repay` or a liquidation withdrawal (which bypasses the gate) unlocks the market.
