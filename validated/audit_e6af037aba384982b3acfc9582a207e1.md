### Title
Attacker-inflated AA asset balance can push aggregate balance to `MAX_BALANCE`, causing all future legitimate deposit triggers to that AA to bounce - (File: aa_composer.js)

### Summary
`aa_composer.js` maintains each AA's per-asset balance in `aa_balances`, and every time a payment reaches the AA (a "trigger"), the incoming amounts are added to this balance in `updateInitialAABalances()`. If the resulting balance for any asset exceeds `MAX_BALANCE = 2**63 - 1 - constants.MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT * constants.MAX_CAP`, the trigger is bounced with `"balance overflow"`. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Because anyone can send an unlimited number of payments (each up to `constants.MAX_CAP`, and for an uncapped custom asset an attacker can also freely issue and forward more of that asset) to a target AA's address, an attacker can keep pushing the AA's tracked balance of a chosen asset arbitrarily close to `MAX_BALANCE`. Once the balance is close enough to the ceiling, any subsequent legitimate deposit (a normal user's trigger sending that asset to the AA) will push the balance past `MAX_BALANCE`, causing `updateInitialAABalances()` to report `"balance overflow"`, which forces the whole trigger to bounce. [4](#0-3) 

This mirrors the report's bug class: an unprivileged actor can manipulate a value that a downstream operation's correctness check depends on (there, `DestinationVault` balance vs. cached balance; here, `aa_balances[asset]` vs. `MAX_BALANCE`), causing an otherwise-valid, legitimate operation from a different user to revert/bounce.

### Finding Description
`updateInitialAABalances()` unconditionally adds every incoming payment's amount for the AA's address to the internally tracked `aa_balances` row for that asset, and flags overflow whenever the running total exceeds `MAX_BALANCE`: [5](#0-4) 

This balance tracking happens for *every* unit that pays the AA, regardless of who sent it or whether the AA's business logic ever intended to accumulate that much of the asset. There is no check that limits how much of an asset a single external, unprivileged sender may deposit into an AA over time (aside from per-output/per-unit `MAX_CAP`, which does not bound the aggregate over many units). [6](#0-5) 

For a custom (attacker-controlled or arbitrary uncapped) asset, or even for the base asset over a very long time horizon, a determined attacker can post many units transferring that asset to the AA's address, repeatedly growing `aa_balances[asset]` toward the `MAX_BALANCE` ceiling. Because the overflow check treats the *sum of all incoming payments in the trigger being processed*, once the stored balance is close to the ceiling, the next legitimate user's deposit trigger (adding even a small amount) will overflow and get bounced: [4](#0-3) 

Since `bounce()` refunds the trigger sender (assuming bounce fees are covered) rather than crediting the AA, the effect is that the targeted AA becomes permanently unusable for that asset — any future user attempting to deposit that asset into the AA has their trigger unconditionally bounced, defeating the AA's business logic (e.g. AMM deposits, payment-channel refills, market maker investments as seen in `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`) for all subsequent users, not just the attacker.

### Impact Explanation
This is an AA-level denial-of-service / fund-freezing bug: once triggered, no user (not just the attacker) can successfully deposit the affected asset into the AA anymore, because every trigger that adds to that asset's balance overflows and bounces. For AAs whose entire value proposition is accepting deposits of a particular asset (AMMs, funds, payment channels), this permanently breaks the contract's primary function, effectively freezing the AA's business logic and any capital already inside it for further asset-specific interactions. This matches the "AA fund loss or freezing" acceptance criterion.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any address can post a payment to any AA address at any time — this requires no special privilege, only being able to hold or issue the target asset and paying the fees for however many units are needed to accumulate the balance close to `MAX_BALANCE`. For assets with a cap close to `constants.MAX_CAP`, or uncapped uncapped-supply assets that the attacker fully controls (issued_by_definer_only assets they define themselves, or any freely issuable asset), the attacker can issue and resend to the target AA repeatedly. The cost is proportional to fees for enough units to reach near the ceiling, which for a custom asset with a large single issue amount could require very few units. The exact economics depend on `constants.MAX_CAP` and `constants.MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT` (I was unable to confirm their exact numeric values within the available context, so I cannot give a precise unit count/cost estimate).

### Recommendation
- Consider bounding per-asset balance growth in `aa_balances` on a more granular, sender-independent basis, or reject/ignore excess amounts beyond a safe threshold instead of bouncing the entire (unrelated) legitimate trigger.
- Alternatively, raise `MAX_BALANCE`'s safety margin, or make the overflow check informational to the specific asset flow so that unrelated legitimate deposits are not penalized by unrelated prior inflation.
- Add monitoring/alerting so that an AA approaching `MAX_BALANCE` for any asset can be flagged before it becomes unusable.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker defines (or picks an existing) asset `X` that is either uncapped or has a very large cap, that they can issue freely (`issued_by_definer_only` with attacker as definer, or any transferable asset they can otherwise acquire in bulk).
2. Attacker repeatedly issues/acquires large amounts of asset `X` and sends payments of `X` to the target AA address across multiple units. Each payment increases `aa_balances[AA_address][X]` via `updateInitialAABalances()`. [3](#0-2) 
3. Attacker continues until `aa_balances[AA_address][X]` is just below `MAX_BALANCE = 2**63 - 1 - constants.MAX_MESSAGES_PER_UNIT * constants.MAX_CAP`. [7](#0-6) 
4. A legitimate user sends a normal deposit of asset `X` to the AA (e.g., to invest in an AMM as in `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`'s "invest in MM" case). [8](#0-7) 
5. `updateInitialAABalances()` computes the new balance, which now exceeds `MAX_BALANCE`, setting `bOverflow = true` and returning `"balance overflow"`. [9](#0-8) 
6. `handleTrigger()` calls `bounce("balance overflow")` for this legitimate user's trigger, rejecting their deposit. [4](#0-3) 
7. The AA is now permanently unable to accept further deposits of asset `X` from any user, since the balance can never be reduced back below the threshold through normal deposit flows.
