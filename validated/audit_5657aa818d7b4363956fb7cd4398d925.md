No vulnerability found for this question.

**Rationale:** The described behavior is exactly the intended function of `StakeAuthorize::Staker`, not a bypass of it. Solana's stake authorization model deliberately separates staker and withdrawer authority so a `staker` key can redelegate to *any* vote account without being able to move principal — that's the entire design purpose of the split. `stake_instruction::authorize`/`authorize_checked` only ever gate the `staker` field for delegation operations and the `withdrawer` field for withdrawal operations, matching the `Authorized` struct's separation, as seen consistently in usages like [1](#0-0)  and CLI authority checks like [2](#0-1) , where `StakeAuthorize::Staker` may be exercised by either the staker or withdrawer, but `StakeAuthorize::Withdrawer` requires the withdrawer alone.

Reward commission for an epoch is paid to whatever vote account the stake is *currently* delegated to at reward-distribution time — this is intrinsic to how inflation/block rewards work, not a consequence of any authority-separation flaw. The reward path in [3](#0-2)  reads `vote_pubkey` straight from the stake's `Delegation`, and commission is attributed to that vote account's collector via `commission_pubkey` [4](#0-3) . Since the staker authority is the party authorized to choose the delegation target (that is its documented privilege), directing commission to the chosen vote account is expected behavior, not diversion of victim funds.

Critically, none of this touches stake *principal* lamports: withdrawal still requires the `withdrawer` signer exclusively, enforced independently of the staker/delegation logic. The scenario describes a known trust assumption of shared/custodial stake setups (granting staker-only authority implies trusting that party to choose delegation targets and thus commission recipients) — it is not a security bug, arithmetic flaw, or missing signer/authority check in the stake program.
