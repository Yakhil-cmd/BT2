The reported issue is specific to an ERC20-style `transferFrom`/allowance pattern, where an approved spending cap is exceeded because a burn fee is subtracted from the transfer amount *after* the approval was fixed, causing the approver to lose more than they authorized. ocore is a UTXO/DAG-based ledger with no allowance/approve concept — payments are fully specified (inputs, outputs, and all fees) and signed atomically in a single unit, so the "approve now, fee computed later" ordering flaw cannot occur:

- Base-asset payments require `total_input === total_output + headers_commission + payload_commission + oversize_fee + tps_fee + burn_fee + vote_count_fee`, enforced entirely within one validated, atomically-signed unit, not across a two-step approve/spend flow. [1](#0-0) 
- Custom-asset transfers similarly require `total_input === total_output` and separately evaluate `issue_condition`/`transfer_condition`, with no fee-splitting/burn-on-transfer mechanic layered on top of a pre-committed amount. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 
- AA bounce/fee logic (`bounce_fees`) subtracts fees from the AA's own received trigger output, computed and applied within a single deterministic execution, not against a separately pre-approved cap.
