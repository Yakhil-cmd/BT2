### Title
Permanent market freeze: accrual panics on `i128` debt-value overflow before the borrow-index cap is reached - (File: common/src/rates/scaling.rs)

### Summary
Analog class: remotely triggered denial of service that hangs/crashes core functionality (CVE-2024-21061 is a repeatable MySQL crash DoS). In XOXNO Lending, every state-changing pool path runs interest accrual first. Accrual computes `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` as a RAY×RAY product in `i128`. On a whale-scale market at sustained high utilization, the compounded index grows the total-debt RAY value past `i128::MAX` while the borrow index is still far below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. From that point every verb that accrues — `update_indexes`, `borrow`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize`, flash paths — panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, permanently freezing the market and all funds in it. The index cap in `update_borrow_index` never engages because it clamps the index, not the value product.

### Finding Description
`scaled_to_original` multiplies scaled shares by the index and panics on overflow (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`). `Cache::calculate_utilization` calls it for both borrowed and supplied totals before any index update (`contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs:19-27`), and `accrue_step` does the same (`common/src/rates/simulate.rs:60-64`). `update_borrow_index` clamps the index at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` but only after multiplying (`common/src/rates/index.rs:13-19`), so a market with very large scaled balances overflows the *value* long before the index ceiling. The repo's own stress test demonstrates the end state: after enough years at ~98% utilization on the steep curve, `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, and `withdraw`/`repay` fail identically, with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-356`). INV-IDX-01 acknowledges the gap ("Debt-value overflow can still revert accrual before that ceiling is reached", `docs/reference/invariants.md:235-236`) but no recovery path exists: accrual runs at the head of every mutation, so nothing can unstick the book.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds. Once the total-debt RAY value crosses `i128::MAX`, every exit and entry path for that hub market panics inside accrual. Suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay, liquidators cannot liquidate, and bad debt cannot be cleaned or recapitalized — all funds held by the pool for that market are frozen permanently, since accrual is unconditional and no admin flag or governance operation bypasses `accrue_step`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the cliff requires a very large market (billions of whole units at high decimals) sustained at high utilization for years so the borrow index compounds far enough — the test reaches it after a few years at 98% utilization on a 175%-max-rate curve. The preconditions are reachable by unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` calls and the fatal accrual is triggered by the permissionless `update_indexes`, but the capital and time requirements make deliberate exploitation impractical; the more realistic trigger is organic drift, since utilization on an untouched book rises over time as debt compounds faster than supply. Medium at best.

### Recommendation
Saturate rather than panic in the total-value computation inside accrual: use a saturating `mul_div` variant for `borrowed_original`/`supplied_original` in `accrue_step`/`calculate_utilization` so utilization pins at 100% and the index marches to `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` instead of trapping. Alternatively, clamp scaled balances during accrual or provide an admin-recovery path that caps the borrow index when the value product would overflow, so exits and bad-debt socialization remain possible.

### Proof of Concept
`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361` — supply `BILLION * 10^18` raw units of an 18-decimal asset, borrow 98% of it, advance time year-by-year calling permissionless `update_indexes`; within 40 years the call returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw` and `repay` calls revert with the same error, confirming the market is permanently frozen.
