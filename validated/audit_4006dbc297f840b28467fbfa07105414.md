### Title
Meta-Transaction Deposit Refunds Are Routed To The Untrusted `sender_id`, Letting It Steal The Relayer's Attached Deposit For Free - ([File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs])

### Summary
In NEP-366 meta transactions, `apply_delegate_action` builds the inner receipt with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` [1](#0-0) . When that inner receipt fails, the deposit refund is routed via `Receipt::balance_refund_receiver()`, which falls back to `predecessor_id()` whenever `refund_to` is unset [2](#0-1) , i.e. to `sender_id` — the untrusted meta-transaction author — even though the relayer, not the sender, actually funded the attached deposit. This mirrors the report's bug class: a value (the deposit/"maker" proceeds) ends up flowing to the party who controls the crafted request (the sender/"maker") instead of the party who actually paid (the relayer/"treasury manager"), at zero cost to the attacker.

### Finding Description
A meta transaction lets a fund-less sender ("Alice") get a relayer to pay all gas and any attached deposits for her actions [3](#0-2) . The relayer purchases and prepays the deposit; the inner receipt executed on the receiver's shard has `predecessor_id = sender_id` (Alice), not the relayer [4](#0-3) .

`refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` whenever `result.result.is_err()` and emits a `Receipt::new_balance_refund` to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` [5](#0-4) . `balance_refund_receiver()` returns `refund_to().unwrap_or(predecessor_id())` [2](#0-1) ; the receipts produced for delegate-action inner calls are plain `Receipt::V0`/`ReceiptEnum::Action`, whose `refund_to()` is always `None` [6](#0-5) . Therefore any deposit refund from a failed delegated action is unconditionally sent to `sender_id` (Alice), never to the relayer who actually paid it.

The nearcore documentation itself flags this exact mechanic as a known, unmitigated hazard: "The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction and agrees to pay for it... But what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer... there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [7](#0-6) 

This is exactly analogous to the Notional finding: an unprivileged, attacker-controlled party (Alice / the "maker") can shape a request such that the party funding the transaction (the relayer / "treasury") pays, but the funds are redirected to the attacker via a protocol-level field (`predecessor_id`-based refund routing) that isn't validated against who actually supplied the funds.

### Impact Explanation
Any account with zero balance can act as `sender_id` in a `DelegateAction`, attach an arbitrary deposit to an inner action (e.g. a `FunctionCall` or `Transfer`) targeting a receiver/method engineered to fail (nonexistent method, contract panic, insufficient prepaid gas for the callee, non-existent account, etc.), and if a relayer submits it, the relayer's attached deposit is refunded entirely to the attacker's account rather than to the relayer. This is direct, unauthorized token theft from the relayer with zero cost to the attacker (the attacker pays nothing — the relayer pays gas and deposit).

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires a relayer that is willing to sponsor meta-transactions with non-trivial attached deposits without simulating execution outcome/predecessor-side risk, which is a realistic operational model for many relayer services (as acknowledged in NEAR's own docs). The attack is fully unprivileged — it only needs a `DelegateAction` signed by any keypair and does not require any special network or validator role.

### Recommendation
Do not default deposit refunds to `predecessor_id` for delegated-action receipts. Instead, propagate an explicit refund destination reflecting who actually funded the deposit (e.g. set `refund_to` on the generated inner receipt to the relayer/signer for delegate-action-spawned receipts, similar to how `promise_refund_to`/`refund_to` already exists for `ActionReceiptV2`), so that a failed action refunds the true payer rather than the request's `sender_id`. Alternatively, require relayers to be warned/forced (at the protocol or SDK level) that deposits in delegate actions are unsafe unless `refund_to` is explicitly set to the relayer.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice (zero balance) creates a `DelegateAction` with `sender_id = alice`, `receiver_id = bob`, and a single `FunctionCall` action with a large `deposit` and a method name that does not exist on `bob`'s contract (guaranteed failure), signs it, and hands it to a Relayer.
2. Relayer wraps it in a `SignedTransaction` (relayer is signer, pays gas + deposit) and submits it, per the standard meta-tx flow shown in `apply_delegate_action` [8](#0-7) .
3. The inner receipt executes on Bob's shard with `predecessor_id = alice`; the `FunctionCall` fails (invalid method).
4. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` builds a full `deposit_refund` receipt to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` = `alice` (not the relayer) [5](#0-4) .
5. Alice's balance increases by the full deposit amount the relayer paid; the relayer's balance is permanently reduced by that amount for zero cost to Alice.

## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
