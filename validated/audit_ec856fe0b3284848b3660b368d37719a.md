## Title
Depositors can front-run bad-debt socialization by redeeming zTokens before a pending liquidation writes down the vault's `lindex`, shifting losses onto remaining suppliers - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar], [File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
When a borrower's position has no remaining collateral, `liquidate()` in `market.clar` calls `socialize-debt-asset` → `vault-socialize-debt`, which writes down the affected vault's `lindex` proportionally to `debt-reduction / old-total-assets` [1](#0-0) . Because `redeem()` on the vault is a permissionless, unqueued, instant withdrawal gated only by an available-liquidity check [2](#0-1) , any zToken holder who observes an incoming liquidation transaction (or the price-feed update that will trigger it, since `liquidate()` accepts `price-feeds` and unwinds them via `write-feeds` at call time [3](#0-2) ) can redeem their full share at the pre-writedown exchange rate before the bad-debt socialization lands in the same block.

### Finding Description
Bad-debt socialization is computed as:
```
new_lindex = current_lindex * (old_total_assets - debt_reduction) / old_total_assets
``` [4](#0-3) 

`debt_reduction` is a fixed absolute USD/token loss amount independent of `total_assets`; the smaller `old_total_assets` is at the moment `socialize-debt` executes, the larger the *percentage* markdown applied to `lindex`, and therefore the larger the loss borne by every remaining zToken holder.

`redeem()` lets any holder withdraw instantly as long as `available-assets >= inkind`, with no queue, cooldown, or lock tied to pending liquidations [2](#0-1) . Liquidations are themselves public, permissionless transactions (`liquidate` / `liquidate-multi`) that carry Pyth `price-feeds` payloads and become visible in the mempool before being mined [3](#0-2) . A liquidation that will strip a borrower's last collateral and leave bad debt (`no-collateral-left`) triggers `socialize-debt-asset` over that borrower's remaining debt list, and this is explicitly acknowledged in-code as an ordering-sensitive step ("Prevents front-running attacks that prevent bad debt socialization" for the `liquidate-multi` batch path) [5](#0-4) ; however, that protection only targets liquidators racing each other, not depositors racing the socialization event itself.

This is structurally identical to the reported Napier bug class: an unprivileged principal (the depositor) observes an imminent, protocol-recognized value decrease (there: LST rebase/slashing event; here: pending bad-debt socialization of a vault's `lindex`) and instantly exits via a permissionless "redeem for underlying if within buffer" path before the loss is applied, shifting the loss onto the honest suppliers who remain in the pool at settlement time.

Attacker: any zUSDC (or other vault zToken) holder monitoring the mempool for liquidation transactions that will result in `no-collateral-left` on an undercollateralized borrower.
Victim: the other zToken holders of that vault who do not redeem before the socialization lands.
Shared state: the vault's `lindex` / `assets` (total-assets), which determines every holder's redemption value.

### Impact Explanation
This causes temporary/permanent freezing (loss realization) of unclaimed yield/principal for remaining suppliers: the loss that should be spread pro-rata across all suppliers at the pre-liquidation total is instead concentrated onto the suppliers who fail to exit in time, because the front-runner extracts their share at the un-marked-down `lindex` and reduces `old_total_assets` right before the markdown is computed. This falls under the in-scope impact class of theft/permanent freezing of unclaimed yield to other suppliers via socialization charged disproportionately to whoever remains.

### Likelihood Explanation
Liquidations that produce bad debt are visible on-chain before finalization (mempool-visible `liquidate`/`liquidate-multi` calls with attached Pyth price-feed updates), giving a clear, cheap, bot-automatable signal. `redeem()` has no queue or delay, and the front-run only requires enough available liquidity in the vault to service the redemption, which is the normal operating condition for a healthy vault. This does not require any oracle manipulation, privileged role, or flashloan trickery — pure transaction ordering within the block/mempool.

### Recommendation
Introduce a mechanism that decouples the socialization write-down timing from same-block redemption exits, e.g.:
- Snapshot/queue-based redemption (a withdrawal delay or two-phase redeem) so pending liquidations that will socialize debt are reflected before withdrawal settles, mirroring the report's suggested fix.
- Alternatively, apply socialization based on `total_assets` measured at the start of the block (or at liquidation initiation) rather than at execution time, so late-block redemptions cannot shrink the denominator used for the markdown.
- Consider pausing/rate-limiting redemptions on a vault when a liquidation that would trigger `no-collateral-left`/bad-debt socialization is in-flight for that vault's underlying/debt asset.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower B holds debt in `USDC` vault fully collateralized by an asset whose price is about to be updated on-chain via a pending `liquidate(...)` call carrying a Pyth `price-feeds` payload that will push B's LTV such that liquidation leaves `no-collateral-left` (triggering `socialize-debt-asset` on `USDC`).
2. Attacker A, a zUSDC holder, observes this pending `liquidate` transaction in the mempool.
3. A submits `redeem(A_shares, min_out, A)` on `vault-usdc` in an earlier position of the same block (or immediately before, if same-block ordering allows), receiving `inkind = A_shares * lindex_before` USDC, decrementing `assets` (`var-set assets (- current-assets inkind)`) [6](#0-5) .
4. The pending `liquidate` executes, `no-collateral-left` is true for B's remaining debt, and `socialize-debt-asset` invokes `vault-socialize-debt` on `USDC` [7](#0-6) .
5. `socialize-debt` computes `new_lindex = current_lindex * (old_total_assets - debt_reduction) / old_total_assets` using `old_total_assets` measured *after* A's redemption reduced `assets` [8](#0-7) . Because `old_total_assets` is smaller than it would have been had A stayed in, the same absolute `debt_reduction` produces a strictly larger percentage cut to `lindex`.
6. Remaining zUSDC holders now redeem at a lower `lindex`, absorbing more than their fair share of the bad debt, while A escaped unaffected — proving the analog to the LST/LRT redeem-before-price-drop front-run.
