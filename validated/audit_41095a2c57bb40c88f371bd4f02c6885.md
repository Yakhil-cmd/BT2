### Title
Donation/rounding attack against share-based Autonomous Agent pools due to unmet minimum-supply invariant - ([File: test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript])

### Summary
The bundled "Uniswap-like market maker" Autonomous Agent (AA) sample computes and issues its liquidity/share asset (`mm_asset`) using the same ratio-based mint formula that causes the ERC-4626 donation attack described in the external report. The AA never enforces a minimum outstanding-supply floor for `var['mm_asset_outstanding']`, and its "first deposit" branch can be re-entered repeatedly during the life of the AA (not only once at genesis), leaving new depositors exposed to unfavorable, attacker-controlled rounding exactly as in the reported vault issue.

### Finding Description
In the "invest in MM" case, the AA computes how many shares (`mm_asset`) to mint for a depositor: [1](#0-0) 

- If either side of the pool (`$asset_balance` or `$bytes_balance`) is currently zero, the AA treats the deposit as an "initial deposit" and sets `$issue_amount = balance[base]` unconditionally — it does **not** check whether `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` is actually zero.
- Otherwise, shares are minted proportionally: `$issue_amount = round($investor_share_of_prev_balance * var['mm_asset_outstanding'])`, which rounds down in favor of existing shareholders, mirroring the ERC-4626 rounding behavior cited in the report.

The "exchange bytes to asset" / "exchange asset to bytes" cases let anyone swap between `base` and `$asset` while leaving `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` untouched: [2](#0-1) 

This means an attacker can freely manipulate `$asset_balance` and `$bytes_balance` (the pool's internal AA balances) without affecting the recorded share supply, exactly like donating tokens directly to a vault contract to inflate `totalAssets` without minting shares.

The "divest MM shares" case lets any shareholder redeem an arbitrary fraction of their shares at any time, which can drive `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` down to a very small value (e.g., 1) while leaving nonzero pool balances, or drive one side of the pool balance to zero, which flips the AA back into the unconditional "initial deposit" branch: [3](#0-2) 

Because the "initial deposit" condition is based on the instantaneous pool balances (`$asset_balance == 0 OR $bytes_balance == 0`) rather than on `var['mm_asset_outstanding'] == 0`, the vault can be forced back into the low-supply/vulnerable state described in the report at any point in its lifetime — not just at the very first deposit — by any unprivileged AA trigger sender who withdraws or swaps to zero out one side of the pool.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker able to trigger the AA (any address that can post a unit paying to the AA, which is the base capability of ocore AAs) can:
1. Wait for/force `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` to become very small (via full/partial divestment) or force one pool balance to zero (via the exchange cases).
2. Use the resulting "initial deposit" branch or the low-supply rounding in the proportional-mint branch to mint a disproportionately large number of shares for a small contribution, or to cause a victim's deposit to be rounded down to fewer shares than economically deserved.
3. Subsequently divest, extracting more of the pool's `base`/`$asset` balance than they contributed, at the expense of the victim depositor — resulting in AA fund loss for other users.

This matches the "AA fund loss" impact category accepted by the validation rules.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any user who can post a trigger unit to the AA (an ordinary, unprivileged capability in ocore) can perform the divest/exchange calls needed to shrink `mm_asset_outstanding` or zero out a pool-side balance, and can time their own deposit to benefit from the resulting rounding. No special privilege, witness cooperation, or network-level manipulation is required — only ordinary AA trigger messages, matching the "unprivileged ... AA trigger sender" reachability required by the validation rules. Because this pattern is shipped as an official ocore oscript example (`test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, also exercised by `test/ojson.test.js`), it is directly reachable/copyable by anyone deploying an AA based on this documented pattern.

### Recommendation
- Gate the "initial deposit" branch strictly on `var['mm_asset_outstanding'] == 0` rather than on the instantaneous pool balances being zero, so the special first-deposit rule can only ever apply once.
- Enforce a minimum non-withdrawable `mm_asset_outstanding` floor (analogous to the Pods Finance fix of contributing a permanent, non-withdrawable initial deposit) so that legitimate share supply can never be driven back down to a value where rounding becomes economically exploitable.
- Consider requiring a minimum initial contribution and disallowing full divestment down to zero outstanding shares while pool balances remain nonzero.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker A defines the `mm_asset` and deposits, e.g., 1e5 bytes + matching `$asset` amount, receiving `mm_asset_outstanding = balance[base]` shares (initial deposit branch, line 39).
2. Attacker A calls the "divest MM shares" case, redeeming all but a tiny fraction (e.g., 1 unit) of `mm_asset`, driving `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` down to a very small number while balances of `base`/`$asset` in the pool remain nonzero (or are driven to zero on one side).
3. Attacker A (or a colluding second address) uses the "exchange bytes to asset" / "exchange asset to bytes" cases to further skew `$asset_balance` vs `$bytes_balance` without touching `mm_asset_outstanding`.
4. A victim V deposits legitimate funds via the "invest in MM" case; because `var['mm_asset_outstanding']` is now tiny and/or one side of the pool balance is zero, V's minted `$issue_amount` is rounded unfavorably (or the unconditional "initial deposit" branch reinitializes pricing), giving V far fewer shares than their contribution's fair value.
5. Attacker A then divests, extracting a disproportionate share of the combined pool (including V's newly deposited funds), realizing profit at V's expense — reproducing the donation-attack fund-loss scenario described in the external report.
