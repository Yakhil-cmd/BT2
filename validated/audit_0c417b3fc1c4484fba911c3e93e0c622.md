### Title
Pool `repay`/`recapitalize` refund is paid out of real custody against a declared, unfunded inbound amount - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The kernel patch fixes a *leak of a held resource on an error/excess path*: memory was allocated and never released when an operation aborted. The lending analog is the pool's excess-refund logic: both `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` compute a refund from the **declared** `amount` (not from a measured inbound transfer or the cash book) and then `transfer_out` that refund to the `payer` from the pool's real token custody, without debiting `cash` or passing `require_reserves`. When the declared amount exceeds what actually needs to be booked, the difference is paid out of custodied user funds.

### Finding Description
`ops::repay::accounting` resolves `amount` into `burned` debt shares plus an `overpayment` remainder, credits only `net_repay` to cash, commits the book, and then `apply` calls `cache.transfer_out(payer, overpayment)` (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:25-67). The refund is derived purely from the caller-declared `action.amount`: on a market whose debt is smaller than `amount` — or zero — the entire amount becomes `overpayment` and is transferred back out of the pool's token balance, while `cash`/`borrowed` accounting reflects only the net portion.

`ops::recapitalize::apply` has the identical shape: `applied = amount.min(backing_shortfall)`, `refund = amount - applied`, then `transfer_out(&payer, refund)` (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:44-66). A market with no shortfall refunds the whole declared `amount` from custody.

The pooled test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611) pins this: with zero debt and zero inbound transfer, `repay(payer, custody_before)` credits nothing yet refunds the full declared amount, draining real custody while the book stays untouched. This is the resource-leak-on-error-path pattern: the "excess" branch frees value (tokens) that was never proven to have been deposited — the inbound transfer is assumed ("the controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call", repay.rs:3) rather than measured like every other settlement path in the codebase (`transfer_amount_measured`, `balance_delta_since`, `refund_controller_balance_delta`).

### Impact Explanation
If the pool's `repay`/`recapitalize` entrypoints do not hard-verify the inbound custody delta (the code comments indicate the invariant is upheld only by convention on the controller side), any path that reaches them with an unfunded or under-funded declared amount pays the "refund" out of suppliers' custodied deposits. That is theft of user funds / protocol insolvency: `cash` stays booked while the real balance is reduced, so later withdrawals revert on `require_reserves`. Even through the controller, an unprivileged caller reaches `repay` with a user-chosen `amount`; safety rests entirely on the controller having pre-transferred the full declared amount and on the refund going only to the true payer — assumptions the pool does not enforce, in contrast to the measured-receipt settlement used for swaps, flash loans, and `claim_revenue`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability depends on whether the pool gates `repay`/`recapitalize` to the controller and whether the controller always pre-funds the declared amount. The in-repo test invokes `pool.repay` directly with an arbitrary payer and an amount equal to full custody and it succeeds, which indicates no measured-funding check on this leg. I could not fully confirm (within the iteration limit) whether `pool::repay` enforces `require_auth` on a stored controller address in `lib.rs`; if such a gate exists, exploitability reduces to controller-side refund-target mistakes (e.g., a strategy leg passing a controllable `refund_to`), which the controller currently mitigates via `refund_controller_balance_delta`. Rated Medium under the conservative assumption that the controller gate holds but a reachable path can produce an unfunded declared amount.

### Recommendation
Measure, don't declare: in `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply`, snapshot the pool token balance before accounting and cap the refund at the measured inbound delta (`balance_delta_since`), exactly as `refund_controller_balance_delta` (contracts/controller/src/payments.rs:41-52) and the router's `transfer_amount_measured` do. Alternatively, have the pool pull `amount` via `transfer_from` before accounting, making custody and book atomic.

### Proof of Concept
The repository already contains the pinning test — `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611): with `borrowed == 0` and no inbound transfer, `pool.repay(payer, amount = custody_before)` returns `actual_amount == 0` yet transfers `custody_before` tokens to `payer`, leaving `cash` booked but custody drained. The same shape holds for `recapitalize` on a market with `backing_shortfall == 0`.
