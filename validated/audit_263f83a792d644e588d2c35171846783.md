### Title
Bad-debt socialization floor resurrects wiped supply shares and lets stale suppliers drain recapitalization - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The pool's bad-debt cleanup is analogous to a use-after-free: the account's debt is destroyed and its collateral position can be removed from the controller, but its pool supply shares remain part of global `supplied`. When `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` would reduce the supply index to zero, it instead clamps the index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, leaving those logically wiped shares with a positive claim. A later `recapitalize` credits cash to cover this manufactured shortfall, after which the stale position can withdraw real tokens.

### Finding Description
`seize::apply` socializes a borrow-side position by converting its scaled debt to the current debt value, reducing the supply index, and burning the debt [1](#0-0) . It does not burn or remove the corresponding supply shares.

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the write-down at total supplied value, but then floors the resulting supply index at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of allowing it to reach zero [2](#0-1) . Therefore, after a complete wipeout, `supplied` remains positive while cash is zero and debt has been burned.

The withdraw path resolves the stale scaled position at the floored supply index, burns it, checks only that the requested amount is covered by cash, and debits/transfers that amount [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . `require_supply_for_debt` only catches the narrower condition `supplied == 0 && borrowed != 0`, so it does not detect this positive-share/zero-cash state [5](#0-4) .

On the controller side, permissionless bad-debt cleanup sends all remaining supply and debt positions to the pool's `seize_positions` entrypoint, then removes the account and burns its NFT [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) . Liquidation can also invoke the same cleanup after its state update [8](#0-7) .

The codebase's own regression tests model the exact consequence: a raw-cache wipeout leaves a nonzero “phantom claim,” fresh cash is added, and the stale position drains that cash, leaving a later claim uncovered [9](#0-8) . A second test similarly demonstrates that a wiped survivor can extract a later deposit and leave the fresh depositor under-collateralized [10](#0-9) .

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of funds contributed through `recapitalize` or another cash-crediting path that bypasses `supply`'s pre-entry solvency check. The stale shares were economically destroyed by bad-debt socialization, but the nonzero index floor gives them a new positive claim. Once cash exists, `withdraw(i128::MAX, stale_position)` can transfer that cash to the stale shareholder.

Depending on market timing and bookkeeping, the impact can also become protocol insolvency: the stale withdrawal consumes cash needed to satisfy newer or remaining supply claims. The affected state is reachable through unprivileged liquidation/bad-debt cleanup and permissionless recapitalization.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires a bad-debt cleanup or borrow-side seizure severe enough to drive the supply index to the floor. That is an exceptional market event, but it is an explicitly supported protocol flow rather than an impossible state.

Afterward, normal `supply` is guarded by `require_backed_market`, which rejects a positive backing shortfall [11](#0-10) . However, `recapitalize` exists specifically to cover a backing shortfall and is permissionless from the controller boundary [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) . Any participant—including an attacker holding or controlling the stale supply position—can provide the cash that makes withdrawal possible.

### Recommendation
Do not preserve economically wiped shares through an index floor. For a complete bad-debt write-down, either:

- explicitly burn/write off the stale `supplied` shares and set the market's live supply share total to zero;
- track wiped-out positions separately and make their claims permanently unpayable; or
- add a stricter post-socialization invariant that a market with `supplied > 0`, `borrowed == 0`, and `cash == 0` cannot later pay those legacy shares.

At minimum, make withdrawal enforce `require_backed_market` or an equivalent “legacy claims cannot exceed market backing” check after bad-debt socialization, not merely `require_reserves(net_transfer)`. If `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` must remain for arithmetic safety, pair it with explicit extinguishment or quarantine of the affected shares.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual transaction sequence:

1. An account is left with more debt than collateral after liquidation.
2. Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` or post-liquidation cleanup submits a borrow-side `PoolSeizeEntry` for its debt.
3. `seize::apply` calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and burns the debt. The write-down reaches total supplied value, but the index is clamped at `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` rather than becoming zero.
4. The global market still has positive `supplied`, while cash is zero and burned debt no longer backs those shares.
5. An attacker or external recapitalizer calls `recapitalize`, transferring cash to the pool.
6. The stale supplier calls `withdraw` with `amount = i128::MAX`.
7. `resolve_withdrawal` converts the stale scaled shares at the floored index, `require_reserves` passes because cash now exists, and the pool transfers the recapitalized cash.
8. The resulting claims/backing mismatch leaves the protocol or later suppliers unable to satisfy their legitimate claims.

/// Transfers funds to the pool, credits the measured receipt up to the backing
/// shortfall, and refunds unused funds. Returns credited cash; rejects flash loans.
pub(crate) fn recapitalize(
    env: &Env,
    payer: Address,
    hub_asset: HubAssetKey,
    amount: i128,
) -> i128 {
    validation::require_authorized_caller(env, &payer);
    require_positive_amount(env, amount);

    let mut cache = Context::new(env);
    let pool_addr = cache.cached_pool_address();
    // Prefund the pool and credit only its measured receipt.
    let received = payments::transfer_amount_measured(
        env,
        &hub_asset.asset,
        &payer,
        &pool_addr,
        amount,
        GenericError::AmountMustBePositive,
    );

    pool_recapitalize_call(env, &pool_addr, &hub_asset, &payer, received).actual_amount
}
