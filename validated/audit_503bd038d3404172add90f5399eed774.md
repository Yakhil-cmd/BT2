### Title
Stale dual-source oracle leg inflates collateral and permits over-LTV borrows - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs](contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs))

### Summary

The price aggregator accepts a dual-source price when both legs are inside their configured staleness limits and inside the configured price-tolerance band, then returns the arithmetic midpoint of the two legs. [1](#0-0)  Because the controller uses that accepted midpoint directly for collateral valuation and debt valuation, a slow-moving but technically fresh anchor can inflate borrow capacity immediately after the market price falls. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description

When two oracle legs are available, `blend` marks the result stale only when either leg is individually stale, or when both legs have `FeedNature::Market` and their timestamps exceed the configured age spread. [3](#0-2)  A `Fundamental` anchor therefore bypasses the cross-leg age-spread check and remains acceptable for its entire `max_stale_seconds` window. [4](#0-3) 

The legs are considered in agreement whenever the larger/smaller price ratio is no greater than `upper_ratio_bps`; the accepted result is then `floor((primary + anchor) / 2)`. [5](#0-4)  This means an old anchor at the high edge of the tolerance band shifts the accepted price by approximately half of the permitted divergence.

The controller’s collateral calculation multiplies each supplied position by this cached aggregator price and applies the stored LTV. [2](#0-1)  After a borrow, `require_post_pool_risk_gates` accepts the transaction when this inflated LTV-weighted collateral covers the new debt and produces a health factor of at least one. [6](#0-5)  An unprivileged account owner can reach this path through `borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` without any privileged oracle call. [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation

An attacker can borrow more debt than the true current collateral value supports under the market’s LTV and withdraw the borrowed assets. [8](#0-7)  The inflated collateral valuation persists while the stale anchor remains inside its freshness and tolerance limits, creating a window in which lender liquidity can be removed against under-collateralized risk. [9](#0-8) 

This can produce bad debt if the collateral remains depressed or falls further before liquidation completes. [10](#0-9)  The issue is especially relevant to asynchronous or low-frequency NAV and fundamental feeds, where a stale observation can remain valid while the paired market leg has already repriced. [11](#0-10) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The exploit does not require compromising an oracle provider or submitting a price outside the configured bands. [12](#0-11)  It requires only a normal stale-but-unexpired anchor whose old price differs from the fresh primary by less than the configured tolerance. [13](#0-12) 

The repository’s live-path regression test configures a 10% tolerance, keeps the anchor 23 hours old inside an 86,400-second limit, changes the fresh price from `$1.00` to `$0.91`, and demonstrates that the same borrower supplies 100,000 XLM and successfully borrows 70,000 USDC. [14](#0-13)  The control case with a fresh honest anchor rejects the same borrow with `InsufficientCollateral`, isolating the stale-anchor midpoint as the cause. [15](#0-14) 

### Recommendation

Do not use the midpoint for collateral risk when the legs disagree. Use the lower leg for collateral valuation and the higher leg for debt valuation, or reject dual-source readings whose observed timestamps differ materially regardless of feed nature. [16](#0-15) 

If midpoint pricing remains a deliberate design choice, reduce the allowable fundamental-feed staleness and tolerance so the largest accepted midpoint error cannot bridge the LTV safety margin. [5](#0-4)  The configuration should enforce that a maximum permitted stale-leg deviation cannot turn a borrow that should fail LTV into an accepted borrow. [17](#0-16) 

### Proof of Concept

The existing live-path harness demonstrates the exploit:

1. Configure XLM with a fresh Reflector primary and a RedStone `Fundamental` anchor using a 10% tolerance and an 86,400-second stale limit. [18](#0-17) 
2. Move the fresh primary from `$1.00` to `$0.91`, while leaving the anchor’s `$1.00` observation 82,800 seconds old. [19](#0-18) 
3. Supply 100,000 XLM from the attacker account and 500,000 USDC of lender liquidity. [20](#0-19) 
4. Call `borrow` for 70,000 USDC. [21](#0-20) 
5. The aggregator accepts `(0.91 + 1.00) / 2 = 0.955` as the XLM price, so the protocol sees approximately `$95,500` of collateral and about `$71,625` of 75%-LTV borrow capacity. [22](#0-21) 
6. With both legs fresh at `$0.91`, the same 70,000 USDC borrow reverts with `InsufficientCollateral`; with the stale anchor it succeeds. [23](#0-22)

pub(crate) fn within_tolerance_band(
    env: &Env,
    anchor: i128,
    primary: i128,
    tolerance: &OracleTolerance,
) -> bool {
    let high = anchor.max(primary);
    let low = anchor.min(primary);
    let Some(upper_ratio_bps) = fp_core::try_mul_div_half_up(env, high, BPS, low) else {
        return false;
    };

    upper_ratio_bps <= i128::from(tolerance.upper_ratio_bps)
}

/// Returns the average of `anchor_price` and `primary_price`, truncated
/// toward zero. Returns `0` if the sum overflows `i128`.
pub(crate) fn midpoint_price_or_zero(anchor_price: i128, primary_price: i128) -> i128 {
    anchor_price
        .checked_add(primary_price)
        .map(|sum| sum / 2)
        .unwrap_or(0)
