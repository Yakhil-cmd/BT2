### Title
Unfunded overpayment refund drains pool custody — pool `repay`/`recapitalize` refund from declared `amount`, not measured receipt - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The pool computes the repay overpayment and the recapitalize refund from the caller-declared `amount` and pays it out of real token custody via `transfer_out`, without verifying that `amount` was ever transferred in. The comments assume "the controller transfers the repay amount into the pool before this call," but nothing enforces or measures that inbound transfer. This is the same bug class as CVE-2018-14615: a length/amount value trusted where it should have been bounded by what actually arrived, producing an outflow larger than the inflow — here, an outflow with zero inflow.

### Finding Description
In `repay::accounting`, `overpayment` comes from `cache.resolve_repay(amount, position)` where `amount = action.amount` is declared, not measured (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:42-44). `apply` then calls `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` (line 32), sending real tokens to `payer`. On a market with no debt, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so the full-close branch of `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment == amount` while `net_repay == 0` passes the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert (lines 44-52). The in-repo test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611) proves this end-to-end: a `repay` with `ract(0, custody_before)` on a zero-debt market transfers nothing in and pays the entire pool balance to `payer`.

The identical shape exists in `recapitalize::accounting`: `refund = amount - applied` where `applied = min(amount, backing_shortfall)` (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:52-55), refunded via `transfer_out` (line 34) with no proof the declared `amount` arrived. The docs themselves flag the gap: "Neither refund debits `cash` or passes `require_reserves`" and the refund derives "from a declared inbound amount, not from the cash book" (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3578-3588).

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. Any address that can invoke the pool's `repay` (or `recapitalize`) entrypoint directly — or reach it through `controller::repay`/`repay_debt_with_collateral`/`recapitalize` with a declared amount exceeding the funded inbound — receives tokens it never deposited. A single call with `amount = pool_balance` on any zero-debt listed market transfers the entire market's custody to the caller, draining supplier funds. The repay path also inflates the `cash` book by `net_repay` without backing, compounding insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation
High if the pool entrypoints are reachable by arbitrary callers: the unit test already demonstrates `client().repay(&payer, &ract(...))` executing with an unfunded `payer` and draining custody. Reachability depends on whether the pool gates `repay`/`recapitalize` to the controller address — I could not confirm an auth/whitelist check on those entrypoints within this review; if none exists, no collateral, position, or price condition is needed beyond a listed market. Even if controller-only, the controller's `transfer_amount_measured` funding in `apply_liquidation_repayments` is per-leg measured, but the plain `repay`/`recapitalize` controller paths must be checked for whether the declared `amount` is ever capped to a measured inbound before being forwarded.

### Recommendation
Measure the inbound transfer at the pool (`balance_delta_since`-style snapshot before/after crediting) and compute `net_repay`/`refund` from the measured receipt, not `action.amount` — mirroring the `transfer_amount_measured` discipline already used elsewhere. Alternatively, require that calls to `repay`/`recapitalize` originate from the controller contract and that the controller itself bounds `action.amount` to a measured transfer-in. Add `require_reserves`/cash-book consistency on the refund leg.

### Proof of Concept
Replicated by the existing test `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611): create a market with zero debt and `custody_before > 0`, call `pool.repay(payer, [action{position:0, amount:custody_before}])` with no prior token transfer; `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment = custody_before`, `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` passes because `net_repay == 0`, and `transfer_out` sends the whole balance to `payer`. The same shape via `pool.recapitalize(hub_asset, payer, huge_amount)` refunds `huge_amount - applied` out of custody.
