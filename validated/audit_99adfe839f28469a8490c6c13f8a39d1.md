### Title
Low quorum requirement in `system_vote` counting lets a minority stake dictate consensus-critical parameters (op_list / threshold_size / tps params) - (File: `main_chain.js`)

### Summary
The DAO report describes how, immediately after deployment, an attacker can accumulate a majority of a small, still-thinly-distributed voting pool and push through governance-altering proposals (up to replacing the DAO itself) before the rest of the stakeholders react. ocore has a structurally similar on-chain governance mechanism: `system_vote` / `system_vote_count` messages let any address holding spendable bytes vote to change `op_list` (the Order Provider/witness list), `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, and `tps_fee_multiplier` [1](#0-0) . The vote tally in `countVotes()` only requires participation from addresses holding **10%** of total supply (`SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`) before it locks in a result computed from that participating minority alone [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description
`countVotes(conn, mci, subject, ...)` determines the effective value of a system variable by:
1. Collecting balances of all addresses that have ever cast a `system_vote` for that `subject` [4](#0-3) .
2. Expanding a lookback time window year by year **only until the cumulative balance of voters within the window reaches `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE * TOTAL_WHITEBYTES`** (10% of the entire fixed coin supply), then stopping — it does not require anything close to a majority of the total supply to participate: [3](#0-2) 
3. Once that 10%-of-supply quorum window is fixed, the outcome is decided by simple plurality (for `op_list`, top-N addresses by summed voter balance win) or by a balance-weighted median (for the numeric parameters) — computed **only over the votes cast within that quorum window**, not over the whole coin supply: [5](#0-4) 

Because the quorum bar is just 10% of supply and the winner only needs a plurality/median of *that* participating balance (i.e., as little as roughly 5%+ε of total supply concentrated in one or few addresses, if turnout stays near the 10% floor), a large early or otherwise concentrated holder can dominate the vote while the broader holder base has not yet engaged — directly mirroring the DAO report's "low initial participation → majority capture" scenario. Any unprivileged address can trigger this by posting ordinary `payment` + `system_vote` messages; validation only checks structural correctness of the vote (address/format validity for `op_list`, numeric bounds for the others) and does not impose any balance-share cap on a single voter [6](#0-5) .

The riskiest subject is `op_list`: this directly overwrites `storage.systemVars.op_list`, i.e., the list of Order Providers/witnesses used for consensus/stability determination going forward [7](#0-6) . This is the ocore analog of the DAO's `moveDao` — a governance action that can hand over control of the entire system to the attacker's chosen set of addresses.

### Impact Explanation
If a concentrated stake dominates the low-turnout quorum window, it can:
- Rewrite `op_list`, replacing the Order Provider set that all future units are stamped against, which can be used to bias/subvert stability determination and consensus going forward (a form of "node disagreement on validity/stability" if honest nodes reject the outcome vs. nodes that accept it, or outright degraded decentralization/security if accepted network-wide).
- Alter `threshold_size`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`, which directly control unit oversize fees and TPS fee economics network-wide, enabling fee manipulation/DoS-adjacent economic griefing for all senders.

This qualifies as a Medium/High-severity governance-capture issue reachable purely by an unprivileged unit poster with sufficient (but not majority-of-supply) byte balance.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires a single actor (or colluding actors) to hold a byte balance on the order of the 10% quorum threshold (or a plurality/median-swinging share within it) and be willing to vote while the rest of the network is not actively voting. Since `system_vote` participation is optional and historically sparse right after each new subject/feature activation (e.g., new voteable parameters added via upgrades), the "low initial participation" window described in the DAO report has a direct parallel here whenever a new votable subject is introduced or existing turnout is low.

### Recommendation
- Raise `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE` substantially and/or require the winning option itself (not just aggregate turnout) to reach a supermajority of total supply, not just a plurality/median of the participating subset.
- For `op_list` specifically, consider requiring a minimum absolute number of distinct large/diverse voters, or a cool-off/finalization delay analogous to the DAO's `coolOffPeriod`, so the community can react to a suspicious concentrated `op_list` vote before it takes effect on the next MCI.
- Emit clearer/monitorable events earlier in the process (not just at final `system_vars_updated`) so wallets/monitoring tools can flag when a `system_vote` is being decided by an unusually small/concentrated set of addresses.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy or observe a network state where `system_votes` for a given `subject` (e.g., `op_list`) has very few or no prior voters (e.g., right after this feature activates, `constants.v4UpgradeMci`).
2. Acquire/hold bytes ≈ 10% of `TOTAL_WHITEBYTES` (or less, if it swings the plurality within whatever window naturally reaches quorum) in a single address or small colluding set.
3. Post ordinary payment units containing a `system_vote` message with `subject: "op_list"` and a chosen 12-address `value` (or `subject: "threshold_size"`/`"base_tps_fee"`/etc. with a chosen numeric value), satisfying only the structural checks in `validateInlinePayload` (valid addresses, sorted/unique, known-and-good witnesses) [8](#0-7) .
4. When the unit stabilizes, `saveSystemVote` records the vote and `countVotes` is invoked at MC stabilization [9](#0-8) .
5. Because the time-window expansion in `countVotes` stops as soon as 10% of total supply has voted (step 2 above), and the plurality/median is computed only over that voter set, the attacker's vote becomes decisive, and `system_vars` is updated (`REPLACE`/`INSERT`) to reflect the attacker-chosen `op_list`/parameter value [10](#0-9) , taking effect from the next MCI onward.
