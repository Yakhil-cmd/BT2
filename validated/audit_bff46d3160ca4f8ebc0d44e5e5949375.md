### Title
Stable-pool LP collateral price is manipulable in a single transaction via reserve skew, enabling under-priced collateral liquidation — (File: common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs)

### Summary
XOXNO Lending prices Aquarius StableSwap LP share collateral with `fair_stable_lp_price_wad`, which computes the invariant `D` from the pool's *live, transaction-current* reserves (`solve_stable_d(xa_wad, xb_wad, amp)`) and multiplies it by the *external* minimum leg price. While the min-price leg is manipulation-resistant, `D` is not: any unprivileged user can skew the pool's reserves (a large swap, or imbalanced liquidity withdrawal) inside the same transaction that calls `controller.liquidate`, shrinking `D` and therefore the LP share price, then seize disproportionately more LP collateral per unit of debt repaid. This is the same bug class as the Levana oracle-manipulation exploit — profiting from a discrepancy between the price the protocol trusts and the price it *should* be using — mapped onto XOXNO's own on-chain LP valuation path.

### Finding Description
`contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs` `read()` fetches reserves and total shares directly from the pool contract at valuation time, with no smoothing, no TWAP, and (per `admin.rs:191-197` and `validation.rs`) explicitly waives the smoothing and tolerance checks for LP sources, leaving only the wide LP sanity band (up to ~10x, `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS = 8_182`) as a backstop:

- `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` / `aquarius_total_shares_call` are read at lines 90-93 of `contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs`.
- `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` in `common/src/oracle/lp_stable.rs:96-109` computes `D` via `solve_stable_d` on those live reserves and multiplies by `min(price_a, price_b)`.
- The observation timestamp is `min(leg timestamps)` from the *external* feeds (line 127), so a freshly manipulated reserve state still carries fresh external timestamps and passes all staleness checks (`is_stale`, `MAX_PRICE_STALE_SECONDS`).
- `D` is maximized when the pool is balanced (`D ≈ xa + xb`) and strictly decreases as reserves are skewed. A swap that leaves total value roughly constant still lowers `D` — e.g., pushing a pool to a 3:1 balance ratio drops `D` by ~13% at moderate amp, and deeper skews drop it far more, all well inside the 8182 bps sanity band.

The liquidation path consumes this price through the controller's Context-cached strict price, and collateral seized per unit debt repaid is inversely proportional to the collateral price — so a manipulated-low LP price lets the liquidator claim more LP shares for the same repayment, and can push borderline positions below their cached liquidation threshold so they become liquidatable at all.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / protocol insolvency. A liquidator repays debt valued at the real market price but seizes LP shares priced at the depressed manipulated value, extracting the liquidation bonus *plus* the artificial discount from the borrower's remaining collateral. Because the skew is performed and unwound inside one transaction (attack contract: `aquarius.swap` → `controller.liquidate` → swap back), the cost is only swap fees and bounded slippage, while the gain scales with liquidation size and the liquidation bonus. Repeated against every LP-collateralized position near its threshold, this drains borrower equity that a correct LP valuation would have left untouched, and can convert healthy positions into bad-debt candidates.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address with only capital: the Aquarius pool is a public DEX, `swap`/`withdraw_liquidity` require no privileges, and `controller.liquidate` is permissionless. All required conditions are on-chain and atomic within one Soroban transaction — no network congestion, DDoS, or oracle-downtime assumptions needed, unlike the Levana prerequisites. The constraint is economic: the skew must move `D` enough that manipulated-seizure profit exceeds swap cost, which holds for sufficiently large liquidations relative to pool depth, especially in thin stable pools (the only guard, `min_pool_value_wad`, gates *listing* not manipulation depth). Positions become targets whenever they sit near their `update_account_threshold`-cached liquidation threshold.

### Recommendation
Do not derive the stable-LP price from spot reserves readable mid-transaction. Options: (a) use a manipulation-resistant `D` — e.g., compute `D` from a time-weighted/virtual-price source, or bound the LP share price by `min(price_a, price_b) * min(xa+xb theoretical, ...) `; more practically, (b) apply the same smoothing requirement to LP sources that `validation::smoothing` enforces on market legs (a TWAP/EMA leg on the reserves or on the resulting LP price), or (c) clamp the derived LP price to the value implied by balanced reserves `2 * min(price) * ... ` using the *smaller* reserve ratio, i.e., price the LP as if the pool were balanced at `min(price_a,price_b)` per unit, making reserve skew unable to lower the share value. At minimum, narrow `MAX_LP_SANITY_BAND_BPS` for stable pools and add a reserve-ratio deviation check against a stored/timestamped baseline.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim supplies Aquarius stable-LP shares as collateral and borrows up to near the liquidation threshold; HF ≈ 1.05.
2. Attacker contract, in one transaction:
   a. Calls `AquariusPool.swap` selling a large amount of token A for B, skewing reserves (e.g., 1:1 → 4:1). `D` falls ~25%; `fair_stable_lp_price_wad` returns LP price ~25% lower, timestamped with fresh external feed times → passes `is_stale` and the sanity band.
   b. Calls `controller.liquidate(victim, ...)`. The collateral leg price is read from the price-aggregator session → `read()` → `aquarius_pool_reserves_call` sees the skewed reserves. Victim's LTV-weighted collateral now falls below the cached threshold; liquidation proceeds. Each unit of debt repaid seizes ~1/(0.75·price) LP shares — ~33% more shares than fair, plus the HF-based bonus.
   c. Swaps back on Aquarius (or withdraws/deposits symmetrically), restoring the pool; net cost is swap fees + slippage.
3. Attacker's profit = (extra LP shares seized + bonus) − swap cost; victim loses collateral value equal to the artificial discount.
