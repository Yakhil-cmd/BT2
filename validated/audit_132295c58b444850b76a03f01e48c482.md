## Title
Arbiter contract "paid" status can be spoofed by unrelated deposits to the public shared address - (File: arbiter_contract.js)

### Summary
The wallet-side arbiter-contract "escrow" flow marks a contract as `paid` purely by summing all outputs ever received at the contract's `shared_address`, without checking who sent them or whether they match the payment structure baked into the shared address's own spending condition. This is the same bug class as the reported `ERC20BalanceGteEnforcer` issue: a balance/amount-received check is used as a proxy for "the intended payment happened," but any third-party or self-sent "side payment" to the same address can satisfy the check.

### Finding Description
When an arbiter contract's shared address is created, `deriveSharedAddress()` builds an address definition that only allows funds to be *spent* from it according to strict `has output` rules (correct payer/acceptor addresses, correct split amounts, and a guard against combining several contracts by forbidding "other address" inputs). [1](#0-0) 

However, the wallet's local logic that decides a contract has been *paid* does not use these same structural constraints. It listens for `new_my_transactions` and simply checks whether the cumulative sum of outputs sent to `shared_address` (for the contract's asset) reaches the contract's `amount`, regardless of which unit or which author sent them: [2](#0-1) 

As soon as that aggregate threshold is met, the contract status is unconditionally flipped to `"paid"` and dependent business logic fires (notifying the peer, starting to watch the peer's address for private-asset releases, enabling `complete()`/`openDispute()`): [3](#0-2) 

The `shared_address` is not secret — it is published on-chain in the contract-signing `data` message when the contract is created (`createSharedAddressAndPostUnit`), so any unprivileged unit poster who observes the DAG can learn it: [4](#0-3) 

Because the "paid" check only looks at `SUM(outputs.amount) >= amount` to that address/asset, an attacker (or even the contract's own payer, prematurely) can post an ordinary payment unit — with any input source, structured however they like — sending the required asset to `shared_address`. That unrelated/side transfer will be indistinguishable from the real, properly structured escrow payment and will falsely mark the contract `paid`, exactly like the report's "any other token transfer that occurs would be called a side payment" scenario for `ERC20BalanceGteEnforcer`.

### Impact Explanation
A false "paid" signal drives real actions in the escrow workflow: the payee's device is notified the contract is paid and may proceed to `complete()` (releasing goods/service, or for private assets, granting the payer's device the ability to claim funds via `CONTRACT_DONE_` data feed watching), and for private assets the counterpart's device begins watching for fund release, before genuine, correctly-structured funds ever reached the shared address in a spendable way. This can lead to a seller believing they were paid and releasing goods/service, or a party proceeding into the dispute/appeal or completion state machine on a false premise — a concrete loss/fraud-enabling condition for a private-payment counterparty, reachable by any unprivileged unit poster who knows the (publicly disclosed) shared address.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-to-High: exploitation requires no special privileges — only posting an ordinary payment unit of the correct asset to a shared address that is openly published on the DAG as part of normal contract setup. No malicious peer, hub, or node behavior is required; a single unprivileged unit poster suffices.

### Recommendation
Do not rely on a cumulative "balance/amount received at shared_address" check to determine payment completion. Instead:
- Verify that the payment arrived in a unit whose outputs/inputs satisfy the exact structural condition encoded in the shared address's definition (correct payer as author, correct acceptor output amount/split, and the "no other address input" restriction), not just an aggregate sum.
- Alternatively, require the payment unit to be authored by the expected payer address before counting it toward `wallet_arbiter_contracts.amount`.
- Avoid treating any deposit that merely tips the cumulative balance over the threshold as proof of the intended, well-formed payment.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice and Bob start an arbiter contract; the shared multisig address `S` is derived and published on-chain in the contract-signing `data` unit (visible to any DAG observer).
2. Before Bob (the real payer) sends his structured payment, an unrelated third party (or Bob himself, cheaply) posts an ordinary payment unit sending `amount` of the contract's asset from an arbitrary address to `S`.
3. Alice's wallet listener `new_my_transactions` sees `SUM(outputs.amount) >= wallet_arbiter_contracts.amount` for `S` and immediately calls `setField(contract.hash, "status", "paid", ...)`, emitting `arbiter_contract_update` with `status: "paid"`.
4. Alice's device/software proceeds as if the properly structured escrow payment was received (e.g., watching for private-asset release, or becoming eligible to call `complete()`), even though the actual funds sitting at `S` were not sent by the correct payer in the structure required to be spendable per the definition rules in `deriveSharedAddress`.
