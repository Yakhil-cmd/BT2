### Title
RAY-denominated market totals can overflow `i128` before index caps, permanently freezing a market - ([File: common/src/rates/simulate.rs](common/src/rates/simulate.rs))

### Summary

A sufficiently large market can reach an unrecoverable `MathOverflow` during routine interest accrual. `accrue_step` multiplies total scaled debt by the borrow index and total scaled supply by the supply index before applying the index ceiling. Once either product exceeds `i128::MAX`, every subsequent pool operation that synchronizes the market reverts before it can reduce debt, withdraw supply, liquidate, or socialize bad debt. Severity: **Medium**.

### Finding Description

Every pool mutation loads a market through `synced_market`, which calls `interest::global_sync` before the requested operation executes. [1](#0-0)  User-facing repay and withdraw actions therefore cannot reduce the offending positions first because `load_leg` performs accrual before returning the position. [2](#0-1)  The permissionless index-update path follows the same sequence through `ops::market::accrue`. [3](#0-2) 

Inside `accrue_step`, the contract evaluates `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` through `scaled_to_original` before calculating utilization, interest, or the updated capped index. [4](#0-3)  `scaled_to_original` is a checked `Ray` multiplication and reverts when the product is unrepresentable. [5](#0-4) 

The borrow-index ceiling is applied only after `old_index * interest_factor`; it does not cap the scaled-debt value itself. [6](#0-5)  Although both indexes are nominally bounded by `10^36` raw RAY, the finite `i128` domain for scaled totals can be exhausted at a much lower index when the market contains enough shares. [7](#0-6) 

The repository contains a regression test for this exact condition: a one-billion-token, 18-decimal market borrowed to 98% utilization eventually causes `scaled_to_original` to overflow, while the borrow index remains below `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation

Once the condition is reached, the market is permanently frozen for that contract version. The test demonstrates that both `withdraw` and `repay` fail with `MathOverflow` after accrual fails. [10](#0-9) 

The same failure blocks liquidation and bad-debt processing because those controller paths ultimately use pool legs that accrue before mutation. [1](#0-0)  Supplier principal and yield cannot exit through the ordinary pool paths, borrowers cannot reduce the broken scaled-debt total, and liquidators cannot restore account solvency.

`recapitalize` does not provide a rescue path because it also operates through the normal interest-synchronized market flow, and transferring tokens does not reduce the stored `borrowed` or `supplied` share values that overflow. [11](#0-10) [1](#0-0) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires an unusually large book and enough time for either index to grow. For an 18-decimal asset, one billion whole tokens normalize to roughly `10^36` RAY-scaled shares; an index above approximately 170 makes the market-total multiplication approach the `i128` maximum. [8](#0-7) 

A user can create the required state using only public flows by supplying a very large amount of a listed high-decimal asset, supplying collateral in another market, and borrowing most of the first market. After the index grows sufficiently, anyone can trigger the irreversible transition through `update_indexes` with the target `HubAssetKey`. [3](#0-2) 

The practical likelihood is constrained by the required token supply, collateral, borrowable liquidity, and sustained high utilization. On a low-price or high-supply asset, however, the unit count can be economically realistic even when the USD value is much smaller than one billion dollars.

### Recommendation

Enforce representability of market values directly rather than relying only on token-unit caps and global index ceilings.

- Track a per-market effective index bound such that `borrowed * borrow_index` and `supplied * supply_index` always remain inside `i128`.
- Clamp or otherwise bound accrual before calling `scaled_to_original` when a new index would make either total unrepresentable.
- Add an emergency accrual or settlement path that can burn debt/share state without first multiplying the already-unrepresentable market total by its index.
- Extend fuzz and integration coverage across all pool verbs to assert that a market approaching the numeric boundary remains repayable, withdrawable, liquidatable, and cleanable.

The current checked arithmetic is preferable to silent corruption, but it needs a safe terminal behavior instead of allowing the market to enter a state where every subsequent accrual reverts. [12](#0-11) 

### Proof of Concept

1. Create or select an 18-decimal market with a steep rate curve and enough collateral in another market.
2. Supply `1_000_000_000 * 10^18` base units to the target market.
3. Borrow approximately 98% of that liquidity, producing `9.8e26` base units of debt.
4. Allow the borrow index to compound until the scaled debt value exceeds `i128::MAX`.
5. Any account calls the controller's `update_indexes` for `hub_assets = [HubAssetKey { hub_id, asset }]`.
6. The call reaches `accrue_step`, which evaluates `scaled_to_original(borrowed, borrow_index)` and reverts with `MathOverflow`. [13](#0-12) 
7. Later `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, or `clean_bad_debt` attempts also revert because they call `global_sync` before reducing the stored positions. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The existing production-code test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` implements this scenario and asserts both the index-cap bypass and the subsequent repayment/withdrawal freeze. [14](#0-13) [10](#0-9)
