### Title
Bad-debt index floor leaves suppliers with withdrawable claims after a full wipeout - (contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
When bad debt consumes the entire supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the supply index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of zeroing supplier claims. The affected suppliers retain their scaled shares and can withdraw the residual value after a new depositor recapitalizes the empty market, transferring the new depositor's cash to the old supplier and leaving the new depositor with an unbacked claim.

### Finding Description
`clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless controller entrypoint, and cleanup submits every remaining borrow position to `pool_seize_positions_call` as a `Borrow` leg. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The pool converts those scaled debt shares to ceiling-rounded debt and calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` before burning the debt. [3](#0-2) 

`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps the loss at total supplied value, computes a zero remaining-value reduction factor for a complete wipeout, but then clamps the resulting supply index to `RAY / 1000`. [4](#0-3) 

Supplier scaled positions are not burned by this operation; only the market-wide `supply_index` changes. Therefore every pre-existing supplier keeps a residual claim equal to 0.1% of its former value even though the write-down was intended to consume all supplied value. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

A subsequent depositor receives shares and credits fresh cash through `supply`, while withdrawal only checks cash reserves and debt-presence invariants rather than requiring the market to remain backed after the payout. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
An old supplier can withdraw their stranded residual claim as soon as another user deposits enough cash to cover it. The withdrawal pays real custody to the old supplier, while the new supplier's claim remains on the books with no corresponding cash, causing direct loss of user funds.

The loss is bounded by the stranded residual value, approximately `total_wiped_supply / 1000`, but scales with the wiped market. A wiped market with $13 million of former supplier value leaves roughly $13,000 withdrawable from future deposits, matching the scale of the reported incident class.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires a market to enter complete bad-debt socialization, where unpaid debt meets or exceeds total supplied value. This is an extreme market event, but it is reachable through the intended liquidation and permissionless `clean_bad_debt` flow rather than governance or privileged intervention.

After the wipeout, exploitation requires only one fresh deposit or recapitalization large enough to cover the residual claim. The first depositor after cleanup can lose their funds to any former supplier that withdraws first.

### Recommendation
Remove the `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` clamp for full bad-debt wipeouts and allow the supply index to become zero, or explicitly burn/void all remaining supplier positions when `remaining == 0`.

The state transition should make the invariant explicit:

```text
if bad_debt >= total_supplied_value:
    supply_index = 0
    supplied   = 0      # or otherwise invalidate all surviving share claims
```

If nonzero indexes are required by downstream arithmetic, keep `supply_index = SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` but mark the market wiped and reject withdrawals of positions created before the wipeout. Add an end-to-end test that performs cleanup, deposits fresh cash, and verifies that pre-cleanup supplier shares cannot withdraw it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Supplier Alice deposits `1_000_000` units; the market has `supplied = 1_000_000` equivalent shares, `supply_index = RAY`, and matching cash.
2. A borrower becomes insolvent with unpaid debt at least equal to the total supplied value.
3. A liquidator seizes the borrower's collateral, then any caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(caller, borrower_account_id)`.
4. The pool's `Borrow` seize leg calls `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`; `remaining` is zero, but `set_supply_index` clamps the index to `RAY / 1000`.
5. Alice's scaled supply remains intact, so `unscale_supply_floor(alice_scaled)` now returns approximately `1_000` units despite the complete wipeout.
6. Bob calls `supply` and deposits `1_000` units, placing real cash into the pool.
7. Alice calls `withdraw` for her stranded claim. The pool's reserve check sees Bob's cash and transfers it to Alice.
8. Bob still holds nominally valued shares, but the pool's cash is gone; his withdrawal later fails or remains unbacked.

This sequence is directly demonstrated by the repository's own raw-cache regression test, which shows a wiped survivor retaining a stranded claim, a fresh deposit creating insufficient backing, and the survivor extracting the fresh deposit. [9](#0-8)

```rust
pub(crate) fn apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(cache: &mut Cache, bad_debt: Ray) {
    let total_supplied_value = cache.supplied().mul(cache.env(), cache.supply_index());

    if total_supplied_value == Ray::ZERO {
        return;
    }

    let capped = bad_debt.min(total_supplied_value);
    let remaining = total_supplied_value.checked_sub(cache.env(), capped);

    let reduction_factor = remaining.div_floor(cache.env(), total_supplied_value);
    let new_supply_index = cache
        .supply_index()
        .mul_floor(cache.env(), reduction_factor);

    cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```
