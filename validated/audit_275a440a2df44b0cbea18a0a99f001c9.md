## Title
Guaranteed back-running / sandwich extraction against price-sensitive Autonomous Agents via deterministic, attacker-influenceable AA-trigger ordering - (File: main_chain.js, aa_composer.js)

### Summary
The reported 0x issue is that `matchOrders()` gives no ordering guarantee, so anyone who observes a profitable pending call can front-run it for free extractable value. ocore has an analogous exposure: the execution order of Autonomous Agent (AA) triggers is fully deterministic and computed from public, attacker-visible unit metadata (`main_chain_index`, `level`, `unit` hash) rather than from any private/protected sequencing, and a poster can deterministically force their own trigger to execute *after* a specific victim trigger they have observed on the network by referencing it (directly or transitively) as a parent. This lets an attacker who sees a pending trade against a price-sensitive AA (e.g. an AMM-style AA) systematically back-run/sandwich it for profit, exactly the "free MEV extraction" problem the 0x report describes, adapted to ocore's DAG/AA execution model.

### Finding Description
AA triggers are executed in a strictly deterministic order once their `mci` becomes stable: `main_chain.js` collects all trigger units for a stabilized `mci` and inserts them into `aa_triggers` "ORDER BY units.level, units.unit, address" (comment: "deterministic order") [1](#0-0) , and `aa_composer.js`'s `handleAATriggers()` later processes the queue in exactly that same order: `"ORDER BY aa_triggers.mci, level, aa_triggers.unit, address"` [2](#0-1) .

The `level` field used as the primary tiebreaker is not random or fee-driven — it is `max(level of parent units) + 1`, computed in `writer.js`'s `determineMaxLevel`/`updateLevel` [3](#0-2) . Any unprivileged unit poster chooses their own unit's parents when composing a transaction (`parent_composer.js`'s `pickParentUnits`, which selects among currently "free" (tip) units and is only constrained by witness-list compatibility) [4](#0-3) . Units are broadcast to peers as soon as they are validated and before they become stable — `handlePostedJoint` in `network.js` immediately forwards a newly accepted joint to other peers [5](#0-4)  — so the full content of a payment/trigger unit targeting an AA (including its outputs and `data` payload) is visible to the entire network well before the AA actually executes at stabilization time.

Because level is strictly `max(parent levels)+1`, a poster who references a specific already-observed unit `V` as a parent (or as an ancestor via its parents) is *guaranteed* to obtain a level strictly greater than `V`'s level, and therefore, if both units end up in the same stabilized `mci`, the attacker's trigger is guaranteed to be ordered and executed strictly after `V`'s trigger. This is a reliable, zero-cost mechanism to force execution order — no gas auction is even needed, unlike the 0x case, making it in some ways easier to exploit deterministically.

Price-sensitive AAs exist in this exact reachable surface: e.g. the sample `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` AA computes trade ratios from the AA's *current* on-chain balance at the moment of execution (`$current_ratio = $asset_balance / $bytes_balance`, `$investor_share = $mm_asset_amount / var['mm_asset_outstanding']`) [6](#0-5) . Any ordinary user (the AA-trigger sender in scope) can post a payment to such an AA; before that trigger unit stabilizes and is executed, it is already broadcast network-wide, giving any other unprivileged party the ability to observe it and immediately compose their own competing/back-running unit that references it as a parent, guaranteeing their own trade is executed with knowledge of, and strictly after, the victim's balance-changing trade in the same `mci`.

### Impact Explanation
This allows systematic, deterministic extraction of value from any balance/price-dependent AA (AMMs, bonding curves, auctions, games with pooled payouts) by any unprivileged network participant who merely observes broadcast-but-unstable units and reacts by choosing parents. Concretely, an attacker can:
- Observe a large "invest"/"divest" trigger to an AMM-style AA before it executes.
- Immediately submit their own trade referencing that trigger (directly or via any descendant free unit) as a parent, guaranteeing their trigger's `level` (and hence execution order within the same `mci`) is strictly after the victim's.
- Extract the resulting price/ratio movement caused by the victim's trade (classic back-running/sandwich profit), draining value that should have accrued to honest liquidity providers/users of the AA.

This is an AA fund-loss / value-extraction issue reachable by an ordinary AA-trigger sender with no special privileges, satisfying the "concrete unauthorized... AA fund loss" bar. It is medium/high severity because it does not require compromising the network, only ordinary DAG participation, and applies to any AA design that reads its own balance or other mutable state to price trades (a documented, encouraged design pattern per the sample AAs shipped with the codebase).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any AA that is balance/state-price sensitive: the attack requires only (1) observing broadcast joints, which is a routine capability of any full node or light client watching an AA address, and (2) choosing to include the observed unit (or any of its free descendants) as a parent when composing one's own unit — normal wallet composition already picks from currently "free" units, so a minimally customized composer can trivially and reliably enforce this ordering constraint. No race against other parties' fees or hashrate is needed, unlike Ethereum-style front-running, which makes the exploit deterministic once the target unit is visible and not yet stabilized.

### Recommendation
- For AA templates that price trades from current mutable state (balances, `var[...]` state, oracle data), consider commit-reveal or time/price-averaging patterns (e.g., TWAP-like averaging over multiple stabilized `mci`s) so that the price used cannot be deterministically influenced by knowledge of one specific pending trigger.
- Document clearly, in AA authoring guidance and in samples such as `uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, that trigger execution order is deterministic and derivable/forceable via parent selection, and that any AA reading live balances to price trades is exposed to guaranteed back-running by an observer who references the pending trigger as a parent.
- Consider AA-level design primitives (e.g., minimum settlement delay, batched execution of same-mci triggers, or slippage/maximum-price-impact bounds enforced by the AA itself) so individual AAs can defend themselves regardless of DAG-level ordering guarantees, mirroring the "matching relayer" mitigation suggested for `matchOrders()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy (or use) an AMM-style AA such as `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, whose trade ratio is computed from `balance[$asset]` and `balance[base]` at execution time [7](#0-6) .
2. Victim broadcasts a large "invest"/"divest" trigger unit `V` to the AA; per `network.js`, `V` is forwarded to all peers immediately upon acceptance, before it is stable [5](#0-4) .
3. Attacker, watching the network, immediately composes and posts unit `A` that (a) also trades against the AA and (b) includes `V` (or a free unit that already includes `V`) among its `parent_units`.
4. Because level is `max(parent levels)+1` (`writer.js` `determineMaxLevel`/`updateLevel`), `A.level > V.level` is guaranteed [3](#0-2) .
5. When the containing `mci` stabilizes, `main_chain.js` enqueues both triggers ordered by `(level, unit, address)` [1](#0-0) , and `aa_composer.js` executes them in that same order [2](#0-1) , so `A`'s trade against the AA is guaranteed to execute strictly after `V`'s, letting the attacker trade with full knowledge of, and after, the price impact `V` caused.
