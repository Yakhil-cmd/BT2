### Title
Third-party dust top-up keeps insolvent account above the bad-debt collateral cap, permanently gating permissionless cleanup - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/mod.rs)

### Summary
The permissionless bad-debt gate `is_socializable_bad_debt` admits an account only when its **total** collateral is at or below the fixed `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` ($5 WAD). Because `supply` deliberately allows any third party to top up an *existing* supply leg of someone else's account, an unprivileged attacker can repeatedly push a dust-insolvent account's collateral just above $5, making `clean_bad_debt` revert with `CannotCleanBadDebt` and making the post-liquidation auto-cleanup path skip socialization. The analog to CVE-2017-3258 (low-privileged, repeatably invoked availability loss) is a low-cost, repeatable denial of the protocol's only permissionless insolvency-recovery path.

### Finding Description
Two paths converge on the same gate:

- `process_clean_bad_debt` → `clean_bad_debt_standalone` → `socialize_bad_debt`, which asserts `is_socializable_bad_debt(totals.total_debt, totals.total_collateral)` and reverts with `CollateralError::CannotCleanBadDebt` otherwise [1](#0-0) .
- `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` uses the identical predicate to decide whether a liquidation cleans up the residual [2](#0-1) .

The threshold is a compile-time constant, `BAD_DEBT_USD_THRESHOLD` = $5 WAD [3](#0-2) , and the docs confirm there is no second permissionless path: above the dust cap an insolvent account "may require the governed force-socialize runbook" [4](#0-3) .

Meanwhile, `supply` explicitly permits third-party top-ups of existing supply legs: `require_third_party_existing_supply` only rejects legs the account does **not** already hold [5](#0-4) , and `process_deposit` credits the measured receipt to the victim's position [6](#0-5) . Tests confirm the path works for non-owners (`test_third_party_supply_to_existing_account_succeeds`, `regression_non_owner_cannot_open_new_supply_slot_on_victim`) [7](#0-6) .

There is no check that the account is solvent-agnostic or that the supplied collateral is owner-provided; the dust gate only compares aggregate USD collateral against $5.

### Impact Explanation
Denial of service of bad-debt socialization (analogous to the CVE's crash/DoS class). Once an insolvent account's collateral sits above $5:

- `clean_bad_debt` always reverts, so the only remaining path is owner/governance `force_socialize_bad_debt` on the Sensitive delay tier — a privileged recovery the attacker forces the protocol into [8](#0-7) .
- Post-liquidation auto-cleanup silently skips, so each liquidation leaves the account in limbo.
- While cleanup is stalled, the unpaid debt keeps accruing interest, so the eventual supply-index write-down (supplier loss / insolvency) grows.

This fits "temporary freezing of funds"/degraded-protocol-operation: supplier funds locked against unbacked debt cannot be released through the designed permissionless route for as long as the attacker keeps topping up.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any address via `supply(caller, victim_account_id, spoke_id, [(existing_collateral_asset, ~$0.01–$5)])`. Costs: the donated tokens are lost (they become collateral that cleanup later converts to protocol revenue), roughly $5 per grief round, needed only each time a liquidation pushes collateral back under the cap. It is most effective when liquidation is already impaired — e.g., a `no_seize`-flagged or unpriceable collateral leg (documented as blocking liquidation and cleanup alike [9](#0-8) ) — because then nothing else drains the collateral back below the cap.

Caveat: I did not fully verify `validate_position_entry_gates` / `enforce_post_pool_solvency` on the supply path — if supplying to an underwater account is rejected, this vector fails. The available tests demonstrate third-party top-ups on solvent accounts only, so this precondition should be confirmed on an insolvent account before relying on the finding.

### Recommendation
Make the dust gate robust to donated collateral, e.g.:

- Exclude supply legs below a meaningful floor from `total_collateral` in `is_socializable_bad_debt`, or evaluate the gate on collateral attributable to pre-insolvency positions; or
- Track per-leg acquisition provenance / flag third-party-credited legs so cleanup can disregard them; or
- Allow `clean_bad_debt` to proceed whenever `D > C` for insolvent accounts regardless of the dust cap (matching the `InsolventOnly` gate), since the dust cap's purpose is cost-of-cleanup, not security.

### Proof of Concept
1. ALICE supplies USDC, borrows ETH; price crash leaves her insolvent with residual collateral worth $4 (≤ $5 dust cap). `clean_bad_debt(ANYONE, alice_id)` would now succeed.
2. Attacker BOB calls `supply(BOB, alice_id, alice_spoke, [(USDC_hub_key, 2_000_000)])` — a $2 top-up of ALICE's *existing* USDC leg. `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because the leg already exists.
3. `clean_bad_debt(ANYONE, alice_id)` reverts `CannotCleanBadDebt`: `total_collateral ≈ $6 > $5`.
4. A liquidation attempt that seizes collateral back below $5 is met by another top-up; `check_bad_debt_after_liquidation` also skips cleanup via the same predicate.
5. Result: the account's bad debt is never socialized through any permissionless path; only owner/governance `force_socialize_bad_debt` can remove it, while interest keeps accruing to suppliers' detriment.

# Force-socialize bad debt

This runbook covers governance-authorized removal of an insolvent account.
Use it when collateral exceeds the fixed $5 dust limit of permissionless
`clean_bad_debt`. Cleanup can also proceed when `no_seize` blocks ordinary
liquidation.

The account must hold debt, with ceil risk debt strictly greater than half-up
unweighted collateral (`D > C`). An unhealthy health factor alone does not
qualify. The forced path has no collateral dust cap.

**Cleanup is irreversible.** All remaining collateral becomes protocol revenue.
All remaining debt is written off against its own markets' supply indexes,
including when collateral and debt share a market. There is no automatic netting
or insurance payment. The account is deleted and its position NFT burns.
