### Title
Debt valuation overflow during interest accrual permanently freezes a market — (`common/src/rates/index.rs`)

### Summary
The rBPF report's class — an attacker-influenced large value added into unchecked arithmetic — maps onto XOXNO Lending's accrual path: `borrowed * new_borrow_index` is evaluated in `i128` inside `calculate_supplier_rewards` (`common/src/rates/index.rs:80-81`) and `scaled_to_original` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:14-16`), and panics with `MathOverflow` when the product exceeds `i128::MAX`. Because every state-changing verb accrues interest first, once `borrowed × borrow_index` crosses the ceiling the market can never accrue again: `update_indexes`, `repay`, `withdraw`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `recapitalize` all revert. The `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` cap (`common/src/constants/pool.rs:19`, 10^36) is applied after the multiplication in `update_borrow_index` (`index.rs:14`) and cannot prevent the overflow, since `borrowed` alone can already be near `i128::MAX` (the balance ceiling is exactly `i128::MAX / RAY ≈ 170B whole tokens`, per `common/tests/validation.rs:346-376` and `require_cap_within_asset_domain`).

### Finding Description
- An unprivileged user calls `controller.supply` to fill a market to its supply cap, and `controller.borrow` (or repeated borrows) to drive borrowed scaled shares toward the market maximum. `caps` can be set at the per-decimals ceiling where `Ray::from_asset` still fits, i.e. `borrowed` (RAY-scaled) ≈ `i128::MAX / index ≈ 1.7e38` raw.
- Interest accrual (`pool` cache/`update_indexes`, routed through `update_borrow_index` then `calculate_supplier_rewards` / `supply_index_reward_shortfall` / `scaled_to_original`) computes `borrowed.mul(new_index)` in `i128`. `Ray::mul` panics on overflow (`common/src/math/fp.rs`, `fp_core.rs`); unlike `mul_div_floor_saturating`, there is no saturation here.
- Once `borrowed * borrow_index > i128::MAX`, the next accrual panics. Since accrual runs before every mutation, the panic is permanent — index caps clamp the index but not the debt value, so there is no recovery path.
- The repo's own harness demonstrates this end-to-end: `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:316-362` shows a whale market at 98% utilization reaching the RAY-value cliff before the index cap, after which `try_update_indexes_for`, `try_withdraw_raw`, and `try_repay` all fail with `MATH_OVERFLOW` ("the market is frozen: exits and repayments accrue first and hit the same panic").

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of all funds in the affected market: suppliers cannot withdraw, borrowers cannot repay (so liquidations also cannot proceed), and every entrypoint that touches the market reverts. This satisfies "permanent freezing of funds" / "contract unable to operate" for that market's book.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by a single unprivileged address with sufficient capital (or via flash-funded supply): push `borrowed` near the cap, keep utilization high so the borrow index compounds, then wait. No governance action is needed to trigger; only time and utilization. The attacker does not profit directly, so the attack is griefing — but any large organic market at sustained high utilization can cross the cliff accidentally, making this a live correctness bound rather than a theoretical one.

### Recommendation
Widen the accrual intermediates: compute `borrowed * new_index` via `I256` (as `fp_core` already does for `mul_div`) or saturate `scaled_to_original`/`calculate_supplier_rewards` at `i128::MAX` so accrual clamps instead of panicking. Additionally or alternatively, enforce a headroom invariant at listing: require `cap_value_ray * MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY <= i128::MAX` (or the effective index ceiling needed before the index cap engages), so the debt value can never exceed the representable domain.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361`: supply ~10^9 tokens of an 18-decimal asset at lifted caps, borrow ~98% of it, then advance time; `try_update_indexes_for` fails with `MATH_OVERFLOW` at borrow index ≪ `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and subsequent `withdraw`/`repay` calls fail identically — a permanent freeze produced purely by unprivileged `supply`/`borrow` calls plus elapsed time.
