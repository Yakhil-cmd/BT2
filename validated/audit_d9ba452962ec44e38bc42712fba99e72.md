### Title

Unpriceable dust collateral blocks liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - ([File: contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs](contracts/controller/src/risk/totals.rs))

### Summary

A borrower can add a tiny supply position in a listed asset whose oracle is currently stale, then make every liquidation and permissionless bad-debt cleanup for that account revert. `supply()` accepts the deposit without requiring a usable price, while liquidation and cleanup value every supply position through the fail-closed `prices` aggregator call.

### Finding Description

`process_supply()` accepts an owner’s new collateral position and deposits it without loading or validating that asset’s current oracle price. [1](#0-0)  `build_liquidation_plan()` calculates account risk before accepting repayments or planning seizure. [2](#0-1)  `calculate_account_risk_totals()` calls `Context::load_markets()` with the union of all supply and borrow keys, so even a dust collateral leg becomes a required price. [3](#0-2)  `load_markets()` bulk-fetches every missing token price through the strict aggregator. [4](#0-3)  The aggregator’s `prices` entrypoint panics when any requested key cannot resolve to a usable feed. [5](#0-4)  Permissionless `clean_bad_debt()` reaches the same all-position valuation through `socialize_bad_debt()`. [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation

A borrower can delay liquidation while their debt position deteriorates, and the same poisoned leg also blocks the dust-gated bad-debt cleanup that would otherwise socialize the insolvency. The harness demonstrates that after a stale WBTC dust leg is supplied, both `liquidate()` and `clean_bad_debt()` revert with `PRICE_FEED_STALE`, while liquidation succeeds again once WBTC becomes fresh. [7](#0-6)  This is at least temporary freezing of the protocol’s liquidation and cleanup path for the affected account; if the outage persists while collateral value falls, it can contribute to protocol insolvency.

### Likelihood Explanation

The path is reachable by an unprivileged borrower using `supply()` on their own account, followed by permissionless `liquidate()` or `clean_bad_debt()`. The attacker does not need to manipulate the oracle maliciously; they need only choose a listed market whose configured feed is already stale or later becomes stale. The added amount can be dust because the implementation requests prices for position keys before checking whether that collateral leg is economically relevant. [8](#0-7) 

### Recommendation

Do not make unrelated collateral legs unconditionally fatal to liquidation and cleanup. Options include valuing a zero-threshold or de-minimis leg as zero for the health and dust gates after proving its scaled balance is economically insignificant, or building liquidation from only the debt legs plus collateral legs that can produce a planned seizure. Bad-debt cleanup should similarly avoid requiring a fresh price for a leg that cannot affect the insolvency decision, while retaining strict pricing for every amount that can be paid out or credited.

### Proof of Concept

The repository already contains a focused reproduction in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/audit_supply_stale_shield.rs`: it supplies WBTC after setting its Reflector timestamp 3,600 seconds old, confirms the supply is accepted, crashes USDC to make the account liquidatable, and observes `PRICE_FEED_STALE` from both `liquidate()` and `clean_bad_debt()`. [9](#0-8)  Refreshing WBTC makes the identical liquidation succeed and reduce the borrower’s ETH debt. [10](#0-9)
