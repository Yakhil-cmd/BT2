### Title
Paused market blocks borrower cure (repay/supply) while liquidation seizure remains executable — ([File: contracts/controller/src/config/asset.rs](contracts/controller/src/config/asset.rs), [File: common/src/types/controller.rs](common/src/types/controller.rs))

### Summary
The M-11 class maps directly onto XOXNO Lending's per-spoke-asset halt flags. `SpokeAssetConfig.paused` blocks "entry and exit legs" for a market (new supply, withdrawals, borrows, repayments), but per ADR-0008 it **deliberately does not gate the liquidation seizure leg**. A borrower whose debt asset or collateral asset is paused therefore cannot restore their health factor by repaying debt or posting collateral, yet any unprivileged liquidator can still call `liquidate` and seize their collateral — the same forced-liquidation class as the Hubble Exchange finding.

### Finding Description
In `common/src/types/controller.rs`, `SpokeAssetConfig` carries three independent halt flags (`paused`, `frozen`, `no_seize`). The type's own documentation states the asymmetry:

- "`paused` blocks entry and exit legs, `frozen` blocks entry but allows exit"
- "`paused` does not gate seizure, because seizure is pro-rata across an account's whole collateral set: pausing one collateral would otherwise halt liquidation of every account holding it. See ADR-0008."

In `contracts/controller/src/config/asset.rs`, governance can tighten flags instantly via `set_spoke_asset_flags` (lines 112–124) with `require_flag_ratchet` (lines 175–187) allowing any false→true transition immediately; relaxation requires the timelocked `relax_spoke_asset_flags` path (lines 129–146). Each flag change also bumps `SpokeFlagsEpoch` (line 162).

The consequence: when a debt asset's market is paused, the borrower's `repay` leg reverts (paused blocks exit), so they cannot reduce debt. When a collateral asset is paused, `supply` (entry) is blocked, so they cannot add margin — the exact `addMargin` analogue. Meanwhile the liquidation path in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs` is designed to proceed: seizure is pro-rata across the whole collateral set and `paused` is not consulted on the seizure leg (only `no_seize` restricts it). An unprivileged liquidator calls `liquidate(account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer | Credit)` and takes collateral with the HF-based bonus while the borrower is locked out of every cure path.

This is strictly stronger than the original M-11 (which needed an unpause + frontrun): here the unfair liquidation can execute *during* the pause itself, since there is no global pause that also halts liquidation.

### Impact Explanation
A borrower loses collateral (plus liquidation bonus and fees) through no fault of their own: the protocol's own halt flag removes every mechanism they have (`repay`, `supply`/`addMargin` analogue, possibly `repay_debt_with_collateral` since its internal repay/withdraw legs touch paused markets) while leaving the seizure path open. This is theft/loss of user funds under protocol state conditions, matching the validated medium-severity precedents (Hubble M-11, Blueberry #118, Notional #203, Perennial #190).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires governance to pause a market that has open borrows — a plausible operational action (the freeze-a-listing runbook and instant flag ratchet exist precisely for emergencies). Once paused, every liquidator bot is incentivized to act immediately; the borrower has zero recourse, not even a frontrun race. The severity mitigations are that pausing is per-(spoke, asset) rather than global and seizure-ungating is a documented design choice for collateral — but the same rationale does not justify blocking the *debt repay* leg while leaving liquidation live, which is what converts this from design trade-off into forced liquidation.

### Recommendation
Decouple the cure paths from the pause flags, mirroring the recommended fix for M-11:

1. Always permit debt-reducing operations (`repay`, and the repay leg of `liquidate`/`repay_debt_with_collateral`) on paused markets — a repayment only shrinks risk and cannot worsen protocol solvency.
2. Allow `supply` on paused markets when the asset remains `is_collateralizable`, or at minimum allow collateral top-ups for accounts with open borrows in that spoke.
3. Alternatively/additionally, introduce a grace period after `relax_spoke_asset_flags` (flag clears) before liquidations on newly unpaused markets execute, e.g., a per-market `liquidatable_after` timestamp written when `paused` clears, checked in the liquidation plan builder in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/plan.rs`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance calls `set_spoke_asset_flags(spoke_id, hub_asset=DEBT_ASSET, paused=true, ...)` — allowed instantly by the ratchet (`asset.rs:182-186`) — after a borrower's health factor deteriorates near 1.
2. Borrower calls `repay(account_id, DEBT_ASSET, amount)` → reverts, because `paused` blocks exit legs (`controller.rs:103-107`).
3. Borrower calls `supply` on their (paused) collateral market → reverts, entry legs blocked.
4. Liquidator (unprivileged) calls `liquidate(account_id, payments, SeizeMode::Transfer)`. The plan builder does not consult `paused` on the seizure leg (ADR-0008), so seizure executes pro-rata across the borrower's collateral with the full HF-based bonus.
5. Borrower is liquidated despite being willing and able to cure — identical loss outcome to Hubble M-11, judged Medium.
