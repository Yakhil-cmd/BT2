### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor preserves wiped-out shares and drains later deposits - ([File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs])

### Summary
When bad debt meets or exceeds the market’s total supplied value, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the reduced supply index to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` instead of recording a complete write-down. [1](#0-0)  Existing suppliers therefore retain a positive residual claim even though the socialized loss was intended to consume all supplied value.

### Finding Description
The permissionless `clean_bad_debt` path admits an account when it has debt, its USD debt exceeds its USD collateral, and remaining collateral is at or below the dust threshold. [2](#0-1)  Cleanup submits all supply positions as deposit-side seizures and all debt positions as borrow-side seizures to the pool. [3](#0-2) 

For a borrow-side seizure, the pool converts the scaled debt to its ceiling-rounded current value, socializes that amount through `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, and burns the debt shares. [4](#0-3)  If that value is at least `supplied * supply_index`, the capped loss leaves `remaining == 0`, making `reduction_factor` and `new_supply_index` zero; the subsequent `.max(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)` restores the index to `RAY / 1000` instead of leaving all suppliers fully wiped out. [1](#0-0) 

A later depositor mints shares at the floored index and adds real tokens to `cash`. [5](#0-4)  A wiped-out supplier can then call controller `withdraw` with amount `0`, which the controller treats as withdraw-all. [6](#0-5)  The pool resolves a full withdrawal to the position’s floor-unscaled value, requires only that booked `cash` cover it, debits cash, and transfers the tokens. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
A supplier whose claim should have been reduced to zero can extract tokens supplied by a later depositor, causing theft of user funds and immediate market insolvency for the later depositor’s claim. The residual payout equals `old_scaled_supply * RAY / 1000`, so a sufficiently large pre-wipeout supplier can drain up to 0.1% of its former scaled position from fresh deposits.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires a borrow-side cleanup where the debt value meets or exceeds the market’s total supplied value, while the account’s remaining collateral is within the public dust threshold. This can arise when accrued borrow interest or collateral-price movement makes a position deeply insolvent; no privileged role, leaked key, bad parameter, direct pool call, or external-token assumption is required because `clean_bad_debt` is permissionless and the later supply and withdrawal use ordinary controller entrypoints. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Recommendation
Represent a complete supply write-down explicitly instead of clamping the resulting index upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, mark the market as wiped out and make existing supply positions resolve to zero, while preventing new supply from minting against a zeroed or floored wiped index until the market is safely reinitialized. Do not merely set the index to zero without a wipeout flag, because share-to-asset and asset-to-share conversions must have a defined zero-index result.

### Proof of Concept
Assume market `X` has zero-decimal accounting, `supply_index == RAY`, no outstanding cash after lending, attacker account `A` holding scaled supply corresponding to `1_000_000` units, and victim account `V` holding debt in `X` whose current value is at least `1_000_000` while `V`’s total collateral is at or below the dust threshold.

1. Any caller invokes `controller.clean_bad_debt(caller, V)`.
2. Cleanup emits a borrow-side `PoolSeizeEntry` for `V`’s `X` debt. [12](#0-11) 
3. The pool calculates `total_supplied_value == 1_000_000`, caps the bad debt at that amount, computes `remaining == 0`, and then stores `supply_index == RAY / 1000`. [1](#0-0) 
4. `A`’s old scaled supply still resolves to `1_000_000 * 0.001 == 1_000` units because supply claims are unscaled by the new index. [13](#0-12) 
5. Victim depositor `B` invokes `controller.supply(B, B_account, spoke_id, [(X, 1_000)])`, transferring `1_000` units into pool custody.
6. `A` invokes `controller.withdraw(A, A_account, [(X, 0)], Some(A))`; amount `0` resolves as a full withdrawal. [6](#0-5) 
7. The pool burns `A`’s old shares, sees booked cash of `1_000`, and transfers `1_000` units to `A`. [8](#0-7) [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 
8. `B` retains supply shares but the market has no remaining cash to satisfy `B`’s `1_000`-unit claim.
