### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor leaves phantom supplier claims that drain recapitalized or repaid cash first-come-first-served - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes a wiped borrow by scaling the supply index down, but clamps the result up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) instead of letting it reach zero (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`). The scaled-share balances (`supplied`, and each account's `scaled_amount` in the controller) are untouched, so every supplier retains a residual claim of roughly 0.1% of face value even when the bad debt consumed 100% of total supplied value. Withdrawals are gated only by `require_reserves` (cash on hand), not by the backing check, so once any cash re-enters the market — borrower repayments or `recapitalize` top-ups — the first wiped supplier to call `withdraw` extracts real tokens they are no longer economically entitled to.

### Finding Description
The bug-class analog of JLSEC-2026-316 (a heap overflow in `H5HG_read` corrupting internal state) is an unchecked state-clamping write: a bounds clamp on the supply index corrupts the accounting invariant that wiped suppliers hold zero claim.

In `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:24-28`, a `Borrow`-side `PoolSeizeEntry` (produced by permissionless `liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt` in `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/bad_debt.rs:35-49`) computes `bad_debt = unscale_borrow_ceil_ray(position)` and calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. When `bad_debt >= total_supplied_value`, `remaining` is zero, `reduction_factor` is zero, and `new_supply_index` would be zero — but line 88 clamps it to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`:

```rust
cache.set_supply_index(new_supply_index.max(Ray::from(SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)));
```

Nothing burns the wiped suppliers' scaled shares. Each account still holds `scaled_amount`, and `unscale_supply_floor(scaled, RAY/1000)` returns a positive amount. The pool's own test demonstrates the mechanics: after a full wipe, `resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, scaled_a)` returns `gross > 0` and `debit_cash` pays it out, draining freshly credited cash exactly (`contracts/pool/tests/cache.rs:339-368`).

Guards do not close the hole:
- `require_backed_market` (`contracts/pool/src/guards.rs:52-58`) blocks *new supply* while the shortfall exists, but not withdrawals.
- Withdrawals only run `require_reserves` (cash ≥ gross) — exits deliberately skip caps and backing checks (`contracts/pool/README.md:141`, `guards.rs:38` "Exits do not").
- `recapitalize` credits cash without minting shares, directly refilling the pot that phantom claims draw against.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user/protocol funds. After a full-wipe bad-debt event, any later cash credited to the market — interest-bearing borrower repayments via `repay`, or a `recapitalize` top-up intended to restore backing — is claimable by wiped suppliers' residual balances. Because withdrawal is first-come-first-served, a wiped supplier (the attacker) front-runs other wiped suppliers and the recapitalizer, extracting up to ~0.1% of their original deposit that should have been fully socialized away. The attacker needs only to have held a supply position in the wiped market — which they can have acquired cheaply, or even hold as the same account that generated the bad debt.

### Likelihood Explanation
The path is reachable by a single unprivileged address:
1. Hold a supply position `scaled_amount` in market `(hub_id, token)` via `controller::supply`.
2. Trigger bad debt (e.g., a dust-threshold liquidation leaves unseizable debt, or a separate position's collateral fully devalues) and call permissionless `controller::liquidate` or `clean_bad_debt`, which emits a `PoolSeizeEntry{side: Borrow}` → `seize_positions` → `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps the index to `RAY/1000`.
3. Wait for (or induce, via their own `repay`) cash to enter the market, or for governance/`recapitalize` to refill the shortfall — the documented recovery path (`docs/reference/formulas.md:397-401`).
4. Call `controller::withdraw` with a large `amount`; `resolve_withdrawal` caps to the phantom scaled balance, `require_reserves` passes against the fresh cash, and tokens transfer out.

Likelihood is Medium: it requires a market that actually reaches a ≥100% bad-debt wipe (rare, but the dust-cleanup and `clean_bad_debt` paths exist precisely for this) and subsequent cash inflow, which the protocol's own `recapitalize` runbook encourages.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp the post-wipe supply index up to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` when `remaining == 0` and the write-down consumed the entire supplied value; instead zero out `supplied`/`revenue` shares (or burn them into a tombstone state) so no residual claim survives. Alternatively, track the clamp residual and subtract it from future claims, or gate withdrawals on `backing_shortfall == 0` for markets whose index sits at the floor. At minimum, `recapitalize` should mint no new withdrawable value for pre-existing phantom shares.

### Proof of Concept
The pool's own unit test `test_raw_cache_floor_clamp_strands_claim_without_supply_guard` in `contracts/pool/tests/cache.rs:373-427` demonstrates the primitive end-to-end at the cache layer:

```rust
apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index(&mut cache, Ray::from(5_000 * RAY)); // bad_debt >> 1_000*RAY supplied value
assert_eq!(cache.supply_index().raw(), SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW);     // clamped UP, not zeroed
let stranded = cache.unscale_supply_floor(old_scaled);              // phantom claim > 0
// ... fresh cash credited ...
let (burn, gross) = cache.resolve_withdrawal(i128::MAX, old_scaled);
assert_eq!(gross, fresh_cash);                                      // drained exactly the fresh deposit
```

On-chain reproduction: supply `S` in market `M`; a borrower position in `M` becomes bad debt ≥ total supplied value; call `clean_bad_debt`/`liquidate` (permissionless) so `seize_positions` writes down the supply index to the floor while leaving `scaled_amount` intact; then `recapitalize`/`repay` credits cash `c ≥ unscale_supply_floor(S, RAY/1000)`; call `withdraw(i128::MAX)` — `require_reserves` passes and `transfer_out` pays the phantom claim, stealing funds meant to restore backing or owed to other suppliers.
