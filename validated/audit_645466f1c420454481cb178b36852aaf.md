### Title
Bad-debt cleanup clamps `supply_index` to a floor instead of zeroing wiped claims, leaving freed collateral claims that drain later depositors - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The wolfSSL report is a use-after-free where an error-cleanup path operates on memory that was already freed. The structural analog in XOXNO Lending is the bad-debt socialization path: `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` frees an insolvent account's positions and writes the loss down into the market's `supply_index` via `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`. That write-down is clamped to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) rather than driving surviving scaled shares to zero, so supply shares whose backing was just "freed" (seized/written off) retain a phantom claim of up to 0.1% of pre-wipeout supply. Any holder of those stale scaled shares can later withdraw real tokens deposited by fresh suppliers.

### Finding Description
- Permissionless `clean_bad_debt` calls `socialize_bad_debt` with `BadDebtGate::DustCapped`, which admits any account whose ceil debt exceeds half-up collateral while collateral is at or below the ~$5 dust cap, then calls `bad_debt::execute_bad_debt_cleanup`. [1](#0-0) 
- The cleanup seizes all remaining supply and debt positions, releases spoke usage, calls `pool_seize_positions_call`, and deletes the account — i.e., it frees the collateral backing that supply shares pointed to. [2](#0-1) 
- On the pool side, the write-down does not zero the freed claims: `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` clamps `supply_index` upward to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`, so `unscale_supply_floor` of pre-existing `scaled_amount` still returns a positive claim even though the corresponding debt/collateral was wiped. The in-repo test proves the sequence: wipeout clamps the index to the floor, the old scaled amount still unscales to `stranded > 0`, a fresh supplier deposits `fresh_cash`, and the stranded position's `resolve_withdrawal` withdraws `gross == fresh_cash`, leaving `cash < fresh_claim`. [3](#0-2) 
- The invariant doc acknowledges the mechanism ("The index floor can leave a shortfall") but documents only recapitalization as the remedy; it does not prevent the stale-claim withdrawal that steals the fresh deposit before any recapitalize can occur. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds and protocol insolvency. After a large bad-debt event (debt ≫ remaining collateral, easily reached when prior liquidation legs stripped most collateral and left ≤$5 dust), the floor clamp preserves ~0.1% of all pre-existing scaled supply as unbacked claims. First-come withdrawals of those phantom claims are paid out of subsequent depositors' principal: the test shows the drained amount equals exactly the fresh deposit. An unprivileged user both triggers the condition (permissionless `clean_bad_debt`) and can hold/collect the surviving shares to withdraw first.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged and deterministic once an insolvent dust-collateral account exists: `process_clean_bad_debt` only requires `require_auth`, no flash loan, open debt, and `is_socializable_bad_debt` — no listing-flag or pause gate blocks standalone cleanup. [5](#0-4)  The clamp engages whenever the write-down factor falls below 1/1000, which is the common case for genuine bad debt (collateral capped at ~$5 against arbitrarily larger debt).

### Recommendation
In `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs`) and the seize/write-off path (`contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs`), burn or rescale surviving supply shares to match actual residual backing instead of clamping `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. If the floor must be retained for math safety, cap each position's withdrawable amount by a global haircut factor derived from real remaining cash, so post-wipeout `resolve_withdrawal` cannot pay unbacked claims out of new deposits.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker supplies dust collateral, borrows heavily, position becomes deeply insolvent; ordinary liquidations strip collateral down to ≤$5 while most debt remains.
2. Attacker (or anyone) calls `controller.clean_bad_debt(account_id)` — permissionless. The cleanup seizes the account, deletes it, and the pool applies `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which clamps `supply_index` to `RAY/1000` rather than zeroing claims. [6](#0-5) 
3. Attacker supplies `fresh_cash` into the market (or waits for any new supplier).
4. A holder of pre-wipeout scaled shares calls `withdraw` — `resolve_withdrawal` unscales at the floored index, `require_reserves` passes because the book counts phantom claims, and the payout equals the fresh deposit while the fresh supplier's claim is now unbacked. [7](#0-6) 

Caveat: I verified the clamp behavior and theft sequence via `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs` and the invariant doc, but did not read the body of `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` in `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs` directly, so the exact clamp expression is inferred from the test and `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` references.
