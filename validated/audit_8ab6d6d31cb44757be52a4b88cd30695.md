### Title
Rounding asymmetry in `normalize` truncates collateral to $0 while rounding debt up to $1, causing solvent sub-dollar positions to be seized in `liquidate-multi` - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`, function `calculate-asset-notional-value`)

### Summary
Attacker A calls `liquidate-multi` targeting victim B's position, whose collateral notional (`normalize (* coll-amount price) decimals false`, floor-rounded) truncates just below $1 to $0 while B's debt notional (`normalize (* actual price) decimals true`, ceiling-rounded) rounds up to $1, making the position appear undercollateralized purely due to rounding rather than true insolvency. B loses their full collateral to A's liquidation call even though the raw (pre-rounding) collateral value exceeds the raw debt value.

### Finding Description
`calculate-asset-notional-value` at [1](#0-0)  computes collateral notional with `round-up = false` (floor) and debt notional with `round-up = true` (ceiling) via `normalize` at [2](#0-1) . This asymmetric rounding is used throughout health/liquidation eligibility checks (`is-healthy`, `get-notional-evaluation`) at [3](#0-2) , and liquidation flows in `process-debt-asset`/`process-collateral-asset` at [4](#0-3)  similarly floor collateral and can trigger seizure once a position is flagged unhealthy.

However, I was unable to locate and fully trace the actual `liquidate` / `liquidate-multi` public function body (only `call-liquidate`, the batch helper, was found) to confirm the exact health-check gate that decides whether a position is eligible for liquidation at all, and whether any minimum-debt threshold, dust guard, or `SEIZURE_BOUND`-style invariant check exists elsewhere in the contract that would reject a liquidation attempt on a position whose debt is only $1 due to rounding. Without seeing that gating logic and the full `liquidate` function, I cannot confirm end-to-end that A's call would actually succeed and fully drain B's collateral versus being rejected by other checks (e.g., minimum liquidatable debt amounts, or the position simply never being flagged unhealthy at these values since $1 debt vs $0 collateral is an edge case requiring extremely tiny position sizes that may be blocked by other minimums).

### Impact Explanation
If confirmed, B would suffer permanent loss of their full seized collateral for a technically-solvent position — Critical severity (direct theft of user funds). This is contingent on verifying the liquidation entry gate.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires extremely fine-tuned position sizing at sub-dollar granularity (collateral notional in the $0.99999...–$1.00000 band, debt notional in the $1.00000001 band) — a narrow, engineered edge case rather than a naturally occurring one, and its exploitability depends entirely on the unverified gating logic in `liquidate`/`liquidate-multi`.

### Recommendation
If confirmed after reviewing the full `liquidate` function: apply consistent (or bounded) rounding direction between collateral and debt notional calculations in `calculate-asset-notional-value`, or add a materiality/dust threshold so liquidations cannot be triggered by sub-unit rounding artifacts alone.

```text
(define-private (normalize (value uint) (decimals uint) (round-up bool))
  (let ((decimal-factor (pow u10 decimals)))
    (if round-up
      (div-up value decimal-factor)
      (div-down value decimal-factor))))
```
