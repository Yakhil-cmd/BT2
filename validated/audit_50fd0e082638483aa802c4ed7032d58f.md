### Title
Unbounded Dust-Output Griefing on AA Custom-Asset Payments Inflates Unit Size and Drains AA Byte Balance - ([File: aa_composer.js])

### Summary
When an Autonomous Agent (AA) composes an outgoing payment in a custom asset (e.g. `{asset: "{$asset}", outputs: [...]}` or a "send-all" output), `aa_composer.js` selects unspent outputs belonging to the AA's address via `readStableOutputs()`/`readUnstableOutputsSentByAAs()` and consumes them one by one in `iterateUnspentOutputs()` until the target amount is reached. For the base asset, the query explicitly filters out dust (`amount>=FULL_TRANSFER_INPUT_SIZE`) specifically to prevent a known dust-spam attack, but this filter is applied **only when `asset` is null (base)**. For any custom asset there is no minimum-amount filter at all, so any unprivileged unit poster can send an unlimited number of arbitrarily small (e.g. 1-unit) outputs of that asset to the AA's address, all of which will later be pulled in as inputs whenever the AA needs to pay out in that asset.

### Finding Description
The dust protection is implemented and explained in-line: [1](#0-0) 
The comment states this filter exists "to prevent dust attack: spamming the AA with very small outputs so that the AA spends all its money for fees when it tries to respond" — but the `amount>=FULL_TRANSFER_INPUT_SIZE` condition is nested inside the `asset ? ... : ...` ternary and only applies to the base-asset branch. Custom-asset dust outputs pass through unfiltered.

These outputs are then greedily consumed in `iterateUnspentOutputs()`, which keeps adding `input`s and increasing `total_amount` until the target is met: [2](#0-1) 

The number of inputs pulled directly increases the composed unit's `payload_commission`/`headers_commission`, which is paid in bytes from the AA's own base balance, regardless of the fact that the payment being sent is denominated in a custom asset: [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation
Since sending outputs to an arbitrary address (including an AA's address) requires no special privilege — any unprivileged unit poster can send a "payment" message — an attacker can pre-emptively flood an AA with a large number of minimal (1-unit) outputs of a custom asset the AA is known to hold/manage (e.g. the market-maker/share/team assets used in `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript` and `test/samples/51_attack_game.oscript`, or any asset issued by an AA). The next time the AA needs to pay out in that asset — including ordinary "send" and "send-all" (`amount === undefined`) messages that are pervasive in bonding-curve/AMM/divest patterns like `balance[$asset]`-based payouts — the composer is forced to consume potentially hundreds or thousands of dust inputs sorted deterministically by `main_chain_index, unit, output_index`. This inflates the unit size dramatically, causing the AA to pay disproportionately large byte fees out of its own base balance (fund loss / drain), and in extreme cases can push the response unit past the maximum allowed message/unit size, causing the trigger to bounce and potentially permanently prevent the AA from ever completing that payout in that asset (fund freezing / denial of service), since the dust inputs remain unspent and keep being re-selected on every subsequent attempt.

### Likelihood Explanation
High likelihood: the attack requires only ordinary payment messages sent to a known AA address holding a custom asset — no special timing, front-running, or privileged access is required, unlike the original front-running-based DoS. It is straightforward to script and cheap to execute since custom-asset outputs of arbitrarily small size are free to create (subject only to normal issuance/transfer rules of that asset).

### Recommendation
Apply the same minimum-output-size / minimum-amount dust filter used for the base asset to custom-asset unspent-output selection in `readStableOutputs()` and `readUnstableOutputsSentByAAs()` in `aa_composer.js`, or otherwise bound/limit the number of inputs consumed per payment message (e.g., cap iterations and fail/skip further dust rather than exhausting the AA's byte balance), and ensure any resulting oversize condition safely bounces rather than silently draining AA reserves.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy or identify an AA that manages a custom, transferable asset (e.g. the market-maker share asset in `test/samples/uniswap_like_market_maker.oscript`, where the "divest" case pays out `balance[$asset]`-derived amounts).
2. As an unprivileged attacker, submit many separate payment units, each sending 1 unit of that asset to the AA's address (no special privilege needed for a transferable asset).
3. Trigger (or wait for) a legitimate user to invoke a case in the AA that causes it to send a payment (including "send-all") denominated in that asset.
4. Observe that `readStableOutputs()`/`iterateUnspentOutputs()` in `aa_composer.js` pulls in all the attacker's dust outputs as inputs (no size filter for non-base assets), inflating `objUnit.payload_commission`/`headers_commission`, and causing the AA to pay excessive byte fees from `balance[base]`, potentially bouncing the response entirely if the resulting unit exceeds size limits.
