### Title
Stale delegate grant silently re-arms when a position NFT returns to its granting owner, restoring arbitrary withdrawal/borrow authority without fresh consent - (File: contracts/controller/src/storage/account.rs)

### Summary
The controller stores delegate grants keyed by `(account_id, granted_by)` and resolves authority as `get_delegates(env, account_id, owner).contains(caller)` where `owner` is the *current* NFT holder (`is_owner_or_delegate`, `contracts/controller/src/account.rs:115-127`). An NFT transfer disables the grant only implicitly — by changing which `granted_by` key is consulted — and never deletes it. When the token is transferred back to `granted_by`, the dormant grant becomes fully active again with no new `add_delegate` call and no event. This mirrors CVE-2022-34468's class: a capability that was disabled in one context (a frame/owner) is reactivated by an ordinary user action (a click / an NFT transfer) without re-establishing the permission.

### Finding Description
Per `contracts/position-nft/README.md` (lines 174-179) and `is_owner_or_delegate` at `contracts/controller/src/account.rs:121-127`:

- `add_delegate` records the grant under the granting owner's address and requires the delegate to be an *active* approved position manager (`set_account_delegate`, `account.rs:242-258`).
- On `position-nft::transfer`, the controller writes nothing; the grant stays in storage keyed by the old owner, so `get_delegates(account_id, new_owner)` returns empty — the delegate looks revoked.
- If the NFT ever returns to `granted_by` (direct transfer, `transfer_from` by a stale operator approval, a marketplace round-trip, or an accidental send-back), `get_delegates(account_id, granted_by)` again contains the manager, and `is_owner_or_delegate` passes immediately.
- Delegates "may borrow or withdraw to arbitrary recipients" (`skills/xoxno-lending-contracts/positions.md:223-226`) and reach `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, `flash_position`, `swap_debt`, `swap_collateral`, `repay_debt_with_collateral`, and `migrate_from_blend` via `require_owner_or_delegate` (`account.rs:130-140`).

The intervening holder's `add_delegate` only overwrites the stale list if they actually call it; a holder who never manages delegates leaves the dormant grant intact. There is no nonce, epoch, or transfer hook that invalidates `DelegateGrant` on ownership change — revocation depends entirely on the *identity* of the current owner matching a key that was written under a previous ownership epoch.

### Impact Explanation
A position manager (or anyone controlling a compromised/exploitable approved manager contract) that was once delegated on an account regains full spending authority over that account the moment the NFT returns to the grantor — including while the account now holds collateral and debt accumulated or modified by an intervening owner. The delegate can `withdraw` all collateral or `borrow` maximum debt `to: <itself>`, draining the account's equity. This is theft of user funds reachable by an unprivileged path (`position-nft::transfer` / `transfer_from`) combined with a stale stored grant.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: (1) the grantor previously delegated to a manager that is still `is_active`; (2) the NFT left and returned to the grantor without the grantor calling `remove_delegate` and without an intervening owner overwriting the delegate list. NFT positions are designed to be transferable/sellable, and round-trips through marketplaces, escrow, or a user's own secondary wallet are plausible. The grantor reasonably believes the delegation ended at transfer ("Delegates lapse on transfer" is the documented behavior), so they have no reason to revoke. Because the re-arm is silent (no `AccountDelegateEvent`), neither the returning owner nor off-chain monitors can observe it. Medium likelihood.

### Recommendation
Invalidate grants on ownership change rather than keying authority liveness on `granted_by == current owner`:
- Store a transfer epoch/generation counter per `account_id` (incremented via a controller-notified NFT transfer hook, or by recording `owner` inside the grant and comparing at check time *and* clearing on first observation of a new owner), and reject grants whose recorded owner differs from a stamped owner-at-grant-time.
- Alternatively, delete the delegate list entry on the first controller call that observes an owner different from `granted_by`, so the grant can never re-arm.
- At minimum, require `add_delegate` to re-assert consent after any ownership change by binding the grant to the owner *at grant time* plus a monotonically increasing transfer counter.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice supplies 100,000 USDC to account `A` and calls `add_delegate(A, M)` where `M` is an active position manager.
2. Alice calls `position-nft::transfer(Alice → Bob, token_id=A)`. `get_delegates(A, Bob)` is empty; `M` is rejected by `require_owner_or_delegate` — the grant appears dead.
3. Bob transfers the NFT back to Alice (sale reversal, escrow release, or Alice's own round-trip). No controller state changes; the `DelegateGrant` keyed by `(A, Alice)` is untouched.
4. `M` calls `withdraw(caller=M, account_id=A, withdrawals=[all USDC], to=Some(M))`. `is_owner_or_delegate` resolves `owner = Alice`, `get_delegates(A, Alice).contains(M)` is true, the solvency gate on a now-collateral-stripped account is evaluated per normal rules, and funds move to `M` — without Alice ever re-authorizing `M` after the transfer.
