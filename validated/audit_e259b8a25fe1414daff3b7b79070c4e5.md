### Title
Overpayment refund pays out of pool custody without debiting the cash book — (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs)

### Summary
The kernel bug is a resource leak on an error path: `ax88179_tx_fixup()` returns NULL without freeing the skb, and the caller's NULL check means the resource is never released. The XOXNO analog is a custody leak on a refund path: `ops::repay::apply` and `ops::recapitalize::apply` transfer the "unused" portion of a declared payment back to the payer out of real pool custody, while only crediting the *applied* portion to `cash`. The refund leg debits no book and passes no `require_reserves` check, so a payment that never actually arrived still drains tokens, leaving `cash` overstating custody — a leak of supplier funds on the overpayment path.

### Finding Description
`repay` resolves `action.amount` into `(burned, overpayment)` via `cache.resolve_repay`, credits only `net_repay = amount - overpayment` to cash, commits, and then unconditionally calls `outcome.cache.transfer_out(payer, outcome.overpayment)` (contracts/pool/src/ops/repay.rs:32,44-57). The refund is derived from the *declared* amount, not from measured inbound receipt or the cash book, and `transfer_out` sends pool-held tokens without debiting `cash` or running `require_reserves`.

On a market with zero outstanding debt, `current_debt_ceil` is zero, so `resolve_repay` takes the full-close branch, `net_repay` is zero, and the `RepayRoundsToZeroShares` assert passes on its `net_repay == 0` disjunct — the entire declared amount becomes `overpayment` and is paid out of real custody while the book is untouched. The same gap exists in `ops::recapitalize::apply`, which refunds `amount - backing_shortfall` the same way (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:34,52-58). This is demonstrated by the repo's own tests: `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` shows a payer refunded `custody_before` in full, the pool's entire custody leaving the contract, and `cash` unchanged so the market "reports itself solvent and cannot pay" (contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3378-3410, 3578-3612).

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged address calling `repay` (or `recapitalize`) with a declared amount exceeding the actual debt/shortfall receives the difference from pool custody — including amounts it never transferred in. Once `cash` exceeds real balance, `require_reserves` admits exits that then fail inside the SAC transfer: withdrawals and borrows revert, suppliers' funds are frozen, and the market is insolvent. This is theft of user funds followed by permanent freezing of funds for the drained market.

### Likelihood Explanation
Single unprivileged call: `repay` on a freshly listed or fully-repaid market, or `recapitalize` with `amount` above the shortfall. No special permissions, timing, or market conditions are required beyond a listed market — the pool endorses the declared amount without verifying custody covers the refund.

### Recommendation
Debit the refund from the cash book (or enforce `require_reserves`/`cash >= refund`) inside `transfer_out` for these legs, and measure the actual inbound transfer rather than trusting the declared `amount` — mirror the measured-receipt settlement used by the flash paths.

### Proof of Concept
On a listed market with zero `borrowed`: call `repay(payer, amount = pool_balance)`. `resolve_repay` returns `overpayment = amount`, `net_repay = 0`, the zero-shares assert passes, `credit_cash(0)` leaves the book untouched, and `transfer_out(payer, amount)` moves the pool's entire token balance to the caller. Subsequent withdrawals revert on insufficient balance despite `cash` reporting solvency — matching `test_unfunded_repay_overpayment_refund_also_pays_out_of_custody` in contracts/pool/tests/flows.rs:3589-3611.
