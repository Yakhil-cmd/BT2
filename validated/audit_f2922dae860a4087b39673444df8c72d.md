### Title
Stale dual-source oracle leg can skew the blended collateral price and enable undercollateralized borrowing - ([File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/engine.rs]) [1](#0-0) 

### Summary

A dual-source oracle accepts two readings independently and publishes their midpoint whenever their prices remain inside the configured tolerance band. [2](#0-1)  The one-hour timestamp-spread check applies only when both legs are declared `Market`; a stale `Fundamental` leg may therefore lag a fresh market leg for much longer without invalidating the blend. [3](#0-2)  A normal borrower can wait for a slow anchor feed to retain an outdated favorable value and call `borrow`, whose solvency check values collateral using the inflated blended price. [4](#0-3) 

### Finding Description

Each feed is stale only when its own timestamp exceeds `feed.max_stale_seconds`. [5](#0-4)  `blend` then marks the result stale for either leg's own stale flag, but compares timestamp ages only for two `Market` sources. [3](#0-2)  `source_nature` maps a plain feed to its provider nature, so a Reflector market leg paired with a RedStone `Fundamental` leg is exempt from the one-hour leg-age spread. [6](#0-5)  If their prices remain within tolerance, the returned price is the arithmetic midpoint rather than the fresh market observation or a rejection. [7](#0-6) 

This configuration exists in production parameters: for example, USDC pairs a one-hour Reflector TWAP leg with a 16-hour RedStone `Fundamental` leg and permits approximately a 5% ratio gap. [8](#0-7)  Other markets permit even longer `Fundamental` ages, up to 26 hours. [9](#0-8) 

On the borrowing path, `borrow` requires only owner or delegate authorization, calls the pool first, and then applies the post-pool solvency gate. [10](#0-9)  That gate prices every collateral position with the cached aggregator price and computes LTV-weighted collateral against debt. [11](#0-10)  Thus an anchor lagging in the favorable direction directly changes the maximum accepted borrow even though the borrower has not changed the true collateral value.

### Impact Explanation

An account owner can use the stale midpoint as collateral valuation and withdraw a larger borrow than the fresh market price supports. [12](#0-11)  If the stale anchor overstates the collateral asset, part of the resulting debt is undercollateralized and can become protocol bad debt after the stale observation is refreshed or expires. [13](#0-12)  This is theft of pool liquidity / potential protocol insolvency, not merely a failed transaction, because the borrowed tokens are paid to the borrower before the solvency check completes. [14](#0-13) 

The in-repo regression test demonstrates the concrete effect: a Reflector primary moves from `$1.00` to `$0.91`, while the RedStone anchor remains `$1.00` after 82,800 seconds under an 86,400-second stale budget. [15](#0-14)  The stale-anchor execution inflates measured collateral by more than 4% and successfully borrows `$70,000` while the fresh-anchor control reverts with `InsufficientCollateral`. [16](#0-15) 

### Likelihood Explanation

No privileged call, forged oracle report, leaked key, or invalid market configuration is required. The trigger is an ordinary `borrow(caller, account_id, borrows, to)` submitted while a configured slow feed still has an accepted but economically outdated observation. [17](#0-16)  Mainnet oracle configurations intentionally allow `Fundamental` legs to remain usable for 16–26 hours, and the deviation check only requires the two values to stay within the configured ratio. [18](#0-17) 

The exploit window exists whenever a fast leg moves and a slow leg's last value remains within tolerance; at a 5% upper ratio the midpoint can still shift the reported asset value by about 2.5% from the fresh observation. [7](#0-6)  The checked-in test reduces this to a deterministic borrowing scenario and proves the control fails for insufficient collateral. [19](#0-18) 

### Recommendation

Do not average legs with materially different observation ages for valuation-critical lending decisions. Apply a cross-leg timestamp-spread bound to all paired sources, not only `Market`/`Market` pairs, and reject or heavily de-weight a leg whose timestamp is older than a short freshness window. [1](#0-0) 

For volatile collateral, prefer the fresher validated market leg or require the blended timestamp to be no older than the risk system's freshness requirement. If long-heartbeat NAV or fundamental feeds must remain configured, use separate collateral-risk settings that account for the delay rather than allowing the midpoint to absorb the stale leg silently. [20](#0-19) 

### Proof of Concept

The checked-in test `audit_borrow_withdraw_liquidate_stale_anchor_blends_5pct_skew_into_ltv` constructs this path directly. [21](#0-20) 

1. Configure XLM with a fresh Reflector TWAP primary and a RedStone `Fundamental` anchor allowed to remain unstale for 86,400 seconds. [22](#0-21) 
2. Move the primary from `$1.00` to `$0.91` while leaving the anchor at `$1.00` with an 82,800-second-old timestamp. [23](#0-22) 
3. Supply 100,000 XLM and call the ordinary borrower path for `$70,000` USDC. [24](#0-23) 
4. The accepted midpoint is about `$0.955`; the test shows collateral valuation inflated by over 4%, the stale-anchor borrow succeeding, and the otherwise identical fresh-anchor borrow reverting with `InsufficientCollateral`. [25](#0-24)

        return false;

}
