### Title
`withdraw` can front-run `clean_bad_debt`/`force_socialize_bad_debt`/`liquidate` to dodge the supply-index write-down, extracting full cash value and concentrating the socialized loss on remaining suppliers - (File: contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs)

### Summary
When bad debt is socialized, `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` writes the market's `supply_index` down pro-rata so every supply share loses value. A supplier who observes a pending `clean_bad_debt`, `force_socialize_bad_debt`, or `liquidate` transaction can front-run it with `withdraw`, cashing out at the pre-write-down index. The escaping supplier's pro-rata loss share is shifted onto all suppliers who did not exit, and the cash removed is no longer available to back the written-down claims.

### Finding Description
Bad-debt cleanup is sequenced, not atomic with supplier exits:

- `Pool::seize_positions` (borrow side) calls `interest::apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index`, which reduces `supply_index` by `floor(supplied·index − bad_debt) / (supplied·index)`, socializing the loss across every live supply share (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/seize.rs:23-28`).
- The controller surfaces that trigger this — `clean_bad_debt` (permissionless once debt > collateral and collateral ≤ $5 dust) and `liquidate` — are submitted as ordinary transactions (`docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-LIQ-04).
- `Pool::withdraw` resolves the payout as `floor(pos_scaled × supply_index)` and transfers it out of `cash` (`common/src/rates/scaling.rs:105-121`, `contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:57-89`). Its only post-mutation gates are `require_reserves`, `require_utilization_below_max`, and `require_supply_for_debt` — all evaluated at the still-elevated pre-write-down index (`contracts/pool/src/ops/withdraw.rs:111-119`).

A supplier watching the mempool sees a `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` tx whose bundle will write down market M's supply index. They submit `Controller::withdraw` for their position in M first. At that moment the index is still unimpaired, so `resolve_withdrawal` pays the full `floor(shares × old_index)` from `cash`. The subsequent cleanup then computes the reduction over a shrunken `supplied` base and with less `cash` on hand, so remaining suppliers absorb a strictly larger per-share loss — including the slice the front-runner shed.

This is the analog of `revokeAll` being front-run by `claim`: a pending administrative/economic event that would reduce a beneficiary's claim can be preempted by pulling the claim at the pre-event rate.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds by ordering: the front-running supplier extracts their pro-rata share of the bad debt as real cash; honest suppliers' claims are written down by more than their true share. Because `cash` leaves the pool before the write-down, the post-cleanup `backing_shortfall` is larger, and if cash becomes insufficient the pool can end up under-reserved (`backing_shortfall > 0`), blocking new supply until `recapitalize`. Magnitude scales with the debt written off: `clean_bad_debt` is dust-capped (~$5), but `liquidate` of a deeply insolvent account and owner-driven `force_socialize_bad_debt` (visible to any watcher) can write down arbitrarily large amounts.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires (a) a publicizable pending cleanup/liquidation transaction carrying a bad-debt write-down, and (b) a supplier of that debt market able to submit `withdraw` ahead of it. Both are realistic on Soroban: liquidation is permissionless and bot-driven, so a pending liquidation tx is observable, and a large supplier of the debt market has every incentive to exit first. The attack is repeatable per write-down event. It is bounded by the supplier's own position and by `require_reserves` — they can only take cash actually present, which is exactly the window before the write-down removes the cover.

### Recommendation
Order the write-down before exit valuation, or make exits at-risk aware. Options:

1. In `Controller::liquidate`/`clean_bad_debt`, apply `seize_positions` (borrow side) for the debt market before the multi-leg collateral flow where feasible, and/or have `withdraw` call `get_bulk_indexes`/simulate the pending socialize so the payout uses the post-write-down index when a liquidation of that market is in-flight.
2. Preferably, treat bad debt as realized the moment the account is insolvent: apply the borrow-side write-down inside the same transaction that first detects insolvency (the liquidator's call), so there is no gap between "insolvency known on-chain" and "index reduced".
3. Short-term mitigation: charge withdrawal valuation on the debt market at `min(committed_index, index after subtracting detected unbacked debt)` — i.e., include a conservative haircut in `resolve_withdrawal` when `supplied_value − cash − debt_value` is negative at the ceil-rounded level.

### Proof of Concept
Setup (standard two-asset market):

1. BOB supplies 100 ETH; WHALE supplies 1,000 ETH (the debt market). ALICE supplies 8 USDC as collateral and borrows 0.002 ETH.
2. USDC price crashes (e.g., `usdc_cents(1)`), making ALICE insolvent; her collateral ≤ $5 dust → `clean_bad_debt(account_id)` becomes permissionlessly callable.
3. A keeper submits `Controller::clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`. The tx will call `Pool::seize_positions` with `AccountPositionType::Borrow`, invoking `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` and writing down ETH's `supply_index`.
4. WHALE observes the pending tx and front-runs with `Controller::withdraw(caller = WHALE, assets = [(ETH_hub, 0)])` (0 = full withdraw). `resolve_withdrawal` pays `floor(whale_shares × old_supply_index)` — the full, unwritten-down value — debiting `cash`.
5. The cleanup then executes: `total_supplied_value` is now smaller by WHALE's claim, and the same `bad_debt` is spread over fewer shares, so `reduction_factor` is smaller → deeper `supply_index` cut for BOB and every remaining supplier. The pool's `cash` is also lower, enlarging `backing_shortfall`.

Differential check (mirroring `test_keeper_clean_bad_debt_decreases_supply_index` in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/bad_debt_index.rs:196-221`):

```rust
let (si_before, _) = get_indexes(&t, "ETH");
t.set_price("USDC", usd_cents(1));
let acct = t.resolve_account_id(ALICE);

// Front-run: WHALE exits at the pre-write-down index.
let whale_cash = t.withdraw_all(WHALE, "ETH");   // pays floor(shares * si_before)

t.clean_bad_debt_by_id(acct);                     // write-down lands after exit

let bob_after = t.supply_balance_raw(BOB, "ETH");
let bob_loss_fr = supplied_before - whale_cash /*...*/ ; // compare vs. no-front-run run

// Compare to a run without step 4: bob_loss_no_fr < bob_loss_fr.
// WHALE's paid-out amount exceeds the post-write-down value of their shares:
assert!(whale_cash > whale_shares_post_writedown_value);
```

The delta between WHALE's actual payout and `floor(whale_shares × si_after)` is value transferred from remaining suppliers to WHALE purely via ordering — the same shape as the vesting `claim` front-running `revokeAll`.
