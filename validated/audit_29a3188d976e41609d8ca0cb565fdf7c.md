### Title
Liquidation accepts a positive debt repayment while every collateral seizure leg floors to zero token units, so the liquidator pays and receives nothing - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs)

### Summary
The bug class from the external report — a calculation returning `0` for a nonzero input due to floor division — is present in XOXNO Lending's liquidation planner, but with a worse consequence than the original usability issue. In `contracts/controller/src/positions/liquidation/math.rs`, each collateral leg's seized token amount is produced by floor-converting a RAY value to asset units (`capped_ray.to_asset_floor(env, feed.asset_decimals)`); when that conversion yields `0`, the leg is silently dropped via `continue` [1](#0-0) . The debt-payment legs, however, still settle in full. The liquidation therefore completes with the liquidator's repayment pulled, the borrower's debt burned, and zero collateral transferred.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` is permissionless: any unprivileged address supplies a vector of debt payments and receives pro-rata seized collateral. Seizure sizing uses directed floor rounding at two stages — the USD→RAY conversion and the RAY→token-unit `to_asset_floor` at `feed.asset_decimals`. When the planned seizure is worth less than one base unit of the collateral token, `capped_amount <= 0` causes the leg to be skipped entirely rather than reverting or clamping [2](#0-1) . The protocol's own documentation confirms the resulting state is valid and accepted: "The contract accepts a liquidation that repays debt and seizes nothing, so the liquidator would pay and receive no collateral" and estimates are an ordered *subsequence* of supply positions precisely because zero-rounded legs are dropped [3](#0-2) . A unit test pins the behavior: `a_leg_whose_seizure_rounds_to_zero_is_skipped_entirely` shows a repayment of 100,000 units against a high-value collateral emits no seizure entry while the repayment still proceeds [4](#0-3) . The zero-rounding is also reachable through the insolvency trim, where kept legs are floor-rounded to whole token units and a leg rounding to zero is dropped while remaining legs' payments still settle [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds: the liquidator's tokens are pulled into the pool and credited against the borrower's debt, while the collateral side delivers zero base units. Unlike the USSD original (a `calculateMint` view returning 0), here the zero result is consumed by a state-changing settlement that debits real tokens. The repayment permanently improves the borrower's position at the liquidator's expense, with no collateral, fee, or refund flowing back — the transfer-mode leg simply does not exist in the executed plan. The condition is reachable in production whenever a fractional seizure falls below `10^-decimals` of a collateral unit, e.g. a small pro-rata repayment against a high-unit-value or low-liquidity collateral leg, or an insolvency trim on an expensive 3-decimal collateral.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Any unprivileged liquidator can trigger it through `liquidate` whenever a planned leg's floor value drops under one token unit — a normal occurrence for small partial liquidations and for accounts whose collateral unit value is large relative to the accepted quote. It does not require an extreme listing: on a 7-decimal asset the leg is dropped whenever the seizure is worth under one stroop, and the SKIILL documentation treats dropped legs as routine enough that bot authors must filter for them. Exploitation requires only that the liquidator not gate on the estimate's `seized_collaterals` being non-empty; the contract itself imposes no such check.

### Recommendation
Reject the whole liquidation when a positive accepted repayment yields an empty seizure vector — i.e. revert with `InvalidPayments` if `seized_collaterals` is empty after the `capped_amount <= 0 → continue` drops, rather than settling the payment legs. Additionally, consider requiring `seized_scaled > 0` per leg *and* at least one surviving leg before pulling any payment, so rounding can never leave a settlement that moves value in only one direction. The `WithdrawLessThanFee`-style precedent (reverting rather than settling a degenerate leg) is the pattern to follow [1](#0-0) .

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual flow, all reachable by an unprivileged caller:

1. Victim account has `HF < 1` with a supply leg in collateral `X` (decimals `d`) and debt in `Y`. A liquidator calls `liquidate` offering a small repayment `r` of `Y` such that the pro-rata seizure `s = floor(r * (1 + bonus) / unit_value_X)` satisfies `s < 10^-d` base units of `X` — trivially satisfiable by choosing `r` small, or when `X`'s per-unit USD value is high.
2. In the planner, `capped_ray.to_asset_floor(env, d) == 0`, so `capped_amount <= 0` and the leg is skipped via `continue` [1](#0-0) .
3. The repayment leg is unaffected: the controller pulls `r` units of `Y` from the liquidator via the signed `transfer` authorization, the pool burns `floor`-resolved debt shares, and the borrower's debt decreases [6](#0-5) .
4. The transaction commits. The liquidator's balance decreased by `r`; `seized_collaterals` is empty; no `X` and no refund is delivered — confirmed behavior per the protocol documentation [7](#0-6) .

On-chain equivalent of the pinned unit test: `plan` on a leg with `decimals = 0`, `price_wad = 1_000_000 * WAD`, `amount = 1`, and a `100_000`-unit repayment returns an empty seizure vector while the payment is accepted [8](#0-7) .
