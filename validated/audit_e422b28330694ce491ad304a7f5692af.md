### Title
Permanent market freeze: i128 ray-value overflow in lazy interest accrual bricks all market verbs (supply/withdraw/repay/borrow/liquidate) - (File: contracts/pool/src/cache/scale.rs)

### Summary
The Erigon bug class is "persistent accumulated state triggers a panic on every flush, so the component crash-loops forever and never self-heals." The XOXNO analog is the accrual path in the pool: every market verb first calls `renewed_market`/`update_indexes`, which recomputes scaled values from stored `borrow_index`/`supply_index`. Once a market's ray-scaled position value exceeds `i128::MAX`, `scaled_to_original` panics with `MathOverflow` on every subsequent call — a permanent crash-loop with no recovery path, exactly mirroring the "panic prevents the DB from ever being cleared" shape of the reference bug.

### Finding Description
Interest accrual is lazy and mandatory: `ops::renewed_market` (used by `recapitalize::accounting` at contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:50, `revenue::accounting` at contracts/pool/src/ops/revenue.rs:40, and analogously withdraw/repay/borrow/liquidate) simulates and commits index growth before any user action. The accrual unscales debt shares via `scaled_to_original` using checked i128 arithmetic that panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` on overflow (same pattern as contracts/pool/src/cache/cash.rs:28-29 and contracts/pool/src/time.rs:18-19).

`MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` is intended to cap index growth, but the ray-value of a whale book overflows `i128` long before the index cap engages. The repo's own harness demonstrates this: `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361) drives a 1e9-token 18-decimal market at ~98% utilization on the XLM stress curve until `update_indexes` reverts with `MATH_OVERFLOW`, then confirms `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` revert with the same error — the index cap never engages and nothing clears the poisoned state.

Unlike Erigon, where the fix was to skip the bad range and continue, there is no "contiguous prefix" to salvage here: accrual is a pure function over stored state, so the same overflow recurs on every invocation of every entrypoint touching that market — `controller.supply/withdraw/borrow/repay/liquidate/clean_bad_debt/recapitalize` and `pool.update_indexes` all funnel through the same accrual and all panic.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds and a contract unable to operate for that market: all supplied liquidity and all borrower collateral linked to the market become unwithdrawable; bad debt cannot be cleaned (`clean_bad_debt` accrues first); `recapitalize` cannot inject cash (contracts/pool/src/ops/recapitalize.rs:50 calls `renewed_market` before crediting). This is the accepted "permanent freezing of funds / protocol insolvency" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by unprivileged addresses through ordinary `supply` + `borrow` on a large, high-utilization market; no privileged role needed. However it requires a whale-scale book (order 1e9 whole tokens at 18 decimals) sustained near maximum utilization for years on a steep rate curve before the cliff is reached, and governance could in principle list only saner curves — so likelihood is low, severity Medium.

### Recommendation
Cap accrual on the binding constraint rather than the index: track the largest ray-scaled total (debt or supply shares × index) per market and halt index growth (or clamp the index) before `scaled_to_original` can overflow. Alternatively, store debt/supply in a wider fixed-point representation, or make the index cap engage on the *value* ceiling — e.g., precompute per-market `borrow_index_max = i128::MAX / scaled_debt` — so `update_indexes` saturates instead of panicking. A panic in the mandatory pre-step of every verb must never be a reachable terminal state.

### Proof of Concept
See tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:321-361 (`a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap`), which supplies `BILLION * 10^18` of a BIG18 market, borrows 98% of it against COL collateral, advances time until `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` returns `MATH_OVERFLOW` with `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, and then shows `try_withdraw_raw` and `try_repay` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` — the market is bricked with no entrypoint able to clear it.
