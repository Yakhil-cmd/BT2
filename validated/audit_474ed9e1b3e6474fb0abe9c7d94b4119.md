### Title
Permissionless `update_account_threshold` lets any address overwrite a foreign account's stored liquidation tuple — (`File: contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs`)

### Summary
`update_account_threshold(caller, has_risks, account_ids)` authenticates only `caller`, then iterates an arbitrary, caller-supplied `account_ids` list and rewrites each target's stored per-position risk attributes (`loan_to_value`, and with `has_risks=true` the `liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus`, `liquidation_fees` tuple) to the current spoke listing values. The anti-abuse gate (`apply_gated_liquidation_params` requiring hypothetical HF ≥ 1.05) is skipped entirely for debt-free accounts, so a stranger can stamp adverse liquidation terms onto accounts it does not own or delegate. This is the direct analog of CVE-2026-5708: an authenticated-but-unprivileged caller modifies another entity's security-relevant attributes through a request parameter (`account_ids`).

### Finding Description
`update_account_threshold` in `contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:124` performs `require_authorized_caller(env, &caller)` — the caller's own auth only — and then calls `sync_account_thresholds` for every `account_id` in the caller-chosen `account_ids` vector, with no owner/delegate check on the target account. `sync_account_thresholds` (line 149) loads the victim's supply positions and applies `refresh_supply_risk_params`, which unconditionally restamps `loan_to_value` (line 35) and, in `FullTuple` scope, calls `apply_gated_liquidation_params`. The gate only protects accounts that already carry debt: `favors_liquidator(...) && !account.debt_free() && !clears_min_hf(...)` returns early (lines 76–88); a debt-free account takes the new tuple unconditionally (lines 90–92), including a lower `liquidation_threshold` and a higher `liquidation_bonus`.

The stored tuple is what the protocol actually reads: HF uses the stored LT, and the liquidation bonus/fee use stored values, per `docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md:361-371`. Crucially, ordinary user flows do not heal it: borrow and withdraw refresh only the stored LTV (`restamp_listed_supply_ltv`, params.rs:44-64), never the LT/bonus/fee tuple. So once an attacker stamps a reduced LT onto a debt-free victim, the victim's next `borrow` is evaluated against the lowered threshold — their borrowing power (`min(stored LTV, stored LT)`) and post-borrow health factor are computed from attacker-installed attributes.

The trigger is a routine governance `edit_asset_in_spoke` that lowers LT or raises the bonus (a normal listing change). Existing positions are deliberately not restamped; the design intends the refresh to happen on the account's own next interaction or via keeper maintenance. Because the entrypoint is permissionless, an attacker can race the listing change and forcibly apply the adverse tuple to chosen debt-free accounts before their owners interact — with no HF floor and no consent.

### Impact Explanation
- **Reduced borrowing power / borrow DoS:** the borrow limit uses `min(stored LTV, stored LT)`; a forced LT downgrade makes the victim's `borrow` under-collateralized under the new stamp and can revert legitimate borrows (temporary freezing of the account's borrowing function).
- **Earlier liquidation:** HF is computed from the stored LT. A victim whose stored LT was forced down reaches HF < 1 at a higher collateral NAV than they would under their position-opening terms, exposing them to liquidation — the attacker profits through the (also attacker-raisable) stored `liquidation_bonus`. Accounts that later acquire debt keep the attacker-stamped tuple since no path re-gates it.
- The batch is unbounded in input (`account_ids: Vec<u64>` with no owner check per element), so the attacker can downgrade many accounts in one call.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a governance listing change that lowers LT or raises bonus on an asset where debt-free accounts hold supply — a routine administrative operation, and the adverse-refresh window is the entire period between the edit and each victim's next supply/withdraw that leaves a balance. The attacker needs only `caller.require_auth()` on their own address; no position, collateral, or delegation is needed. The 1.05 HF floor, which is the documented mitigation for keeper abuse, provably does not apply to debt-free accounts (`params.rs:77`).

### Recommendation
Bind the refresh to the account's authority or make it self-consistent:
- Require `require_owner_or_delegate(account_id)` (or the target NFT owner's auth) for `FullTuple` refreshes, keeping `LtvOnly` permissionless if desired; or
- Apply the same HF-style gate to debt-free accounts by evaluating the *prospective* state (e.g., refuse tuples that would push the account below the floor at its max historical borrow), or simpler: only apply adverse tuples to a debt-free account when initiated by its owner; or
- Have `borrow`/`withdraw` restamp the full tuple through the same gate so attribute freshness is symmetric and keeper-applied stamps cannot diverge from what the account would get on its own interaction.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice opens account `A`, supplies USDC on spoke `s` (LT 8000, bonus 500 bps stamped at position creation). `A` is debt-free.
2. Governance executes `edit_asset_in_spoke` lowering USDC's LT to 6100 and raising the bonus (as in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/keeper.rs:317-322`, which shows exactly this edit pattern).
3. Attacker Bob calls `controller.update_account_threshold(caller = bob, has_risks = true, account_ids = [A])`. `require_authorized_caller` passes on Bob's own auth; `sync_account_thresholds` resolves Alice's NFT owner but never checks Bob's authority; `apply_gated_liquidation_params` skips the HF gate because `account.debt_free()` is true, and writes LT 6100 onto Alice's position (`params.rs:90-92`).
4. Alice calls `borrow`. `restamp_listed_supply_ltv` refreshes only LTV; her HF and borrow ceiling are computed with the attacker-installed LT 6100 instead of the 8000 her position carried — her borrow reverts or is capped, and her liquidation floor is now the adverse stamp Bob chose the timing of.

The test at `contracts/controller/tests/risk/params.rs` / `tests/integration/flows/admin.sh:75-105` already exercises Bob (a third party) successfully restamping foreign accounts, confirming the permissionless reach; it only asserts the HF-gate protection for accounts *with* debt, leaving the debt-free downgrade ungated by construction.
