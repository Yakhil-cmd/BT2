### Title
System-parameter voting (`op_list`, `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, `tps_fee_multiplier`, `threshold_size`) weighs votes by the voter's *current* balance at count-time rather than a fixed snapshot, enabling a majority/flash-balance attack — (File: `main_chain.js`)

### Summary
The network-wide system-vote mechanism (`system_vote` / `system_vote_count` messages, tallied in `countVotes()`) determines critical protocol parameters — including the Order Provider (`op_list`, formerly witness) list and TPS-fee parameters — by summing the *current* stable balance of every address that has ever cast a vote for a subject, at the moment a `system_vote_count` unit stabilizes. Because balance is measured at count time instead of at (or shortly after) the time each vote was cast, any unprivileged unit author can temporarily acquire a large GByte balance, cast a vote, keep the funds until a `system_vote_count` trigger stabilizes, and then move the funds away — exactly the "current-balance voting power" flaw described in the reference report.

### Finding Description
`countVotes()` in `main_chain.js` computes voter weight from the outputs table at the time the recount executes: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

This query selects `DISTINCT address FROM system_votes WHERE subject=?` — i.e., every address that has *ever* cast a vote on that subject, no matter how long ago — and then sums each address's **current** unspent, stable, good-sequence base-asset outputs. There is no requirement that the balance existed at the time the vote was cast, and no lock-in or minimum holding duration is enforced for regular (non-emergency) vote counting.

The only throttle is a time-window expansion loop that requires a minimum aggregate balance share (`SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE * TOTAL_WHITEBYTES`) among addresses that voted within the window, expanding by whole years until satisfied: [3](#0-2) 

This only limits *how far back* votes are looked at — it does nothing to prevent a single address from dominating the balance sum with tokens acquired seconds before the count. The final outcome is chosen either as the top `COUNT_WITNESSES` OP addresses by summed balance: [4](#0-3) 

or as the balance-weighted median for numeric subjects: [5](#0-4) 

Triggering a recount is itself unprivileged: any regular unit (not authored by an AA) may include a `system_vote` message and a `system_vote_count` message. Validation only checks structural well-formedness, not voting power, timing, or ownership duration: [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

Once the `system_vote_count` unit stabilizes, `markMcIndexStable()` queues the subjects and calls `countVotes()` for each, using the balances that exist at that exact moment: [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who can transiently control a large fraction of the circulating GByte supply (via exchange loan, temporary large transfer, or coordinated large buy) can:
1. Post a `system_vote` for `op_list` proposing a malicious/compromised set of Order Providers, or for `base_tps_fee` / `tps_interval` / `tps_fee_multiplier` proposing extreme values.
2. While still holding the balance, post (or wait for someone to post) a `system_vote_count` unit for that subject.
3. `countVotes()` sums the attacker's *current* balance (not the balance at vote-cast time) together with any other addresses that ever voted, and the attacker's balance can dominate the total, deciding the outcome.
4. Immediately after the count stabilizes and `storage.systemVars` / `op_list` is updated, the attacker can dispose of the borrowed/acquired funds.

Because `op_list` directly determines Order Providers, which govern main-chain stability determination (`readWitnesses`, `determineWitnessedLevelAndBestParent`, `validateWitnesses`) and witness-list mutation checks (`main_chain.js`/`storage.js`/`validation.js`), forcing a bad or attacker-controlled OP set can cause **nodes to disagree on unit stability/validity** or degrade the network's ability to confirm new units (e.g., unreachable/malicious OPs stalling main-chain progress). Manipulating `base_tps_fee`, `tps_interval`, or `tps_fee_multiplier` to extreme values can make posting units prohibitively expensive network-wide, i.e., **a network unable to confirm new units** for ordinary users. This satisfies the "node disagreement on validity/stability" and "network unable to confirm new units" impact classes required for validity.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only:
- The ability to post two ordinary units (`system_vote`, `system_vote_count`) from any address, with no special privilege (explicitly rejected only for AA authors).
- Temporary access to a large GByte balance (borrowed/looped/purchased), no different in spirit from a flash-loan-style capital acquisition — feasible given GByte's exchange liquidity, and the code enforces no minimum holding period before the balance counts.
- Timing the `system_vote_count` unit to stabilize while the balance is still held; this is a matter of seconds to minutes on ocore's DAG, well within an attacker's control window.

The `SYSTEM_VOTE_MIN_SHARE`/expanding-year window only guards against *too few total participants*, not against balance concentration by one address, so it does not mitigate this specific attack.

### Recommendation
- Use a fixed historical snapshot of each voter's balance taken at (or shortly after) the time the individual `system_vote` was cast/stabilized, rather than the balance at count time, mirroring the report's recommendation to use a prior block/mci snapshot instead of "current" balance.
- Require voters to hold the balance backing their vote for a minimum lock-in period (e.g., require the referenced outputs to have been stable for N mci/timestamp before the vote), analogous to a voting-power freeze.
- Consider decaying or invalidating very old votes whose balance snapshot could be stale/manipulated, and re-verifying that the same output isn't reused to inflate weight across recounts.
- Add rate limiting or minimum inter-count intervals for `system_vote_count` per subject to reduce the window in which a transient whale balance can be exploited.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker acquires (via exchange withdrawal, temporary loan, or large market buy) a GByte balance representing a large fraction of `TOTAL_WHITEBYTES`.
2. Attacker posts a unit with a `system_vote` message, `subject: "op_list"`, `value:` a set of `COUNT_WITNESSES` addresses under attacker's control (must individually satisfy `checkWitnessesKnownAndGood`), per validation in `validation.js` lines 1861–1883.
3. Attacker (or a colluding third party) posts a `system_vote_count` unit with `payload: "op_list"`; this passes validation (`validation.js` 1913–1923) since it only checks the subject name.
4. Once both units stabilize, `main_chain.js` `markMcIndexStable()` invokes `countVotes(conn, mci, "op_list")` (lines 1657–1667), which sums the attacker's **current** stable balance (lines 1757–1773) against all historical voters and, if it exceeds the required aggregate share and outranks other candidates, sets `storage.systemVars.op_list` to the attacker-controlled list (lines 1850–1871).
5. Attacker transfers the balance elsewhere immediately after the count, having paid only transaction/holding costs, not a share of the actual long-term stake, to control the OP list or fee parameters.
