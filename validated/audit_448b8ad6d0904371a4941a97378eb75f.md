### Title
Accrual `MathOverflow` on overgrown RAY book permanently freezes every market operation - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
The libsndfile CVE is a crafted-input crash during buffer processing: a specially prepared input drives the library into a fault that aborts all work on that object. The analog in XOXNO Lending is an attacker-cultivated market book whose accrued borrow value outgrows the finite `i128` RAY domain *before* the borrow-index ceiling engages: `accrue_step` (called from `global_sync`, which runs first inside every pool verb) panics with `GenericError::MathOverflow` inside `scaled_to_original`. Since every operation — `supply`, `withdraw`, `repay`, `borrow`, `liquidate`, `clean_bad_debt`, `flash_loan`, `recapitalize`, `update_indexes` — accrues first, the market is permanently frozen and all supplier/borrower funds in it are bricked.

### Finding Description
`interest::global_sync` chunks elapsed time and calls `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` for each chunk, unconditionally, before any op logic (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`). The arithmetic lives in `common/src/rates/` (`index.rs`, `compound.rs`, `scaling.rs`), where position/market totals are computed as `scaled_amount * index` in the RAY domain; `common/src/math/fp.rs::checked_sub_nonneg`/`checked_add_raw` and the `mul_*` helpers panic with `MathOverflow` when a product exceeds `i128::MAX` (`common/src/math/fp.rs:13-25`).

The documented bound is that the borrow index caps at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` (10³⁶, 10⁹× the initial RAY index). But the *value* ceiling `i128::MAX` on `scaled_borrowed * index` is reached much earlier on a large book: the harness test `a_whale_market_at_sustained_high_utilization_hits_the_ray_value_ceiling_before_the_index_cap` (`tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`) proves it — a 1-billion-whole-token 18-decimal market at 98% utilization on the XLM curve panics with `MATH_OVERFLOW` inside `scaled_to_original` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`, after which `try_withdraw` and `try_repay` of even 1 unit both revert with the same panic.

The book size is attacker-controllable: supply caps are admitted per listing and can be set near `max_cap_for_decimals` (≈170B whole tokens, `docs/reference/formulas.md:405-439`), and an unprivileged address can `supply`/`borrow` to reach that scale, then keep utilization pinned in the curve's steep segment by its own borrow. No privileged call, oracle manipulation, or malformed-input parsing is required — the "crafted file" is the crafted market state, and the "write access violation" is the unchecked RAY-domain product overflowing in accrual.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds: once the accrued value overflows, every subsequent call that touches the market reverts in `global_sync` before any leg executes, so suppliers cannot `withdraw`, borrowers cannot `repay`, liquidators cannot `liquidate` (so even deleveraging is impossible), and `recapitalize`/`clean_bad_debt` also accrue first and fail. All tokens custodied in that market's pool book are permanently locked, and `claim_revenue` on the book fails too.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a high-decimal market admitted with a near-maximum supply cap and an attacker willing to hold ~98% utilization (borrowing their own supplied liquidity) at the steep part of the rate curve until the value ceiling is hit. The capital cost is large but not privileged, the attacker can recycle most of the borrowed principal, and the freeze can also arise organically on a whale-dominated market — the harness documents the index growing past 170× within the simulated horizon. Medium severity: high impact, gated by capital and a governance-admitted cap configuration.

### Recommendation
In `accrue_step`/`scaled_to_original`, saturate value computations at `i128::MAX` (as `mul_div_floor_saturating` already does elsewhere, `common/tests/math/fp_core.rs:213-223`) and/or stop index growth at `MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY` *before* recomputing the accrued value, so accrual degrades gracefully instead of trapping. At minimum, order the accrual so the index cap is applied before the value overflow can panic, and add a config-time bound relating `supply_cap`, `asset_decimals`, and the index ceiling so no admitted cap can push `scaled * index` past `i128::MAX`.

### Proof of Concept
See `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/large_positions_and_long_horizons.rs:320-361`: build a market `BIG18` (18 decimals, XLM curve) with caps lifted; `supply_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 10^9 * 10^18)`; `borrow_raw(ALICE, "BIG18", 98% of principal)`; advance time in 1-year steps calling `try_update_indexes_for(&["BIG18"])` until it returns `MATH_OVERFLOW`; then assert `try_withdraw_raw(BOB, "BIG18", 1)` and `try_repay(ALICE, "BIG18", 1.0)` both revert with `MATH_OVERFLOW` while `borrow_index < MAX_BORROW_INDEX_RAY`. The market, and all funds in it, are frozen permanently because every entrypoint accrues first via `global_sync` → `accrue_chunk` → `accrue_step` (`contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:20-53`).
