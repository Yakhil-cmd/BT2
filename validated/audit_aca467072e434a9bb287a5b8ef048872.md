### Title
Unauthenticated `last_ball_timestamp` used to satisfy oscript `timestamp` spending conditions - (File: definition.js)

### Summary
The oscript `timestamp` operator, used in address/asset spending-condition definitions, evaluates against `objValidationState.last_ball_timestamp` — a value derived from the self-declared `timestamp` field of whichever `last_ball_unit` the unit's author chooses to reference. This mirrors the sigstore-js bug class (CWE-345): a timestamp that is only loosely bound (not independently, freshly authenticated against real elapsed time) is treated as a trusted time source for a security-relevant decision (unlocking/locking funds by time).

### Finding Description
`definition.js` implements the `timestamp` spending-condition operator entirely off `objValidationState.last_ball_timestamp`: [1](#0-0) 

`last_ball_timestamp` is populated in `validateParents` directly from the `timestamp` column of whatever unit is referenced as `last_ball_unit` by the *unit being validated* — i.e., a value chosen by the poster of the spending unit, not a network-wide, freshly-agreed clock: [2](#0-1) 

That referenced unit's own `timestamp` field is itself self-declared by its original author, subject only to two weak checks at the time it was originally validated: it must not exceed the validating node's local clock by more than `max_seconds_into_the_future_to_accept` (default 5s), and it must not be lower than the timestamps of its own direct parents: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Crucially, there is no *lower*-bound check tying a unit's timestamp to real elapsed time, and no requirement that the `last_ball_unit` chosen by a later unit be recent relative to the current tip — only that its `main_chain_index` not retreat below what the chosen parents already committed to: [5](#0-4) 

This lets a unit's author, when constructing a new unit (posting normally as an unprivileged wallet, or as an AA trigger sender), pick an older, still-valid `last_ball_unit` whose `timestamp` lags behind real time, as long as it satisfies the non-retreat constraint relative to its chosen parents. Because the `timestamp` spending-condition operator trusts this chosen `last_ball_timestamp` rather than any independently-verified, tamper-evident clock, the operator can be evaluated against an attacker-influenced timestamp rather than the actual current time — directly analogous to sigstore-js trusting an unauthenticated `integratedTime` for validity-window decisions.

### Impact Explanation
Any address or asset definition using the `timestamp` operator (a documented Obyte oscript primitive for time-locked or time-windowed spending conditions, e.g. `["timestamp", ["<", X]]` to permit spending only before a deadline, or `[">"...]` to permit spending only after a vesting date) can potentially be manipulated by the spender who chooses which `last_ball_unit` to reference. An attacker who is a party to such a contract (e.g., in an escrow/vesting/expiry address definition, or an AA-adjacent construct relying on `timestamp`) could attempt to satisfy a "before deadline" clause using a stale referenced last-ball timestamp even though real time has passed the deadline, or vice versa, leading to unauthorized spending, fund loss for the counterparty, or freezing of intended conditions.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on whether an attacker can actually construct a unit referencing an old, still-non-retreating `last_ball_unit` while satisfying all other DAG-consistency constraints (parent selection, `latest_included_mc_index` non-retreat, `witnessed_level`, etc.), which in a live, actively-used network is constrained because free/tip units age out quickly as others build on them. This significantly limits how far behind real time the chosen `last_ball_timestamp` can practically lag in a highly active DAG, but the protocol itself provides no explicit binding between `last_ball_timestamp` and current wall-clock time, so the trust gap is structural rather than merely theoretical.

### Recommendation
Do not rely solely on the self-declared, author-chosen `last_ball_timestamp` for time-based spending decisions. Consider requiring that `last_ball_unit` used to satisfy a `timestamp` condition be within a bounded number of MC indices/time of the current known tip, or bind the `timestamp` operator to a value with stronger consensus freshness guarantees (e.g., require it to reference the *unit's own* MC-derived stabilization time once stable, not an arbitrarily old but valid last ball chosen by the spender).

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual reproduction path (protocol-level, not executed):
1. Construct an address definition containing `["timestamp", ["<", DEADLINE]]` as a spending condition (e.g., "refund allowed only before DEADLINE").
2. Wait until real time has passed `DEADLINE`.
3. Craft a spending unit whose `last_ball_unit` references an older, still valid-to-reference stable unit (satisfying `readMaxParentLastBallMci`'s "last ball mci must not retreat" check at `validation.js:668-674`) whose own `timestamp` field is still `< DEADLINE`, while its `parent_units` are current, valid free units.
4. Submit this unit; `validateParents` sets `objValidationState.last_ball_timestamp` from that older unit at `validation.js:737-738`, and the `timestamp` operator in `definition.js:1036-1048` evaluates true, permitting the "expired" refund spend after the real deadline.

Note: full end-to-end feasibility (whether a sufficiently old but non-retreating `last_ball_unit` can be found/crafted against a live, active main chain) could not be independently verified within the scope of this review and would need to be confirmed with a running node/testnet.
