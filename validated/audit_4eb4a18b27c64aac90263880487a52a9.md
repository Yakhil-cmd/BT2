### Title
Attacker-controlled Aquarius LP price outage permanently shields an underwater account from liquidation and bad-debt cleanup - (File: contracts/price-aggregator/src/providers/aquarius.rs)

### Summary
The Aquarius LP price provider rejects any read when the pool's total value falls below the configured `min_pool_value_wad`. Because a borrower can add a dust-supply leg of an Aquarius LP share as collateral without needing its price, any unprivileged LP holder can deliberately drain that Aquarius pool below the floor and make every risk computation for the account fail closed. `liquidate` and `clean_bad_debt` both revert, so the account's debt accrues interest while collateral cannot be seized or socialized — an availability-class analog to CVE-2026-60191's repeatable crash/hang DoS.

### Finding Description
`aquarius::read` computes `pool_value_wad` from live reserves and total shares and returns `Err(OracleError::InsufficientAquariusLiquidity)` when it is below `lp.min_pool_value_wad` [1](#0-0) . Reserves are read directly from the Aquarius pool contract at query time [2](#0-1) , so an LP's own withdrawal lowers the measured value in the same transaction's subsequent reads.

On the controller side, `supply` does not require a price, so an indebted borrower can attach a dust leg of any listed collateral — including an Aquarius LP share — to its account [3](#0-2) . Liquidation builds its plan from `Context`-cached strict prices over the account's legs [4](#0-3) , and `execute_bad_debt_cleanup` consumes the same `AccountRiskTotals` valuation [5](#0-4) . The threat model itself records that such a feed outage "blocks valuation-dependent actions, including liquidation" and "the same leg blocks bad-debt cleanup and force-socialization" [3](#0-2) .

### Impact Explanation
While the LP leg's price read fails, no liquidator can build a valid plan against the account (any offered payment reverts during planning), and `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize`-adjacent force-socialization cannot value the account either. The position's debt keeps accruing interest and cannot be reduced by liquidation or socialized by cleanup. For a deeply insolvent account this converts a recoverable bad-debt event into unbounded insolvency growth — suppliers' backing erodes while the protocol's only unprivileged remedies are bricked. This matches the accepted "protocol insolvency / permanent freezing of collateral seizure" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully unprivileged: the attacker needs (1) a borrowing position that is or becomes HF < 1, (2) a dust supply leg of a listed Aquarius LP collateral (cheap; supply is unpriced), and (3) enough of that pool's LP shares to withdraw reserves below `min_pool_value_wad` — easily achievable for thin pools, and the attacker can withdraw their own liquidity at cost of only swap fees. The mainnet config carries Aquarius-sourced markets with configured `min_pool_value_wad` floors . Persistence requires keeping the pool drained, but the attacker controls that unilaterally; anyone restoring liquidity merely restores normal liquidation.

Caveat: this scenario is explicitly cataloged as STRIDE DoS.1 in the threat model, so reviewers may treat the mechanism as a known design consequence rather than a new defect; the attacker-controllable trigger via own LP withdrawal is the actionable part.

### Recommendation
- Treat a failed LP-price read for one leg as a skippable/zero-valued collateral leg during liquidation planning rather than aborting the whole valuation, or allow `clean_bad_debt` to proceed on unweighted/price-free eligibility when a single collateral leg's feed is down.
- Alternatively, make bad-debt eligibility computable from prices of the remaining healthy legs so a single dead feed cannot freeze cleanup.
- At listing time, size `min_pool_value_wad` high enough that reaching the floor via a single LP withdrawal is economically prohibitive, and alert on pool value approaching the floor.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance lists `AQUA-LP` (Aquarius share token of pool P) as collateral with `min_pool_value_wad = V`.
2. Attacker: `supply(attacker, collateral_token, X)` for cheap collateral, `borrow(attacker, debt_token, D)` near max LTV.
3. Market moves (or attacker uses `multiply`/price drift) so HF < 1.
4. Attacker calls `supply(attacker, aqua_lp_share, dust)` — succeeds without pricing the leg.
5. Attacker withdraws own liquidity from Aquarius pool P so `pool_value_wad < V`.
6. `liquidate(liquidator, attacker_id, [(debt, D)], SeizeMode::Transfer)` → reverts with `InsufficientAquariusLiquidity` during `build_liquidation_plan`.
7. `clean_bad_debt(attacker_id)` → same revert via `AccountRiskTotals`.
8. Attacker keeps the pool drained; debt compounds indefinitely. Net cost: LP withdrawal fees only.
