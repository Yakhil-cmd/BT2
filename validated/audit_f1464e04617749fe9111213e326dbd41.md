### Title
Bad-debt supply-index floor clamp resurrects wiped-out supply shares into claims on fresh deposits - (File: contracts/pool/src/interest.rs)

### Summary
`apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` socializes bad debt by scaling `supply_index` down, but clamps the result UP to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` (`RAY/1000`) when the write-down would push the index below it. Supply share counts (`supplied`, per-position `scaled_amount`) are left untouched while the index is floored, so wiped-out suppliers retain a residual floor-valued claim — a claim that was supposed to be destroyed by the write-down. The first fresh deposit after a near-total wipeout is drained by a stranded holder withdrawing that phantom claim. This is the direct analog of XSA-224's count mismanagement: the teardown path (index write-down) is mismatched with the resource count (supply shares), recreating value that should have been zero.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/pool/src/interest.rs:73-89`, `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` computes `reduction_factor = (total_supplied_value - capped_bad_debt) / total_supplied_value`, then sets `supply_index = max(supply_index * reduction_factor, SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW)`. When bad debt ≥ total supplied value, `reduction_factor` is 0, but the floor clamps the index back UP to `RAY/1000`. No shares are burned: `cache.supplied()` and every account's scaled supply are unchanged. Each scaled share therefore still claims `share × floor` of value, while the pool's `cash` is untouched by the write-down. The repo's own test `test_raw_cache_floor_residual_can_consume_fresh_cash_without_supply_guard` (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:317-369`) demonstrates the full theft: after a wipeout clamps the index to the floor, `unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a) > 0` ("phantom claim"), userB deposits `c` tokens of fresh cash (`mint_supply` + `credit_cash`), and userA withdraws `gross == c`, emptying the pool and leaving userB's honest claim unbacked. The withdraw path passes `require_reserves` because cash exists again — the guards in `contracts/pool/src/guards.rs` check backing/cash, not whether the index was floor-clamped, so there is no supply-side guard against this.

The attack path is fully permissionless: `clean_bad_debt` is a permissionless entrypoint requiring only caller authorization (`contracts/controller/src/lib.rs:160-165`), bad debt is written down via `ops/seize.rs`/liquidation apply, and `supply`/`withdraw` are open.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds. Any supplier depositing into a market whose index has been floor-clamped after a wipeout effectively donates their deposit to stranded share-holders; the pool's cash is drained to zero while outstanding claims remain. Repeated across deposits it is a permanent redistribution of new suppliers' principal to accounts whose claims were already socialized to zero.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a near-total bad-debt wipeout (bad debt ≥ total supplied value in one market), achievable via `clean_bad_debt` on an account whose collateral collapsed below the dust cap after a price spike — the same scenario exercised in `tests/test-harness/tests/controller/same_market_bad_debt_cleanup_arithmetic.rs` and `bad_debt_index.rs`. After clamping, exploitation is a single `supply` by any victim (or attacker bait) plus `withdraw` by the stranded holder; no timing race or privileged role needed. The magnitude per event is bounded by fresh deposit size, but it is deterministic once the market is in the floored state.

### Recommendation
When the write-down would drive the index below the floor, burn the residual claim instead of re-inflating it: e.g., set `supply_index` below the floor is unavoidable, then proportionally reduce `supplied` (and coordinate with the controller's per-account books per INV-ACCT-10), or floor the index only when `remaining > 0` and zero/burn supply shares otherwise. Alternatively, mark the market as wiped out and block new supply/withdraw until recapitalized via `recapitalize`. At minimum, reject `supply`/`withdraw` when `supplied > 0` and `supply_index == SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW` was reached by clamping (tracked via a flag), so fresh cash cannot be captured by dead claims.

### Proof of Concept
1. Market M has supplier A holding `scaled_a` shares at index ≈ RAY.
2. An account's debt in M becomes unpayable (collateral price collapses); any caller invokes `clean_bad_debt(caller, account_id)`; `apply_bad_debt_to_supply_index` caps `bad_debt` at `total_supplied_value`, computes `reduction_factor ≈ 0`, then clamps `supply_index` to `SUPPLY_INDEX_FLOOR_RAW`. A's shares still claim `scaled_a × floor > 0` (see `contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:339-341`, asserting `stranded > 0`).
3. Victim B calls `controller.supply` depositing `c` tokens into M; pool `cash` becomes `c`.
4. A calls `controller.withdraw` (amount `0` = all); `resolve_withdrawal` returns `gross = unscale_supply_floor(scaled_a) = c`, `require_reserves` passes, pool transfers `c` to A (`contracts/pool/tests/interest.rs:351-368` asserts `gross == c` and `cash() == 0`).
5. B's withdrawal reverts or is unbacked: honest supplier lost the deposit to A's phantom claim.
