### Title
Permissionless third-party `supply` overwrites a foreign account's stored liquidation terms (threshold / bonus / fees) via `merge_supply_leg` - (File: contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs)

### Summary
The controller deliberately lets an open supply position keep the liquidation tuple it was created with (`liquidation_threshold`, `liquidation_bonus`, `liquidation_fees`) until a refresh path restamps it. One of those refresh paths is `supply`, which is permissionless for any hub asset the victim already supplies. An unprivileged attacker can deposit a dust amount into a victim's existing collateral leg and force `refresh_supply_risk_params(.., RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple)` to overwrite the victim's stored liquidation terms with the current spoke listing - including terms strictly worse for the victim (a lower `liquidation_threshold`, a higher `liquidation_bonus`, or a higher `liquidation_fees`). This is the lending-protocol analog of the reported bug class: improper permission handling granting an unprivileged caller write access to protected state, leading to loss.

### Finding Description
- `process_supply` restricts third parties only to *existing* supply keys: `require_third_party_existing_supply` asserts `account.supply_positions.contains_key(hub_asset)` for non-owner/delegate callers (`contracts/controller/src/positions/supply.rs:78-97`).
- Every supplied leg then flows through `merge_supply_leg`, which unconditionally calls `refresh_supply_risk_params` with `RiskRefreshScope::FullTuple` on the victim's position (`supply.rs:288-296`).
- `refresh_supply_risk_params` copies `effective_config.loan_to_value` unconditionally and delegates the liquidation tuple to `apply_gated_liquidation_params` (`contracts/controller/src/risk/params.rs:34-37`).
- `apply_gated_liquidation_params` only blocks the write when (a) the new tuple `favors_liquidator` **and** (b) the account has debt **and** (c) the hypothetical HF with the new threshold is < 1.05 (`params.rs:76-92`). `favors_liquidator` is defined as `new LT < old LT || new bonus > old bonus || new fee < old fee` (`params.rs:96-100`).

Two unprivileged attack shapes follow:

1. **Raised `liquidation_fees` applies with no HF gate at all.** `favors_liquidator` does not consider a *fee increase* harmful (only a fee *decrease* is treated as favoring the liquidator). If governance raises `liquidation_fees` on a listing (e.g., during a risk tightening), a stranger can supply 1 unit of the asset the victim already holds and stamp the higher fee onto a position at *any* health factor - including an account sitting just above or even below HF 1. At the next liquidation the victim pays the increased fee on every seized leg.
2. **Lower `liquidation_threshold` / higher `liquidation_bonus` applies while HF >= 1.05.** After a governance LT cut or bonus raise, a victim who would otherwise keep the old, more favorable tuple indefinitely (per `docs/explanation/decisions-r23.md:190-209`, positions keep their stamped terms until refreshed) can be force-restamped by a dust third-party supply as long as the gate's *pre-supply* HF check passes at 1.05 - the victim had no say in accepting the adverse terms, and no final-HF assertion exists on this path (unlike `update_account_threshold`, which asserts HF >= 1.05 at `params.rs:229-233`).

The owner-only counterpart `update_account_threshold` enforces a post-refresh HF >= 1.05 on the *entire* account, but the supply path has no equivalent final check and `clears_min_hf` evaluates HF *before* the new collateral is added (`decisions-r23.md:397-400`), so a dust supply refreshes terms at the weakest possible collateralization.

### Impact Explanation
Theft of user funds / loss of collateral value for an existing borrower. The stored tuple is exactly what `calculate_account_risk_totals`, `get_account_bonus_params` and the liquidation sizing code read (`docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md:360-389` - "HF uses the stored LT ... the base bonus b0 is the USD-weighted stored bonus ... the fee is the stored fee"). By force-restamping:

- a higher `liquidation_fees` reduces the net collateral the victim retains through every subsequent liquidation leg;
- a lower stored `liquidation_threshold` lowers the victim's HF computed on stored params, making the account liquidatable earlier (at higher collateral prices) than under the tuple it agreed to;
- a higher stored `liquidation_bonus` increases the bonus paid out of the victim's collateral to liquidators.

Each outcome transfers value from the victim to liquidators/protocol that the victim's stamped terms would have preserved, and the attacker needs only gas plus a dust deposit in a market the victim already holds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. Preconditions: a governance `edit_asset_in_spoke` (or a listing whose params differ from an older position's stamps) that raises `liquidation_fees`, lowers `liquidation_threshold`, or raises `liquidation_bonus` on a market where a leveraged victim already holds supply. Listing edits are an expected operational flow (the repo contains a full runbook for such an LT change, `docs/reference/runbooks/liqvid-listing-params.md`), and whenever one occurs, every unre-restamped position is a target - the attacker just calls `supply(attacker, victim_account_id, victim_spoke, [(hub_asset, 1)])`. The fee-increase variant has no HF gate, so it works even on near-insolvent accounts; the threshold/bonus variants work while the victim's HF is >= 1.05, i.e., precisely the solvent-borrower population that has the most collateral to lose. Bounded by: the attacker cannot choose the new values (they come from the listing), and the gate prevents pushing an account below HF 1.05 via LT/bonus changes alone - but not via fee increases.

### Recommendation
Decouple risk-parameter restamping from the permissionless top-up path:

- In `merge_supply_leg` (or in `process_deposit`), skip `refresh_supply_risk_params`/`apply_gated_liquidation_params` when the caller is neither the account owner nor an active delegate - the `is_owner_or_delegate` result is already computed in `require_third_party_existing_supply`, so thread it through. Third parties may still fund the leg; only the param write is gated.
- Alternatively, extend `favors_liquidator` to also gate a *raised* `liquidation_fees` (a higher fee harms the account exactly like a lower threshold does), and require `clears_min_hf` whenever the effective tuple is strictly worse for the account holder in any component, not just the currently enumerated directions.
- Consider applying the same post-refresh HF >= 1.05 assertion used in `sync_account_thresholds` (`params.rs:221-234`) to any non-owner-initiated `FullTuple` refresh, so foreign-triggered restamps can never leave an account weaker than the keeper path permits.

### Proof of Concept
Setup: victim `V` supplies `USDC` in hub `H` / spoke `S` and borrows `ETH`; the position carries stamps `LT=8000, bonus=500, fee=100`. Governance later calls `edit_asset_in_spoke` setting `liquidation_fees=500` (and/or `liquidation_threshold=7000`, `liquidation_bonus=900`) on `(S, H, USDC)`.

Attack transaction (any address `A`, no ownership of `V`):

```
controller.supply(
    caller:     A,                       // A.require_auth() - A controls A
    account_id: V_id,                    // existing victim account
    spoke_id:   S,
    assets:     [({ hub_id: H, asset: USDC }, 1)]  // 1 raw unit, dust
)
```

Trace:
1. `process_supply` → `require_authorized_caller(A)` passes (A signs for itself); `require_third_party_existing_supply` passes because `V` already holds a `USDC` supply position (`supply.rs:86-96`).
2. `process_deposit` pulls 1 unit from `A` to the pool and calls `merge_supply_leg` (`supply.rs:133-134`).
3. `merge_supply_leg` calls `refresh_supply_risk_params(.., FullTuple)` (`supply.rs:288-296`).
4. For the fee-only change: `favors_liquidator` returns `false` (fee went *up*, not down), so the HF gate is skipped entirely and `position.liquidation_fees = 500` is written - even if `V` is at HF 1.01 or below 1 (`params.rs:76-92, 96-100`).
5. For the LT/bonus change: `favors_liquidator` returns `true`, but `clears_min_hf` passes while `V`'s pre-supply HF >= 1.05, so the worse tuple is written anyway, with no post-write account-level HF assertion (`params.rs:103-118`; contrast `update_account_threshold`'s final assert at `params.rs:229-233`).
6. `update_or_remove_supply_position` persists the overwritten tuple (`supply.rs:323`).

Result: `V`'s stored liquidation terms are now the adverse listing values. On the next liquidation, `get_account_bonus_params` reads the stored (higher) fee/bonus and (lower) threshold, seizing more value from `V` than under its original stamps. Attacker cost: 1 token unit plus gas.
