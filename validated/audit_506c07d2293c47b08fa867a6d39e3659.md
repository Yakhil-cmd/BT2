**Analysis:**

The scenario describes a normal, expected behavior rather than an admission-boundary defect. `max_gas_amount` is simply a `u64` field chosen by the sender when constructing the transaction — it is never "bound" to a specific `GasScheduleV2.feature_version` at signing time [1](#0-0) . Both `vm-validator`/mempool admission and VM execution independently fetch the *current* on-chain gas parameters at the moment they run via `self.gas_params(log_context)`, and `check_gas` only enforces a floor comparison (`max_gas_amount >= total_rounded_intrinsic_cost`), not an equality binding to a particular schedule version [2](#0-1) .

`gas_schedule::on_new_epoch` only swaps the buffered `GasScheduleV2` in during reconfiguration and is `friend`-gated to `aptos_framework::reconfiguration_with_dkg`, callable only with the framework signer — never by an unprivileged sender [3](#0-2) . `set_for_next_epoch` is likewise gated by `system_addresses::assert_aptos_framework` [4](#0-3) .

`AptosVM::validate_transaction` re-derives `gas_params` fresh at call time and re-runs `validate_signed_transaction`/`check_gas` exactly the same way execution does, so any epoch-boundary schedule change is applied consistently and symmetrically to both admission and execution — there is no scenario where a stale binding lets a transaction execute under rules the VM itself didn't just re-check [5](#0-4) .

Critically, none of this touches sender identity, signer set, sequence number, chain-id, expiry, or replay/domain binding — the boundary conditions require a break in *those* guarantees, not a timing difference in a floor-based fee-parameter check that both admission and execution independently and correctly re-derive. Epoch-boundary gas repricing affecting whether a transaction narrowly passes or fails a `>=` check is an inherent, intended consequence of the versioned config-buffer/reconfiguration design (used identically by other configs like `consensus_config`, `execution_config`, `randomness_config`, etc.) [6](#0-5) , not a sender-binding corruption.
