### Title
Partial deleveraging via `repay_debt_with_collateral` is blocked by the max-LTV gate even while the position stays above the liquidation threshold - (File: contracts/controller/src/risk/validation.rs)

### Summary
The Blueberry finding is a max-LTV check applied to a debt-reducing flow, which forbids partial repayment for positions sitting between the maximal LTV and the liquidation threshold. XOXNO Lending replicates this shape in `repay_debt_with_collateral`: the strategy ends in `strategy_finalize` → `enforce_post_pool_solvency` → `require_post_pool_risk_gates`, which requires `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` (the maximal-LTV-style gate) in addition to `health_factor >= 1e18`. An account whose debt exceeds its LTV-weighted collateral but whose health factor is still above 1 cannot partially pay down debt with its own collateral, even though each unit repaid strictly shrinks the LTV shortfall.

### Finding Description
`process_repay_debt_with_collateral` nets same-market collateral against debt (`net_settle_collateral_against_debt`) or withdraws supply, swaps it, and repays (`repay_via_collateral_swap`), then unconditionally calls `strategy_finalize`, which applies the post-pool solvency gate. That gate, `require_post_pool_risk_gates`, reverts with `InsufficientCollateral` whenever `totals.ltv_collateral < totals.total_debt`, using each supply leg's stored `min(LTV, liquidation_threshold)` weight — strictly tighter than the liquidation health factor, which uses the full `liquidation_threshold` weight.

A position can drift into the band `ltv_collateral < total_debt <= weighted_collateral` (LTV above the strategy maximum but HF ≥ 1) through accrued borrow interest, price movement, or a governance LTV reduction — the test `lowering_ltv_between_borrow_and_withdraw_gates_the_withdraw_but_not_the_stamp` demonstrates the band is reachable by an unprivileged user's position after `edit_asset_in_spoke`. Inside that band, a partial `repay_debt_with_collateral` call reverts even though the operation reduces both debt and LTV-weighted collateral and monotonically narrows `total_debt - ltv_collateral` (removing X USD of debt and X USD of collateral changes the gap by `−X·(1 − ltv)`, always negative since `ltv < BPS`). Only a full close that empties `borrow_positions` (debt-free accounts skip the gate via `account.debt_free()`) can succeed.

The plain `repay` entrypoint applies no solvency gate, so token-funded repayment is always possible; the defect is specific to the collateral-funded deleveraging path, which is the only path available to a user whose repayable assets are locked inside the protocol as collateral.

### Impact Explanation
A borrower in the (max-LTV, liquidation-threshold) band with no external tokens cannot reduce leverage at all without fully closing the position. Partial deleveraging — which would improve health factor, reduce interest accrual, and keep the account away from liquidation — is rejected. The position instead keeps accruing debt until HF < 1, at which point it is liquidated with bonus and fees, or deteriorates into bad debt requiring `clean_bad_debt`/`recapitalize` socialization onto suppliers. This matches the reported impact: forced binary choice between full closure and passive drift into liquidation, converting a recoverable position into protocol bad debt.

### Likelihood Explanation
The band is entered through ordinary, unprivileged mechanisms: borrow-index accrual, collateral price decline, or an LTV parameter change (governance `execute` of a ready operation, which is an allowed reach). Once in the band, any user holding only in-protocol collateral hits the revert on every partial `repay_debt_with_collateral` call. The path is reachable in one transaction: `supply` → `borrow` near max LTV → interest accrual or price move pushes `total_debt` above `ltv_collateral` → `repay_debt_with_collateral(collateral_amount < full)` reverts with `InsufficientCollateral`.

### Recommendation
In `process_repay_debt_with_collateral`, replace the LTV-weighted gate with a health-factor-only gate when the operation does not increase risk — e.g., require `health_factor >= 1e18` post-operation and additionally require that the post-operation `total_debt - ltv_collateral` shortfall (or health factor) is strictly better than pre-operation. Alternatively, exempt strictly debt-reducing strategies from the `ltv_collateral >= total_debt` leg of `require_post_pool_risk_gates`, keeping that leg for `borrow`, `withdraw`, `multiply`, and `swap_collateral` which can increase risk.

### Proof of Concept
1. ALICE supplies 100,000 USDC to spoke S and borrows ETH sized so that `ltv_collateral ≈ total_debt` (near max LTV, HF comfortably > 1).
2. Time passes; the borrow index accrues, pushing `total_debt` above `ltv_collateral` while `weighted_collateral` (threshold-weighted) still covers debt — HF > 1.
3. ALICE holds no external ETH/USDC; her only assets are the supplied collateral. She calls `repay_debt_with_collateral(account_id, collateral=USDC, collateral_amount=X, debt=ETH, swap=<route>, close_position=false)` for a partial X.
4. `repay_via_collateral_swap` withdraws X USDC, swaps to ETH, repays the ETH debt; then `strategy_finalize` → `enforce_post_pool_solvency` → `require_post_pool_risk_gates` computes `ltv_collateral < total_debt` and reverts with `CollateralError::InsufficientCollateral` (validation.rs lines 41–45).
5. Every partial amount X reverts; only a full close (all `borrow_positions` emptied, triggering the `debt_free()` early return) succeeds. ALICE cannot deleverage; the debt accrues until HF < 1 and the position is liquidated — or becomes bad debt.

Supporting code: `process_repay` deliberately skips `enforce_post_pool_solvency` (debt.rs lines 69–91), confirming repay itself is ungated; `require_post_pool_risk_gates` applies the LTV leg unconditionally to any account with debt (validation.rs lines 29–61); the strategy funnels through that gate via `strategy_finalize` (repay_debt_with_collateral.rs line 91); the in-band state is reachable per `governance_change_between_legs.rs` and documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` INV-RISK-01.

Uncertainty note: the gate's application to strategies is documented in `docs/reference/invariants.md` and `math.md`, which could be argued a deliberate design choice; however, the Blueberry precedent treats an intentional-but-overbroad solvency check on debt-reducing flows as a valid Medium, and nothing in the docs states a rationale for blocking health-improving partial deleverage specifically.
